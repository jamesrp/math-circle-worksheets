Week 72: A robot that remembers area

Mathematical kernels

The robot state is integer position (x,y) and counter z. E/W change x by +/-1; N changes y by +1 and adds the current x to z; S changes y by -1 and subtracts the current x. Beginning (0,0,0), the six monotone two-E/two-N paths to (2,2) give EENN:4, ENEN:3, ENNE:2, NEEN:2, NENE:1, NNEE:0. E,N,W,S is a closed unit loop with counter +1; reversal changes the sign. Thus position does not determine the state, and loops leave signed area memory. The group law (x,y,z)(a,b,c)=(x+a,y+b,z+c+xb) is an integer Heisenberg model. This is distinct from merely maximizing area: the new destination is noncommuting motion and extra central memory. Avoid conflating the integral convention with symmetric height conventions differing by xy/2.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.5276 (Heisenberg geometry and signed area, PDF p. 3).

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 4–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Concrete paired exploration first; proof and generalization late. No forced K–1 or separate Grades 2–3 version. These are new, unpiloted prototypes for organizer review.

Materials

Per pair: integer-grid mat, robot counter, movable number line or counter tray for memory, E/W/N/S cards and pencil. Printed columns carry their values to externalize the addition rule. A partner operates the counter and checks the robot, rotating roles. Demonstrate a non-task path with position and memory shown at meaningful intermediate steps.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four focused student pages, each with a substantial investigation; a justified shorter or longer packet is fine. Include unfamiliar-convention visual examples before first use, but do not give away the main discoveries. Keep state recoverable and bookkeeping light. Use partner enforcement of rules, clear next actions, no generic encouragement or decorative flourishes. Honest prerequisite gates matter more than nominal age.
