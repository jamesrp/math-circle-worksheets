Week 70: Four shields in a portal room

Mathematical kernels

In a square flat torus with side length 4, source S=(0,0) and target T=(2,2), every straight trajectory from S to T has a lift ending at (2+4m,2+4n). Its midpoint (1+2m,1+2n), reduced modulo 4, is one of (1,1),(1,3),(3,1),(3,3); therefore four point shields block every trajectory. Four are necessary because the four diagonal half-period paths from S to T have disjoint interiors and require separate shields. Shields cannot occupy source or target. Intermediate visits to the target count as an earlier hit. Distinguish continuous straight travel and wraparound from graph walks. This is finite blocking, not merely the torus lift apparatus in Week 41.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2975 (Lemma 12, PDF p. 12, torus midpoint blocking).

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 4–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Concrete paired exploration first; proof and generalization late. No forced K–1 or separate Grades 2–3 version. These are new, unpiloted prototypes for organizer review.

Materials

Per pair: portal square with opposite edges explicitly paired, four small point counters, pencil and straightedge, plus a tiled unfolded square window for tracing. Use points, not discs with a positive blocking radius. Duplicate seam labels represent one location. Show a simple seam crossing before asking children to use an unfolded map.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four focused student pages, each with a substantial investigation; a justified shorter or longer packet is fine. Include unfamiliar-convention visual examples before first use, but do not give away the main discoveries. Keep state recoverable and bookkeeping light. Use partner enforcement of rules, clear next actions, no generic encouragement or decorative flourishes. Honest prerequisite gates matter more than nominal age.
