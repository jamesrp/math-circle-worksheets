Week 78: Three-armed lines

Mathematical kernels

1. Use the min-plus convention. For a junction (a,b), the corner locus of min(x-a, y-b, 0) consists exactly of three rays: east from the junction, north from the junction, and southwest along slope 1. A point belongs precisely when the least of the three quantities is attained at least twice, not merely when any two quantities tie. The geometry is a tropical line, not an arbitrary three-way road. Adding the same constant to all three quantities does not change it. A nonnegative-cost example min(x,y,2) has junction (2,2), permitting a concrete route into ties before signed expressions are needed. The adult overview should explain both the corner-locus meaning and the fixed orientation.

2. Two such lines always meet. If their distinct junctions share neither an x-coordinate, a y-coordinate, nor an x-y value, their intersection is exactly one point. If they have the same x, they share the north ray from the higher junction. If the same y, they share the east ray from the rightmost junction. If the same x-y, they share the southwest ray from the more southwesterly junction. Equal junctions give identical whole lines. A rectangle between two nonaligned junctions gives an elementary proof: for northwest/southeast junctions use its northeast corner; for southwest/northeast junctions follow the northeast junction's southwest diagonal to the left or bottom side. Equal rectangle width and height is the diagonal shared-ray case. A cropped drawing may hide an intersection; rays continue without end.

3. Dually, two distinct points lie on exactly one such line when they share none of the three special alignments above, and on infinitely many in each aligned case. Finding a junction is a real inverse construction problem. No theorem of ordinary Euclidean lines should be imported automatically. The core uses ordinary set intersections only. A shared ray is not secretly a single point: stable intersection and multiplicity are different notions and need not be taught in this prototype.

Sources: David Speyer and Bernd Sturmfels, Tropical Mathematics, https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Speyer.pdf (min-plus arithmetic and tropical lines). Precise elementary ray classifications and prototype coordinates are independently derived and checked; record their primary-source relationship in the source note. No external diagrams or downloaded papers are packaged.

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 4–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Plot points on an equal-scale square grid, follow horizontal/vertical/diagonal rays, compare small integer amounts, preserve one fixed orientation, and reason about continuation beyond a page. A supported younger child may enter through drawings and comparisons; no separate K–1 or Grades 2–3 edition is forced. Pair construction and counterexamples should precede general proof.

Materials

Per pair: large square-grid working boards, two contrasting pencils, ruler or straightedge, two junction counters, and optional tracing paper for translating a three-armed template without turning it. A transparent sheet is optional; the printable packet must work with ordinary paper and pencil. Rays are straight from the junction in the three stated directions and continue beyond the printed window. Give all grids equal x/y scale and enough working space. Do not require negative-number arithmetic for the concrete first route. Mark intersections and overlaps distinctly; account for all real points, not just grid dots. Physical preparation/handling and classroom use remain untested.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four coherent, substantial student pages; the writer chooses the problems. Preserve the actual geometry and theorem rather than turning the packet into cost-table arithmetic. Introduce an unfamiliar tie or ray convention with a compact non-task visual example, then give children meaningful choice of junctions, constructions and counterexamples. Keep bookkeeping light. Do not reveal the intersection classification before children have useful examples to investigate.
