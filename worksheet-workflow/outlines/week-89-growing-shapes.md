Week 89: Growing shapes and accumulation machines

Mathematical kernels

1. A specified nested tile building law gives differences by removable layers: side-n squares have n² tiles and layer 2n+1 when growing to side n+1; n-step stairs have n(n+1)/2 and next row n+1; n by n+1 rectangles have n(n+1) and next layer 2n+2. The staircase pairing proves its total geometrically. Second differences are respectively 2,1,2. Work with actual layers before number rows. Cubes give n³ and next shell 3n²+3n+1; partition a shell into three disjoint faces to explain it.
2. Accumulation A_0=b, A_n=b+u_1+...+u_n is inverted by consecutive differences. Initial value matters: shifting b shifts every total. Two accumulators use the NEW first total as the second input at each turn, retaining both totals. For input multiset {2,2,1,0,0}, final second total is 5u1+4u2+3u3+2u4+u5; maximum21 descending and minimum9 ascending, proved by adjacent swap. Zero input is still a turn, so second total can change although first does not. Repeated accumulation of ones from zero yields n, n(n+1)/2, n(n+1)(n+2)/6. Three-dimensional staircase piles with coordinates i,j,k>=0 and i+j+k<n realize the third total.
3. Finite observed values never force an arbitrary next value. Two full building laws can agree for steps1–4 and differ next: side-n square always versus side-n square with a tail of n−4 extra tiles after step4. Constant higher differences are an additional law, not established by a short sample. For adult background Δ binom(n,k)=binom(n,k−1), polynomial degree reduces by one, but no formal polynomial arithmetic is needed for entry.

Sources: Ted Alper, Computer Programming in the 18th Century (OK, really, finite differences), Berkeley Math Circle 2012, §§2–3,5–6. Repeated cards on blanks permitted. A third tray and one shared64-cube kit support late visits. Layer cards if needed must be actually supplied and materially usable, not hypothetical. Print actual-size US Letter and equal scaling. The physical cascade has not been rehearsed, state honestly in source notes.

A full non-task old shape → visible added layer → new shape example before growth counts; one complete one-stage move before first use of a running total. For two stages use ONE unchanged turn: e.g. first total1, second total1, input2 → first becomes3, copy its NEW total3 → second becomes4. Show the actual incoming counters or card and keep the same input through all panels. A zero-input turn deserves a later example or task too, but do not let an unchanged first total conceal whether old or new contents were copied. Both retained total trays remain labeled throughout. This complete-operation bridge is essential; three consecutive inputs alone do not demonstrate within-turn copying. The goal (what children seek) must precede terminology. Clear action, explicit building laws, no rule-guessing disguised as a unique next number. No compulsory log of every intermediate total. Reader may read/record but child retains choices.
