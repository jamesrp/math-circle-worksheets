Week 84: Public knowledge

Mathematical kernels

Knowledge means all remaining worlds compatible with private view give the same answer. For cafe private Y/N cards and sequential answers to Does everyone want a drink?, an uncertain first player must want one; a personal N determines no. For three hidden-to-self dolls, public at-least-one-blue removes RRR. Simultaneous proof-blue/not-yet rounds with perfect evidence-complete reasoning produce blue declarations on round k when k blues; red-knowing doll still says not-yet. Need fixed public state within a round. Optional bounded grid0–6 differs from unbounded number puzzle: A=3,B=2 yields ignorance deleting rows0,6; then columns0,1,5,6; then only row3; B then knows.

Sources: Hamkins epistemic-logic slides (Oxford2019), https://jdh.hamkins.org/now-i-know/ ; https://jdh.hamkins.org/cheryls-rational-gifts/ (teacher inspiration only). Classic cafe/blue-eyes mechanisms, original finite doll adaptation.

Suggested emphasis by level: Grades 2–3: three-person cafe and coached configurations; Grades 3–5: dolls, simultaneous public rounds; Grades 4–5 optional finite number grid. No K–1 edition, no transfinite student dialogue.

Materials

Three dolls, reversible labeled B/R headbands hidden to each doll, eight movable configuration cards (RRR removed only after announcement), yes/no preference cards and response cards, large possibilities board. Students coach dolls and physically remove cards. No real-student perfect-reasoner roleplay required.

Draft scope: one selected-band students/materials PDF; substantial independent investigations, essential worked visual before unfamiliar action, large reusable props, no printed theorem answers on student boards. Preparation, optional prompts and mathematical follow-ups belong with adults.
