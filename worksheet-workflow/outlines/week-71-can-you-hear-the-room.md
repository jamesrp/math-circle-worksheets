Week 71: Can you hear the room

Mathematical kernels

A bounce word records the successive side labels struck by a non-corner billiard trajectory in a labeled polygon. For two adjacent sides A and B, the finite word ABA is impossible in a rectangle but possible at an acute 60-degree corner of a 60-degree rhombus. Exclude corner hits. A witnessed word can eliminate a candidate only when impossible there; failure to find one by trial is not proof. All labeled rectangles, whatever their aspect ratio, have the same full bounce-word language by coordinate scaling and unfolding, despite lengths/angles of individual trajectories changing. A finite observation cannot generally determine the room. The activity should involve choosing and physically checking candidate rays, not memorizing a catalog of hidden answers.

Sources: https://justinlanier.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/bounce.pdf (introductory examples, pp. 3–6); https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05690 (bounce spectra and rigidity).

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 4–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Concrete paired exploration first; proof and generalization late. No forced K–1 or separate Grades 2–3 version. These are new, unpiloted prototypes for organizer review.

Materials

Per pair: labeled candidate room drawings (square, nonsquare rectangle, 60-degree rhombus), ruler, pencil, and small mirror or reflected paper copies where useful. Build exact reflected candidate rays using a provided tracing window rather than require children to aim a real ball precisely. Provide explicit input/intermediate/output example for a bounce word, using a non-task word.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four focused student pages, each with a substantial investigation; a justified shorter or longer packet is fine. Include unfamiliar-convention visual examples before first use, but do not give away the main discoveries. Keep state recoverable and bookkeeping light. Use partner enforcement of rules, clear next actions, no generic encouragement or decorative flourishes. Honest prerequisite gates matter more than nominal age.
