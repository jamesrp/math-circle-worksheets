Week 86: Pursuit and escape

Mathematical kernels

1. Finite connected undirected visible-position graph game: cop places first, robber places second, cop moves first; alternate one edge or stay; shared vertex is immediate capture. On finite trees the cop advances on the unique path to the robber, shrinking its possible component. An n-vertex path has optimal worst-case capture time floor(n/2) cop moves.
2. Bare cycles of length >=4 permit perpetual evasion outside the cop's closed neighborhood before each cop turn; a triangle is cop-win. Adding roads can help either player; C4 needs one diagonal to be cop-win, C5 needs two chords (one-chord C5 retracts to C4). Tree branches cannot provide a route around the cop. Concrete board design distinguishes mere play outcomes from forced strategies.
3. Return visits: dominated-vertex fold N[v] subset N[u] preserves cop-win status; successive folds certify a cop strategy. A map can contain a triangle/leaf yet retain an evasive square core. Two cops catch on any cycle by a stationary blocker and a sweeping cop. The full necessity of dismantling is adult-level and not compulsory child work.

Sources: Nowakowski/Winkler, Vertex-to-vertex pursuit in a graph (1983); Quilliot (1978); Bonato/Nowakowski, The Game of Cops and Robbers.
