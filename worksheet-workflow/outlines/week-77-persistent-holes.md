Week 77: Persistent holes

Mathematical kernels

1. A filtration here is a growing subcomplex of a fixed, noncrossing planar triangulation: vertices, edges and filled triangular faces may be added but not removed, and every face of a piece must be present when that piece arrives. Crossed edges are not silently identified as a vertex. A loop is an edge cycle; a loop becomes null when it is the boundary, with double edges canceled, of filled triangles already present. Work over mod 2: using a triangle toggles its three edges, and two copies of an edge cancel. This gives a precise finite test children can enact with edge tokens. A triangle perimeter is a genuine hole before its face arrives and no longer represents a hole afterward. Merely counting drawn graph cycles after triangles fill is wrong. For a finite planar subcomplex, independent hole count is E - V + C - F; the elementary geometry or an independent boundary calculation should justify its use.

2. Filtrations can have the same number of holes at every stage but different persistent history. In a square ABCD with diagonal AC, let the outer rim O = AB+BC+CD+DA close at time 2, add AC at time 4, fill ABC at time 5 and ACD at time 8. Keep other rim edges already present before time 2, and put all vertices at time 0. Writing P = AB+BC+AC and Q = AC+CD+DA gives O = P+Q by canceling AC twice. The hole counts from just before time 2 through times 2,4,5,8 are 0,1,2,1,0. Filling P makes [O]=[Q] nonzero; O dies only when both faces have arrived. Thus intervals are [2,8) and [4,5), whichever half fills first. In two triangular cycles sharing only one vertex, with the first closing at 2 and the second at 4, filling the older at 5 and newer at 8 gives [2,5), [4,8), despite the identical hole counts. Reversing fill order changes this latter pairing. These are statements about inclusion maps on loop classes, not an arbitrary visual decision about which child cavity inherits an old name.

3. The core child-controlled question is what can be built, repaired or scheduled so an older loop survives while newer loops disappear, and what is impossible under the available fillings. A fixed original loop can be tested at each stage by toggling boundaries of filled triangles; this is a valid finite mechanism for the persistence calculation. Ribbons/barcodes are an optional compact record of a discovered history, not the main task. Half-open [birth,death) means the loop is already gone at the death stage. Simultaneous additions must be treated as one stage; zero-duration internal bookkeeping events are not lasting features. Do not claim a persistent feature is necessarily meaningful signal or a short-lived feature necessarily noise, and do not infer a general stability theorem from a few altered stage numbers. Matrix reduction, quotient vector spaces and the general persistence theorem belong to adults; the children's cancellation/tiling acts must stand on their own.

Sources: Herbert Edelsbrunner, David Letscher and Afra Zomorodian, Topological Persistence and Simplification, https://pub.ista.ac.at/~edels/Papers/2002-J-04-TopologicalPersistence.pdf, sections 2–3 for growing complexes, mod-2 boundaries and persistence. The square and joined-triangle teaching models and their exact schedules are independently devised examples, not reproduced source exercises. Record exact source locations and independent algebra in the research note. No downloaded source paper is packaged.

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 4–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Children can follow short edge labels, preserve an unchanged recorded earlier loop, place physical triangular fillings only on present boundaries, and cancel identical edge pairs with support. Concrete building, testing and counterexamples are core; general barcode algebra is an adult extension. A younger child's participation depends on actual command of these representations; no forced K–1 or separate Grades 2–3 edition.

Materials

Per pair: large printed square-with-one-diagonal and joined-triangle boards, a small set of matching paper triangular face tiles, reusable edge markers or short yarn pieces, duplicate small edge-label tokens for canceling, a few stage-number cards, pencil and two contrasting colors. Use nonoverlapping printed planar pieces. Filled triangles should visibly distinguish covered from uncovered interiors; avoid transparent filling ambiguity. Boards should be large enough for handling and duplicate boundary-edge tokens. A partner enforces that a face's full boundary is present first and that earlier pieces cannot disappear. Keep a single physical model and a recoverable snapshot or edge record, not several simultaneous tallies. No distance estimation, precision cutting, complex software or large enumeration is required. Physical preparation, handling and classroom pacing remain untested.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four coherent substantial student pages. The writer chooses the actual problems rather than reproducing a prescribed exercise list. Explicitly demonstrate any unfamiliar boundary-cancellation recording with a small non-task input/intermediate/output visual. Let children choose legal construction orders and test which histories can occur; avoid turn-by-turn teacher-operated algebra, arbitrary matching of visible cavities, or completing many birth/death tables for their own sake. Preserve a concrete first route and readiness-dependent later proof.
