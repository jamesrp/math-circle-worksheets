Week 67: Meeting on shortest roads

Mathematical kernels

For three vertices of a rectangular unit grid with Manhattan distance, the unique vertex belonging to some shortest route for each pair is (median x, median y). For homes (0,0), (4,1), (1,4), it is (1,1). It need not lie on every chosen shortest path, and is not merely the location minimizing total distance. A tree has an analogous unique three-way meeting vertex. The graph that is just a 3-cycle has no vertex on shortest paths for all three pairs. On a cube, the common vertex is coordinatewise majority: 000,110,101 give 100. These are median graphs and intervals. Keep the three pairwise conditions simultaneous on one unchanged board.

Sources: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/ggt.pdf (median graphs, printed pp. 177,180; PDF pp. 197,200).

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 3–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Concrete paired exploration first; proof and generalization late. No forced K–1 or separate Grades 2–3 version. These are new, unpiloted prototypes for organizer review.

Materials

Per pair: three home counters and one meeting counter, three colored pencils, square-grid board and tree/cycle drawings. Roads are horizontal or vertical grid edges, never diagonals. Optional cube uses a paper net or eight connected vertex dots with three binary labels; no specialist blocks required.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four focused student pages, each with a substantial investigation; a justified shorter or longer packet is fine. Include unfamiliar-convention visual examples before first use, but do not give away the main discoveries. Keep state recoverable and bookkeeping light. Use partner enforcement of rules, clear next actions, no generic encouragement or decorative flourishes. Honest prerequisite gates matter more than nominal age.
