Week 92: Fold-and-stretch predictions

Mathematical kernels

1. A deterministic map can amplify uncertainty about its initial state. On a continuous track from 0 to 16, send a point to twice its distance from the nearer end: T(x)=2 min(x,16-x). The point may be between ticks. This is one complete move, repeatedly applied on the same track. On either half, distances between points double, but points on opposite sides can merge: T(x)=T(16-x). Pair separations can shrink or vanish. Interval widths never decrease, but strict increase can fail for a symmetric band crossing the middle. The image of a whole interval is the complete range of possible outputs, not only the outputs of its endpoints. If it includes the middle 8, its maximum output is 16. Example [7,9] maps to [14,16], then [0,4], [0,8], [0,16]. All endpoints here are included. Each state represents uncertainty about one unchanged deterministic run, not a new random choice at each step. Intervals are not probability distributions.

2. For every n>=1, each dyadic cell [16k/2^n,16(k+1)/2^n], 0<=k<2^n, maps onto the entire track after n moves. A direct fold-and-stretch induction shows T^n is affine and onto on each such cell, with alternating direction. Every nonzero-width interval contains a sufficiently small complete dyadic cell, so its iterates eventually cover the track. Initial intervals may grow or keep their widths for a while; they never shrink. Eventual full coverage is the theorem. For [a,b] crossing 8, the image width is twice max(8-a,b-8), at least b-a. The exact point 0 stays at 0, but [0,16/2^n] fills the track after n steps: limited initial precision limits guaranteed prediction. Whole-number marks on a length-16 track all eventually reach 0, but that is an artifact of this finite dyadic grid, not a theorem about all real starts. The fixed point 32/3 supplies a counterexample; the fractional point is adult continuation unless introduced concretely.

Suggested emphasis by level

- K-1: oral optional paired token/folding demonstration only, with an adult; no forced K-1 packet.
- Grades 2-3: concrete tokens, doubling small distances, finding starts that merge or separate, and moving whole shaded intervals using the apparatus; adult reading is normal. No formulas or fractional calculations required.
- Grades 4-5: exact possible ranges, a counterexample to endpoint-only reasoning, constructing long-lived precision, and a dyadic subdivision proof as a readiness-dependent continuation. Understanding every position in a strip, not only numbered marks, is essential.

Materials

Per pair: two 160-mm tracks representing 0-16 with 10-mm unit spacing, two markers, transparent or paper interval strips, ruler, scissors, pencils and whiteboard. Provide an explicit physical rule aid: folding overlay maps both ends toward 0 and the middle toward 8, then a doubled-scale output track turns 0-8 into 0-16. A simpler V-shaped input-to-output map or matched distance strips can serve if mechanically unambiguous. Include a complete small worked visual BEFORE first use: point input, measured nearer-end distance, doubled output. BEFORE the first whole-range task, a non-task interval crossing the midpoint must visibly include the midpoint and show the entire resulting output band; matching labels on every view. Choose a non-task example; the outline examples are mathematical data, not prescribed problems. Give an area to mark possible ranges and enough room for independent experiments, without a long diary table. Launch together after children handle the materials; the apparatus has not been physically rehearsed. Print 100% only if ruler dimensions matter; exact tick coordinates, not paper tolerances, certify answers.
