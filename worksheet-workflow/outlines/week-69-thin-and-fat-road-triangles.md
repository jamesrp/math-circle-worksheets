Week 69: Thin and fat road triangles

Mathematical kernels

A geodesic triangle chooses one shortest path between each pair of three vertices. Every tree triangle is a tripod: each chosen side lies in the union of the other two, so it is 0-thin. In the integer square grid, take A=(0,0), B=(n,0), C=(n,n), AB along the bottom and BC along the right, and AC along the left then top. The corner (0,n) on AC is distance n from the other two sides, measured using any roads in the full grid, not only the triangle boundary. This family prevents a uniform thinness bound for the infinite grid. Finite experiments are not a proof about all triangles or all scales. Do not claim grid triangles must all be fat.

Sources: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/ggt.pdf (thin triangles, printed p. 363; PDF p. 383).

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 4–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Concrete paired exploration first; proof and generalization late. No forced K–1 or separate Grades 2–3 version. These are new, unpiloted prototypes for organizer review.

Materials

Per pair: 3 colored pencils or strings, home counters, ruler for counting grid edges, tree and square-grid boards. Shortest distance to a side means the fewest road edges to any point of that side; roads in the interior remain usable. Child controls where the triangle sides go, subject to being shortest.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four focused student pages, each with a substantial investigation; a justified shorter or longer packet is fine. Include unfamiliar-convention visual examples before first use, but do not give away the main discoveries. Keep state recoverable and bookkeeping light. Use partner enforcement of rules, clear next actions, no generic encouragement or decorative flourishes. Honest prerequisite gates matter more than nominal age.
