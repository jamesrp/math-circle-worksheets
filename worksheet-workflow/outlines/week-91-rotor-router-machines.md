Week 91: Rotor-router machines

Mathematical kernels

1. A persistent two-exit switch displays its next departure. Follow that arrow, then flip it; do not reset between counters. In any consecutive even number of visits the two exit counts agree, and in any number differ by at most one. A complete downward binary tree of depth d sends every consecutive block of 2^d counters once to every leaf and restores all switch states. This follows by splitting even bundles successively. Starting states affect order, and not every proposed order is achievable.

2. For the path with vertices 0,...,m, endpoints absorb, interior next-exit arrows alternate after use, and counters launch one at a time from fixed interior s. Every walk stops: an infinitely revisited vertex would send infinitely many visits along each neighboring edge and hence to an absorbing endpoint. If S counts next-right arrows, each step preserves position+S. Over n counters, mR+S_final=ns+S_initial. Thus every consecutive m launches has exactly s right exits from every initial state, with no burn-in: m(R-s)=S_initial-S_final is divisible by m and has absolute value less than m. Full arrow states need not immediately recur: for m=4,s=1, initial LLR gives LLRL and finishes RLL. Do not conflate endpoint balance and state return.

3. Deterministic balance is not independent randomness. A switch cannot emit XX but fresh coin tosses can. Fair random-walk endpoint probability s/m is optional adult background, with its assumptions distinguished from the rotor theorem.

Sources: Holroyd and Propp, Rotor Walks and Markov Chains, arXiv:0904.4507v3, introduction and Theorem 1 (uses increment-before-moving convention; our next-exit card is an equivalent phase). Levine, The Rotor-Router Model, arXiv:math/0409407, section 1 and Lemma 1.1 (follow-then-change and finite exit). Elementary tree/path proofs above are independently derived special cases, not copied general theorems.
