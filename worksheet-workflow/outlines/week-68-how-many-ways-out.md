Week 68: How many ways out

Mathematical kernels

Ends describe components continuing infinitely far after deleting a finite region, as larger finite deletions are considered. A two-way infinite line and an infinite ladder have two ends. The infinite square grid has one end: any finite blocked region fits inside a box, and sufficiently far-out points can connect around that box. An infinite 3-regular tree has infinitely many ends; removing a center vertex gives 3 infinite components, then centered balls of radius 1 and 2 give 6 and 12 components. A finite drawing is only a window with explicit continuation; boundary exits on a finite board are not themselves a proof of ends. Distinguish a finite isolated pocket from a component continuing forever.

Sources: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~kapovich/EPR/ggt.pdf (ends, printed pp. 287–289; PDF pp. 307–309).

Suggested emphasis by level: one shared Grades 3–5 prototype, by prerequisites. Concrete paired exploration first; proof and generalization late. No forced K–1 or separate Grades 2–3 version. These are new, unpiloted prototypes for organizer review.

Materials

Per pair: a printed road window, blackout counters or opaque paper squares, pencils in several colors. Arrows at every continuation edge identify the intended infinite pattern. Cover vertices and incident roads, never ambiguously cover just a drawn region. Use finite windows to test conjectures and enlarging-window reasoning to discuss forever.

Prototype scope

Aim for about four focused student pages, each with a substantial investigation; a justified shorter or longer packet is fine. Include unfamiliar-convention visual examples before first use, but do not give away the main discoveries. Keep state recoverable and bookkeeping light. Use partner enforcement of rules, clear next actions, no generic encouragement or decorative flourishes. Honest prerequisite gates matter more than nominal age.
