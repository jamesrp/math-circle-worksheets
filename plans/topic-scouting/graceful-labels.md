Week NN: Graceful labels

Mathematical kernels

1. Labelling a network gracefully. A network with m links is graceful when its dots can be given different whole numbers from 0 to m so that the differences along the links are exactly 1, 2, …, m, each once. Paths are graceful (label along the path 0, m, 1, m − 1, 2, …), and so are stars (centre 0). The graceful tree conjecture (Ringel and Kotzig, 1960s) says that every tree is graceful; it is recorded as open in the 2013 edition of Gallian's survey, which reports every tree with up to 35 dots checked, and every tree shape with up to 8 dots is graceful (the 23 shapes on 8 dots checked by computer here). Check the current survey before telling children it is unsolved.

2. A parity obstruction for loops. A loop (cycle) with n links is graceful only when n leaves remainder 0 or 3 on division by 4. Going round the loop, the differences, each taken with a plus or minus sign according to whether the label goes up or down, add up to zero, so the plain sum 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n + 1)/2 must be even. Rosa (1967) showed that those loops are all graceful: the triangle 0, 1, 3, the square 0, 2, 1, 4, and loops of 7 and 8 links (168 and 384 labellings by computer), while loops of 5 and 6 links have none.

3. Complete networks and rulers. The network joining all of four dots is graceful with labels 0, 1, 4, 6: a ruler with marks only at 0, 1, 4 and 6 measures every length from 1 to 6 exactly once. No such ruler exists with five marks and length 10, so the complete network on five dots is not graceful (computer), and none on more dots is (Golomb).

Library: Week 10 uses a parity count on a network (odd islands), Week 29 asks which lengths rods can build, and Week 55 adds sets of numbers; no theme labels networks. New here: graceful labelling, a parity proof that some loops cannot be labelled, the ruler connection, and an open conjecture children can test.

Suggested emphasis by level: K–1 labels paths of three and four dots with cube towers of 0 to 4 cubes (a dot with no tower is 0), measures each link by how many cubes taller one tower is than the other, and tries to get every difference once; counting to 4 and comparing towers, no reading. Grades 2–3 label paths, stars, small trees and loops of 3, 4 and 5 links with number cards, and decide which cannot be done; subtraction within 10. Grades 4–5 prove the loop rule by parity, find the four-mark ruler and see why five dots fail, and test trees of their own against the conjecture.

Materials

Number cards 0 to 10, one set per pair (five sets), each number used once per network. Snap cubes for the K–1 towers, about 15 per pair. Printed networks with dot circles about 3 cm across and a small box beside each link for its difference: paths of 3 to 6 dots, stars, small trees and loops of 3 to 8 links. For the ruler question, a strip of 11 squares numbered 0 to 10 and marker counters. The launch labels a non-task path of three dots with towers and measures both links side by side.
