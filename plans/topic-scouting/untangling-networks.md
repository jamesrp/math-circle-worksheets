Week NN: Untangling networks (planarity)

Mathematical kernels

1. Some networks can never be untangled. A network is dots joined by links. Moving dots (with links that bend and follow) can remove crossings from some networks but not others. If a connected network is drawn with no crossings, Euler's formula V − E + F = 2 holds (dots minus links plus regions, counting the outside). When there are at least three dots and no double links, every region is bordered by at least three links and each link borders at most two regions, so 2E ≥ 3F, which gives E ≤ 3V − 6. The network joining all five of five dots has 10 links, more than 3·5 − 6 = 9, so it can never be drawn without a crossing. Three houses each joined to three wells (K3,3) has no triangles, so every region needs at least four links, E ≤ 2V − 4 = 8, and its 9 links force a crossing. Kuratowski's theorem says these two networks are, in a precise sense, the only obstructions.

2. The fewest crossings, and other surfaces. K5 and K3,3 can each be drawn with a single crossing; the network joining all of six dots needs 3. On a torus, which is Week 41's portal board where leaving one edge brings you back on the opposite edge, both K5 and K3,3 can be drawn with no crossings at all (even the complete network on 7 dots can). By Fáry's theorem, anything that can be untangled with bent links can also be drawn with straight ones, so straight segments and bendy links ask the same question.

Suggested emphasis by level: K–1 untangles small tangled networks of four to six dots made of pipe cleaners until no link lies on another, and tries the houses-and-wells puzzle with two houses (possible) and then three; no reading. Grades 2–3 sort networks into "untangled" and "still stuck", count dots, links and regions in each untangled one and look for a rule, and find a one-crossing drawing of the stuck ones. Grades 4–5 use the region count to prove K5 and K3,3 impossible, look for the fewest crossings, and draw K3,3 on the portal board.

Materials

Dots: paper circles about 5 cm across with a letter or picture, each with a paper clip that link ends twist around. Links: pipe cleaners about 30 cm long, which bend round obstacles and lie visibly on top of each other where they cross. A starter kit per pair: 6 dots and 15 pipe cleaners (five sets; about 20 minutes to prepare the first time, reusable). Printed tangled pictures to copy into pipe cleaners, with each link's two end letters listed beside the picture so the connections are unambiguous. For the older tables, printed dot pictures and pencils, and Week 41's 3-by-3 portal board, if available, for drawing on a torus. A crossing means one link lying across another; links may touch only at dots. The launch untangles one four-dot network on the board.
