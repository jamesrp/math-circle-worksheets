Week NN: Hidden armies (Colonel Blotto)

Mathematical kernels

1. No plan is safe. Each player secretly splits the same number N of soldiers (cubes) among three hills, possibly leaving a hill empty; both plans are revealed together. A hill goes to whoever has more soldiers on it, and an equal hill goes to nobody; the player who wins more hills wins. From N = 4 on, every plan loses to some other plan. Sort a plan as a ≥ b ≥ c soldiers. Since a ≥ 2 when N ≥ 4, the plan with b + 1 on the second hill, c + 1 on the third and the remaining a − 2 on the first wins the second and third hills, so it wins. With 3 soldiers, 1-1-1 is never beaten, and with 1 or 2 soldiers the most even plans are never beaten (checked by computer for every N up to 12).

2. Beating goes round in circles, and some plans are still better than others. With 6 soldiers there are 28 plans; 0-1-5 beats 2-0-4, which beats 1-2-3, which beats 0-1-5. Among the 28, the even plan 2-2-2 is beaten by only 3 (the arrangements of 3-3-0) and beats 12, while 6-0-0 is beaten by 15 and beats none (computer). Because every fixed plan can be beaten, good play has to be unpredictable: the game has no plan that is best against everything, and its solution is a random mixture of plans (Borel, 1921, who began the theory of such games; Roberson, 2006, for the general continuous case). For children the theorem to reach is the counter-plan rule and the cycles; the mixture is an adult remark or a 4–5 continuation.

Sources: Émile Borel, "La théorie du jeu et les équations intégrales à noyau symétrique", Comptes Rendus de l'Académie des Sciences 173 (1921); Brian Roberson, "The Colonel Blotto game", Economic Theory 29 (2006), 1–24; Competitive Kids, "Blotto" (web page, accessed October 24, 2026, https://www.competitivekids.org/games/blotto), a classroom version with 100 soldiers on 3 to 7 fronts that also reports "every fixed plan has a counter". Library: Week 24's decks beat each other in a circle, Week 87's majority rankings cycle, atlas AP-22 builds a randomizer a rival cannot exploit, and Week 58 divides goods fairly. New here: a simultaneous allocation game, the counter-plan construction that shows no plan is safe, and ranking plans by how many plans they beat.

Suggested emphasis by level: K–1 splits 5 or 6 cubes onto three hill mats behind a folder, reveals, compares the towers hill by hill with a win token on each hill won, and tries again to beat the partner's last plan; counting to 6 and comparing towers, no reading. Grades 2–3 hunt for a plan nobody can beat, find a counter-plan for any plan the partner names, and build circles of three plans. Grades 4–5 explain the counter-plan rule for any number of soldiers, find why 3 soldiers is different, and rank the 28 six-soldier plans by how many they beat, pooling the work.

Materials

Cubes, 6 to 10 per child, all alike. A folder or card screen per child. A hill mat per child with three circles about 7 cm across, labelled 1, 2, 3 so that plans can be compared hill by hill. Win tokens (counters), three per game. A plan strip with three boxes for recording a plan as three numbers, and paper for the older tables' tables. The rule that an equal hill goes to nobody, and that a hill may be left empty, must be stated once at the start. The launch plays one game between two adults with 5 cubes each.
