## Recommended revision: Problem 2's question can carry more mathematics

Student page 2, Problem 2 gives four particular labels and asks for their move days. Those are legitimate contrasting finite/unsupplied cases, but after a complete twelve-day run some children may fill in the answers very quickly without posing or testing a general rule. This is an age-fit hypothesis, not an observed classroom failure.

Keep the particular labels and day fields. Add the central goal of finding a rule that works for **any** label, or replace the final prediction sentence with that goal while keeping card 20 as a test. For example, the question may ask when a chosen numbered card moves to B, with 3, 6, 10 and 20 as test cases. Do not tell students the answer, prescribe a table, or require a concurrent running tally. An adult can retain the existing particular-case route as a hint. This would make the named-label investigation more likely to support five minutes of thinking after the first trial.

The physical limit of 24 cards is handled honestly: card 20 enters within the supplied twelve days, but its transfer day lies later. The request to predict without enacting twenty days is appropriate. Retain the guide's placeholder option for an unseen label; do not add forty cards just to permit rote continuation.

## Practical guide changes

1. **Choose one physical state representation.** Guide page 2 lists both two shallow bowls and an A/B mat, then says to use one mat repeatedly. The students' mats already provide two large bowl regions. State whether the cards live on those regions or in actual bowls, and make the other option optional. Do not create two copies of the same state. As drafted, a parent could place bowls on the mat and lose the easy visual inspection of the labels. The supplied mat is a good primary choice; real shallow bowls are also legitimate if card visibility is maintained.
2. **Make the optional finite-label observation operational.** Guide page 3 proposes a cardboard “camera frame” showing labels 1–6 and then 1–12, but no frame is supplied or listed in preparation, and the labels need not form a spatially contiguous patch. Either specify how to make/use this aid, or simply ask children to attend to a named finite set of labels in the current bowls. State explicitly that the cards do not move for this observation. This avoids a new recording system or hidden craft preparation. The finite-window idea itself is excellent and should remain an adult follow-up.
3. **Restrict the day-witness sentence to positive days.** Guide page 3 says that for any proposed finite day N, label N+1 is in A. This is correct for N≥1. At the explicitly included day 0, A is empty and label 1 has not yet been introduced. Write “for any positive finite day N” or handle day 0 separately. The no-single-finite-day conclusion remains correct.

No other guide/student mismatch was found. The guide correctly treats the changed rule as a new experiment and keeps the odd/even answer off the student board.
