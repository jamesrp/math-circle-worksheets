# Week 81 adversarial review

Reviewed 9 October 2026 as the independent critic stage. No draft source or guide edits were made.

## Verdict

The mathematical investigation is substantial and recoverable after a normal launch. The student rules distinguish the capped game from unrestricted *finite* refills, the worked action has an input/intermediate/output, and neither the strategy nor the ending theorem is printed as a discovery answer. Keep those strengths. One material-fit defect needs revision, and the separate guide needs preparation and route alignment before delivery.

## Required revision

**R1: Quantity cards do not fit the reusable bucket board.** Student page 2 draws each bucket 56 mm wide, with 5 mm gaps. Page 6 draws quantity cards 82 mm wide and 55 mm high. A card placed in one bucket crosses its boundary; three quantity cards cannot occupy the three columns simultaneously without overlap. These cards are the actual representation for upper-grade large refills, so their location must remain unambiguous. Make the cards approximately 50 mm wide, or supply a separate three-slot quantity board whose slots fit them. Preserve large legible writing space. This is a verified mismatch in source dimensions, not a claim that a physical trial was performed. Counter fit and handling remain untested.

## Separate facilitator-guide revisions

**G1: State the actual print/cut preparation.** Guide page 2 currently specifies a board, thirty counters and supply but omits the sixteen Problem 2 cutouts and the six quantity cards. Identify student page 2 as the reusable board, page 3 as a sorting-card sheet to cut before class, and page 6 as blank quantity cards. State a realistic number of copies per pair/table and whether quantity cards use pencil or a sleeve with an erasable marker. For the two-bucket task, say which two board columns to use. Keep these directions with adults; no student-page preparation prose is needed.

**G2: Give different first routes by readiness and map them to numbered problems.** The overview places capped play with Grades 2–3 and uncapped length/termination with Grades 4–5. But the single first route sends everyone to the uncapped challenge and calls parity an encore, although Problem 3 already asks for a general winning rule before Problems 4–5. Give a concrete route such as Problems 1–2 for the younger table, with Problem 3 as a readiness-dependent continuation; fluent upper children may proceed from 1–2 to 4–5 and return to 3. The organizer can vary this order, but the guide should make that decision visible. Preserve the parity investigation rather than deleting it for timing concerns.

**G3: Clarify the large-quantity representation in adult handling notes.** The 12-counter → card 12 → card 11 visual correctly establishes a one-counter decrement. Explain briefly how a refill changes an existing quantity card and how one card denotes a bucket's entire contents. For the 100-move target, adults can accept a described run and compute its duration without requiring 100 actual turns; a quantity-card update still corresponds to removing exactly one counter. This avoids quietly changing the move rules when bookkeeping becomes tiresome.

**G4: Add compact answers keyed to the early tasks.** The general parity proof gives the answers, but an adult should not have to reconstruct every small start while managing play. In Problem 1, the first three starts are first-player wins and (2,2,2) is a second-player win. In Problem 2, the second-player group is (0,0), (0,2), (2,0), (2,2); all other cards are first-player wins. Problem 3 has losing starts (2,2,2) and (0,2,0), with the four other displayed starts winning. This can be one short paragraph or table in the guide, after the theorem-first overview.
