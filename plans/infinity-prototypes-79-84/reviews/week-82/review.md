## Mathematical audit

- The finite three-customer investigation has 3! = 6 bijections; only the increasing permutation preserves order.
- In the prepended case, star maps to 1 and original n maps to n+1. The inverse sends 1 to star and m ≥ 2 to m−1. This is genuinely bijective and order-preserving on every customer, not an extrapolation from the drawn prefix.
- An appended star is last. Its image under an order-preserving bijection would be last in the ordinary endless queue, but no numbered customer is last. This is the correct impossibility argument; inability to walk to infinity is not the argument.
- The appended queue has the same cardinal size via star → 1, n → n+1, now without preserving order.
- In the consecutive-block queue, B1 has infinitely many predecessors and no immediate predecessor. Every customer in an endless list of finite pairs has finitely many predecessors. An order isomorphism cannot change that property. Mapping each colored n to its matching colored n in the paired queue gives a full size bijection that violates order.
- The stated ordinal identities 1+ω=ω, ω+1≠ω, ω+ω=ω·2, and 2·ω=ω are correct under concatenation/ordinal multiplication. The guide explicitly separates this operation from commutative surreal addition; retain this warning for adults.

## Age-fit hypotheses and first route

The finite match is a good concrete launch. The main reasoning demand is accepting that an arrow gives a rule for later customers, then testing that rule on arbitrary named positions. This can be retained by some third graders after an ordinary demonstration, but that readiness is not established by grade alone. A useful first route is finite matches and the prepend/append pair, letting children name customers beyond the printed prefix and challenge each other's rule. Keep two blocks and invented arrangements available for children ready to continue or for a return visit. Do not remove them merely because they are abstract.

If children repeatedly read the end of the drawing as the end of the queue, return to a named unseen customer such as 20 and let them place its card/describe its partner. Adults may read, scribe a rule or fetch a requested label; the mathematical choices should remain with children. If the adult has to supply every partner and every obstruction, record that as evidence to revisit the representation. These are operational hypotheses for piloting, not bans or claims of observed classroom failure.
