# Primary-source design map

This batch adapts established mathematics into new hands-on elementary investigations. It does not enumerate the current research frontier or claim to resolve an open problem. The broader literature/open-problem project is separate from this six-family draft delivery.

- **Numbered balls:** the organizer's two-per-day/one-transfer process. The guide proves the finite set formulas, label-specific finishing day, failure of uniform convergence on labels and failure to exchange an infinite count with a pointwise membership limit. A changed transfer rule produces odd/even membership while keeping identical finite counts.
- **Midnight:** James F. Thomson, “Tasks and Super-Tasks,” *Analysis* 15(1), 1–13 (1954), pp. 5–6, [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/15.1.1); Paul Benacerraf, “Tasks, Super-Tasks, and the Modern Eleatics,” *Journal of Philosophy* 59(24), 765–784 (1962), pp. 768–770, [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.2307/2023500). The owned model proves two distinct facts: alternating states have no left limit; pre-endpoint instructions alone permit different separately assigned endpoint states. No physical realization is assumed.
- **Finite games with unbounded durations:** Joel David Hamkins, [Buckets of fish!](https://jdh.hamkins.org/buckets-of-fish/) (March 4, 2017). Fixed finitely many buckets and finite refills ensure termination. A two-bucket start permits arbitrarily large finite durations. The parity strategy survives our capped warm-up; the Take-3 encore explicitly changes the refill cap. These theorems are credited, not new conjectures.
- **Ordinals:** Hamkins, [Counting to Infinity and Beyond](https://jdh.hamkins.org/counting-to-infinity-poster/) (September 2, 2021). We use independently drawn queue/matching boards and explicit inverses or obstructions. Cardinality, order type and the meaning of concatenation stay separate. Poster images are not republished.
- **Surreals and games:** Dierk Schleicher and Michael Stoll, [An Introduction to Conway's Games and Numbers](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0410026v2), Sections 2–3. Red/blue finite stalks give dyadics; zero is a second-player win, and number cuts select the earliest-born permitted number. Game equality in every sum context is an established theorem, broader than finite experiments. Our finite solver supports all printed examples and response proofs.
- **Public knowledge:** Hamkins's [Oxford epistemic-logic presentation](https://jdh.hamkins.org/i-know-that-you-know-that-i-know-that-you-know-oxford-october-2019/) presents classic café/islander mechanisms. We adapt three named dolls and movable configurations, keeping private views temporary and public rounds simultaneous. [Now I know](https://jdh.hamkins.org/now-i-know/), [Cheryl's rational gifts](https://jdh.hamkins.org/cheryls-rational-gifts/), and the [transfinite epistemic solution](https://jdh.hamkins.org/solution-transfinite-epistemic-logic-puzzle/) are adult inspiration only. The optional bounded 0–6 grid has both endpoints and its own finite proof; it is not a truncation that inherits an unbounded puzzle's answer.

Pedagogy checked in authorized originals: Natasha Rozhkovskaya's *Math Circles for Elementary School Students*, Lesson 3 “At the lesson” item 1 (try before organization), Lesson 7 item 1 (missed legal moves), Lesson 8 item 2 (slow copying); *Math Circle by the Bay*, preface viii–x (accessible depth, materials and varying pace). The new adult-scribed records, referee roles and visual possibility cards are our proposed responses. They remain untested. Books are not redistributed.

No full sources or external worksheets are copied. Public source URLs and citations are sufficient to trace the mathematical mechanisms; no private Library IDs, folder links or credentials are needed in the published teaching material.
