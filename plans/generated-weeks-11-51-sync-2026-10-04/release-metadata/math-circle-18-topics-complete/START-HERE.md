## Choose a starting point

Grade labels suggest an entry, not a placement rule. Use the concrete gates on the following pages, let an adult read, move materials or scribe, and choose one or two substantial problems. Finishing the packet is not the session goal. Page/count triples use K-1 / grades 2-3 / grades 4-5.

## Two gates to check before choosing

**Stable matching, slot 27:** K-1 is optional. A child must first apply the demonstrated rule that BOTH members of an unpaired pair prefer each other to their current partners. Otherwise choose another independent activity.

**Flat folding, slot 28:** the adult physical pretest is still unperformed. Try each intended valid demonstration with the actual paper before teaching. Mathematical checks and diagrams do not replace this preparation.

### 11. Chip firing with a sink

Different legal move orders reach the same stable state. Track firing counts and staged addition, then see why the process cannot generally be reversed.

- Entry gates: K-1: count small piles and send one chip along each incident line. 2-3: keep chips remaining separate from firing tallies. 4-5: read ordered states and follow a reason covering every legal choice.
- Materials / prep: Allow 24 identical counters per child, at most 20 mm wide; measure them against the 44 mm working circles. Keep the 60 mm sink distinct. Drawn chips or paper squares are exact alternatives.
- Student pages 6 / 6 / 6; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 6. Adult guide 12 pages.
- Folder: `chip-firing/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1aFYFmblg6DquQwYRAN5WNGGRuQgOTtbw)

### 12. Catalan bijections

Noncrossing pairings, up/down paths and ordered rooted trees encode the same information. Reversing the encoding and splitting at the first return explain complete counts.

- Entry gates: K-1: pair fixed positions and distinguish arrangements. 2-3: track left-to-right one-step rises and falls. 4-5: follow ordered branches and nested instructions. Work deeply in one representation before adding another.
- Materials / prep: Per child: five 40 cm strings, ten 15-20 mm endpoint discs and ten U/D cards. Test disc clearance on the actual print. Pencil arcs can replace string. Keep labels and branch order fixed.
- Student pages 6 / 6 / 6; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 6. Adult guide 9 pages.
- Folder: `catalan-bijections/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1BJroX4fvms8e6SWdaxakG3O9dV4DKheX)

### 13. Route packing and bottlenecks

A route collection and an equally sized blocking set certify a maximum. Rerouting can repair a locally stuck choice while final routes still follow the original arrows.

- Entry gates: K-1: follow arrows, distinguish edges from junctions and count to three. 2-3: organize short routes and closure pairs. 4-5: track reserved arrows, cancellations and a reason covering all routes.
- Materials / prep: Boards, pencils and 12 closure markers per child, 15-20 mm wide. Test markers on short edges; retain visible arrowheads. Use route labels or line patterns as well as color. Sharing a junction is allowed; sharing an arrow is not.
- Student pages 6 / 7 / 7; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 7. Adult guide 10 pages.
- Folder: `route-packing/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1tdvvK8dOdqHa0wD19JqqLj_rqIKyGhDq)

### 14. Polygon triangulations and flips

Complete triangle fillings form a connected space of one-diagonal changes. A pentagon has an odd five-flip return loop; a fan target admits an exact distance certificate.

- Entry gates: K-1: recognize original corners and triangles, draw or direct straight lines, and compare pictures. 2-3: preserve labels and organize a collection. 4-5: compare a construction with a lower bound on the number of flips.
- Materials / prep: For ten: ten straightedges, 20 tracing sheets and at least 30 spare sheets, plus pencils. Label every traced corner. Pattern-block triangles do not model these variable-shaped triangles. No crossings or extra vertices.
- Student pages 6 / 8 / 7; numbered entries 6 / 7 / 7. Adult guide 13 pages.
- Folder: `triangulations/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/15wtB_hzzAhl2fNbq9aH3OBo12DETNISj)

### 15. Nearest-site regions

Pairwise distance comparisons produce bisector half-planes; their intersection explains convex regions, shared ties and why adding a site can only shrink old regions.

- Entry gates: K-1: compare two lengths after a spoken rule. 2-3: compare several lengths, draw straight boundaries and retain all tied winners. 4-5: reason about whole regions and overlapping allowed sides. Coordinates are not required.
- Materials / prep: For ten: ten non-stretch 12-inch strings, ten straightedges, 40 translucent foldable sheets and 20 spare sheets. Rehearse a fold; compare dot centers. Exact symmetry settles ties, not small measurement errors.
- Student pages 8 / 8 / 8; numbered entries 8 / 8 / 8. Adult guide 15 pages.
- Folder: `nearest-sites/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1XtDIUaXochXsBQ9yCUG2xkvgviR1bdSV)

### 16. Three-color triangles

Whole-side labeling restrictions force an odd number of three-label little triangles. Door paths and pairing give the boundary-to-interior parity reason in Sperner's triangle lemma.

- Entry gates: K-1: follow spoken side rules and recognize three different labels. 2-3: trace edges and pair objects as odd/even. 4-5: follow complete local cases and paths. This is a different theorem from subset antichains.
- Materials / prep: For the three youngest children: 135 counters, 15 of each label per child, about 0.4 inch wide. Add letters or symbols to every color. Test fit and leave edges clear for tracing. Label vertices, not faces.
- Student pages 6 / 7 / 7; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 6. Adult guide 11 pages.
- Folder: `three-color-triangles/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1F2MLP9Ej9kMuYcNsTU4YOi9VZsfn6jTs)

### 17. How much memory does a machine need?

Histories requiring different answers after the same continuation must remain distinguishable. This supplies exact lower bounds on the number of memory states.

- Entry gates: K-1: follow an arrow and match an R/B symbol after an oral demonstration. 2-3: keep restart separate from continuation. 4-5: compare the same continuation after two histories, treating the current state as the sole memory.
- Materials / prep: Per table: twelve 4 cm state discs, two markers and twelve R plus twelve B cards. Use one marker per run; the spare compares runs. Hide consumed inputs and avoid extra memory from marker orientation or discarded-card layout.
- Student pages 5 / 5 / 5; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 6. Adult guide 10 pages.
- Folder: `finite-state-memory/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1cuUxX3ZjeUXmst4WO3ubbiUhswYdN24_)

### 18. Error-correcting codebooks

Separating codewords by enough changed positions makes one-error correction possible. Disjoint error neighborhoods turn reliable decoding into a packing-bound question.

- Entry gates: K-1: compare short rows of two symbols. 2-3: keep positions fixed and list every zero-or-one-change outcome. 4-5: distinguish testing one codebook from a bound on all possible codebooks.
- Materials / prep: Per table: 30 two-sided symbol counters, ten five-cell strips, four message pictures and two opaque folders. Cells need at least 2 cm for counters. Mark the left end; hide the original message and strip, including whether an error happened.
- Student pages 6 / 6 / 6; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 6. Adult guide 10 pages.
- Folder: `error-correcting-codebooks/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1h30QF-d5P9TvYAmeEsq94seyS_L6KB6E)

### 19. Subset antichains

A family with no containment can be large, but a partition into chains bounds its size. Matching constructions and chain certificates establish exact maxima.

- Entry gates: K-1: recognize whether all symbols on one card occur on another. 2-3: distinguish containment from equal size. 4-5: follow chains and organize all cards without omissions. Formal set notation is optional.
- Materials / prep: Per table: two separately marked complete 16-card decks with four symbols, 20 selection counters and blank paper. Cut cards beforehand. Keep the empty subset. For a smaller deck remove cards using unused symbols; do not erase symbols and create duplicates.
- Student pages 5 / 6 / 6; numbered entries 8 / 8 / 8. Adult guide 11 pages.
- Folder: `subset-antichains/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1kdzUOTsGkSMmdzklTz6wnZEgnyUfOdUB)

### 20. Averaging and the maximum principle

At a largest-valued averaging vertex, every neighbor must match that value. Propagation explains boundary bounds and, using differences of two fillings, uniqueness.

- Entry gates: K-1: count through six and share into two or three equal piles. 2-3: check neighboring averages simultaneously. 4-5: follow forced equalities; reserve the uniqueness proof for signed differences, and the fraction task for halves and thirds.
- Materials / prep: Per table: about 80 cubes/counters, six stack mats with 3 cm nodes, twelve number cards and eight edge strips. Fixed squares and variable circles must differ without color. Use spare copies for sharing; do not redistribute reference stacks.
- Student pages 7 / 6 / 7; numbered entries 7 / 6 / 7. Adult guide 25 pages.
- Folder: `averaging-maximum-principle/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1O4SC-n6z0yCdg4NISgE07OX_YmOjc2Nu)

### 21. Shortest reflected paths

Reflecting one endpoint unfolds a bent route into a straight-line lower bound. Checking the allowed contact segment distinguishes an attainable minimum from an invalid construction.

- Entry gates: K-1: compare two-leg routes with adult folding/tracing help. 2-3: match reflected points and explain equal lengths. 4-5: distinguish a tested guess from an argument for every permitted contact. Numerical angle measurement is unnecessary.
- Materials / prep: Per table: two 60 cm non-stretch strings, two straightedges, four tracing sheets and ten spare sheets. Rehearse reflections on full-size boards with paper on both sides. Use dot centers and verify the contact lies on the permitted segment.
- Student pages 7 / 7 / 7; numbered entries 7 / 7 / 7. Adult guide 18 pages.
- Folder: `shortest-reflected-paths/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/105QATJ6DYuy8jfh078uAE8YwUMIY_qMf)

### 22. Four-point convex partitions

Any four plane points can be divided into two nonempty groups whose filled convex regions meet. Geometric cases give a finite existence certificate.

- Entry gates: K-1: recognize segments and points inside simple triangles with adult language support. 2-3: use each of four labels once and identify a shared point. 4-5: compare point, segment and filled-triangle cases and explain why they cover the possibilities.
- Materials / prep: Per table: two A-D marker sets, four tracing sheets, a straightedge, pencils and tape. Shade the whole triangle, not only its outline. Tiny gaps or intersections must be checked geometrically; a thick stroke must not manufacture a meeting.
- Student pages 8 / 8 / 11; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 7. Adult guide 22 pages.
- Folder: `four-point-convex-partitions/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1wqfpu_h24mYa75Rfwi6DEs_CgbkMWUjB)

### 23. Sorting networks and fixed machines

A fixed compare-exchange machine can be certified by binary tests via the zero-one principle. Swap histories give exact small lower bounds on the number of comparisons.

- Entry gates: K-1: compare lengths or dot counts and follow three lanes. 2-3: retain the same comparisons for every start and cover cases. 4-5: distinguish a counterexample, a complete audit and a minimum proof; threshold/history arguments have separate gates.
- Materials / prep: Per table: a four-lane mat at least 24 x 30 cm, sixteen cards at least 3 cm square and eight comparator strips plus spares. Printed input boxes are too small for these cards. Move cards together; compare only the bar's endpoints.
- Student pages 6 / 6 / 7; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 7. Adult guide 13 pages.
- Folder: `sorting-networks/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1boVdvsyr0KMG-Psf3E3h-4aCKwuR3Pp5)

### 24. Nontransitive dice and decks

Three fair decks can each beat another on more than half of all pairs. Complete pair counts prove the cycle; small-deck impossibility explains why fewer cards cannot always do the same.

- Entry gates: K-1: compare values through nine and keep deck identities. 2-3: organize every pair, including repeated physical cards. 4-5: follow complete case counts and order-based impossibility arguments. Fractions are not a concrete-entry requirement.
- Materials / prep: Per table: three opaque bags or separately shuffled decks, nine 4 x 6 cm cards, nine spares and eighteen counters. Replace cards between independent draws. Some extensions need duplicate cards or 10-12. Sticker-modified dice are not automatically fair.
- Student pages 8 / 7 / 7; numbered entries 8 / 7 / 7. Adult guide 14 pages.
- Folder: `nontransitive-dice/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1HRCZ5ybbHJKALLreLUa00_MzQrpLm1Lr)

### 25. Discrete tomography and hidden pictures

Row and column counts may hide different pictures. Rectangle switches preserve them; nested rows certify uniqueness, and two-row boards admit an exact shortest-switch rule.

- Entry gates: K-1: count through four and match labeled row/column counts. 2-3: count through six and coordinate both conditions. 4-5: reason about complete reconstructions and a general transformation. A shadow is a whole-line count, not a nonogram run clue.
- Materials / prep: Per table: two labeled grids, twelve counters at most 18 mm wide, count cards 0-6 and recording paper. Printed cells are 22 mm at actual size. Preserve both compared pictures; draw each discovery before reusing counters. One counter per cell.
- Student pages 7 / 6 / 7; numbered entries 6 / 5 / 5. Adult guide 19 pages.
- Folder: `discrete-tomography/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1WfG8bd1Ncp0YhJqv3AEymQvMqCF-tDXD)

### 26. Polyomino perimeter and shared edges

Each shared edge cancels two boundary edges. Connectivity certifies the maximum perimeter; row/column bounds and near-square constructions certify the minimum for a fixed area.

- Entry gates: K-1: build edge-connected shapes and count exposed unit edges. 2-3: distinguish area from perimeter and track cancellations. 4-5: compare a lower bound with an attaining construction. Square-root notation is not required.
- Materials / prep: Per table: 24 equal roughly 20 mm tiles, forty boundary markers and matching grid paper. Measure printed scale. Use a separate 24 x 24 cm mat or table space for long shapes so the sheet does not impose a false bound. Hole edges count too.
- Student pages 6 / 6 / 6; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 6. Adult guide 19 pages.
- Folder: `polyomino-perimeter/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1VVrlR5gA51zwhtn2MV3j4fHfKNCay6xc)

### 27. Stable matching and two-sided choices

With strict, complete rankings on two equal sides, deferred acceptance ends without a blocking pair. Comparing proposing sides shows that stable need not mean unique or best for both sides.

- Entry gates: K-1 is OPTIONAL: first demonstrate that BOTH members of an unpaired pair prefer one another to their current partners; use another topic if this gate is absent. 2-3: read ranks with help and check both preferences. 4-5: track proposals, holds and rejections.
- Materials / prep: Per table: two sets of four tokens, eight complete 120 mm preference strips plus spares, four links and sixteen proposal markers. Use 20 mm icons; older packet diagrams are not cut-out templates. Keep holds tentative and remove unused labels from every list. Never rank real children's friendships.
- Student pages 6 / 5 / 6; numbered entries 6 / 5 / 6. Adult guide 18 pages.
- Folder: `stable-matching/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1xdcCG7fJr0eKMYCf9FOcx4v8cFw7evB6)

### 28. Single-vertex flat folding

Alternating sectors give an obstruction to closing flat; mountain/valley counts and constructions classify four right-angle rays. Necessary tests, sufficient witnesses and physical trials remain distinct.

- Entry gates: K-1: match four ray positions and M/V labels on adult-prepared models; adults can fold. 2-3: compare angle wedges with a half-turn. 4-5: track alternating sectors and justify scope. Fine-motor difficulty is not the mathematical gate.
- Materials / prep: REQUIRED adult physical pretest, still unperformed. For ten: thirty thin 18 cm disks, ten spares and six demo disks; tabs and adult measuring tools. Prepare the guide's three separate wedge sets before use. Printed disks are records, not folding models. Adults cut in advance; loose wedges never replace an intact specimen.
- Student pages 6 / 6 / 6; numbered entries 6 / 6 / 6. Adult guide 17 pages.
- Folder: `single-vertex-flat-folding/` | [Open private Drive topic](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/11ePq2E3nrAG7bt-fB0acwuh3jf5wk4sH)
