Week 9: Bouncing paths - return visits

Mathematical kernels

1. Prescribed TWO-bounce wall words are an inverse path problem distinct from Week21's existing shortest one-contact mirror routes. In an ideal rectangle mark distinct strictly interior S and T whose x coordinates differ and whose y coordinates differ; the displayed pairs meet this nondegenerate condition. Seek all ordered pairs of walls that a path from S to T can touch with exactly two specular bounces, excluding corners and any extra wall contacts. Repeat wall names are allowed as candidate words but must be tested. Reflect T through the last wall, then through the first wall: the straight line to that image is the sole candidate for that word. Fold back and CHECK ACTUAL CROSSING ORDER; an image alone does not prove legality. Same-wall words are impossible. Each of LR,RL,BT,TB is legal for strictly interior endpoints. Perpendicular wall reflections commute, but for their shared target image only one traversal order works, unless it hits a corner and neither works. On4x4, S=(1,1), T=(2,3): legal words LR,LT,RL,RT,BL,BR,BT,TB (8). With T=(3,3): LR,LT,RL,BR,BT,TB (6); LB/BL and RT/TR hit corners. These are independently exact traced. For the first target squared route lengths are LR53,RL85,BT37,TB101,LT25,BL25,RT41,BR41; optional shortest comparison belongs with adults and is not a separately counted investigation. Children can construct/check using rulers, tracing paper or mirrored copies without fractions or roots. A small non-task one-bounce incoming/wall/outgoing visual establishes the reflection convention only; do not count it as a fourth investigation or reveal the two-bounce enumeration. Ordered wall words provide a light recoverable record.
2. Interior starting dots can make loops rather than corner finishes. On a6wide4high table with45degree NE start at an interior lattice dot(x,y), a corner is reached iff x-y is even (CRT condition modulo gcd=2). Among15interior lattice dots,8reach a corner and7never do. For the latter, the first return to the same dot WITH THE SAME direction is24 diagonal unit steps (direction is part of the state), because2lcm(6,4)=24. A visual marker on a moving counter keeps its direction visible. Returning to the point with different direction is not the stopping condition. This changes initial state and termination, not only table size.

3. Self-crossings of45degree corner-to-corner paths: for a/g=u,b/g=v coprime, number of distinct interior transverse crossings is(u-1)(v-1)/2. Scaling both dimensions does not change the number of crossings. No segment retraces before the first terminal corner. For coprime2x3,3x4,4x5 there are1,3,6crossings;4x6 also has1. An elementary proof counts interior reduced-grid lattice points(i,j) of equal parity. Each is visited twice by opposite slopes, and there are half of the(u-1)(v-1)points because at least one ofu-1,v-1 is even. Other interior crossings cannot occur: slope+1 and-1lines from integer reflecting walls have even integer intercepts. Crossing two pencil lines is one location, not two events; wall contacts and terminal corners are excluded.

Current Week9 F09-v4 fully develops corner/bounce/lcm rule, so no repeat of those tasks. Archived grades6-7 extra also contains rational-slope launches and one midpoint periodic example. Week21 already treats shortest one-contact mirror paths, so that kernel was removed. Its parallel return-visit companion optimizes two contacts on parallel walls; both use repeated reflection, but this investigation instead enumerates rectangle wall words and rejects wrong orders/corner hits. Reflection itself is not counted as new. Here prescribed two-bounce wall words, a complete interior-dot corner/loop classification, and intersection geometry are distinct continuations; acknowledge those ancestors rather than claiming rational slopes or looping are new themes.

Suggested emphasis by level: grades2-3: try two-bounce wall words and mark crossing points; ideal reflection introduced with a small visual. Grades4-5: interior loops, new launch rules and crossing explanations. K-1 can join an adult's floor walk, but no forced independent packet: direction memory, ordered wall contacts and crossings are genuine prerequisites. Reading of short labels, rulers and following a slope; no lcm prerequisite for physical entry. Discrete steps label direction without requiring simultaneous counts.

Materials

Per pair: ruler, coloured pencils, one small counter with a visible NE/SE/NW/SWdirection arrow or four direction cards. Grids at equal horizontal/verticalscale, enough room to trace exact paths;12mm squares are sufficient for pencil work, bigger for counterwalk. The table is idealized, not a real billiard experiment. For two-bounce paths, use ruler-straight rays and equal-angle reflection; a mirror copy or folded tracing paper can check a candidate. No fraction/root arithmetic is an entry requirement. Adult may demonstrate with a taped6x4floor grid if available; no physical fit pretest claimed.

Return-visit scope and format

This is a separate companion to the current week, not a replacement or a one-hour completion plan. Create one shared student PDF `draft/return-visit.pdf`, with three distinct numbered investigations (normally one page each, more if space is needed). Use page-level headers for genuine suitable bands. Do not make three copies or invent a K-1 version of a kernel that lacks a young-child entry. This explicit scope overrides the harness's three-band filenames. Number Problems 1, 2, 3 consecutively. Provide enough purposeful examples and work space to sustain an investigation. Use existing manipulatives and short shared rules; any unfamiliar representation needs a small worked visual before first use, using a non-task instance. The visual must show input, a meaningful intermediate and output with matching labels, without giving away the intended discovery. Leave problem choice to the writer, but each of the three kernels must support one substantial investigation. Sources and novelty facts below are mathematical context, not prescribed problems.
