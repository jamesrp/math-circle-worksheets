Week 6: Code breaking - return visits

Mathematical kernels

1. Three outcomes can carry more information than two. In the equal-pan weighing model exactly one of nine otherwise identical objects is heavier; its identity stays fixed. A weighing puts equal numbers on two pans, and returns LEFT, RIGHT or BALANCED. Splitting nine into three groups of three, then the remaining group into three singletons, identifies the heavy object in two weighings. One weighing cannot distinguish nine objects because it has at most three outcomes. Actual mass need not be measured: a keeper knows the heavy label and gives the outcome mechanically according to which pan contains it. This is a new measurement/decision connection, rather than base yes/no or exact-position scores.

2. A strategy that minimizes the most questions need not minimize the total over unequal frequencies. For four messages used 4, 2, 1, 1 times in a batch of eight, an adaptive yes/no tree with depths 1,2,3,3 costs 14 questions in total; balanced depth2 costs16. Full binary trees with four leaves have depth multiset 2,2,2,2 or 1,2,3,3, so14 is optimal. Questions may name any subset, responses are truthful, and identifying a final remaining object costs no extra confirmation question. The physically counted eight-message batch is an elementary entry to average cost; no probability notation is needed.

3. Concatenated variable-length counter words can be ambiguous even when each object has a different word. A=R, B=RY, C=Y makes RY mean either B or AC. With A=R, B=YR, C=YY no word begins another, and left-to-right decoding is unique. But the prefix condition is sufficient, not necessary: A=R, B=RY is uniquely decodable (each Y must attach to the immediately preceding R), though A is a prefix of B. Children can join word tiles, remove spaces, and find two different messages with the same row. This concerns segmentation and compression, not Week18's damaged fixed-length transmissions.

Sources: elementary decision-tree and prefix-code arguments derived above; current Week6 F06-v4 checked for fixed-row scoring and worst-case search; Week18 codebook topic deliberately excluded. No external activity is copied.

Suggested emphasis by level: K-1: an adult can hold the secret and enact simple three-outcome searches with three objects; a full nine-object guarantee is a grades2-5 investigation. Grades2-3: physical searches and joining/splitting short counter words. Grades4-5: optimal total cost and sufficient versus necessary unique-decoding rules. Reading: short labels read aloud; counting up to9; no division/probability required for entry.

Materials

Per pair: nine cards labelled1-9, one folder, nine counters or blocks, two paper pan areas; keeper points LEFT/RIGHT/BALANCED without revealing the heavy label. No true balance is required. For message problems, four distinguishable blocks, eight frequency cards (four A, two B, one C, one D), two-colour counters or R/Y cards and slips for word tiles. Counter rows are records, not calibrated templates. No cutting requirement for printed sheets; scraps suffice. Keep the original fixed-code rules off this separate weighing page.

Return-visit scope and format

This is a separate companion to the current week, not a replacement or a one-hour completion plan. Create one shared student PDF `draft/return-visit.pdf`, with three distinct numbered investigations (normally one page each, more if space is needed). Use page-level headers for genuine suitable bands. Do not make three copies or invent a K-1 version of a kernel that lacks a young-child entry. This explicit scope overrides the harness's three-band filenames. Number Problems 1, 2, 3 consecutively. Provide enough purposeful examples and work space to sustain an investigation. Use existing manipulatives and short shared rules; any unfamiliar representation needs a small worked visual before first use, using a non-task instance. The visual must show input, a meaningful intermediate and output with matching labels, without giving away the intended discovery. Leave problem choice to the writer, but each of the three kernels must support one substantial investigation. Sources and novelty facts below are mathematical context, not prescribed problems.
