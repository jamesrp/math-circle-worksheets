# Editorial notes for the next eight-family batches

These are concerns to investigate, not an answer key or substitute for source checking. Keep the original mathematics, but improve the exact investigation freely. Apply AUTHOR-BRIEF.md and REVIEW-CONTRACT.md. Do not reuse an already-solved instance just because a second atlas card uses a neighboring topic. A satisfying general proof may require a supplied theorem and an honest advanced gate.

## ad2: AD-09 through AD-16

- AD-09: Week 4 already explored modular orbits. Compatibility of noncoprime clock reports and constructing a joint schedule offer a new destination. A general CRT claim needs proof of existence and uniqueness in its stated scope.
- AD-10: The triangular-square recurrence can generate an impressive next example; distinguish preserving solutions from proving that all positive solutions arise this way. If completeness is claimed, provide a descent proof.
- AD-11: Build and test genuine four-element field operations. The symbol in GF(4) is not an integer modulo four. Learner choices and a structural failure of the competing ring can make the distinction substantive.
- AD-12: No rational root alone does not prove a length nonconstructible. Use an explicitly supplied constructible-degree theorem plus an irreducible cubic, and mark that gate honestly. Do not pretend a paper-folding surrogate proves the original restriction.
- AD-13: A monomial staircase can have an accessible game; its quotient-space dimension needs a basis argument, not only counting dots. Keep degree truncation and the ideal's actual rule distinct.
- AD-14: Dual numbers should reach the derivative/product rule and the meaning of first-order information, with discarded higher powers explicit. Do not conflate them with ordinary small nonzero real numbers.
- AD-15: Parameterization should explain completeness and the exceptional basepoint or infinite slope. Consider design/decoding a rational point, not only plugging into a supplied formula.
- AD-16: In F5 the curve y²=x³−x has seven affine points and a point at infinity. Modular dots must not be connected into a misleading smooth real curve. Group-law assertions require nonsingularity, exceptional cases and the right gate; decide what is a satisfying finite core.

## ga2: GA-09, GA-10, GA-12 through GA-17

- GA-09: Real wave/heat equations need derivatives and partial derivatives. A picture alone does not establish the evolution law. Give a design or discrimination problem, not just differentiation practice.
- GA-10: The atlas uses the tent map, not a doubling map. Symbolic itineraries need a convention for midpoint/boundary ambiguity; proving a periodic itinerary requires a genuine inverse-branch/fixed-point argument.
- GA-12: Separate geometric and harmonic tails; a finite numerical list does not prove divergence. The grouping proof can show H16>3 strictly. Let learners choose grouping or budgets.
- GA-13: The minimax affine approximant to x² on [−1,1] is 1/2; prove both optimality and uniqueness if claimed. The alternation/lower-bound argument is the payoff.
- GA-14: Frequencies 1 and 5 alias at times n/4. Let learners design an extra observation, and state exactly which candidate class it distinguishes. Do not overclaim recovery of every signal from finitely many samples.
- GA-15: Four Walsh patterns can offer a real basis/decoding investigation. Supply enough concrete representation to avoid a naked matrix worksheet; check all scaling conventions.
- GA-16: Window convolution should let learners move an actual window, predict breakpoints and derive the formula. Endpoints have zero measure but may matter for the pointwise input definition.
- GA-17: The rank-one integral equation has a critical parameter λ=1 in the original normalization. Derive the consistency condition and full solution classification; do not conflate no solution with nonunique solutions.

## ap2: AP-11 through AP-18

These are mostly actual mathematical physics. Do not force them into K–5 or hide model assumptions.

- AP-11: Conservation of flux uses cross-sectional mean velocity unless uniform velocity is explicitly assumed. Distinguish volume flow, mass flow and speed.
- AP-12: Fastest shoreline crossing needs an explicit speed model and allowed route class; calculus establishes the optimizing refraction law. Check endpoint and global-minimum cases, not only a stationary point.
- AP-13: Interference requires coherent amplitudes/phases and consistent intensity normalization. A brightness sketch is not a proof of the wave addition rule.
- AP-14: Carnot/entropy claims need consistent Kelvin temperatures, heat signs and reversible-versus-irreversible scope. Avoid claiming that a chosen toy bookkeeping game independently proves thermodynamics.
- AP-15: A real two-state quantum model can make measurement order concrete. State normalization, update rule and which observables/measurement basis are used. This is a restricted model, not all quantum theory.
- AP-16: Four-spin Ising weights are tractable enough to enumerate fully. Define energy and boundary conditions before counting configurations or quoting a partition function.
- AP-17: Lorentz/proper-time excursion needs explicit units, inertial segments and treatment of the turnaround. The traveling-clock result is not obtained by a naive symmetry argument.
- AP-18: Circular Newtonian orbit inference is model-dependent. State the central-force and circular-orbit assumptions, distinguish radius and diameter, and derive inverse parameters with correct dimensions.

For every batch, make every printed prompt useful, fully keyed and visibly gated. Avoid a fixed number of blanks that reveals an answer count before a discovery question. Plan realistic work space rather than shrinking text. Keep complete solutions in the guide.

## ad3: AD-17 through AD-24

- AD-17: Avoid repeating AP-01/GA-05 averaging as the same worksheet. The useful new question is a change of coordinates that gives exact convergence time bounds and separates finite equality from a limit; allow learners to design an initial pair or mixing parameter.
- AD-18: Track signed area, ordinary area and invertibility as different objects. A visible shear needs a cut/reassembly or determinant argument, not only an inaccurate grid count. A singular map cannot be inverted on the whole plane.
- AD-19: Quiver decomposition is a real linear-algebra gate. Let learners choose bases to expose killed, transmitted and unreachable directions. A rank example with all three block types may be stronger than one with no unreachable target. Prove basis existence and inventory independence in the stated field/dimension scope.
- AD-20: Matrix commutators should reach a structural identity, not stop after three multiplications. Prove Jacobi by expanding ordered triple products; a successful E/F/H instance is only an example. Keep the bracket's nonassociativity separate from matrix multiplication's associativity.
- AD-21: Matching colors is a legitimate finite pullback. Its substantive destination is the unique mediator preserving both records, with existence and uniqueness separately argued for arbitrary token assignments.
- AD-22: Define cycles by even degree and legal face moves by F2 toggles. Be precise about which faces are filled and which diagonal crossings are vertices. Complete the quotient classification, not just one unerasable loop.
- AD-23: A two-inventory signed bookkeeping game alone does not establish K0(Q×Q). Keep the actual module/idempotent/projectivity argument at its honest algebra gate. Explain precisely what the concrete group-completion model preserves.
- AD-24: Rotations only unless reflections are explicitly added. Nonuniform orbit sizes are the reason naive division fails. Give a fixed-pair counting proof, not an unexplained Burnside formula. Avoid preallocating exactly the number of final necklaces.

## ga3: GA-18 through GA-24 and GA-27

- GA-18: Let learners choose the loss rule and see its consequence. Squared, absolute and maximum error can yield different optimizers; only claim the cases actually proved, with interval lengths weighting the integral.
- GA-19: Actual derivatives are essential. Small function values alone do not control slopes. A family beating every proposed bound is stronger than one narrow spike, and the norm conventions must remain explicit.
- GA-20: General least quadratic effort needs an admissible class (continuous piecewise C1, finitely many pieces is enough), a fundamental-theorem argument and equality characterization. A two-speed comparison alone is not the whole variational proof.
- GA-21: Reflection gives a global lower bound if the crossing lands on the allowed mirror segment. If it misses, a boundary optimum needs its own argument. A ruler/string construction can precede coordinate arithmetic.
- GA-22: Cover convex quadrilateral, point-inside-triangle and collinear cases; decide explicitly whether coincident labeled points are allowed. A partition of all four points must have two nonempty groups. An attractive example does not prove the general Radon case.
- GA-23: A sphere arrow lives in the moving tangent plane. Specify the rule for transport along each great-circle arc; show how turns at vertices are handled. A 3D physical model is useful, but the route and all direction vectors must be exact. Avoid implying a flat compass suffices.
- GA-24: “Stretch without tearing” needs a precise continuous bijection with continuous inverse for the invariant argument. Distinguish removing a point of a geometric loop from removing an entire combinatorial edge.
- GA-27: Flooded sets include the equality boundary. At level zero, meeting at one point really connects the two wedges. Prove within the actual clipped square, where boundary effects can otherwise invalidate a whole-plane claim; calculus only for Morse/Hessian interpretation.

## ap3: AP-19,20,22,24,25,27,28,30

- AP-19: The shallow-water travel-time model is supplied and has actual assumptions. Nonuniqueness from one measurement should be exhibited by distinct depths; design an additional measurement and prove what it identifies.
- AP-20: A feasible production plan plus feasible resource prices gives a complete optimality certificate. Explicitly distinguish indivisible and fractional production. Do not require a general LP theorem when the elementary weighted-resource inequality proves the chosen instance.
- AP-22: Random choices must be independent and privately sampled. A best response to one opponent is not a worst-case guarantee. Matching lower and upper certificates establish the minimax value; define which player pays whom.
- AP-24: Independent with-replacement parent sampling makes the model exact. An unchanged conditional expectation does not mean a typical realized population remains mixed. State how finite survival probabilities imply eventual absorption.
- AP-25: Stoichiometric invariants constrain reachability but need not prove it in an arbitrary network. For the selected single reaction, show every claimed state is reached by legal nonnegative steps. Distinguish molecule count from conserved quantities.
- AP-27: Sensor observability needs the actual evolution and output law. Equal decay rates lose a direction; unequal rates can identify both components from two readings. Nonnegative-state constraints and noisy-data extensions should be handled explicitly if included.
- AP-28: This has overlap with Week 6 coding; the new destination is optimal length for four messages under one-bit correction, with a sphere-packing lower bound and an attained construction. Actual corrupted-word examples and all pair distances need checking. A given codebook is a scaffold only if the task's construction claim is framed accordingly.
- AP-30: The meter adds an actual branch. Label ground, source, resistances and measured nodes unambiguously. Kirchhoff balance must use the loaded circuit; consider a learner choice of meter resistance and an error-tolerance design question.
