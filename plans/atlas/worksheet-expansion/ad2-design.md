## Design arcs and decisions

### AD-09 — Can both schedules be right?

The learner invents possible/impossible reports for synchronized four- and six-position clocks, then repairs an inconsistent notice by changing exactly one coordinate one step. The finite core reaches a complete classification: same parity, one report per twelve-step period. This is a new destination after Week 4 modular movement and Week 9 common multiples. An eight/twelve-clock transfer distinguishes recognizing a pattern from copying a parity slogan.

The advanced page supplies Bézout and asks for necessity, constructive sufficiency and uniqueness for arbitrary positive moduli, including modulus one. The guide derives the gcd obstruction and the first nonnegative representative, with a deliberately negative intermediate Bézout solution. The extension combines three reports by repeated two-clock solving. Remainders and two coordinated labels are the actual early gate; integer divisibility and Bézout are the theorem gate. Promising for a pilot with those prerequisites.

Production needs clock faces, an open timeline and a full candidate-report matrix. Do not premark compatible cells or supply exactly twelve answer slots.

### AD-10 — Can the next match hide between our answers?

The 36-counter arrangement openly revisits the original card and figurate-number work. It is an entry, not the intended proof destination. Learners re-encode triangular-square equality as x²−2y²=1, test the proposed forward machine, and discover that generation alone does not establish “next.” Inverting the machine exposes the substantive task: prove the positive predecessor inequalities, decreasing integer coordinate, and base case (3,2). Reversing the descent proves that every positive solution occurs once, in increasing order.

That proof certifies 1,413,721 as the first matching total above one million, with the next total 48,024,900. Exact arithmetic is useful but is not the completeness argument. The extension derives the recurrence for totals and again distinguishes generation from exhaustiveness. Algebra, inequalities and positive-integer descent are explicit; counters alone do not give the main payoff. Advanced/specialist assessment. The general Pell classification is not assumed.

Production needs open ledgers and a small optional counter mat, without a preset number of iterations or solved dot arrangement.

### AD-11 — Choose a rule that never loses a symbol

Keep four two-bit cards fixed but let learners choose t²=t or t²=t+1. Under the first rule multiplication loses information; under the second every nonzero multiplier has an inverse. Supplied polynomial-quotient facts establish the ring laws and unique remainders honestly. The learner supplies the complete inverse certificate, tests both quadratic moduli, and refutes identification with ordinary arithmetic modulo four.

The new destination is a hidden affine machine: two distinct input-output reports determine exactly one machine over the successful field, even when outputs coincide. A chosen pair of reports has no solution under the competing ring, while a separate example demonstrates nonuniqueness there. The Frobenius extension is an exact nontrivial automorphism. This revisits bits and modular arithmetic but makes their different algebraic roles explicit. Promising with distributivity and inverse/affine-algebra prerequisites.

Production needs separate rule tables and coefficient cards. Keep inverse partners blank and preserve the distinction between t, u and integer residues.

### AD-12 — Which cube volumes can these tools reach?

Learners choose exact square-root constructions, identify what their equations actually certify, and contrast an exact cube edge with close rational bounds. The new obstruction uses volume three rather than the original volume two, then classifies all positive integer cube volumes. A monic cubic with no rational root is irreducible; the explicitly supplied constructible-degree theorem then excludes noncubes. Zero is a separately labeled degenerate case; negative physical volumes are excluded.

The intersection explanation includes line/circle degeneracies and the square-of-radius detail. The theorem is stated as necessary, with degree 2^r for r≥0; it is not misread as a general sufficient criterion. A fourth-root construction shows why “irrational” is the wrong obstruction. The guide extension derives the 20-degree cubic and the limited universal-trisection conclusion. Actual core gate: rational polynomials, cubic irreducibility, algebraic degree and the supplied quadratic-tower theorem. Exact geometry is an earlier stop. Advanced/specialist assessment.

Production needs blank construction space, an actual unit segment and a labeled geometric-mean semicircle; it must not imply that decimal accuracy constructs a cube root.

### AD-13 — Move a corner, change the quotient

Four incomparable forbidden corners define a staircase in the unbounded nonnegative grid. Learners choose a search organization, certify that no survivor lies off the page, test redundancy, and move exactly one corner right or up to save exactly two positions while retaining finiteness. All eight possible moves are keyed, including two that create infinitely many survivors. The chosen instance and redesign question replace the original small staircase.

The algebra continuation proves a unique surviving-monomial remainder: both spanning and independence are essential. It uses the ideal's divisibility rule rather than a false total-degree cutoff. The final question identifies the joint kernel of multiplication by x and y, including why different shifted survivors cannot secretly cancel. The rectangle extension includes r=1 or s=1. First-pilot candidate for grid-ready learners at the finite-boundary stage; polynomial linear combinations and independence gate the actual quotient dimension.

Production needs continuing-axis arrows and supplied corners, with no pre-shading, survivor count or one-slot-per-answer scaffold. The guide should show worked before/after staircases at production stage.

### AD-14 — Can one calculation remember a rate?

Exact formal pairs with ε²=0 begin with learner-designed zero products, inverse products and a complete unit classification. Learners then run a chosen seed through (z²+1)(z−2), preserving both coefficients at every operation. A pair of distinct polynomials with the same dual output exposes what first-order information forgets.

The proof derives f(a+bε)=f(a)+bf′(a)ε by counting surviving expansion terms. It then proves product and chain rules by coefficient comparison. The student introduction supplies the finite-root fact needed to pass from all rational evaluations to a polynomial identity. The extension keeps ε² by changing the relation to ε³=0 and derives the complete second-order coefficient, including a nonzero quadratic seed. This goes beyond the original inverse examples and differs from AD-13's monomial-basis question. Signed rational algebra and the polynomial power rule are honest prerequisites; ε is never an ordinary tiny real number.

Production needs a spacious two-column calculation graph, not prefilled derivatives. The number of intermediate operations is supplied by the chosen program, not a hidden solution count.

### AD-15 — Encode a rational point with one slope

A rational secant from (−1,0) on the new ellipse x²+2y²=1 is a message to be encoded and decoded. Learners choose slopes, calculate exact points and recover a supplied slope. Factoring out the known root leads to the complete forward formula; its inverse proves uniqueness and completeness. The exceptional basepoint is explicitly the infinite-slope parameter.

Clearing denominators produces generalized integer triples; the claim is about coordinate ratios, not an unsupported claim that every unreduced integer triple equals the raw formula. The extension classifies their common divisor for coprime p,q, including p=0. A cubic through a known rational point has no distinct rational second point on the selected horizontal line, precisely exposing the degree step that made the conic method work. This revisits the original circle method with a changed conic and encoding/decoding destination. Fractions, factoring and symbolic completeness are actual gates.

Production needs the correctly scaled ellipse, with y-semiaxis 1/√2, and an accurately marked basepoint. Use no pre-drawn solution secants or second points.

### AD-16 — Can these finite points make one cycle?

The new curve y²=x³+x+1 over F5 replaces the original y²=x³−x. Learners choose how to enumerate points, certify completeness, predict inverse pairing and test a coefficient change. This finite core is accessible at the modular-arithmetic gate and is a valid early stop.

The advanced stage normalizes projective triples and proves that exactly one point is outside the affine chart. The discriminant is nonzero modulo five. The group theorem, including associativity, is explicitly supplied; formal partial derivatives are explicitly gated for the singular comparison. Full addition rules include identity, inverse pairs, doubling and vertical exceptions. A learner-chosen orbit can expose either a shorter subgroup or a generator; the displayed successful orbit is independently checked and gives a bijection with Z/9. That representation then solves equations with unique, absent and multiple answers. The extension classifies all point orders and all subgroups. We do not infer arbitrary associativity from a drawing or claim security from a tiny curve.

Production needs a discrete labeled grid, no connecting smooth curve, an open orbit ledger and the point-at-infinity card only at the appropriate stage. Do not preallocate eight point blanks or nine orbit positions. Enumeration is promising with gates; projective/group work is advanced/specialist.

## Source evidence
