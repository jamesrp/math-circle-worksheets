# AD-01–08 investigation designs

Design handoff, September 25, 2026. The eight original cards and accepted ten investigations are unchanged. This batch has **25 staged student pages, 50 keyed student prompts, eight solved guide-only extensions**, prerequisite lines for the contents, and exact diagram/card data. Every printed prompt has a solution and at least one delayed hint. All eight families are unpiloted.

Files: [data](ad1-data.json), [exact audit program](ad1-checks.py), [audit results](ad1-checks-results.json). This is the design stage only: no PDF, student renderer, or artifact-operation marker has been produced by this author.

## Shared design decisions

The root README, trial DESIGN-BRIEF, trial README assessment, trial REVIEW and relevant `plans/lesson-format-source-notes.md` passages were read. The source-supported teaching lessons are concrete exploration, flexible pace, manageable copying, repeated explanations and optional deeper work. The exact activities, staged page counts, movement breaks and prerequisite lines below are editorial choices. They are not source claims about age suitability or measured engagement.

Each family begins with a choice of object, pattern, collection, network, calculation or renaming. A second representation or proof then earns its place: four property types, mismatch witnesses, chain partitions, an inverse code, road-incidence counts, common bounds, least closed supersets, or translated equivalences. Starting diagrams contain neither the key construction nor a completed answer table. Later reference cards deliberately supply a method only after an initial attempt. Source definitions and convention differences are recorded in the data. No uniform grade label hides the reasoning gate.

## Substantive changes and source decisions

### AD-01

The atlas's one converse example has become a sequence of smallest countermodels and complete inference checks. Adding the promise that a square exists makes the difference between a universal constraint and an existence witness tangible. The four-type certificate applies to all finite worlds, not just the objects on the mat. The last page separates negation of a universal from a universal negation, including a nonempty world with an empty red class. An empty tray is explicitly a game convention: the cited standard first-order structures have nonempty domains. The arrow extension has a proved minimum of two people.

The Open Logic satisfaction definition was revisited directly. No compactness, completeness or general decision-procedure claim is made. Prior-use overlap is the year-1 truth material and Week 5 consistency, not the exact counterworld task.

### AD-02

The input words differ from the atlas example. The learner first finds any escape and then faces an opponent's list. A later method card is a purposeful handout. Five rows in four positions distinguish “this recipe no longer assigns one position per row” from “escape is impossible”; all sixteen words give the sharp boundary. The last page asks why adding the newly missing sequence to the top does not complete an infinite list, guarding against a fixed-universal-counterexample misconception.

Open Logic's binary-sequence result is **Theorem 4.17**, printed pp. 57–59, not Theorem 4.18 (the power-set statement). That passage was read directly. The optional infinite argument shares its mechanism with accepted-trial **GA-29** and is labeled as a revisit; the finite adversarial game is the new destination. Week 6's code identification is a distinct question.

### AD-03

The full deck is supplied. The first optimization is paired with an explicit six-chain partition that the facilitator can reveal gradually. The new forced-singleton variant drops the optimum from six to four and turns the original deck into a smaller subset problem. Complementation explains the corresponding forced-triple case. This also proves that a mixed-size antichain has at most four cards in the four-symbol problem.

Stanley's Sperner statement and nearby proof were revisited. Our specific chain partition and its restricted variant are original finite certificates, exhaustively checked. The guide-only five-symbol continuation has a complete permutation-counting argument and an explicit fractions/permutations gate, rather than claiming that the n=4 picture proves the general theorem.

### AD-04

The main arc is communication and recovery, not a copying catalog. New exact tree/message instances lead to a five-label prediction problem: the code determines every degree before the tree is drawn. Both inverse directions are justified by the degree-minus-one occurrence invariant and construction backward by leaf attachments. The final general count is conditional on understanding that induction, not on noticing 16 and 125.

Stanley's appendix uses the **largest** leaf; this design consistently uses the **smallest** leaf. Reversing label order converts the conventions, and the guide contains a checked mixed-convention failure. General tree counting, labeled versus unlabeled trees, and drawings versus edge sets are explicitly separated. The accepted GA-25 grid-tree work is acknowledged; an invertible code is new mathematics.

### AD-05

The original eight-tree count is now a foundation for a new choice: which single road closure preserves the most network options? The diagonal belongs to four trees and each perimeter edge to five, so closing the diagonal leaves four options while any perimeter closure leaves three. This counterintuitive comparison gives the inventory a purpose. Adding BD gives a 4+8+4 classification of K4's sixteen trees and restores symmetry among all six roads. Crossings are explicitly not vertices.

The determinant page is separately gated and states the Matrix-Tree theorem as supplied. Its cofactor calculation is not advertised as a proof. A guide-only explanation gives the incidence-minor/Cauchy–Binet argument, naming Cauchy–Binet as supplied. The relevant Stanley definitions, lemmas and theorem proof were read. No stochastic interpretation or weighted formula is smuggled into the road-count result. Accepted **GA-25** already treats spanning trees/exchanges/duality; the new target is edge-closure counting.

### AD-06

The set-card experiment establishes what the two calculation recipes mean, rather than dropping unfamiliar symbols onto a diagram. The five-tile diamond then supplies a precise failure with every operation still defined. The six-tile diagram is a different kind of failure: common upper bounds exist but there is no least one. Adding one comparison repairs both missing operations, and a full pair-type argument verifies the resulting lattice.

Burris–Sankappanavar's definitions and distributivity passage were revisited. Their five-element diamond is called **M5**, whereas the atlas uses the common alternative **M3**; the data explicitly records the convention. The subspace extension is reserved for learners who already understand spans and intersections, and its small example is proved directly. No representation theorem is assumed.

### AD-07

The core moves beyond computing three closures. Learners choose legal orders, classify the minimal full-generating starts, find a union that becomes unfinished, and explain why intersections never do. The order-independence proof uses the least-closed-superset property and says precisely what a completed run means. Replacement rules then break the persistence hypothesis and produce two different terminal outcomes.

The source's closure axioms and formula for meet/join of closed sets were revisited. The finite algorithm proof is written in full. Full closure output for every start is recorded in the exact results, and all legal successful-addition orders are explored. Every set and rule remains visible, avoiding an artificial memory puzzle.

### AD-08

The learner first chooses an arbitrary two-bag experiment, so parity is not announced as the desired answer. Failure witnesses hold the two input bags fixed while changing representatives. Minimal repairs then force the pair-translation argument used to classify all valid renamings. A supplied unary operation makes a previously valid quotient fail, showing that well-definedness depends on the operations being preserved.

The source's congruence compatibility discussion was read directly. The four-ring classification is fully proved by neighboring/opposite pairs. The optional six-ring extension gives all four partitions and a complete subgroup/coset argument, with the advanced gate stated. All 15 four-element and 203 six-element partitions are independently checked against every representative quadruple.

## Exact mathematical audit

The standard-library checker passes. It verifies:

- AD-01: all 16 possible presence/absence worlds, smallest counterworlds, all forced claims and the minimum arrow-world extension.
- AD-02: all 65,536 ordered four-row binary lists, including repetitions, and all 24 witness-coordinate assignments on the supplied example.
- AD-03: all 65,536 collections of the sixteen cards, exact optimal and forced-card sizes, both chain certificates and complementation.
- AD-04: independent tree edge-subset enumeration versus every code for n=2 through 6 (1,3,16,125,1296 trees), both inverse directions and every degree identity.
- AD-05: all edge subsets, all five closure counts, the K4 split, every principal cofactor and all ten reduced-incidence minors.
- AD-06: all 512 set triples, 25 diamond pairs, 125 diamond triples, all six distributivity failures, and both one-comparison repairs.
- AD-07: every starting set and every legal new-token-addition order; the full closed-set list, all closure axioms, all closed intersections and both minimum generators.
- AD-08: all set partitions for n=4 and n=6 with all representative choices, both least repairs and the added-operation survivors.

These are finite audits. Generality rests on the written arguments: arbitrary-index diagonalization; membership proof for set distributivity; inductive tree-code inverse; positive-rule leastness; and the congruence translation argument. The advanced Matrix-Tree page openly uses a supplied theorem.

## Initial production concerns (now addressed)

At the initial handoff, independent design review was still pending. The next renderer must preserve staged disclosure: especially the AD-02 method card, AD-03 chain hints, AD-04 decoder and AD-08 parity result. AD-06 needs readable upward diagrams with no accidental comparison implied by page position, and AD-05 must visually distinguish the overpass from a vertex. AD-01 needs monochrome-safe color labels. Workspace should be sized for moving and drawing the actual objects, not filled with repeated generic answer boxes. A dense guide proof may need more pages than its student spread; do not shrink body type to maintain a target count.

Independent design review closed all four findings: explicit antichain legality, deletion-minimal connectedness, the simple-graph Matrix-Tree hypothesis, and the decoded-tree suffix-degree invariant proving the second inverse direction. Custom production now yields 25 staged student pages plus contents and a 40-page complete guide. `ad1-maker-qa.md` records the fresh `ad1-preview-r2/` preview, every page covered, exact hashes, mathematical checks and answer-scaffold repairs.
