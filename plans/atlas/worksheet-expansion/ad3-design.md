## Investigation arcs and gates

- **AD-17 — Design the round when closeness begins.** Two asymmetric simultaneous rules share a disagreement multiplier but have different weighted limits. Learners choose a rule, discover a weighted invariant, prove a first tolerance-entry time and design every initial pair crossing a chosen threshold. The final page classifies all allowed parameters, including no movement, one-round meeting and endless swapping, then designs both order-preserving and alternating convergence. Fractions/inequalities are the core gate; eigenvectors are a separate guide extension. AP-01 and GA-05 static harmonic averaging are explicitly distinguished.
- **AD-18 — Can you preserve area without preserving shape?** An oblique image family u=(2,1),v=(k,2) offers choices with opposite orientation and a collapse. Projection onto a left unit normal proves the signed-area formula, including the zero first column. The learner classifies area1 parameters and inverses, compares two composition orders, and proves that area plus two preserved unit lengths does force all lengths. The projection and matrix-algebra gates are explicit. A lattice-bijection extension has a full necessity/sufficiency argument.
- **AD-19 — Choose bases that reveal what an arrow does.** The Q⁴→Q³ map has two transmitted, two killed and one unreachable target direction. Learners first invent collisions and targets, then choose their own bases and certify minimal nonzero matrix entries. Basis extension gives the general one-arrow theorem over any field; an indecomposability proof and rank/dimension inventory establish completeness. Similarity and a two-arrow composite-rank example show exactly where the theorem does not apply. This is genuinely linear algebra throughout.
- **AD-20 — Find what commutes, then explain the cancellations.** The nonstandard nilpotent N=[[1,1],[−1,−1]] starts a commuting-partner design and complete centralizer classification. A repeated-C example witnesses nonassociativity of the bracket. The main payoff is a general Jacobi cancellation proof and the related product derivation identity, followed by the scalar center. The guide extension proves a finite-dimensional trace obstruction. This retains the original Lie-algebra mechanism without repeating its E,F,H calculation sheet.
- **AD-21 — Make a catalog that remembers every choice.** Repeated-color fibers on both sides produce a larger pullback. One badge recoloring is an optimization choice, and token reuse prevents confusion with injective matching. Existence and uniqueness are proved for arbitrary sets, including the empty set. Duplicate and missing records separate the two failures; universal properties then prove a unique record-preserving bijection between rival correct catalogs. Named cards provide the entry; function arguments are the separate categorical gate.
- **AD-22 — Which face cards erase the same edge pattern?** The square-center graph has four spokes meeting at an actual center vertex. Initially opposite triangle cards are available. Four outer-edge bits classify every cycle; two untouched outer edges classify all move-equivalence classes. Learners buy the minimum missing face cards for one of two targets. The exact boundary matrices and all face subsets identify H₁; a guide-only formal cap gives a checked H₂ continuation. Parity is an accessible entry; vector-space quotient claims retain their gate. The exact vertex/edge/face orders are explicitly printed in page3’s introduction following independent review.
- **AD-23 — How much free space completes this module?** The actual Q×Q ring action and idempotents justify the two-component model. Learners choose equal-total nonisomorphic modules, prove splitting and finite-dimensionality, and find every module’s smallest free complement. Stable-free and arbitrary-complement claims are separated. Formal differences lead to an explicit inverse pair of homomorphisms with Z² and the universal extension proof. The guide’s noncancellative monoid warns against overgeneralization. Counters are an aid after the module theorem, not a surrogate K-theory lesson.
- **AD-24 — Count necklaces when every bead count is fixed.** Six beads with exactly three of each color yield unequal orbit sizes and an actual chiral pair. Learners choose a catalog proof, audit naive division, derive fixed-turn counts, and prove the averaging rule by a fixed-pair count. Reflection changes the equivalence relation and really merges a pair. A gcd/divisibility formula handles general fixed weight and a new eight-bead request. The coprime extension identifies exactly why naive division is justified in that special setting.
