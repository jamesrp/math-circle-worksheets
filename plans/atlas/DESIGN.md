# Atlas design contract - first edition

Prepared 25 September 2026. The organizer endorses the broad research-to-student direction; `math_circle_atlas_plan.md` is an input, not an authority. This edition makes the mathematics and concrete investigations the main product. The classification is an omission detector, not a compulsory teaching sequence.

## Scope and evidence

Survey every one of the 63 top-level MSC fields, including support fields 00, 01, and 97. Preserve the entire downloaded taxonomy so its unexamined branches remain visible. Develop substantive investigation families across the map, and distinguish each family's exact mathematical content from its research continuations. No finite collection can enumerate all interesting problems or all future mathematics. First-edition completion means the explicitly enumerated survey and design work is present and checked; it never means that every subject, problem, or child-access bridge has been exhausted.

The three surveys are locked as the first-edition *territory map* after editorial inspection. Stable IDs can then be used in design and review. New discoveries may extend the map without breaking those IDs. A field dossier is research screening; a family is a proposed teaching design; a reviewed finite example is a verified example; none of these is classroom evidence.

## Family records

Each family is an independently usable planning card. Fields: stable ID and title; primary MSC field and secondary connections; interesting adult question; exact mathematical anchor and hypotheses; source with inspected locator; fidelity class; child/adolescent objects and moves; opening question and meaningful choices; entry/explore/explain/prove prerequisites; smallest worthwhile example; checked solution and boundary/counterexample; materials/preparation; a flexible one-hour arc; prompts and hint ladder; extensions and hard stop; related/prior-use notes; status.

Fidelity classes are `exact-special-case`, `faithful-representation`, `shared-mechanism`, and `motivation-only`. A graph approximation to a PDE may be an exact graph problem and a shared-mechanism bridge to a continuous equation. Record both scopes. A family does not confer coverage on an entire field or on any descendant MSC code automatically.

## Capability gates

Gates describe tools, not ages or ranks. A participant may have some and not others.

| Code | Capability |
|---|---|
| O | Follow a short spoken rule; sort, match, move or compare objects. Reading optional. |
| C | Count small sets and track cases; add or subtract whole numbers as required. |
| M | Multiply/divide and reason about factors or remainders. |
| F | Fractions, ratios and signed numbers. Specify which are actually needed. |
| V | Coordinates, vectors or geometric measurement. Specify the representation. |
| A | Variables, equations and function notation; specify linear, quadratic, polynomial, etc. |
| P | Systematic proof: cases, induction, contradiction or quantified claims. Name the needed method. |
| L | Linear algebra: vectors, matrices, systems, eigenvectors as specified. |
| D | Differential calculus; limits and derivatives as specified. |
| I | Integral calculus; integrals and their meaning as specified. |
| X | Named advanced prerequisite, such as metric spaces, groups, complex differentiation or measure theory. |

O is not shorthand for cognitively easy. Working memory, spatial tracking, language, and abstraction must be stated independently. Use grade ranges only as tentative search aids. Explicitly distinguish joining the activity, finding examples, explaining a finite result, and proving a theorem. Stop at a real prerequisite when removing it would remove the mathematics.

## Facilitation

The default 60-minute menu is 0-10 explore materials; 10-15 launch and check legal moves; 15-35 investigate; 35-40 movement/reset; 40-55 choose a deeper question; 55-60 share a finding. Every family supplies a satisfying early stop. It is a menu, not a pace requirement. Advanced investigations can use the same rhythm with sketches or simple computations as the initial objects.

Provide movable pieces or a drawing alternative, oral instructions, a recorder/partner option and low-copying records. Make the central decision belong to the learner. Parent preparation should normally fit 10-20 minutes with reusable counters, paper, string and dice; flag exceptions. Computational extensions need an unplugged core unless the mathematical question itself requires computation.

## Source basis actually revisited

We re-read *Math Circle by the Bay*, preface printed pp. viii-x (PDF pp. 9-11), in the local collection. It describes themes that grow from elementary to advanced mathematics, manipulatives, variable pace, additional challenges and repeated work on explanations. It does not validate our new families or prescribe our exact timetable. Our operational adaptations and the separately located Rozhkovskaya evidence are in [lesson-format-source-notes.md](../lesson-format-source-notes.md).

The organizer's year-1 DOCX handouts were inspected directly. They include figurate numbers, truth/logic constraints, subtraction games, tilings, probability, combinations, jug reachability, Fibonacci strips, divisibility and paradoxes. Existing questions must be flagged when reused. One historic handout has a winning/losing wording slip; it is a record of prior material, not a mathematical authority. The redesigned Weeks 1-10 provide the current standard of exact rules, learner choices and checked explanations.

We also inspected the locally downloaded JRMF *Four Corners Activity Guide*, PDF pp. 2-3, and *Graceful Graphs Festival Guide*, PDF pp. 2-3. Their facilitator pages model short rule demonstrations followed by learner exploration, and provide concrete per-station material lists. The atlas adopts that practical format; its new examples are original designs, not claims that JRMF has tested them. These are distinct from borrowing a named mathematical result from an authored research source.

## Review and publication gates

1. Survey first; reconcile all top-level codes and preserve subarea backlog.
2. Draft families with exact examples and separate advanced continuations.
3. Independent mathematical and access review; fix material findings before printing.
4. Generate a facilitator planning atlas, with its review scope made visible. Do not label plan cards as polished student worksheets.
5. Render all PDF pages, check text/geometry and inspect page images; use independent PDF reviewers, then a repair pass and final recheck.

The first edition is a plan-level atlas, as requested. Detailed student packets can subsequently be produced from selected and piloted families. The PDFs in this edition are the reviewed plans themselves; they do not silently turn every exploratory seed into a classroom-ready worksheet.
