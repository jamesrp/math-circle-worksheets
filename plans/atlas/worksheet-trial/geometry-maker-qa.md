## Candid teaching assessment

- **GA-25 is the strongest immediate pilot candidate.** A learner can construct freely, encounter the right-count/wrong-structure trap, explain a count, find and certify a shortest change sequence, then see the complementary regions form another tree. Use the first two pages and choose either swaps or duality within one hour.
- **GA-26 is a substantial candidate with a real spatial gate.** Children must successfully trace over/under arcs before the invariant is meaningful. Solving the finite coloring contradiction is a satisfying early stop. A full knot obstruction uses additional proof cases and explicitly supplied theorem facts; do not describe the rainbow drawing alone as a complete proof.
- **GA-29's finite core is more reliable than its ambitious atlas headline.** Addresses, endpoints and the simultaneous count/length challenge provide genuine finite mathematics. The uncountability page needs infinite-sequence and quantified reasoning, even though it needs no calculus. It is optional and may deserve a separate meeting.

The designs are mathematically checked, not classroom piloted. The larger-grid question 25.15 and invented-budget question 29.11 remain complete facilitator back-pocket prompts rather than squeezing more material onto student pages.
