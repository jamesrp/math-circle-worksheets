# Connections, revisits, and near-duplicates

These links are editorial comparisons of mathematical objects, legal actions, questions and reasoning. They do not multiply coverage. A connected thread may be an excellent multi-session route precisely because learners recognize an earlier object and ask a different question.

| Families / prior material | Shared structure | What must change to justify the return |
|---|---|---|
| AD-05 and GA-25; prior Week 10 | A square with one diagonal, connected subgraphs and spanning trees | AD-05 counts all spanning trees and connects to a determinant. GA-25 proves the number of independent cycles and the minimum deletions. Reusing the same five-edge graph is deliberate; do not surprise returning learners with an allegedly new board. |
| AD-22 and GA-25; prior Week 2 | Boundary maps, cycles and graph incidence | Adding filled faces and asking which cycles are boundaries changes the quotient being studied. Counting visible graph loops is only preparation for homology. |
| GA-05 and AP-01 | Harmonic averaging on a finite path with fixed endpoints | A number landscape asks about extrema/uniqueness; a random walk asks for hitting probabilities and requires an absorption argument. The numerical recurrence may coincide. |
| AD-17 and the contraction/feedback families | Iteration, invariant averages and shrinking differences | Distinguish a specific linear map, a general contraction certificate, and gain-induced instability. Merely changing starting numbers is not a fresh mechanism. |
| GA-13 and GA-18 | Best approximation under a chosen error rule | Worst vertical error and integrated squared error can select different answers. The change of norm is the actual investigation. |
| GA-16 and transform/communication families | Convolution, shift and superposition | A continuous overlap-length graph is an integral. Finite echo sums or code convolutions require their own definitions; a shared word is not equivalence. |
| AD-08, AD-21 and information/inference families | Forgetting distinctions while preserving a specified structure | Congruence requires compatible operations; a pullback pairs equal labels; statistical conditioning restricts outcomes under a sampling model. These are different questions. |
| AD-09; prior Weeks 4 and 9 | Remainders and gcd | Two simultaneous residue constraints introduce existence and uniqueness modulo a combined period. State how this differs from one orbit or a repeated bounce. |
| GA-21 and AP-12; prior Week 9 | Replacing routes by a one-parameter geometry problem | Mirror reflection certifies shortest length; two speeds optimize time with refraction. Boundary constraints and unequal speeds change the answer and proof. |
| GA-28 and AP-06 | Exactly the same Newton cubic and 0-to-1 cycle | AP-06 investigates the failure itself. GA-28 reuses it while comparing a continuity-based bracketing guarantee. Treat this as a linked extension or an alternative entry, not two entirely new mechanisms. |
| AD-16 and GA-06 | Polynomial solution sets under different scalar systems | Finite-field enumeration and complex branches versus a real slice ask different questions. Neither covers schemes, general singularities, or all algebraic geometry. |
| AD-01 and AP-08; year-1 logic work | Rules, models and finite counterexamples | A logical countermodel falsifies an implication; distinguishable histories prove a machine-memory lower bound for all word lengths. |
| AP-02 through AP-05 | Finite probability spaces | Conditioning, randomization testing, biased estimation and interval coverage are distinct inferential questions. Keep the information/assignment/sampling mechanism explicit in each. |

Connections to later undeveloped work are invitations, not coverage credits. Examples include optimal transport from matching/convexity, persistent homology from growing sublevel sets, and learning theory from inference plus optimization. They need independent source and design passes before becoming families.
