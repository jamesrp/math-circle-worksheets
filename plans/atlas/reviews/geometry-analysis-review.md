## Findings and closure

### GA-10 — blocking mathematical error, repaired and closed

The original extension said that itinerary RLL cycles through the same states as LRR. In fact LRR gives 2/7→4/7→6/7→2/7, while RLL gives 8/9→2/9→4/9→8/9. Both are genuine period-three orbits, but they differ.

Exact repair proposed: use RRL starting at 4/7, or RLR starting at 6/7. The saved card now uses **RRL: 4/7→6/7→2/7→4/7**, explicitly identifies cyclic relabeling, and asks learners to verify the half labels. Independently rechecked; closed.

### GA-22 — substantive completeness clarification, repaired and closed

The promised complete geometric case split needed the case of exactly three collinear points and one off their line, as well as a point on a triangle's boundary. The original account could be read as covering only a strict interior point, a convex quadrilateral, and all four collinear.

Exact repair proposed: if a point lies in **or on** the triangle of the others, put it alone; in the three-collinear case choose the middle collinear point. Otherwise use a convex quadrilateral's diagonals, or an inner point when all four are collinear. The saved solution now states these cases and the distinct-position hypothesis explicitly. Rechecked; closed.

### GA-36 — substantive domain clarification, repaired and closed

“Replace the factor one-third by another factor of absolute value below one” could produce a rule that leaves [0,1], undermining the interval self-map comparison. The fixed-point problem also needed the closed interval named explicitly.

Exact repair proposed and now saved: **f_q(x)=1/2+q(x−1/2), −1<q<1**. This preserves [0,1], has fixed point 1/2, and obeys |x_n−1/2|=|q|^n|x_0−1/2|; negative q alternates the sign. The instance now names the closed interval [0,1]. Rechecked; closed.

## Source hypothesis sample

This review independently inspected the following source portions, not every source in the volume. The other source-check statements remain the authors' documented checks; I do not upgrade them to independently verified citations.

- **GA-22, GA-31:** Hug and Weil, *A Course on Convex Geometry*, printed pp.22–23, Theorems 1.2.1–1.2.3. Radon assumes affine dependence; n+2 points supply it. Finite Helly assumes convex sets, m≥n+1, and all (n+1)-wise intersections. The separate infinite-family version uses compactness. The cards' finite claims match. [Source](https://users.fmf.uni-lj.si/lavric/hug%26weil.pdf)
- **GA-23, GA-30:** Petrunin and Zamora Barrera, *What Is Differential Geometry?*, Proposition-Definition 16.2, the proof around 16.6, Proposition 17.10, and §18G. Parallel transport preserves oriented angle along each geodesic segment; corner changes are handled without resetting the vector. The spherical formula uses a positively oriented simple boundary, satisfied by the octant. Wrapping the plane into a cylinder is length-preserving, supporting the independent lift argument. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.11814)
- **GA-26:** Kauffman, *Knots*, “Three Coloring a Knot,” Figures 12–14, and the subsequent quandle identities. The local all-same/all-distinct rule and preservation through Reidemeister moves match. I also checked the numerical identities independently. A finished learner sheet still needs a correct over/under trefoil drawing; text correctness does not validate a future drawing. [Source](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~kauffman/Tots/Knots)
- **GA-05, GA-35:** Hunter, *Notes on Partial Differential Equations*, §2.1 Theorems 2.1–2.2 and the maximum-principle discussion. The mean-value theorem assumes a C² harmonic function on an open set with the closed ball contained inside; the polynomial lives on the whole plane. The converse needs the mean property for the relevant circles/balls, not four samples. GA-05 accurately supplies its own finite graph proof rather than claiming that the continuous theorem applies directly. [Source](https://math.ucdavis.edu/~hunter/pdes/pde_notes.pdf)

No source retrieval used for this sample failed. General continuations such as higher-dimensional Borsuk–Ulam, global monodromy, Hartogs phenomena, arbitrary Fredholm operators, spectral theory, the full Morse and surface-classification theorems, sampling reconstruction, and general contraction theory were **not independently source-audited in this round**. They are appropriately described as gated continuations rather than conclusions of the checked finite or explicit examples.

## Access, usefulness, and optional improvements

Every card offers a learner-controlled choice: a counterexample to seek, an object to construct, a parameter or test to choose, or a certificate to challenge. None requires that every participant reach its most advanced gate. The inexpensive paper/marker/strip core is plausible for plan-level preparation. The 5–15 minute preparation figures and one-hour session estimates remain unpiloted estimates, especially for GA-23 tangent-arrow transport, GA-26 knot diagrams, and GA-32 seam construction.

The strongest lower entrances preserve the exact object: interval overlap in GA-16, set partitioning in GA-22, seams in GA-32, and circle root tracking in GA-04. GA-06, GA-08, GA-17, GA-19, and GA-33 correctly retain substantive algebra/calculus gates. GA-05 explicitly uses a shared mechanism; it does not disguise a finite graph as the continuum theory. Infinite constructions distinguish finite physical samples from a rule/proof about all stages.

Optional refinements, with exact wording if adopted:

1. **GA-11 gate wording:** Replace “real extension requires continuity” with “Extending the rational linearity conclusion to all real inputs uses a stated regularity assumption, such as continuity; constructing nonstandard additive extensions is a separate algebraic task.” This avoids reading continuity as necessary for every extension of the function itself. The present anchor, limits, and solution already make the intended mathematical distinction correctly.
2. **GA-29 measure definition:** If the accompanying glossary defines outer measure using only open interval covers, append: “Enlarge the finitely many stage-n closed intervals slightly, adding arbitrarily little total length; they then form an open cover with the same limiting bound.” This is a pedagogical completion, not a defect in the zero-measure result.
3. **GA-23 physical directions:** On the route card, mark A,B,C and both “forward tangent” and “transported arrow” with different colors. Keep the arrow fixed at each corner before beginning the next arc. This makes the existing rule executable without silently substituting a compass-heading rule.
4. **GA-26 worksheet production:** Use a trefoil with three distinctly labeled undercrossing-bounded arcs and an explicit overpass at every crossing. Include a separate local-move diagram for each identity. Review those diagrams independently when produced; the present plan does not yet supply them.

Repeated mechanisms are honestly distinguishable: GA-02/28/36 use continuity for different questions; GA-05/35 separate discrete and continuous averaging; GA-13/18 change both target and error geometry; GA-08/34 separate uniqueness from global existence; GA-23/30 separate curvature from cylindrical wrapping. AD-05/GA-25 share a graph and AP-06/GA-28 share a Newton example, so the recorded cross-volume revisit labels should remain visible during scheduling.
