# Independent breadth check

Audit begun 25 September 2026. The main scouts work from MSC fields. The coordinator separately checked two university curriculum sources as an omission test: [Cambridge's full undergraduate course list](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/undergrad/node/40) and [Harvard's 2024-25 mathematics concentration brochure](https://www.math.harvard.edu/media/Undergraduate-Brochure-Electronic-2024-2025.pdf), its branch/course overview. These are coverage prompts, not evidence that an activity suits children.

The check deliberately looks beyond the elementary circle's strong discrete core: algebra, analysis and geometry; probability and inference; numerical and asymptotic methods; automata and computability; biological and physical models. The Cambridge list also prompted explicit checks for representation theory, Riemann surfaces, integrable systems, quantum information, and financial stochastic models. A top-level field tag alone does not settle any of these subarea searches.

Protected frontier: detailed investigation of those subareas remains separate from importing their codes. No automatic inheritance of 'reviewed' from a field to its children is permitted. A reviewed finite-dimensional example does not close an infinite-dimensional research branch. The full lower-level taxonomy is retained as an unvisited queue in this first edition.

## Map audit result

The three completed surveys reconcile to all 63 official top-level fields with no missing or duplicate headings. Their 231 numbered anchors are *problem seeds*, not 231 independently reviewed, distinct mechanisms. The full taxonomy import and seed extraction are reproducible. The map is suitable for the first design pass, with the gaps below retained. This audit does not independently approve family mathematics; that review follows design.

| Syllabus prompt | Located territory | First-edition limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Representation theory | 16, 20, 22, 43 | Small actions, maps and transforms do not cover irreducible representation theory. |
| Riemann surfaces | 30, 57 | Square-root monodromy is a specific entrance; analytic surface theory remains a separate search. |
| Integrable systems | 34, 37, 70 | Qualitative motion and conserved quantities do not constitute a designed integrability strand. |
| Asymptotic methods | 33, 41, 65 | The survey locates asymptotics; singular perturbation and matched expansions remain undeveloped. |
| Number fields | 11, 12, 13 | Quadratic examples and local obstructions do not cover class groups or units. |
| Coding and cryptography | 11, 68, 94 | Error-correcting communication is distinct from cryptographic security; the latter remains a frontier. |
| Quantum information | 81, 94 | Measurement/amplitudes give one entrance; entanglement and quantum computation require their own designs. |
| Machine learning mathematics | 62, 68, 90 | Fitting, inference and optimization connections are located; generalization and learning theory are not yet surveyed in depth. |
| Stochastic finance | 60, 91 | Strategic games are not stochastic finance; martingale pricing and no-arbitrage need a protected algebra/probability or calculus strand. |
| Mathematical biology | 92 | Population and reaction examples do not cover all biological modeling or sequence methods. |
| Probability and measure | 28, 60 | Both have explicit separate dossiers; finite probability does not imply measure-theoretic coverage. |
| Waves and fields | 35, 78, 80 | Wave, heat and transport models are distinguished; PDE existence/convergence is not supplied by an experiment. |

Additional breadth checks: historical support currently has narrow European-source anchors, explicitly recorded as a global-history research gap; mathematical education is a support field with evidence distinctions, not a disguised student topic. General/format facets are retained rather than silently removed. University syllabi themselves are selective, so passing this check is no proof of exhaustive mathematics coverage.
