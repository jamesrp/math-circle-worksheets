# Three final breadth proposals for Weeks 98 to 100

Recommend these three investigations to bring the numbered library to **100**, then consider breadth complete. Future work can strengthen these and existing topics through depth, encores, connections and classroom evidence. The numbers identify proposed library slots, not three scheduled meetings or 100 equally ready classroom sessions.

| Week | Topic and concrete question | Mathematical destination | Readiness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **98** | **Growing from a few seeds — bootstrap percolation.** Choose starting squares; a new square fills when two side-neighbors are filled. How few seeds can fill the board? Can a larger start still need every one of its seeds? | A monotone growth process; a perimeter bound proves an n-by-n board needs at least n seeds, and a diagonal attains it. “No seed can be removed” differs from “fewest seeds.” | Grades 2–5 for construction and prediction; perimeter certificates and minimal-versus-minimum comparisons usually 3–5. |
| **99** | **Moving piles with the least work — optimal transport.** Slide counters between neighboring bins until they match target piles. Which moves are unavoidable, and when does taking the nearest delivery first waste work? | A distance between whole distributions. On a line, minimum total travel is the sum of absolute prefix surpluses; matching counters in order attains the bound. | Grades 2–5 for moving and comparing small piles; grades 3–5 for certificates and invention. |
| **100** | **Distance in nested worlds — ultrametric geometry.** Put objects into nested groups, read distance from their smallest common group, and reconstruct a hidden grouping from distances. Can its balls overlap without nesting? | The strong triangle inequality: the two largest of three distances are equal. Balls are nested or disjoint, and any member can serve as center. A later number-card route realizes a scaled 2-adic distance. | Grades 3–5 recommended; younger children who retain the new distance rule may join the concrete grouping route. |

The [bootstrap plan](week-98-bootstrap-percolation.md), [transport plan](week-99-optimal-transport.md), and [ultrametric plan](week-100-ultrametric-geometry.md) each come with a theorem-first overview, a worked launch, five substantial investigations, materials, solutions and a first-visit route. No K–1 packet is forced; an established younger-table activity remains appropriate when a family is used with the older tables.

These are **new activity plans, not produced worksheets or classroom-validated lessons**. Examples have exact finite checks and written arguments. Handling, preparation times, age fit and pacing are unpiloted hypotheses. Worksheet production, if requested, should use the existing fresh writer → critic → mathematical review → reviser workflow; it has not been run here.

The [research and overlap note](RESEARCH.md) compares five candidate directions and identifies exact connections to existing material, including all completed Weeks 92–97 and the reserved Weeks 85–91 plans. The selected topics are elementary entrances to research fields, not new research results. In particular, Week 98 does not teach a random critical-threshold theorem, Week 99 does not claim general optimal transport is solved by sorting, and Week 100 does not construct the complete field of p-adic numbers.

[Reproducible checks](check_examples.py) and [results](checked-examples.json): all 512 seed sets on a 3-by-3 board; 4,900 pairs of four-counter histograms on five sites compared with every assignment; a tree transport minimum independently checked by breadth-first search; and all triples and balls of an eight-point ultrametric. These finite checks support the examples; the accompanying arguments support the general statements.
