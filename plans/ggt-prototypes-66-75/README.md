# Ten geometric-group-theory prototypes: Weeks 66–75

Prepared October 6, 2026 for organizer review. These library identifiers are not an adopted teaching schedule. The organizer requested all ten prototypes and authorized saving checked batches to public `main`. All remain unpiloted; digital review does not establish physical or classroom readiness.

The primary-source trail connects Moon Duchin's November 3, 2005 Reed talk, *Billiards and Geometric Topology*, to concrete investigations in metric geometry, geometric group theory and flat surfaces. The Reed abstract is contextual motivation, not evidence that these exact activities occurred in that talk. Established mathematics is adapted into newly authored wording and figures. No whole books or downloaded source PDFs belong in this release.

See `topics.json` and the ten owned outlines under `worksheet-workflow/outlines/` for mathematical destinations, source locations, constraints and prototype bands. Weeks 66–68 offer shared Grades 3–5 entry points; Weeks 69–75 are readiness-dependent Grades 4–5 prototypes. No K–1 edition is forced.

## Workflow and scope

Use the repository's unchanged `make_prompts.py` and stage set, with a recorded run-local addendum replacing its automatic three-band outputs by one `students.pdf`. Each topic goes through a fresh writer, independent adversarial critic, and fresh reviser. Enumeration-heavy claims also receive independent code-based mathematical review. The adult guide is a separate stage and opens with a theorem-first overview. Its compact length reflects prototype scope, rather than the ordinary full-session guide template's six-to-ten-page expectation.

Stage prompts containing borrowed style exemplars remain local build inputs, excluded from new portable source bundles and published review records. Record the useful review findings and decisions in owned prose instead. Keep current students/adult PDFs, editable source, authored provenance, verifiers, source ZIPs and extracted-build checks synchronized.

The intended outputs are about four substantial student pages and a short adult guide per topic, with precise assumptions, solutions, preparation and realistic limitations. Each final PDF page must be rendered and visually inspected. Each portable ZIP must be extracted, rebuilt without the repository, and checked against the final release. Rehearsal of physical materials remains untested unless actually performed.

## Distinctness from the existing corpus

These investigations target lamplighter states and dead ends, median vertices, ends, thin triangles, midpoint blocking, inverse bounce words, Heisenberg area memory, least Lipschitz stretch, exponential elevator shortcuts, and boundary-fixed Dehn twists. Their familiar props are not the mathematical destination. They avoid substituting Week 39's free-word cancellation, Week 41's torus lifts, Week 3's inversions, Week 12's path surgery, Week 26's polyomino perimeter, Week 50's priced diagonals, Week 29's generator redundancy, or Weeks 64–65's octagon periodicity and growth.
