# Room plans

A room plan is a one-page plan for running a week's activity in the real Wednesday room: about eleven children, mostly grades 2–3 with a few younger and older, after a school day, and three or four adults. The week's facilitator guide covers the mathematics and the launch. The room plan covers the room: who does what at every moment, when things change, and how the hour ends before it falls apart.

Started November 2, 2026, after James asked for them (aim 2 in the app repository's [roadmap](https://github.com/jamesrp/small-math-adventure/blob/main/docs/plan/roadmap.md)). They are unpiloted. After a session, add a dated line under "How it went" in that plan, in the organizer's words.

## What every plan has

- **Launch on the floor, three minutes or less.** Children are part of the object (they sit in the chairs, hold the chips), and everyone else predicts out loud.
- **Pairs with jobs, swapped every round.** One child moves the pieces and the other records. Nobody sits without a job.
- **Short timed rounds.** About eight minutes, with a visible timer. A round ends with one pair showing one result, never a whole-group lecture.
- **A planned midpoint move.** Children drift between tables anyway (Colin, October 21), so make the move part of the plan: everyone comes back to the floor once, around minute 30, then goes to a new challenge.
- **A reset signal.** One signal for "hands off the pieces, eyes here", practised once in the launch. Suggested: the adult says "Machines off!" and everyone puts both hands on their head.
- **Something for early finishers** at each table, already printed.
- **A floor finale before the last five minutes.** Colin noted engagement drops in the last five minutes, so the last activity is physical and short, and tidying up is a job.
- **Children who've seen it before get a role, not the answers.** Jay and Rose have done some of these activities at home. Make them floor captains or checkers who may say "try again" but not why.
