## The mathematics in one paragraph, for adults

Circles joined by lines hold chips. A circle with at least as many chips as it has lines may share: one chip goes along each line. The sink only collects. Whatever order you choose, sharing stops, and it always stops with the same piles and the same number of shares at each circle (the abelian property). The children's discovery is the surprise that the order doesn't matter.

## Before the children arrive

- **Floor board for the launch:** the packets' triangle at floor size. Two masking-tape circles, A and B, and a bucket as the sink, about 4 feet apart, each joined to the other two by a tape line. So A and B each touch two lines and need two chips to share.
- About 10 large counters or soft balls for the floor; about 24 small counters per child at the tables (pairs can share a pile).
- At each table: that band's packet pages face down, whiteboards, pencils. One visible timer.
- Early finishers go on to the next problem in their own packet (K–1 Problem 3, 2–3 Problem 4, 4–5 Problem 4); nothing extra to print.

## The hour

| Time | Everyone | Jobs |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrive and run around. | |
| 0:05–0:09 | **Launch on the floor.** Two children stand in A and B holding chips: 4 at A, 2 at B (not a start in the packets). Anyone holding at least two chips may share: one chip to the other circle and one into the bucket. Run it until nobody can share (it ends with 0 at A and 1 at B). Then run the same start again with the room choosing a different order. Ask only: "Same ending?" Practise the reset signal once. | Two standers; one child calls who shares next; everyone else counts. |
| 0:09–0:27 | **Tables, round 1**: Problem 1 in every band (two orders from each start on the triangle). Each pair runs a start in one order, then the other order, and compares. | **Sharer** moves chips; **Recorder** writes the sharing word (the letters in order). Swap every start. |
| 0:27–0:32 | **Back to the floor.** Two teams take turns on the floor triangle with the same start, 5 at A and 1 at B, each team choosing its own order. Did they finish the same? Did A share the same number of times? | Two pairs of standers, one caller per team; the rest keep a tally of shares. |
| 0:32–0:48 | **Tables, round 2**, by band. K–1: Problem 2 (every finish from 4 chips, then 5). 2–3: Problem 2 (the four-cycle), then Problem 3 (every 6-chip start that ends at one and one). 4–5: Problems 2 and 3. | Same pairs, swapped jobs: the Recorder now predicts the final piles before the Sharer starts. |
| 0:48–0:55 | **Floor finale: the big pile.** Put 8 chips at A and none at B, and ask for predictions of the final piles before anyone moves. Run it (it ends with 0 at A and 1 at B). | Two new standers; predictions on whiteboards held up before the run. |
| 0:55–1:00 | Counters counted back into cups, pages collected. | Each pair counts one cup. |

## If it goes sideways

- A pair says the order mattered: they almost always skipped a share. The adult asks them to run it again with the Recorder reading the sharing word aloud.
- Arithmetic gets slow at 2–3: keep totals to 12 as the packets do, and let the Recorder use tallies.
- Jay and Rose have done chip firing at home: make them floor callers and checkers, not explainers.
