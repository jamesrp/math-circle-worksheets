## Why this follows November 4

On October 29 the children counted the orders of 3 and 4 cards and built orders from swaps; on November 4 (planned) a shuffle machine moves a whole order at once. A row of towers of different heights is an order of the heights too: the same object, read a new way, by how many towers you see from an end. The opener's animal line is such an order.

For adults: a row of towers 1 to n tall is a permutation, and the towers seen from an end are its records. The tallest is seen from both ends and every other seen tower stands on its own side of it, so the two counts satisfy L + R ≤ n + 1, and 1 and 1 never happens. A city is a Latin square with these counts as edge clues: a clue set can fit one city, several or none; every 3-by-3 city's twelve numbers add to 22; the fewest clues that fix a city are 2 for 3 by 3 and 3 for 4 by 4 (by computer); and 204 of the 576 4-by-4 cities share all sixteen numbers with another, so no puzzle singles them out (4–5 Problems 4, 5 and 9).

## Opener: line up for the photo (8 minutes)

On the tables as children arrive, then acted out on the floor so its answer becomes the launch's row. An adult reads clues aloud at any table. The sheet pictures the heights: Mouse 1 cube, Cat 2, Dog 3, Horse 4, Giraffe 5.

**The puzzle (most of the room).** Five animals stand in a line for a photo. Write each animal's name in its place in the line.

1. Horse stands in the middle.
2. Dog stands at one end of the line.
3. Mouse stands between two taller animals.
4. Mouse stands right next to Cat.
5. The animal at the left end is shorter than the animal at the right end.

**Answer:** Cat, Mouse, Horse, Giraffe, Dog (heights 2, 1, 4, 5, 3). It is the only answer, and every clue is needed (checked by computer, [photo-line_check.py](openers/photo-line_check.py)).

**Hint ladder** (give one step, then walk away):

1. "Which clue tells you exactly where one animal stands? Write that one first." (Horse, the middle.)
2. "Every animal is taller than Mouse. So where can Mouse stand?" (Second or fourth.)
3. "Try Mouse second: where do Cat and Dog go? Now try Mouse fourth. Which passes clue 5?" (Mouse fourth puts Dog left and Cat right: clue 5 fails.)

**Easier.** Mouse, Cat and Dog: the tallest animal stands at the right end; Mouse stands between two taller animals. *Answer:* Cat, Mouse, Dog, the launch's row.

**Harder.** The same five, and no clue names a place: every animal taller than Cat stands somewhere to the right of Cat; Cat stands right next to a shorter animal; Horse stands right next to Mouse; the two tallest animals stand side by side. *Answer:* the same line; every clue is needed.

**Early finishers.** "Cross out clue 5. How many answers now? Write a new clue that makes it exactly one again." (Two: the line and the same line turned round, since clue 5 alone tells left from right. "Giraffe stands somewhere to the right of Horse" fixes it, and so do others.)

**On the floor (minute 6).** Five solvers take the signs and towers and stand in the answer order facing the room, Cat by LEFT END. The room checks each clue, thumbs up or down. The bridge question: "A photographer lies down at the left end, chin on the floor. How many animals are in the photo?" Take guesses; the five set their towers on the base in order, and one child looks from the end of the paper: 3 (Mouse hides behind Cat, Dog behind Giraffe). From the right end: 2.

## If it goes sideways

- A table finishes early: its early-finisher card, then later problems (K–1 5–7, 2–3 6–7, 4–5 6–9). A pair is stuck: "Chin down. Which tops can you see?", nothing more.
- Counts disagree: eye at the end of the paper, a foot away; slide a city's middle row out to look (guide). A table runs out of attention before 0:48: its fallback in the guide (K–1 the adult hides a row; 2–3 a hidden row of four; 4–5 fewest questions).
- 4–5 Problem 5: a second city with all the same numbers is not a mistake but twins (Problem 9). The room gets loud: reset signal, then the next floor activity.
- Returning children: the use log shows nobody doing tower cities, at the circle or at home (Jay and Rose did shuffle machines at home). If a child knows the app's tower puzzles, make them a photographer at 0:48 and a checker who may say "try again" but not why.
