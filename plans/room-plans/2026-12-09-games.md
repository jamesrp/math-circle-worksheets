## Questions to ask while children play SET

Ask one when a game pauses; let the children argue it out. The idea behind each question is in brackets, for the adult.

1. "Pick any two cards. How many cards in the whole deck finish the set?" (Exactly one: for each feature, keep it if the two match, take the missing one if they differ.)
2. "Can a set have two red cards and one green?" (No: each feature is all the same or all different, so "two and one" never happens, for any feature.)
3. "How many cards are in the deck? Why that number?" (81 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3: three choices for each of four features.)
4. "Take the nine cards with one red shape. How many sets can you find in them?" (12, with every card in 4 of them.)
5. "From those nine, how many cards can you lay out with no set? Can anyone do five?" (Four is the most; any five contain a set.)
6. "Could twelve cards on the table have no set? What about twenty-one?" (Twelve can, which is why the game adds three. The most cards with no set is 20, so 21 always hold a set.)
7. "Hold one card. How many different sets is it part of?" (40: pair it with each of the other 80 cards; each set is counted twice, once for each partner.)

## One question for each other game

- **Blokus:** "Your 21 pieces include every shape you can make from five squares. How could you check that none is missing?" (12 five-square shapes; a shape flipped over counts as the same.)
- **Genius Square:** "Count the squares in all nine pieces. Why is that exactly the right number?" (29 squares; with the 7 blockers that makes 36, the whole board.)
- **Prime Climb:** "Why is 12's circle cut into those coloured pieces? What will 30's circle look like?" (Each colour is a prime factor: 2 orange, 3 green, 5 blue; 12 is orange, orange, green and 30 is orange, green, blue. Check the colours on the real board.)
