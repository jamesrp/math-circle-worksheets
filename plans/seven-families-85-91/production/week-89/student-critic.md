## R1 — Supply the small movable input deck (medium; requested revision)

**Evidence:** student pages 4, 6 and 7 ask children to order input slips, including repeated zeros and repeated twos. Page 4 tells children to write the inputs on scraps. `materials.pdf` contains only two working boards; its lower halves have ample unused space. The illustrated slips are compact reference pictures, not a usable cut-out deck. AGENTS says to use supplied movable cards when sorting is the mathematics. The outline permits reusable total cards instead of a huge deck of possible totals; it does not require manufacturing a large deck.

**Why it matters:** ordering is the mathematical action in Problems 7 and 11–13. Requiring each pair to make its own duplicated input cards consumes setup and introduces avoidable omissions, especially the second zero. It also leaves the organizer to improvise a small but central component despite abundant spare materials-page space.

**Minimal fix:** add a small, clearly separated cuttable input deck, with at least the multiplicities needed to form `0,1,2,3,4`, `2,2,1,0,0`, and `1,3,0,0` on separate visits, plus a few reusable blank total cards if useful. One reusable multiset `0,0,1,2,2,3,4` is sufficient. Use approximately 20–25 mm or larger input cards, rather than the tiny reference-slip graphics. Put cutting/preparation instructions with the adults; change the page-4 instruction to use the supplied inputs. Keep total values writable and reusable: no exhaustive total-card deck is wanted. This does not require an additional worksheet problem or extensive instructions.

## Important guide requirements, not additional student-page blockers

1. **Routes and prerequisites must remain explicit.** Pages 1–2 offer a credible optional K–1 route with adult reading and counting help. Page 3 moves to general growth laws; pages 4–5 use addition, inverse addition and unknown starts; pages 6–7 require retaining two totals and respecting turn order. Pages 9–11 are readiness-dependent later visits. The `Grades K–5` header identifies the menu, not a promise that every page is independently suitable for every age. The guide should name first routes and later returns rather than distribute all 11 pages indiscriminately.
2. **Do not let adults operate the entire two-tray search.** After a brief demonstration, ask the child to run a turn while the partner checks which new total is copied. If children cannot retain this convention, return to the one-tray route. This is an unpiloted hypothesis about operational fit, not evidence that the upper-band route fails.
3. **Accept a geometric/recursive explanation before a closed formula.** Problem 18 asks for a rule after any number of turns; the first two totals may be explained using the earlier squares/stairs work. The third total is deliberately harder. Do not require children to discover `n(n+1)(n+2)/6` before allowing the concrete pile investigation in Problem 19. The guide should explicitly permit moving to Problem 19 and returning to 18. Preserve Problem 20 as a substantial continuation.
4. **Material counts and use:** 72 tiles cover one active shape and the largest two-staircase pairing (42 tiles). A side-4 cube needs the full 64-cube kit; one shared kit supports a rotating group, not several simultaneous side-4 builds. The rule-B step-6 shape extends 8 tile widths, beyond the supplied 7-column board, so use the already permitted tabletop. Total cards are necessary for the third-tray route with only 40 counters. Keep print-at-100%, tile-size checking, and physical rehearsal status in the adult/source notes.
5. **Counter domains and limits:** in Problem 9, the earlier physical definition makes the starting total a nonnegative whole number. The guide should state that assumption when claiming all solutions. In Problems 14–15, explicit rules, not four observed pictures alone, determine later steps. Avoid describing a finite table of matching values as proof of a general formula.
