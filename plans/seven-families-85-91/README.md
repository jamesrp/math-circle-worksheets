## Choosing a visit

Use the current eleven-child context: KK11 / 3333 / 445 at three fixed tables, one anchored adult per table. The outlines specify reading, counting/arithmetic, manipulation and reasoning requirements separately. Age labels are approximate. K–1 has genuine optional touch/build/play entries where the mathematical choices survive; no family is required to manufacture a separate K–1 packet. Keep an already prepared familiar activity available for a table that does not take a selected family.

Begin with material exploration and one short legal-action demonstration before distributing starting tasks. A normal first visit can stop after one or two substantial problems. A second visit should resume a saved mathematical question or introduce a new direction, not merely use larger numbers. Adults may read prompts and write the children's chosen records on a whiteboard; they should not silently take over every move, comparison or state update.

The first three families need different kinds of support. A drawing machine needs hardware prepared before class, so construction is not the whole first session. Pursuit needs a visible turn marker and opponent enforcement before interpreting a “winning strategy.” Voting needs rankings that stay fixed across every match, so any change in outcome really comes from the agenda. Do not ask children to reconstruct these rules from memory.

Packing, growing shapes and slides give children recoverable states directly in the pieces. Exact circles and close tray tolerances still need physical checks. For growth, handle complete shapes and removable layers before writing number rows. For tableaux, distinguish inner shaded squares, the selected hole and absent outside squares visually; only one hole travels at a time. A few precise examples are preferable to a long transcribed catalog.

Rotor switches keep their arrows between chips. Use a one-junction convention example before a multi-junction board; then allow enough runs to notice what persistence does. The early balance theorem is substantial on its own. Feedback is a separate readiness-dependent return visit, with an adult helping record endpoints rather than simultaneous tallies at every junction.

## Proofs and honest limits

For the pantograph, bar lengths alone do not guarantee the claimed motion on every crossed assembly. The intended noncrossed parallelogram branch, midpoint holes, anchored O and matched tracer/pencil holders are essential. The algebraic/diagram argument concerns an ideal mechanism; a stiff or loose prototype is not evidence against the theorem. Hardware construction and child handling remain untested.

For pursuit, experiments suggest a strategy; a finite winning streak is not a proof of guaranteed capture or eternal escape. The tree argument shrinks the component available beyond the cop, while the cycle argument maintains sufficient distance before each cop turn. A graph containing a cycle can still be catchable; the statement is about the specified bare cycles. Any folding continuation must state its neighborhood condition.

For voting, the three ranking cards ABC, BCA, CAB give A over B, B over C, and C over A by 2–1. Giving A, B or C the knockout bye then produces that same candidate as final winner. No voter changes a ranking. In the separate line model, at least three favorites lie on the appropriate side of the median and all three strictly prefer it to a distinct opponent. Other voters' equal distances can be treated as abstentions without weakening this three-vote certificate. Include zero-valued favorites as full participants. The theorem assumes the median location is an available choice.

For packing, successful placement establishes a lower bound. The equal-disk angle argument supplies the upper bound of six and the rigidity at six. Changed-size experiments and tray constructions do not automatically prove a maximum. The late 4–3–4 row problem has a proved height bound under its row restriction; it does not claim an unrestricted tray optimum. No general density proof or three-dimensional kissing upper bound is assigned.

For discrete calculus, the geometric growth rule and starting value determine the intended continuation. A finite prefix by itself does not. Accumulation needs an initial condition, and signed open-row differences differ from the existing cyclic gap machines. Formula notation can stay with adults while children compare layers and reconstruct shapes.

For tableaux, the smaller-right/below rule, legal inner corner, increasing rows/columns, and distinct labels are assumptions. Small complete checks and a worked trace do not prove the general rectification theorem. The theorem is attributed and stated for adults, with its full proof honestly gated; the concrete child route remains worthwhile without it.

For the feedback rotor route, write the path as vertices 0,…,m, so **m is the edge count**. Endpoints absorb; every internal arrow means next exit and alternates after use; launches start at one fixed site s and finish before the next chip starts. Let S count internal arrows whose next exit is right, and R count right exits. Each chip step preserves position+S, so `mR + S_final = ns + S_initial`. Since `0≤S≤m−1`, every consecutive m launches gives exactly s right exits from arbitrary initial arrows, without burn-in. An infinite walk would visit an internal vertex infinitely often; alternating exits propagate those visits to an absorbing endpoint, a contradiction. Full arrow states may have a transient: on 0–1–2–3–4 with input1, initial next exits LLR give endpoint word LLRL and end RLL after four. Immediate exact output balance therefore does not require immediate arrow-state recurrence. Balance is not random independence.
