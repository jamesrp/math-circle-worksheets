## Required clarity revision

**M1 — Page 1 shared rules: say that the counter is removed after the decision.** The current consecutive sentences are “Remove one turn counter after each offer. With one counter left, you must take the offer.” A partner can reasonably interpret “after each offer” as immediately after revealing it, remove a counter, and then apply the compulsory-last-offer test to the same offer. That incorrectly forces acceptance of the first offer in a two-offer round. This is exactly the kind of rule-order error that a visible material is meant to prevent.

The practice visual demonstrates the intended timing and the source README explicitly says removal occurs after the decision, so this is a wording ambiguity rather than a wrong model. Bring the printed rule into agreement: check the one-counter condition while deciding, and remove a counter **after deciding**. This can be a short shared-rule edit; do not repeat it in every problem or add a strategy recipe.

## Smaller design concerns and guide recommendations

**L1 — Problem 2 is a short bridge rather than a substantial independent investigation.** “Can the scores from those six rounds settle…” can end at a quick yes/no answer unless a child or adult supplies contrasting imagined samples. It has a sound mathematical purpose: separate observed play from exact policy values before using the full cards. Consider attaching it to Problem 1, or giving contrasting short score records if it is intended to occupy five or more minutes by itself. This is a limited format concern, not a reason to turn the whole packet into directed substeps. The surrounding work already gives children plenty to do.

**L2 — Preserve an explicit core route in the later adult guide.** Page 6 returns to Grades 3–5 after the Grades 4–5 pages 4–5. Its tie/non-tie bags are worthwhile concrete work, not an appendix only for children who finish the proof. The guide should make pages 1–3 and 6 available to the third-grade table, with pages 4–5 and 7 chosen by readiness or on another visit. The honest page bands and README already make this route possible; reordering the student packet is not required.

**L3 — Rehearse scoring Plan A and Plan B with the actual cards.** Page 3 has one score area per card, and the README proposes scoring one plan, saving its total, erasing, and scoring the other. That is coherent and keeps state light. However, the two totals differ by only four points, and 54 evaluations plus reuse of the score areas can amplify one missed decision or an unerased score. Pooling a complete table set and adult addition are appropriate; children must still choose/apply the legal action. The guide should retain the recoverable full words and identify which plan is being scored. A second printed score line is an optional design choice, not a required fix. No physical/card-handling rehearsal has yet established whether erasing or wipeable overlays work well.

**L4 — Average arithmetic is a real gate, not evidence of poor age fit.** The first core comparisons use totals on equally large collections and do not require fractional expected values. Page 4 then compares a certain 4 with averages over three or nine cases; page 7 requires fractional continuation values. The README declares these gates. Adult help with reading, addition, and expressing a fractional average can support the task while children retain the accept/pass choice. If a group does not already know total-to-average conversion, give a brief non-target concrete example before page 4. Do not print the threshold calculation as a method for the target problem. This is conditional support for unfamiliar arithmetic, not a requirement to scaffold a stated prerequisite mechanically.

## Mathematical and operational boundaries

- The printed rules supply a known equal-ticket bag, replacement after every draw, a fixed horizon, no recall, and acceptance ending the round. The practice visual includes forced acceptance of a low final offer; the shared rules explicitly include zero. The source notes specify an independent display copy, indistinguishable randomizing tickets, visible distribution, TAKE/PASS places, and partner/referee enforcement. These are plausible implementations, not rehearsed independence guarantees.
- The goal develops from high total in played rounds to exact equal-case totals and optimal average. There is no rank-only secretary claim, accumulated-score objective, guaranteed-score claim, or conflation of a short play sample with proof. Problem 4 compares two policies; Problem 6 separately asks for optimality.
- Independent critic spot checks give Problem 4 totals **130 and 134**, original two-offer optimum **40**, and new-bag two-offer optima **36** (two middle-offer choices) and **44**. The continuation values match the intended contrasts: original bag 10/3 then 40/9; 0/5/6 bag 44/9 then 143/27, so a current 5 is accepted with three offers including it and passed with four. No mathematical defect was found in these checks. A complete independent mathematical audit remains the next separate stage.
- The playing and card tasks externalize state well. Each child need not reproduce the entire collection; an adult can add pooled scores without taking over the mathematical decisions. Upper proofs require mathematical conversation, which is consistent with the anchored mathematician staffing and declared readiness limits.
- Keep the later guide's shared launch brief: initial handling, then the legal practice round with a partner; establish the current offer/counter timing before distributing starts. The README already records this. Physical mixing, display handling, refusal to reclaim passed offers, counter enforcement, scoring/erasing, role rotation, staffing and classroom fit are all **untested**.
