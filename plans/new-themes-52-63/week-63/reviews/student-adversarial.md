## Findings for the reviser

### R1 — Medium: the first overlapping-group action is stated but not concretely demonstrated

**Location:** students p. 2, Problem 2; materials p. 2; `students.tex` immediately after the first page.

**Observed:** p. 1 explicitly bridges a physical placement, the unchanged home checks, and a left-to-right row record. The six outcome cards then introduce a second kind of object: each card represents an entire placement. Materials p. 2 correctly says that, and Problem 2 says that “A card may belong to both groups.” However, no student visual shows the same complete outcome object carrying two memberships. In this packet, “card” has meant an individual A/B/C card and now also means a whole-row outcome card. The detailed one-object/two-property-marker convention appears only in the source README.

**Risk hypothesis:** a child can make a correct row check yet try to sort the individual A and B cards, make disjoint piles, or copy the shared outcome into two apparent outcomes. With only one physical copy of each complete row, the simultaneous-membership action must be understood before children repair the count. This is especially relevant to the supported Grades 2-5 entry; an adult should not have to operate the sorting investigation for the child.

**Recommended minimal change:** make “one outcome card = one whole row” and “the same outcome can belong to both groups” explicit at first use. Prefer a small non-task convention visual that reuses the p. 1 `VUWX` row and shows that one row carrying both the W-home and X-home memberships, without showing the A-C solution or the add-back calculation. This can be a single outcome with two labeled property markers or an overlap of two open rings. Keep the grouping/counting organization open to children. If an ordinary whole-group ring demonstration is used instead, record that exact required convention in the later launch guidance; do not claim this student bridge is already visually supplied.

The inclusive-intersection convention for later work is otherwise present in time: p. 5 says an overlap includes rows with the specified matches even when additional cards are home. Preserve that sentence.

### R2 — Low: “any number” omits the recurrence's exceptional base cases

**Location:** students p. 9, Problem 9, last sentence.

**Observed:** the task requests “a counting rule for any number of distinct cards.” The intended two-family recurrence in the outline applies for at least two cards. The empty arrangement counts once, and one card has no home-free placement; neither base convention is introduced on the student pages.

**Implication:** a correct general two-family argument does not by itself cover the zero/one-card cases. A child could give the intended recurrence as though it applies without limits, or be expected to invent an unfamiliar empty-case convention.

**Recommended change:** ask for the two-family rule for two or more cards, or explicitly make the exceptional starting cases part of the general-rule question. Do not print the recurrence or its numerical answer as a procedure. The independent math reviewer should check that the final formulation and guide state the domain and base cases correctly.
