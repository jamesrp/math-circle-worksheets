Week 63: Cards away from home and correcting overlaps

Mathematical kernels

1. Arrange n distinct labeled cards in n fixed labeled homes, with every card used exactly once and every home occupied once. A derangement has no card in its own home. Homes do not move or get relabeled during a comparison; matching is position by position, with no equivalence under turns, reversals, or renaming. The counts !0,!1,!2,!3,!4,!5 are 1,0,1,2,9,44. The empty arrangement counts once for the adult recurrence. The two three-card derangements in homes A,B,C are BCA and CAB. For four cards there are nine, not just the six four-cycles: three are two disjoint swaps. A procedure that forbids self-swaps is not in general the same as listing every derangement. All small counts have been independently checked by exhaustive permutations.

2. Inclusion-exclusion corrects overlap when excluding several properties from one finite universe. For sets A and B of bad outcomes, the number avoiding both is total minus |A| minus |B| plus |A intersection B|; subtracting each bad set removes shared outcomes twice. For several sets, add back every pair intersection, subtract every triple intersection, and continue with alternating signs. Applied to permutations, A_i is the set with card i in its own home. Fixing a chosen set of k homes leaves (n-k)! completions; this does not require those remaining cards to avoid home. Hence !n = sum from k=0 to n of (-1)^k binomial(n,k)(n-k)!. For n=3 this is 6-6+3-1=2; for n=4 it is 24-24+12-4+1=9; for n=5 it is 120-120+60-20+5-1=44. With four cards and only the first two homes forbidden, 24-6-6+2=14; this differs from forbidding all four. Children should handle actual outcome cards, including outcomes in more than one bad group, before any symbolic formula. A finite checkable collection or multiplicity argument proves a count; sampled shuffles do not.

3. For n>=2, !n=(n-1)(!(n-1)+!(n-2)). View an arrangement as arrows from card identity to destination home and select distinguished card n. It can go to any of n-1 other homes k. If card k goes to home n, remove the reciprocal pair; the other n-2 cards form a derangement. Otherwise, if card x goes to home n, remove n and redirect x to k; the remaining n-1 cards form a derangement. These operations are reversible within each case, so the two cases are exhaustive and disjoint. For n=4, each of the three possible destinations contributes !2+!3=1+2=3 arrangements; for n=5, each of four contributes !3+!4=2+9=11. The recurrence is a concrete reversible-construction continuation, not an unexplained formula to substitute into. Mapping a card row to arrows, or performing remove/redirect/restore, requires a small labeled non-task input/intermediate/output visual before first use. Keep arrows and recurrence optional until children can independently retain fixed homes and check no home match.

Sources: Alan Frieze, CMU Discrete Mathematics D14, “Derangements,” pp. 2-4 (definition, small counts, reciprocal-pair versus longer-cycle recurrence), and D16, pp. 1-2 and 4-7 (inclusion-exclusion and fixed-home intersections). Exact URLs, local sources, prior-packet comparison, and verified complete finite records are in source-notes.md and small-instances.json beside this outline. The row/home materials, wording, contexts, diagrams, and task selection are to be newly authored, without copied source prose or figures.

Suggested emphasis by level:

K–1: No independent main packet recommended; an optional adult-read two/three-card placement trial is appropriate only when the child retains fixed homes, uses every card once, and makes the placements.

Grades 2–3: Supported concrete entry with three distinct cards and a supplied movable collection of their six orders; find home-free rows and correct a two-group overlap by moving/counting actual cards. The chiefly Grades 3-5 packet may label this opening Grades 2-5 by prerequisites.

Grades 4–5: Four-card complete counting and fixed-home intersections, pooled five-card cases, and reversible reciprocal-pair/longer-cycle construction; inclusion-exclusion and recurrence explain the same count by different structures, with notation optional.

Materials

Per pair: one five-card deck A-E with different pictures and persistent letter labels (each card about 30 by 40 mm); a fixed labeled five-home strip with cells at least 35 by 45 mm; a second identical deck/strip for retaining a reference row; one movable six-outcome mini-deck for the three-card universe, each outcome identified by its complete row; two transparent or open paper grouping rings, markers and a folder/tray. Use only A-C or A-D and their matching homes when reducing n; do not compress or relabel a mixed subset unnoticed. Outcome-card groups may overlap; a shared outcome is one object with two property markers, not two different permutations. If duplicate physical copies support two groups, retain identical outcome IDs and explicitly reconcile copies. Printed record rows can be smaller because children move the real cards on a separate mat.

For eleven children, six paired/checker kits give 60 working cards and 12 strips, with six reusable six-outcome mini-decks (36 mini-cards). The upper third child rotates as arranger/checker/recorder. Provide cut-ready outcome cards for sorting and pool enumeration into one recoverable table collection; no child should transcribe all 120 five-card permutations or all 44 derangements. Five-card work can be divided by the destination of a distinguished card. Adults may read or record while children own choices. Allow about 15-25 minutes for cutting reusable materials; this estimate and physical handling are untested. Begin with brief free arrangement and a whole-group fixed-home check on non-task labels. Keep one unchanged home/reference strip throughout a check. Offer one investigation at a time; the deeper overlap and reversible-construction work can continue on later visits.
