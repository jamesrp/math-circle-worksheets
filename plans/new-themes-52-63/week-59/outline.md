Week 59: Shapes of constant width

Mathematical kernels

1. Width of a compact convex filled shape is the perpendicular gap between parallel enclosing lines that both touch it. A circle of diameter 60 mm has width 60 mm in every direction; an ellipse with full axes 60×40 mm has widths from 40 to 60 mm; a 60 mm-side equilateral triangle has widths from 30√3≈51.962 to 60 mm. Contrasting physical rotations motivate a conjecture; finitely many ruler readings cannot prove an all-direction claim. Width is not a chord through a marked center, an arbitrary boundary-point distance or perimeter.

2. The Reuleaux triangle is the intersection of three radius-w disks whose centers are equilateral vertices of side w; its boundary is three minor 60-degree circular arcs. It has constant width w. For each pair of parallel support lines, one touches a vertex A and the opposite one the radius-w arc centered at A; their perpendicular gap is the radius. The radius-normal sectors at the three vertices and their opposites cover every orientation. The complete independent proof in research.md establishes why the vertex line really supports the whole body; checking a few symmetric turns is insufficient. At w=60 mm its underlying equilateral centroid is about 34.641 mm from a vertex and 25.359 mm from the opposite arc midpoint, so radial distances and marked-point height vary despite constant total width. A fixed-axle wheel need not stay at constant height; two embracing parallel planes can stay 60 mm apart. No square-hole claim or tested rolling apparatus is assumed.

3. An optional older continuation distinguishes width and perimeter: each Reuleaux arc is one sixth of a radius-w circumference, so the three arcs total πw, the same perimeter as a circle of diameter w. At w=60 mm both are about 188.496 mm. This direct arc calculation needs halves/sixths or adult conversation; the circle's radius is w/2 while the Reuleaux arc radius is w. It is not a rule that all boundary distances or radii are equal.

Materials

Five pair kits for eleven children at three fixed adult-led tables, upper third child rotating as referee: each kit contains pre-cut 0.5–1 mm stiff-card models of a 60 mm diameter circle, 60×40 mm ellipse, 60 mm-side equilateral triangle and 60 mm-width Reuleaux triangle; two straight 300 mm rulers/rails with a 100 mm contacting edge; one 150 mm measuring ruler; two movable right-angle guides; one reusable 10 mm-grid mat at least 200×200 mm; blank paper and pencil. One extra marked-centroid Reuleaux copy per kit supports the upper comparison. Six compasses and six 60 mm equilateral templates are optional construction materials; adults manage compass points/scissors. All circular arcs and equilateral geometry use equal x/y scaling. Print Letter single-sided at 100% with a measured 100 mm check bar; compact page diagrams are not substitutes for full-size cutouts. Rotate and translate the shape while both rails adjust: no fixed center pin. Keep enclosing touching edges parallel without squeezing and measure their perpendicular gap. Millimeter records and cut error are experimental limits; no tolerance is yet established. Print size, rail handling, cutout contact, compass stability, apparent constancy within an intended ±1–2 mm trial tolerance, timing and classroom piloting are all physically unpretested. No axle, platform or drill demonstration is required.
