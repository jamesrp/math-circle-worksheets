Week 58: Fair division with different preferences

Mathematical kernels

1. For a complete allocation of nonnegative additively valued goods, each person uses their own value scale. Proportional means own value at least 1/n of that person's total; envy-free means own value at least that of every other bundle. Envy-free implies proportional by adding the n comparisons; with two people the converse holds. Equal counts do not guarantee either: with R,R,B,B and personal values (R=3,B=1) versus (R=1,B=3), the swapped color bundles each have own value 2 versus 6, while the preferred color split gives 6 versus 2. Exactly five of the 16 labeled complete allocations are envy-free.

2. Two-person cut-and-choose is envy-free for a divisible resource with additive nonatomic values: the cutter makes two pieces of equal own value; the chooser takes a preferred piece. The cutter is indifferent and the chooser receives at least half their own total. Equal value need not mean equal length: on two equal panels with densities 3 then 1, a cutter halves own value at 2/3 of the first panel; a chooser with densities 1 then 3 values the pieces 2/3 and 10/3. Three identical positive-value indivisible cards cannot be fully allocated envy-free between two people; permitting one card to be halved changes that obstruction. Physical approximation does not prove exact equality.

3. Three-person proportional division can still cause envy: observer rows (4,8,0), (0,4,8), (8,0,4) for panels assigned A,B,C each give own value 4 out of 12, while each person prefers another panel worth 8. Cutting three equal pieces and letting two people choose does not inherit the two-person guarantee. The accessible upper destination is a checked counterexample and its explanation; a full trimming algorithm is a later, readiness-dependent subject.
