Week 62: Conflict networks and the fewest slots

Mathematical kernels

1. A finite simple undirected graph encodes pairwise conflicts: one vertex is one activity, and an edge means its two activities cannot share a slot. Each activity takes one whole slot, every activity is assigned exactly once, all slots are interchangeable and have unlimited capacity, and there are no precedence, duration, or availability constraints. A proper vertex coloring is exactly a legal schedule; the chromatic number is its minimum number of slots. A clique of size r needs r slots, but this lower bound need not be sharp. A five-cycle with an extra leaf has clique number 2 and chromatic number 3, although it has no triangle. The four-vertex path AB, BC, CD needs 2 slots; the graph AB, AC, BC, AD, BD needs 3; K4 needs 4. Construction and a matching lower bound establish a minimum; a failed attempt alone does not.

2. A finite simple graph can be colored with at most two colors if and only if it contains no odd cycle. The statement includes disconnected graphs and isolated vertices; an edgeless nonempty graph needs only one color. On any cycle the two colors must alternate, so an odd cycle prevents closure. Conversely, within each component color vertices by even/odd shortest-path distance from a root: an edge joining equal parities would create an odd cycle after removing the common beginning of the two root paths. Children can find an alternating assignment or an actual odd-cycle obstruction in networks with branches, chords, or several components. Ring alternation by itself was already used in Week 33 and is not the new investigation.

3. First-fit assigns vertices in a chosen order, always using the first numbered color absent from already colored neighbors, without recoloring. It makes a proper coloring and uses at most maximum degree plus one colors, but it may use more than the minimum. On AB, BC, CD, order A,D,B,C produces A=1,D=1,B=2,C=3, although A,C in slot 1 and B,D in slot 2 works. Changing the ordering or allowing recoloring changes what the procedure can achieve. This is a distinction between a valid construction and an optimal one, not a rule children must follow throughout. Optional later counting retains fixed vertex names and named slot labels: the path has k(k-1)^3 proper colorings; the graph AB,AC,BC,AD,BD has k(k-1)(k-2)^2, giving 6 with three colors and 48 with four. These count assignments, not schedules modulo permuting slot names. General deletion-contraction P_G(k)=P_(G-e)(k)-P_(G/e)(k) is adult context or a readiness-dependent return visit, not required algebra at the concrete entry.

Suggested emphasis by level:

K–1: Optional adult-read trial entry only when children can independently match each endpoint pair and retain its conflict; no separate K–1 packet or full theorem/counting claim is recommended.

Grades 2–3: Child-owned placement and repair of activity cards or colored counters in several small, contrasting pairwise conflict networks; fewest slots and visible two-color obstruction, with arithmetic limited to counting through eight.

Grades 4–5: Minimum certificates, arbitrary-network two-colorability, an ordering that makes first-fit overuse colors, and readiness-dependent fixed-label counting; branching networks and scheduling add the new direction beyond prior ring coloring.

Materials

Per pair: eight distinct activity cards about 30 by 40 mm, identified by a picture and a fixed letter A-H; four reusable slot-header cards 1-4; eight counters of each of four distinguishable colors (32); two large network mats, pencils/erasers, spare paper, and a folder or tray for preserving one candidate. Use only the needed subset of cards for each graph. Slot headers are placed on the table with enough room beneath them for any number of cards; no printed cell count may silently impose a capacity. Use counters no larger than 15 mm on network sites at least 20 mm across, with separated sites and visible endpoints. Compact recording diagrams are for pencil marks, not card placement. No precision-fit manipulative is needed.

For the current eleven-child KK11 / 3333 / 445 room, six reusable pair/checker kits cover all tables (48 activity cards, 24 slot headers, 192 counters); the main Grades 2-5 work needs three kits at the older tables, while younger children can use an accepted earlier activity. The upper third child rotates as scheduler/checker. Adults cut/label in advance; allow about 15-20 minutes to prepare reusable kits. Begin with brief material handling and a whole-group demonstration of putting two conflicting activities in different slots on a non-task instance. Introduce a graph-to-slot or first-fit recording convention with a small input/intermediate/output visual before its first use. Keep the mathematical choices with children, and use a partner to check each edge. Physical handling, preparation time, and classroom response are untested.
