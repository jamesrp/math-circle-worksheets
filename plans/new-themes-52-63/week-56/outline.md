Week 56: Corners of a solid

Mathematical kernels

1. A finite closed convex polyhedron has V - E + F = 2, where V counts assembled vertices, E assembled edges, and F polygonal faces. More generally this holds for a cell decomposition of a surface homeomorphic to a sphere, with disk faces meeting along whole edges. Cube: (8,12,6); tetrahedron: (4,6,4); triangular prism: (6,9,5); octahedron: (6,12,8); square pyramid: (5,8,5). Subdividing a face by a diagonal adds one edge and one face; splitting an edge adds one vertex and one edge, so both preserve the alternating count. A proof removes one face, draws the remaining surface as a disk, deletes cycle edges one at a time (one edge and one region disappear), and reduces to a tree, whose E = V - 1. Restore the missing face to get 2. Face incidences count each edge twice: the sum of the face side counts is 2E. An opened net has split seams and vertices and is a different counted object. A bare wire frame needs its intended faces made visible before counting. An open box, a band and a torus do not have the sphere's assumptions.

2. At a vertex of a convex polyhedron, its angular defect is 360 degrees minus the sum of the planar face angles meeting there. This is the missing sector when its incident face corners are laid around one point, not the dihedral angle between faces. The sum of the defects over all vertices is 720 degrees. Proof: an n-sided planar face has angle sum 180(n-2) degrees; every edge belongs to two faces, so all face angles total 360(E-F). The defects total 360V - 360(E-F) = 360(V-E+F) = 720. A cube has eight defects of 90 degrees; a regular tetrahedron four of 180; a regular octahedron six of 120; a right triangular prism with equilateral ends six of 120. Three square corners or three, four or five equilateral triangle corners can fold into convex corner fans; four squares or six equilateral triangles fill a flat turn. The positivity condition is necessary for a genuine convex vertex, not a guarantee that any collection of local fans assembles into a solid. Face subdivision does not create curvature. Experiments on models motivate a conjecture; the cancellation/counting argument explains the general fact. The degree-free entry compares gaps with complete paper circles; the upper continuation connects local gaps to the global count.

Suggested emphasis by level: K-1 is an optional adult-read physical entry only: handle closed solids, match face corners and compare flat/full-turn versus folded/gapped fans, with adults recording. Grades 2-3: make/check assembled models, account for shared edges and vertices, compare count changes and gap sizes using objects. Grades 4-5: explain the unchanged V-E+F count and the 720-degree defect total, with ready children using face-angle sums and concrete subdivision arguments. The main packet honestly serves approximately Grades 2-5; arithmetic symbols and proof are readiness-dependent, not compulsory younger tasks.

Materials

At each of the three fixed tables: one closed cardstock cube, regular tetrahedron, regular octahedron and right triangular prism with equilateral ends; removable stickers or small washable marks to prevent double counts; pencils and a whiteboard or spare paper. These are surface models with visible faces, not only flexible frames. Prepare three identical sets; partners share and rotate models, with the upper trio rotating builder/checker/recorder. Models may be made from supplied printable nets or face pieces; a volunteer does not need to buy a construction kit. Use a common face edge of 30 mm, with separate tabs outside the counted faces. A fold or drawn subdivision is an edge only when the task explicitly counts a surface subdivision; tabs, glue overlap and incidental wrinkles are never faces. Assemble and label one example before class; mark the remaining pieces so actual edge pairings are checkable.

Per pair: eight cardstock equilateral triangle corners and eight square corners, all from 30 mm edge faces, tape or reusable adhesive, and two paper full-turn circles about 80 mm across. Keep each fan's vertices at a single marked point, and preserve the face angles while folding. Each triangle corner is 60 degrees and each square corner 90; their gap is an angle, not an area. Printed regular polygons must have equal x/y scale. Models sit beside worksheets, so small diagrams are records rather than full-size working mats; any supplied net/face cutouts must print at 100% scale and state 30 mm edge length. Adult scissors handle preparation. A brief shared demonstration must identify a face, one shared edge, one assembled vertex and one corner fan before those conventions are used. Do not require children to infer hidden faces from a perspective drawing alone. Fit, tape handling and timing have not been physically rehearsed.
