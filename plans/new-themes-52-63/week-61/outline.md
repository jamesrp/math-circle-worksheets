Week 61: Triangles on a ball

Mathematical kernels

1. On an ideal round sphere of radius R, great circles are intersections with planes through its center. They provide the locally straight surface paths. Two distinct nonantipodal points have one shortest surface path, the shorter arc of their great circle; antipodal points have infinitely many shortest semicircles, all of length pi R. Two distinct great circles meet at two antipodal points. A long great-circle arc is not automatically a shortest route. Small latitude circles other than the equator do not supply the same geometry.

2. For a nondegenerate convex spherical triangle contained in an open hemisphere and bounded by the shorter great-circle arcs, its interior angles satisfy alpha + beta + gamma > 180 degrees. Its area is R^2 times the excess E = alpha + beta + gamma - pi when angles are in radians. A lune between semicircles separated by alpha radians has area 2 alpha R^2; a six-lune covering argument proves the triangle formula. In degrees, the triangle's fraction of the whole sphere is (angle sum - 180)/720. These formulas apply to the smaller convex region, not arbitrary curved-edged shapes, self-crossing routes, or the complementary region.

3. A north-pole/equator triangle between meridians separated by theta, with 0 < theta < 180 degrees, has angles 90, 90, theta, so its excess is theta. At theta = 90 it has three right angles and covers one eighth of the sphere; theta = 60 covers one twelfth. Scaling the radius doubles lengths and quadruples areas without changing angles. These checked instances are mathematical background, not prescribed student problems.

Materials

Per pair/trio: one firm nearly round plain ball or globe, about 15-20 cm diameter, on a stable bowl or ring; washable surface marks; at least two flexible strings about 80 cm long; three movable adhesive point markers; a narrow paper right-angle corner; paper and pencil. The string must remain against the surface between fixed endpoints; a chord lifted off the ball is a different route. Elastic loops may help show complete circles, but friction and slipping can prevent an apparent tight loop from being a great circle. Use a verified equator/meridian model for exact-angle examples; do not assert exact angles from a projected worksheet picture or an uncalibrated ball.

For the upper area continuation: a ball whose equator and meridians can be checked, removable colored arc strips, and equal-angle sector templates or marked meridian references with chosen degree measures; optional quarter-sphere pieces or a second ball permit comparison. The exact sphere, radius, angle convention, shorter arcs, and chosen smaller region stay fixed within a task. Provide a non-task worked visual of any unfamiliar sphere-to-record or degrees-to-area-fraction convention before its first use. An orthographic sphere picture is a schematic, not a cut-and-fit template or a flat protractor target. US Letter pages at 100%; retain equal scale in both axes for circles. The actual ball, string, marker, angle-corner and lune handling pretest is unperformed.
