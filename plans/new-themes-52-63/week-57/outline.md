Week 57: Area from dots

Mathematical kernels

1. Pick's theorem: for a nondegenerate simple polygon with straight noncrossing sides, all vertices on a unit square lattice, one connected interior and no holes, its area in lattice-square units is A = I + B/2 - 1. I counts lattice points strictly inside and B counts every lattice point on the boundary, including points in the middle of a straight side. B is not the number of corners or the perimeter length. A 3-by-2 rectangle has I=2, B=10, A=6; the slanted parallelogram (0,0),(2,0),(3,3),(1,3) has I=4, B=6, A=6. The inward-corner polygon (0,0),(3,0),(3,1),(1,1),(1,3),(0,3) has I=0, B=12, A=5. Different shapes with the same (I,B) have the same area; the same area need not imply the same two counts. Areas are positive multiples of one half. A tilted or inward-corner polygon is allowed; curved sides, off-lattice vertices, crossings and touching/pinched boundary loops are outside these assumptions. Counting examples can suggest the formula, but does not prove it. Before any count record, make the boundary/interior convention explicit with a small non-task input/intermediate/output visual that marks dots on a straight side as boundary dots.

2. The count expression is additive under a legal cut: if two lattice polygons meet only along a full common side containing k lattice points including its endpoints, and their union is again a simple polygon, then I = I1 + I2 + k - 2 and B = B1 + B2 - 2k + 2. Hence I+B/2-1 equals the sum of the two separate expressions. This explains why seams vanish from area but change dot categories. A proof first counts axis-parallel rectangles; a diagonal splits a rectangle into two centrally congruent lattice triangles with equal counts. Triangles with an axis-parallel side follow by legal joins to their bounding rectangle. For a general triangle, the repaired proof in research-notes.md uses a lattice quadrilateral with two diagonals: three comparison triangles each have an axis-parallel side, and legal full-side additivity proves the fourth. Bounding-rectangle complements of arbitrary triangles are not always three right triangles. Finally, remove and reattach polygon ears so each join satisfies the assumptions. This correction was made after prompt generation; historical generated prompts remain unchanged. Actual area must initially be checked independently by cuts, congruent copies or checked rectangle subtraction, not by the candidate formula itself. Ready upper children can follow seam cancellation or the full proof on return visits; it need not be a first-visit requirement. Optional upper continuation: a connected lattice polygonal region with h disjoint holes strictly inside its outer boundary and with pairwise disjoint simple boundary loops has A = I+B/2-1+h, counting all hole-boundary points in B and no points in a hole in I. A 4-by-4 square with the open central 2-by-2 square removed has I=0, B=24, h=1 and A=12. This correction requires new assumptions and is not to be introduced before the simple-polygon core has had substantial concrete work.

Precise source, novelty and readiness notes: plans/new-themes-52-63/week-57/research-notes.md.

Suggested emphasis by level: K-1 only an optional adult-supported boundary/inside sorting and physical half-square entry; no claim of an independent Pick investigation. Grades 2-3, chiefly third graders with half-unit area readiness: make polygons, distinguish side dots from corners, check area by physical decomposition and compare recoverable (I,B,area) records. Grades 4-5: investigate exact area from the counts, challenge candidate rules, and explain seam cancellation or a triangulation; holes are an optional later continuation. Main band is approximately Grades 3-5, with concrete supported entry in Grade 2 where prerequisites fit.

Materials

Five partner sets for eleven children at the fixed KK11 / 3333 / 445 tables, one adult anchored per table. Per set: two 7-by-7 square-lattice dot sheets with 20 mm spacing (six unit intervals across), a straightedge, pencil/eraser, two styles of removable dot marks or colored pencils, ten 20 mm paper unit squares and twenty diagonal half-square triangles, spare matching dot sheets, and scissors with adult assistance. A pegboard with exactly the same spacing and rubber bands may replace one paper grid if available; do not require a purchase. Polygons lie on dot centers and remain visible while counts are recorded. A unit square's area is 1 regardless of its physical 20 mm side; a diagonal half-square's area is 1/2. Do not silently swap between a finer grid and the original units. Provide enough copies to preserve one candidate while a partner checks or cuts a duplicate. No child must simultaneously keep separate boundary, interior and area tallies in memory. One marked shape plus one short record is sufficient.

Work directly on roomy dot grids; one or two usable grids per page is preferable to many tiny boards. Dots must remain visible under polygon edges, particularly intermediate boundary dots; distinguish vertices without concealing other lattice points. Use equal x/y scale. Printed pieces and any physical grids must state 20 mm spacing/100% print scale. The whole-group launch briefly demonstrates making a legal noncrossing polygon, marking a boundary dot in a straight side and an inside dot, then finding its area with a duplicate shape; it must not disclose the full Pick rule. Children choose their constructions and comparisons. Optional hole models need clear separated boundary loops, never a hole touching the outer boundary. Cutting, pegboard fit and pacing have not been physically rehearsed.
