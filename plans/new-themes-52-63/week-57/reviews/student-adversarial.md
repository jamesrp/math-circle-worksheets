## Concrete findings for the reviser

### 1. Medium: the supplied core never encounters odd boundary counts or half-integer area before giving the formula

**Where:** pp. 1–5, especially P2, P4 and the convention panel on p. 1; `assets/polygons.json`.

All printed examples before P5 have even B and integral area. The non-task area triangle has area 1; the count model has `(I,B,A)=(1,8,4)`. P1's shapes both have area 6. P2 supplies `(0,12,5)` and `(3,8,6)`, and P3 forces `(2,6,4)` for both constructions. Thus the first concrete work does not show why “half of B” must accommodate an odd B or why exact area need not be a whole number. The first fixed half-integer example is the P8 triangle, area 11/2, well after the candidate rule and supplied theorem. A child may independently choose an odd-B polygon in P4, but the packet does not ensure this contrast.

This is a **coverage and sequencing issue**, not an incorrect formula or a claim that fractions must be taught from scratch. The source README honestly requires half-unit readiness. Give the concrete core at least one purposeful nonempty polygon with odd B and independently checkable half-integer area before P5, preferably as another contrasting case under an existing substantial problem rather than a new tiny task. Do not turn the investigation into the Week 31 empty-triangle catalog. A compact alternative or supplement is to change the non-task copy demonstration to two halves of a unit square, so its output actually includes 1/2. That visual is useful if the launch is intended to establish half-square area; it is not mechanically required for children who already possess the stated prerequisite.

**Verification after revision:** independently count the new polygon, check its area geometrically without Pick, and inspect its full-sized rendered figure and answer space. Preserve intermediate side dots in at least one core example.

### 2. Medium, small correction: P2 excludes the independent-area method that the opening visual demonstrates

**Where:** p. 1 area panel and P1; p. 2 P2.

The worked visual teaches joining a second congruent copy. P1 accepts “a cut or a second copy,” and P3 again accepts “cuts or copies,” but P2 says “Check area with pieces or a cut.” Its sloping triangle has sides with slopes 3 and −1. Uncut unit squares and the supplied diagonal half-square triangles alone cannot tile that entire triangle: the slope-3 edge cannot be the boundary of a union of those pieces. Therefore the ostensibly simplest material option is not available for the triangle, and the practiced copy option is omitted.

Retain the independent area check, but allow copies in P2 as well. This lets a child use the method just demonstrated and keeps an unnecessary method restriction from becoming an adult rescue. Cutting still needs a duplicate or a recoverable original, as the source README already states. This finding does not claim that the triangle is impossible to dissect or that the slope makes it unsuitable.

### 3. Low: put the hole boundary-separation rule at the first hole construction

**Where:** shared opening rules on p. 1; P9 on p. 9; P10 on p. 10.

P9 correctly defines the new I/B convention and draws disjoint loops. However, “Keep every boundary loop separate” first appears in P10, after P9 has already asked children to invent a second one-hole shape. The opening “one closed outline” is relaxed implicitly by “Leave no holes until Problem 9,” so the rules for their new second loop must be recovered from context. The global no-touch rule substantially protects the intended mathematics; this is a clarity improvement, not proof of an admitted counterexample.

Move or restate the essential separated-loop condition where P9 first permits holes, and make clear that its hole stays inside the outer outline. “Count dots on both outlines in B” is slightly less ambiguous than “either outline.” Keep this brief; no worked count of the target ring is needed, and the +1 correction should remain the investigation.

## Adult-route requirements, not reasons to flatten the student mathematics

**P4 is conjecturing, not an elementary entrance exam.** There are only four requested records before it, three distinct ones: `(0,12,5)`, `(3,8,6)` and `(2,6,4)` twice. The panel adds `(1,8,4)` if the pair uses it. Fitting an expression in two counts is much more demanding than making polygons and classifying dots. The README already explicitly says failure to discover a formula must not bar P5, and the cited elementary source reports that discovery was not expected universally. Carry that route into the separately authored guide: accept attempted rules and counterexamples, or supply the theorem and let children continue constructing. Do not require an adult to invent the rule while pretending the child did so. A small wording change to “Try to find…” is reasonable but is not a necessary substitute for the adult route.

**P7–8 are readiness-dependent explanations.** The P6 examples support them well: the rectangle seam has three dots and the slanted diagonal has four, so they are purposeful contrasts. Keeping endpoint dots distinct from seam-interior dots is the central reasoning. General rectangle/triangle arguments and a lattice-vertex triangulation are an additional abstraction demand beyond independent area checking. The source notes honestly identify that demand and multiple visits. Preserve this material, and let the guide distinguish a checked example, a conjecture and a general argument, with optional adult hints rather than prescribed tiny student steps. Formal proof of all polygons is not a realistic universal first-visit requirement for the printed band; that is an unpiloted readiness hypothesis, not a ban.

**Physical support remains essential.** The source README already calls for spare full grids and duplicate shapes, adult-assisted scissors, anchored adults, and saved records. P5 has one large board despite asking for many constructions; it is usable with those extra copies. Prepare enough copies to preserve candidates rather than expecting repeated erasure or cutting the only record. No facilitator guide exists at this stage by design, so I have not treated its absence as a student-writer failure. The later guide must include its theorem-first overview, preparation, routes, solutions and explicit unperformed physical/pilot checks.
