# Source notes for the Weeks 1–17 return-visit work

Consulted 2026-10-04. These are source observations, not claims that our new companions have been piloted.

Natasha Rozhkovskaya, *Math Circles for Elementary School Students*, downloaded EPUB in `external-resources/msri-math-circle-books/` (author verified from the EPUB's `content.opf` metadata):

- Lesson 1, “At the lesson” (`OEBPS/part0011.xhtml`): the semester combined hands-on activities with ordinary problem discussions. The first drawing procedure took longer than planned, especially the over/under crossing step; adults helped correct it. This source reports only one or two completed knot drawings despite a larger plan.
- Lesson 2, “At the lesson” (`part0012.xhtml`): children enjoyed coded pictures and requested more; some were not fluent readers but understood sophisticated rules by listening. Adequate grid size and a marked start mattered. This supports distinguishing reading help from adults performing the reasoning.
- Lesson 3, “At the lesson” (`part0013.xhtml`): the group had very different preparation; extra problems were offered to quicker participants and were usually not discussed with the whole group. Some substantial notions were discussed through examples rather than full definitions or proofs.
- Lesson 4, “At the lesson” (`part0014.xhtml`): physical square/cube constructions exposed misconceptions and generated children's patterns. The random-maze experiment was followed by its combinatorics in the next meeting. This is a documented source example of work continuing across meetings.
- Lesson 13, “At the lesson” (`part0023.xhtml`): children's actual random choices and reported die outcomes sometimes departed from the intended random procedure. The instructor did not equate those observed frequencies with a validated theoretical distribution.

Our design inferences: offer several investigations within a theme as selectable return visits; demonstrate unfamiliar conventions briefly; use recoverable physical records; keep claims from experiments distinct from explanations; retain optional depth in the adult guide. The precise format and entry recommendations in the new companions are our proposals, not Rozhkovskaya's classroom observations.

Existing project classroom evidence: `worksheet-workflow/context.md`, updated October 3, records the current eleven children and three adults, pleasure in physical tiling, difficulty retaining paper lamp rules, fixed pairs, and a short common launch. The return-visit work uses this context and does not claim a new classroom trial.
