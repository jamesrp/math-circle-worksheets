Week 1: Pattern-block return visits

Mathematical kernels

1. Perimeter from joining pieces: for edge-to-edge connected arrangements of t small triangles and r small rhombi, P = 3t + 4r - 2s in unit edges, where s counts internal full unit sides shared by two pieces. Joining along one side removes two perimeter edges. Holes contribute their boundary; touching at a point does not join pieces. For fixed inventory, different adjacency/contact patterns change P, while area stays t+2r small triangles. Explore contrasting compact vs stretched arrangements and seek extremal boundaries, without assuming the formula gives realizability.

2. Corner packing: angles at an interior meeting point total 360 degrees. A green corner or acute blue corner consumes one 60-degree sector; an obtuse blue corner consumes two. Thus g+a+2o=6 is necessary, and fans of unit-edge corners can realize the locally allowed sector patterns; a full tiled region imposes additional global restrictions. Concrete fitting should precede numbers/degrees. Discover which counts of pieces can close a gap around a marked point and which mixtures cannot.

3. Rotational symmetry of a fixed tiled hexagon: a rotation is a symmetry only if every piece and seam lands on a corresponding piece/seam. Six green triangles meeting at the center admit 60-degree rotations; three rhombi meeting there admit 120-degree rotations and not 60-degree rotations. A finite pattern's symmetry rotations form a subgroup of the six hexagon turns, so possible orders divide 6 (1,2,3,6). Children create and test mosaics, including asymmetry and reflection distinctions; no need to enumerate every tiling.

Sources: elementary geometry, graph dynamics, permutations, modular arithmetic and Latin squares respectively; kernels independently derived here, not adapted problem statements. Pedagogy: Math Circle by the Bay, Preface printed pp. viii–x (PDF pp.9–11), supports themes that deepen over years and variable pace; the three-investigation format is our design inference.

Suggested emphasis by level: K–1: genuine concrete construction/turn-taking entries only; Grades 2–3: manipulate, compare, find examples; Grades 4–5: organize complete small cases, establish bounds/impossibility, extend conjectures. One Grades 2–5 packet with adult-read concrete entry where suitable; do not manufacture a separate young-child problem.

Materials

Per pair: 12 small green equilateral triangles and 12 small blue rhombi, one-inch edges; other blocks set aside. A 1-inch scale bar if drawing a working mat; favor free tabletop building and compact recording sketches, not fit-sensitive printed boards. Paper/pencil and tracing paper optional. Green corners are 60 degrees; blue corners are 60 or 120 degrees. Rotations of a finished design carry its internal seams with it.
