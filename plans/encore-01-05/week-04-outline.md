Week 4: Stars-and-wheels return visits

Mathematical kernels

1. Two moving counters meet: on an n-position ring, counters beginning at a,b and moving u,v each turn meet at t iff (u-v)t=b-a modulo n. Meetings exist iff gcd(n,u-v) divides b-a. If they start together, the first positive meeting is n/gcd(n,u-v). Explicitly both move before checking; a crossing between dots does not count. Varied examples show both delayed and impossible meetings.

2. Two permitted hops: with choice of clockwise hops a or b each turn, reachable positions from 0 are exactly multiples of gcd(n,a,b), because on a finite ring backwards steps can be replaced by enough forwards steps. Allowing two noncoprime hops may reach all dots together: n=12,a=4,b=3. Shortest routes are a different question, found by BFS on directed choices. Compare to base fixed-hop orbits; child-owned route search comes first.

3. Coloring necklaces: binary colorings around a ring may have smaller repeat blocks. A coloring with rotational period d on n beads has d dividing n and n/d symmetry rotations. For n=6 with exactly three red/three blue beads there are four classes under rotation only; under rotation AND reflection there are three classes (two rotation classes are mirror partners). Explicit rotation-equivalence conventions essential; all20 labeled patterns give 4 rotation classes. Concrete necklaces and comparing hand rotations ground the classification.

Materials

Per pair: two differently colored counters, numbered rings of 8–12 positions at least 20 mm wide (one wide ring/page when working on it), 12 red and 12 blue counters, pencil and paper. Counters jump clockwise unless stated. Time advances in synchronized turns. Pattern equality includes the colors; no reflection equivalence unless explicitly asked. Offer drawings and hand turns instead of fractions or modular notation.
