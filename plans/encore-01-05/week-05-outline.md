Week 5: Tower-city return visits

Mathematical kernels

1. Local city trades: in a Latin city, an alternating 2-by-2 rectangle a,b / b,a can swap a and b at all four corners without breaking rows/columns; the rectangle need not consist of adjacent squares. A 3-by-3 Latin square has no such two-height trade; order4 cities have either four or twelve such trades (four in the cyclic-type family, twelve in the Klein-type family); smaller embedded rectangles need not admit a trade. Concrete attempts should reveal the condition rather than print it. A changed city may change visibility clues, so this is a repair rule, not a claim of clue preservation.

2. Diagonal city conditions: require each main diagonal as well as rows/columns to contain one of every height. None of the12 order3 Latin squares meets BOTH diagonal conditions; an order4 example exists, and of576 labeled order4 Latin squares48 satisfy both. A main diagonal is corner-to-opposite-corner, explicitly highlighted. A city can satisfy one diagonal without the other. Enumeration of3 plus a constructive4 example is elementary; do not require listing576.

3. Climbing roof routes: from a square move to a side-neighbor of strictly larger height. Every route terminates because height strictly increases. A peak is a square with no higher side-neighbor; even a non-tallest tower can be a peak. In Latin cities rows/columns unique, all tallest towers are peaks, but others can also be. For3 and4 enumerate possible peak counts and find contrasting city designs; attempt to make every start reach a tallest tower is equivalent to having no lower peaks. This is an increasing-path/local-versus-global question, different from base skyline visibility.

Materials

Per pair: 40 snap cubes to build a 4-by-4 city or 18 to build a 3-by-3 city, 25-mm minimum grid squares, pencil. All rows and columns have exactly one of each height. Touching means sharing a side, not a corner. A city may be sketched using a single height number per square after building. No roof walk rule relies on line-of-sight.
