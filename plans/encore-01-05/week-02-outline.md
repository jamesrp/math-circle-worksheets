Week 2: Lamp return visits

Mathematical kernels

1. Hidden wiring identification: a switch toggles a fixed subset of lamps; seeing both before and after identifies that subset. For three independent unknown switches on three lamps, three separate one-switch experiments suffice and fewer experiments cannot distinguish all 2^9 possible panels (each observed three-lamp result supplies at most three binary facts). A partner hides three subset cards and operates honestly. The first discovery is wiring from observed changes; the deeper question is minimal guaranteed trials, not base solvability.

2. Three-lamp moves on a ring: switch i toggles lamps i,i+1,i+2 cyclically. For n>=3, null press choices satisfy x_i+x_(i-1)+x_(i-2)=0 modulo 2, hence repeat with period 3. If n is not divisible by 3 only the zero choice changes nothing, and every target has a unique subset of presses. If 3 divides n there are four do-nothing choices and only 2^(n-2) reachable targets; the three residue-class lamp parities must agree from all OFF. Use n=4,5,6 for concrete work. Keep a visible press marker rather than mental state.

3. Lamp transport: a NEW switch exchanges the two endpoint counters rather than toggling them. On a connected graph exactly the ON-count is invariant; every picture with the same count is reachable. On a line, list ON positions a_1<...<a_k and target b_1<...<b_k: minimum adjacent exchanges = sum |a_i-b_i|, because ON particles cannot cross one another and each exchange moves one ON one step. Compare line versus cycle shortcut experiments; give the exact formula only to adults.

Sources: elementary geometry, graph dynamics, permutations, modular arithmetic and Latin squares respectively; kernels independently derived here, not adapted problem statements. Pedagogy: Math Circle by the Bay, Preface printed pp. viii–x (PDF pp.9–11), supports themes that deepen over years and variable pace; the three-investigation format is our design inference.

Suggested emphasis by level: K–1: genuine concrete construction/turn-taking entries only; Grades 2–3: manipulate, compare, find examples; Grades 4–5: organize complete small cases, establish bounds/impossibility, extend conjectures. One Grades 2–5 packet with adult-read concrete entry where suitable; do not manufacture a separate young-child problem.

Materials

Per pair: 6 reversible counters (clearly different faces), paper numbered lamp boards with counter positions at least 20 mm wide; card switch strips may physically span the selected lamps. A partner changes ALL specified counters. Blank paper, pencil, folder to hide wiring in one investigation. No real electrics. ON/OFF state is externalized in counters.
