Ten weekly meetings of about one hour. Weeks 2–10 now develop explicit undergraduate and research mathematics through concrete investigations, using revised Week 1 as the benchmark. The [redesign rationale](fall-weeks-02-10-mathematical-redesign.md) explains the progression; the [print index](../lowell-math-circle-year-2/source/fall-weeks-02-10/README.md) provides a shared Week 2 collection, three student levels and optional grades 6–7 investigations for Weeks 3–10, and separate facilitator solutions. The [companion activity guide](fall-k-5-year-a-activities.md) links full instructions, hints, and checked solutions. Record actual use in the [session log](../LOCAL-RESOURCES.md#private-records).

Each week shares a mathematical idea, a launch, and most of its materials. The levels change how children enter the problem and how far they investigate it. All three use physical exploration. Explanations can be spoken, drawn, or demonstrated with objects.

## Choosing an entry point

These labels guide preparation for the other weeks, not placement by age. Week 2 uses a common opening and routes chosen from a shared collection; no child must finish an earlier route to enter another. A child can use picture messages while doing advanced code reasoning, or explore a small game deeply while another child plays on a larger board.

| Starting version | Reading and arithmetic | Reasoning to support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **K–1** | No independent reading or writing required. Match symbols, compare heights, count small collections with support. | Follow one rule at a time; test a choice; show what changed. Use large pieces, visible examples, and short rounds. |
| **2–3** | Instructions may be read aloud. Recognize the supplied letters or replace them with pictures; small counts and subtraction through play. | Coordinate constraints, classify small cases, and give a reason covering more than one example. |
| **4–5** | Counting, addition/subtraction, and odd/even reasoning support most tasks. Multiples, remainders, and binary bundles are introduced concretely where used. | Distinguish examples from proofs; establish uniqueness, impossibility, completeness, or optimality. Formal proof notation is unnecessary. |

The original feedback still guides the whole plan: shapes, ciphers, blocks, games, logic, and billiards were popular. Formal combinatorial counting is optional at every level. The older version should first offer a satisfying puzzle; its general explanation is a further invitation.

## A shared hour

**Current roster, reported September 28, 2026:** ten children, **K, K, 1 / 3, 3, 3, 3 / 4, 4, 5**, with three adults: the organizer, another mathematician, and one non-mathematician parent. Start with three nearby working groups of 3 / 4 / 3. Placement remains flexible according to prerequisites and observed readiness. This update does not establish who attended the earlier Week 1 meeting.

The books support shared themes, different depths, independent attempts, and substantial adult help. They do not supply a tested arrangement for these ten children and three adults. The following is our current proposal; the [September 18 lesson-format source notes][lesson-format] preserve the source evidence and the earlier seven-child/two-adult proposal.

### Adult coverage and timing

Give each group a named adult: **parent → KK1; other mathematician → 3333; organizer → 445**. Before the session, give every adult the common launch, one worked example, and a concrete next task. The parent's job is to read one prompt at a time, check the visible rule, help a child reset, and invite a demonstration; it does not require supplying a proof. The unified Week 2 adult guide includes the parent script, page choices, and answers for all routes.

Keep the older groups close enough for an explicit help handoff. If KK1 needs mathematical help, the parent keeps them on a familiar short task and signals the organizer. The organizer can answer from the adjacent workspace; if a visit is needed, first ask the other mathematician to cover both older groups on their known tasks. The parent stays with KK1 throughout. No adult silently leaves their group or expects children to seek someone elsewhere. If two groups need help at once, pause the new question and replay a familiar example until coverage is available.

| Approximate time | Children | Adult attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0–10** | Explore the week's materials; returning children can try a familiar kind of challenge. | Each adult joins their group and notices who needs help handling materials. |
| **10–15** | One brief shared demonstration of the legal action, goal, and one record. | Organizer demonstrates; the other adults help children follow. Save level-specific extensions for the tables. |
| **15–20** | Begin the starting tasks and repeat the action just demonstrated. | Parent supports KK1; the other mathematician starts 3333; organizer starts 445. Give a new representation its own short local demonstration when it becomes useful. |
| **20–35** | Investigate, play, and compare attempts. KK1 may do several short rounds. | Adults remain anchored. Give each child a work surface and a clear next action. Use the agreed handoff for questions needing another adult. |
| **35–40** | Brief movement/reset, with an optional discovery to show. | Check engagement and adjust the next task; there is no requirement to have reached the same point. |
| **40–55** | Continue a good investigation, change a constraint, or choose another prepared activity. | Same coverage. KK1 may move into the Week 2 drawing reserve while older groups continue. |
| **55–60** | Each group can show one discovery, question, or failed attempt; tidy up. | Help explanations use objects or drawings. The older extension need not become a lesson for everyone. |

These times are flexible. A productive construction or game can run across a boundary; a stalled group needs a change sooner. The important rhythm is trying, talking about an attempt, and trying again. The [Week 2 shared plan](week-02-shared-collection.md) gives its specific launch, routes, stopping choices, and earlier 30–35 movement break. Its drawing choices are open to every table.

Except for Week 2’s specific timing, the individual weekly plans and facilitator packets retain their mathematical activities and the **35–40 movement/reset**; any older references there to seven children, two adults, or a lone fifth grader are superseded by this September 28 coverage plan. Each group has a satisfying stopping point plus an optional proof continuation. A proof can wait until another visit; note whether a claim was tried, conjectured, checked in named cases, proved, or given as a theorem in the [use log](../LOCAL-RESOURCES.md#private-records). For the general arithmetic in Weeks 4 and 9, the [shared divisibility scaffold](coprime-divisibility-scaffold.md) provides an adult-supported bridge beyond finite examples.

### Working in threes and fours

- **Independent construction or drawing:** give every child a work surface and enough materials for their own attempt before comparing. A group need not share one construction. Invite each child to show a move or observation before settling on an answer.
- **KK1 and 445:** rotate maker, solver, and checker in short puzzle exchanges, but offer solo attempts whenever waiting would dominate. For KK1, the adult can handle recording and checking while each child chooses or makes a move.
- **3333:** start as two pairs for exchanges or games, then compare answers across pairs. Rotate partners or roles without making fast finishers responsible for teaching everyone else.
- **Take-away and Rook's Move:** preserve two opposing sides, since three-player rules change the mathematics. In a group of three, two children can play while the third checks legal moves and chooses the next start, rotating after every short round regardless of who wins. Or have the adult play the third child so all children play at once. In a group of four, use two pairs.

### Supporting the upper group

The two fourth graders and fifth grader now have peers and a dedicated mathematician. Begin from a shared problem when it is interesting, then offer deeper questions by readiness. Use independent attempts followed by comparison, or let two children play while the organizer plays or discusses with the third; rotate so everyone gets direct mathematical conversation. Being oldest does not assign the fifth grader a teaching role.

For secret-code games, a child or adult can hold the secret and provide feedback while their partner solves. In Weeks 6–8, budget time for actual play as well as strategy discussion. Record how the 445 group works together and where any child waits for feedback; these are questions to observe, not established outcomes.

## Preparation and future reuse

One shared kit per theme should do most of the work. The new [weekly plans](fall-weeks-02-10-mathematical-redesign.md) supersede the earlier Weeks 2–10 proposals, which are preserved in [the archive](archive-pre-depth/fall-k-5-year-a.md). Vary the outlines, number of code positions, clue sets, starting piles, and board sizes through small task cards. Use visible icons and dot counts where possible. For concurrent groups, label the take-away move cards clearly so 1-or-2 and 1/3/4 games do not get mixed up.

Keep a record of the actual challenges each cohort encounters, not only the theme. Returning children should meet new diagrams, messages, clue sets, or questions when they move to another level. An older version need not repeat the youngest starter if a child already knows it. The saved Lowell materials contain only part of last year's history, so check additional prior use before choosing the final instances.

The common-theme approach is supported by [Math Circle by the Bay][bay], preface pp. viii–x (PDF pp. 9–11), which describes using themes across age groups with different pace and depth, manipulatives, and repeated practice explaining ideas. The specific level assignments here are our proposed adaptations, to be adjusted after observing the children. Relevant MSRI teaching notes and the broader source review remain in the [original plan][year-a].
