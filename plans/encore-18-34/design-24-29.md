## Chosen companion layout

One selected-band shared student PDF per week, named `week-NN/week-NN-bonus.pdf`; one separate adult companion `week-NN/week-NN-bonus-facilitator.pdf`. Portable editable sources and builder belong in `source/week-NN-bonus/`, with the matching ZIP. These are the agreed names, **planned outputs**, not files produced by this design stage. Student header is `Week N / <base topic> encore / Grades <selected band>`; footer retains Bellingham Math Circle, week and unique bonus packet ID. Problems should be numbered consecutively within each companion. The guide can route different tables to suitable concrete starts without forcing a separate K–1 adaptation.

Each final guide must open with precise facts/limits from the final tasks, then supply readiness/materials, launch, solutions, hints and extensions. The outlines contain mathematical support rather than a prescribed sequence of student problems. No base PDF, base guide, Week 1 bonus, AGENTS, global index or remote copy was edited.

## Source attribution and design lessons

- The current six editable outlines and mathematical overviews were read as local precedents. Each new outline records their source titles and avoids attributing our new examples to a paper that was only cited for the base model.
- Rozhkovskaya, *Math Circles for Elementary School Students*, downloaded EPUB `OEBPS/part0010.xhtml`, “Introduction: Berkeley 2009”: actually reports individual adult assistance, additional instructors and actively involved parents. This supports keeping reading/recording assistance explicit; the current one-adult-per-fixed-table staffing is local context, not a claim from that book.
- Same book, Lesson 12, `OEBPS/part0022.xhtml`, Problems 12.4–12.12 and “At the lesson”: actually reports that imagined two-mirror work became difficult without mirrors and that a later workshop provided physical mirrors; it describes matching transformed physical figures to their shadows. Our folded-strip, marked-sheet and unfolded-hole choices are adaptations/inferences, not reproductions of its exact tasks.
- Same book, Lesson 13, `OEBPS/part0023.xhtml`, “Gnomes”/“Random choice with equally likely outcomes” and “At the lesson”: actually reports children altering or coordinating outcomes to obtain desired colors and uncertainty about die fairness. Our Week 24 explicit replacement, independence, private mixtures and distinction between complete counts and trials respond to that lesson; it does not prove the new probabilities or optimal mixture.
- Organizer's year-one experience and the current context emphasize time handling real materials, with children retaining choices after the launch. That is local evidence, distinct from mathematical verification or predictions about these unpiloted encore pages.
