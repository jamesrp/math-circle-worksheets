# Week 116 (draft plan): Kaleidoscopes

**Strand:** symmetry (Patterns and symmetry). **Deep-math field:** geometry, group theory (dihedral and reflection groups). **Status:** draft plan, November 2026. No student pages, guide, review card or pilot.

## The deep idea

Two mirrors hinged at an angle θ show copies of whatever lies between them. When θ = 180°/n, the copies fill the circle exactly: there are 2n wedges, n facing one way and n mirrored, and the reflections in the two mirrors generate the dihedral group of order 2n (n turns, including doing nothing, and n flips). Two flips in mirrors at angle α make a turn by 2α, which is why the turns are multiples of 360°/n. When 360°/θ is an odd whole number (72° gives 5, 120° gives 3) or not a whole number, the copies meeting at the far seam are not mirror images of each other, so the picture is inconsistent and depends on where the eye is. Leonardo's theorem says this exhausts the possibilities for finite figures: every finite symmetry group of a plane figure is cyclic (n turns only, like a pinwheel) or dihedral (n turns and n mirrors, all mirrors through one centre and equally spaced). The proof: a finite group fixes the centre of any orbit, its turns are multiples of a smallest one, and once there is one mirror, composing it with the n turns gives exactly n mirrors. With three mirrors forming a triangular tube, the reflected triangles tile the plane consistently only when every angle is 180°/k for a whole number k and the angles add to 180°, that is 1/p + 1/q + 1/r = 1, which has exactly three solutions: 60-60-60, 90-45-45 and 90-60-30. A 120-30-30 triangle tiles the plane but not by reflection, because 120° is not 180°/k. Proved: all of the above. Checked here: the group generated by two mirrors at 180°/n has 2n elements for n = 2 to 9, the three triangles, and (as an extension) the arithmetic for three mirrors meeting at a corner, where 90-90-90 shows 8 copies. Children can find the angle-to-copies rule, notice the failures at odd counts, sort found designs into the two Leonardo families, and test which paper triangles close up when flipped round a corner.

## A kid-friendly way in

Each pair of children gets two small craft mirrors (acrylic, about 10 cm square) taped along one edge into a hinge, or Mylar glued to card as in the RAFT sheet, and a printed sheet of wedges drawn from a corner point at 90°, 60°, 45°, 36°, 30°, 72°, 40° and 120°. Each wedge holds a small drawing that touches one edge (half a heart, an arrow, a dot). Stand the hinge on a wedge, look in, count the copies. Prep: tape 8 to 10 hinges and print the wedge sheet, about 25 minutes. Launch (2 to 3 minutes): stand the hinge on the 90° wedge with an arrow; everyone counts four arrows, two pointing one way and two the other; then close the hinge to 60° and ask how many now.

## By band

- **K–1:** count copies on the wedge sheet; make a "flower with 6 petals" and a "flower with 8 petals" by choosing the wedge; sort picture cards of finished designs by the wedge that made them. Counting only, no reading.
- **2–3:** a table of angle and copies; predict 30° and 20° before trying; look closely at 72° and 120°: what goes wrong at the back seam? Then a sorting task with printed logos, flowers and wheel hubs: which have mirror lines, which only spin (a pinwheel)? Count spins and mirrors for each and look for a rule.
- **4–5:** why there are 2n copies (two flips in hinged mirrors make a turn by twice the angle; check it with an arrow); why every symmetric design has as many mirrors as spins or none at all (Leonardo's theorem, argued with tracing paper); the triangle test: cut card triangles 60-60-60, 90-45-45, 90-60-30, 120-30-30 and 72-54-54, flip-trace each one around a corner and see which close up exactly. Extension: three mirrors at the corner of a box show 8 copies.

## Checked facts

Computed in `check_symmetry_games.py`, `check_week_116()`:

- two mirrors at 180°/n generate 2n symmetries (n turns, n flips) for n = 2 to 9;
- copies 360/θ: 4 at 90°, 6 at 60°, 8 at 45°, 10 at 36°, 12 at 30°; odd counts 3 at 120°, 5 at 72°, 9 at 40°, which clash at the far seam;
- 1/p + 1/q + 1/r = 1 has exactly the solutions (3, 3, 3), (2, 4, 4), (2, 3, 6), giving 60-60-60, 90-45-45, 90-60-30;
- three mirrors through a point: 4/(1/p + 1/q + 1/r − 1) gives 8, 24, 48, 120 for (2, 2, 2), (2, 3, 3), (2, 3, 4), (2, 3, 5) (arithmetic only; the formula is standard but not cited here).
