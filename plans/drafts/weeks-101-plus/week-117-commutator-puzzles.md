# Week 117 (draft plan): Commutator puzzles

**Strand:** symmetry (Patterns and symmetry). **Deep-math field:** group theory (permutation groups, commutators, parity). **Status:** draft plan, November 2026. No student pages, guide, review card or pilot.

## The deep idea

In a puzzle of turning rings, each turn moves every token on one ring one spot along, so a turn is a cycle, and the arrangements you can reach form the group generated by the turns. The commutator of two moves A and B is "do A, do B, undo A, undo B", written ABA⁻¹B⁻¹. If the spots moved by A and the spots moved by B overlap in exactly one spot, the commutator moves exactly three tokens round a little cycle and leaves everything else home (Charlton's notes, Theorem 1); this is the engine of every human method for Rubik-type puzzles, and a conjugate (set up, do the commutator, undo the setup) moves the three-cycle wherever it is needed. Three-cycles generate all even arrangements. Parity gives the obstruction: a turn of a ring of k spots is a k-cycle, which is even exactly when k is odd, so if every ring has an odd number of spots, every reachable arrangement is even and no turn sequence swaps just two tokens. Concretely, with two rings of 5 spots sharing one spot (9 tokens) exactly half of the 9! = 362880 arrangements, 181440, can be reached; with two rings of 4 sharing one spot (7 tokens) all 5040 can. Parity is not the only obstruction: two rings of 4 sharing two neighbouring spots, or a 2-by-3 grid with two turning 2-by-2 squares, reach only 120 of the 720 arrangements, an exceptional case (a copy of the group PGL(2, 5)) in Montenegro, Huckaby and Harmon's classification of rotating-square puzzles. Proved: the three-cycle theorem, generation by three-cycles, the parity obstruction, the rotating-squares classification. Checked here by listing every reachable arrangement: the counts above, that the commutator moves 3 tokens when the rings share one spot and 4 when they share two, and the most turns ever needed (6 for two rings of 3, 17 for two rings of 5). Children can discover the three-token move, use it to solve, and meet the "you can never swap just two" wall.

## A kid-friendly way in

A printed "figure-eight" board: two circles of spots sharing one spot, with numbered or picture tokens. A turn slides every token on one ring one spot clockwise (L or R), or anticlockwise (l or r). Three boards: two rings of 3 (5 tokens), two rings of 5 (9 tokens), two rings of 4 (7 tokens). Children record a turn word on a strip as they go. Prep: print boards on card and count out tokens, about 15 minutes. Launch (2 to 3 minutes): on a large board, turn the left ring once and show how the shared spot's token moves into the left ring; undo it with l. Then everyone does L R l r on the small board and looks at which tokens moved.

## By band

- **K–1:** the two-rings-of-3 board with five animal tokens. An adult scrambles with two or three turns; the child brings the animals home. Then "do, do, undo, undo" (L R l r) and point to the animals that moved: always three. No reading.
- **2–3:** the two-rings-of-5 board. Find a short turn word that moves only three tokens; use it to fix a scramble in which three tokens have traded places; then the open problem: can you swap just two tokens and leave all the others home? Compare with the two-rings-of-4 board, where you can.
- **4–5:** explain the three: follow each token through L R l r (a token on neither ring never moves; a token on one ring only goes and comes back). Build a conjugate (setup turns, the three-move, undo the setup) to cycle any three chosen tokens. The parity argument: a turn of 5 spots is 4 swaps, so every turn word is an even number of swaps, and a single swap is odd. Extension: the 2-by-3 turning-squares board reaches only 120 of 720 arrangements; ask children to find two arrangements that cannot reach each other, and tell them the full reason is beyond a parity count.

## Checked facts

Computed in `check_symmetry_games.py`, `check_week_117()`, by listing every reachable arrangement:

- two rings of 3 sharing a spot: 60 arrangements (all even), at most 6 turns to restore; a two-token swap is unreachable;
- two rings of 5 sharing a spot: 181440 = 9!/2 arrangements, at most 17 turns; two rings of 4: all 5040, at most 14 turns, and a swap is reachable; rings of 3 and 4: all 720;
- with one shared spot, L R l r moves exactly 3 tokens on every board; with two shared spots it moves 4;
- the 20 three-cycles of 5 tokens generate exactly 60 arrangements;
- two rings of 4 sharing two spots and the 2-by-3 turning-squares board: 120 of 720 arrangements, at most 7 turns, and the commutator swaps two pairs.

Cited, not computed: the general theorems (Charlton; Montenegro, Huckaby and Harmon).
