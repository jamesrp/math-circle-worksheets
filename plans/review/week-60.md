## The mathematics

Offers are fair draws, with replacement, from a known bag; a player takes one and stops, or passes it for good, and must take the last. The best average obeys V₁ = E[X], V_m = E[max(X, V_{m−1})]. For 0, 4, 6 that is 10/3, 40/9 and 134/27, so a 4 is taken with two offers left and passed with three, and no plan, even one remembering passed offers, beats 134 on 27 cards (P6). An extra offer raises the optimum unless every ticket is equal (P9). A mathematician would enjoy the flipping 4, the 0, 3, 6 tie (P7) and the 5 that flips at four offers (P8).

## App fit

B, size M, confirmed, but change the idea: themes.md says "Set thresholds; the app scores all cases", and two offers of three tickets allow only eight first-offer rules, so live scores let a child try them all.

- **Take or pass.** Complete words as cards in rows by first offer, each row with a Take/Pass switch; the app lights the taken offer, fades the unseen tail and shows no totals. One Best claim per board; the certificate, shown after, is two towers per row. Pick bags where wrong rules fail: passing only the smallest (0, 2, 6), or comparing with the next draw's mean (a three-offer 4 from 0, 4, 6). The 0, 3, 6 tie is a find-every.
- **Where plans differ.** Keep every P4 card where A and B differ, then That's all (five), on the case engine's shelf.
- **Make a bag.** Pin two tickets: the b in 0, b, 6 taken with two offers and passed with three (only 4); the third ticket up to 20 that ties the middle of 1, 9 and it (5 or 17). Free design is guessable: 35 of the 120 bags of three distinct tickets from 0–9 have a middle that flips.

Pitfalls: cases.md's row says "Every order of the offers; `catalogHTML` marks the orders a stopping rule wins". Offers repeat, so the cases are `sequences(tickets, n)` with tails kept, each scored; `catalogHTML` only marks a class per case, so scores and towers are new. Keep random play and predicting an average out of the solves. Draw on P3–P5, P7, P8 and P9's equal case; leave P2 and the proofs on paper.
