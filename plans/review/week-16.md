## The mathematics

With corners R, B and Y, side dots limited to their side's two corner letters and inside dots free, the number of little triangles with all three letters is odd, on any edge-to-edge triangulation. Call an R–B edge a door: a cell has one door exactly when it is R-B-Y, two when it has R and B but no Y, never three. The R…B side has an odd number of doors, so pairing door incidences (T + 2U = b + 2I), or following routes, leaves an odd number of all-three cells. Relax one side rule and zero is possible. A mathematician would enjoy this. In 4–5 the order carries it: P1 (only 1, 3, 5 occur), P2 (routes, one wholly inside), P3 (local door cases), P4 (R…B rows odd, R…R even), P5 (proof), P6 (one relaxed dot gives zero). 2–3 follows the same line; K–1 meets impossibility (P2), maxima (P3, P4) and the broken hypothesis (P6).

## App fit

A, size M, confirmed, on the shared triangle grid (`docs/tri-grid.md` already sketches Week 16) rather than Neighbor Lanterns. The child taps a point to cycle its letter; side points cycle only through their two letters, so the app enforces what paper can't. All-three cells light up.

- Put the only rainbow cell on a marked cell (K–1 P1; all 16 places on side 4 for K–1 P5, the child declaring "done").
- Exactly k: 3 on side 4 (2–3 P5). For 2 or 4 the child answers "can't"; the app checks by search, and the door view, where the outside routes can never all pair up, is the reason.
- Most: 5 on side 3, 7 on the fan (K–1 P3, P4), 9 on side 4; the child claims best, the app checks.
- Relaxed rules as a separate group: one starred dot or a whole side allowing Y; make zero (K–1 P6, 4–5 P6).
- Rows: an R…B or R…R row with exactly k doors; the wrong parity is "can't" (4–5 P4).

Pitfalls: no "3 of 5" counter; no "how many rainbow cells?" predictions; routes are the child's taps, not an animation. The fan board is not a lattice board: give it its own points, or add a "split a cell" move (a new centre changes the count by an even number). Draw on K–1 P1 and P3–P6, 2–3 P2 and P5, and 4–5 P1, P2, P4 and P6.
