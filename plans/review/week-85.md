## The mathematics

Two midpoint-pierced long bars and two half-length bars make a rhombus. With O fixed, every open pose has P = O + 2(T − O): a dilation by 2. Lengths double, shape and orientation stay, area goes up four times. Swapping tips halves. Moving the anchor by d moves the copy by −d, so the input must move d/2 to compensate. Shared P1–P3 reach the invariant by experiment; upper P1 proves it with the bars (XT is parallel to QP and half as long); upper P2–P4 carry area, the inverse and the centre. A mathematician would enjoy it.

## Fix

Guide p. 2: "printed circle centers are not a complete manufacturing specification." The source README says "Rigid bar stock, pivot hardware, spacers, coaxial tip mounts, a board … must be supplied." Nothing is named, and the guide rules out the easy shortcut ("A pencil taped beside a bar invalidates the exact scale model"). A parent would have to engineer three pantographs. Week 52's unnamed stock was a should; here every task and the launch need both tips on their pivot axes. Fix: name one build on materials p. 2 and in the guide, for example 3 mm hardboard or stiff plastic strips cut from p. 1; bolts, washers and lock nuts at X, Q and Y; a pin at O through the mat into the board; a pencil pushed snugly through the T holes and the P hole as the axle, with a rubber band or weight for pressure. Give parts, cost and time. Name a fallback: a bought pantograph set to 2, pinned at O, runs every task but upper P1 if it reaches both zones (check).
