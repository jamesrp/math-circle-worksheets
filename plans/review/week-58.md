## The mathematics

Each person adds their own card values. A division is proportional when a person's tray is worth at least 1/n of their total, and envy-free when it is worth at least each other tray to them. Adding one person's comparisons proves envy-free implies proportional. For two people the two coincide. For three, the rows (4,8,0), (0,4,8), (8,0,4) give everyone a third while each envies someone. Cut-and-choose guarantees both on a divisible strip if the cut is equal in the cutter's eyes. A cut at the colour join fails. Three identical whole cards block both until one is halved. A mathematician would enjoy the summation proof, the envy cycle and P4's test of the hypothesis. P1–P2 carry the catalogs, P3–P4 cut-and-choose, P5 indivisibility, and P6–P7 three people.

## App fit

B, size M, confirmed, but change the idea. A live value table does the arithmetic, and four cards give 16 divisions to click through (themes.md, "Guessing"). Drag goods into trays beside each person's dot card. Solves:
- **Find every** division where nobody envies, with "That's all" and no visible count: 4 in P1, 5 in P2. The certificate is the catalog sorted by A's red count.
- **Can't, then split.** Identical tastes, odd count (P5). The pairing is the certificate; then a halving tool.
- **Favourites.** P6 with four or five people, one item each. Envy-free means everyone holds a favourite, and "can't" has a tappable certificate: k people whose favourites are fewer than k items.
- **Cut-and-choose duel.** The child cuts a two-colour strip, the app takes its better piece, and a star marks a guaranteed half. Then swap roles.

Pitfalls: ties count as no envy. Never compare one person's number with another's. No "will B envy?" screens. P4 and P7 stay on paper. Draw on P1–P3, P5 and P6.
