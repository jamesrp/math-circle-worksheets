## The mathematics

A ball leaves the corner of a w by h grid table at 45° and stops at the first corner. Unfolded, the path is a straight line through mirror copies that first meets a copy corner q tables across and p up, where p by q is the table in lowest terms: it stops right iff q is odd, top iff p is odd, after p + q − 2 bounces. Children reach scaling (K–1 P4, 2–3 P2–P3), parity bans (2–3 P4, 4–5 P6), the one-bounce tables (2–3 P6), never home (K–1 P6, 2–3 P8, 4–5 P7) and the fold (K–1 P7, 2–3 P7, 4–5 P3–P4). A mathematician would enjoy it.

## Fix

1. **must**, guide p. 2, "How a child walks the path": "After a side wall, keep going up the grid but slant the other way; after the far end, keep slanting the same way but come back down the grid." K–1 P1 hint (1) and P2 hint (2) on p. 4 repeat "keep going up". The left-dot walk bounces at (3,3), (1,5), (0,4), (3,1), (2,0), (0,2); at (0,4) the ball is coming down, and the rule sends it back to (1,5). It fails again at (3,1), and at 5 of K–1 P2's 9 bounces. Fix: "At a side wall, keep going the same way along the grid (up or down) and slant the other way; at either end, keep the slant and turn back along the grid", and the hints as in math check item 1.
2. **should**, guide p. 1, section 1: its three facts omit the corner-and-bounce rule the 4–5 pages build to and the parity fact behind 2–3 P4 and 4–5 P6; they appear only in section 4's answers and on p. 9. Add: "In lowest terms, p wide by q high, the ball stops on the right exactly when q is odd, at the top exactly when p is odd, after p + q − 2 bounces (45° paths from a corner, whole squares)."
3. **should**, guide p. 2, test 1: "Measure the squares: 3/4 inch on the K–1 pages, 1/2 inch on the others." The first grid on each p. 1 is the smaller rules picture, so an adult who measures it reprints a correct print (math check item 2). Write "Measure a square in a numbered problem."

## App fit

A, confirmed: shipped as Mirror Couriers; replace the row's note with the touch-up below, size M. My exact simulator (`app_check.py`) confirms all 12 instances. Four are predictions; in the other eight the child picks a width, or a rise and run, from menus and presses Launch. Nobody moves the ball or drags the room, and the unfolded rooms are only a help panel after a launch. No target is impossible, and none asks for every answer.

From this worksheet, toward clicking to solve:

- **Shape the room** (K–1 P5, 2–3 P4, 4–5 P6): drag the room's corner; Launch checks the target. One target per set is impossible, answered with Can't; the certificate is the unfolded corner map, each copy corner coloured by where it folds and labelled across + up − 2, all top-right labels even.
- **Every one-bounce room** up to 6 by 6 (2–3 P6), declared Done; certificate "one side twice the other". **Send it home**, with Can't; the reveal is the halfway crossing (4–5 P7).
- **Aim in the sheet** (2–3 P7, 4–5 P3–P4): tap the first crossing the diagonal reaches, or place the copy walls for a 4 by 3 room; the app folds the staircase of rooms into the path. This is the 45° start of Week 71's aiming group, and tapping a copy corner can replace the rise and run menus of billiard-05, -06, -09, -11 and -12.
- **Walk the ball** as the demo or a K–1 playground, wrong-way taps refused; the path is forced, so it is not a puzzle.

Pitfalls: a live path while dragging turns design into a scan, so draw it on Launch and keep the road's launch count; no "3 of 6" counter; a crossing is not a wall (billiard-02); K–1 taps counts rather than typing them; prediction stays a one-launch star.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** In the app as Mirror Couriers. Wave 9's touch-ups of in-app families rework it in place toward clicking to solve, as this card proposes: shape the room by dragging its corner to hit a target, with one impossible target per set answered with Can't and the unfolded corner map as the certificate; find every one-bounce room and claim done with no counter; send it home, answered with Can't; and aim in the sheet of unfolded rooms by tapping the first crossing, which then folds into the path. The path draws on Launch, not while dragging. Weeks 71 and 21 join the family as groups in Wave 8, and the 45° aiming group is their starting point.
