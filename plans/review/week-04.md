## The mathematics

Hop k around n evenly spaced dots, restarting at a bare dot until every dot has a line. The drawing needs gcd(n, k) starts, each piece the first one turned; hops k and n − k draw the same lines, and every hop reaches every dot exactly when n is prime. The wheel is the same ring with 26 places: settings add, 26 − s undoes s, and the twelve odd settings other than N reach all 26 letters. A mathematician would enjoy it: one theorem, reached by drawing, then used to predict (2–3 P3, P6; 4–5 P3), prove an impossibility (2–3 P7), invert (4–5 P9) and classify (4–5 P8, P11). 4–5 P12 (times tables) is a coda.

At those dots the curve touches the circle; the cusps lie R/3 from the centre toward dot 12 (×2) and R/2 toward dots 6 and 18 (×3). ## App fit

A, size S, confirmed: a star-drawing group in Clockwork Gates, whose gear mode already fixes one jump x ↦ x + k. The child picks a hop and taps starts; the app draws each piece in its own colour. Solves: exactly g pieces on n dots (2–3 P7); n and k for an erased-dot star (4–5 P9); every hop for a picture (K–1 P5); every ring from 4 to 20 where all hops give one piece, declared done without a "3 of 6" count (4–5 P8). Certificate for "can't": the pieces are equal, so 3 pieces on 16 dots need 3 equal piles. Pitfalls: guessing starts first (2–3 P3, P6) is predicting, which children enjoyed less; a child can try all n − 1 hops, so targets should need both n and k; accept k and n − k. The cipher is thin, as the row says: one short cracking set (2–3 P8, 4–5 P4) at most.
