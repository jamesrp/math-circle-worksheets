## The mathematics

A key gives each picture a row of filled and empty counters. A changer flips at most one counter, unseen. Recovery is guaranteed exactly when every two rows differ in at least three places (2t+1 for t flips), because then their sets of possible received rows are disjoint. Each row has n+1 received rows, so M(n+1) ≤ 2^n. Four pictures need five counters, and three pictures fail at length four although 15 ≤ 16. A mathematician would enjoy this. K–1 P3–P6 reach "opposites only" and two impossibilities. 2–3 P2, P4 and P6 carry the criterion, a parity argument and two flips. 4–5 P3, P5 and P6 carry the theorem, the bound and its limit.

## Fix

1. **should**, guide p. 2 launch: "Never reveal whether a change occurred." P. 3 adds that "revealing the original" alters the task. The guide never says how a round ends or how a pair covers three roles, and tables work in pairs (context.md). Without a reveal the receiver never learns whether they were fooled, which is K–1 P1's question; in a pair only the changer sees how many counters turned. Fix: "After the receiver answers, show the picture; everyone checks the answer and counts the changed counters. In a pair, one child sends and changes, the other receives." Begin the quoted sentence "Before the receiver answers,".
2. **should**, guide p. 2 supplies: "30 two-sided counters (filled circle on one face, empty on the other)", "cells at least 2 cm wide". No counter size is given. The Week 6 kit is red/yellow counters in 1-inch slots (Week 6 guide p. 1); the printed K–1 and 2–3 cells are 2.0–2.05 cm. Fit untested. Fix: "Red/yellow counters work; say at the launch which colour is filled. Make strip cells at least 1 inch (2.54 cm), Week 6's slot for this kit; the printed strips are then records."
3. **should**, K–1 p. 6, P5: "Use both kinds of counter in each row". Its only effect is to drop 1111 from 0100's list (my enumeration). It is one more rule for a K–1 child, and it hides the pattern guide p. 5 offers as an extension: every row has five partners, its opposite and the opposite's four one-change neighbours. One square strip under "find every" suggests one answer. Fix: "For each triangle row, find every square row that always works. Use more paper if needed." Add two blank square strips per key as extra workspace. On guide p. 5, add 1111 to 0100's list.
4. **should**, all bands, p. 1: "Example: a practice row" is a heading neg.md removes; "The receiver sees only this row." narrates the diagram; "The changer may also leave the row as it is." repeats "zero or one" from the rules. Cut all three. Keep the visual and "In the game, the start row and the changed position stay hidden behind the folder." Add a no-change send to the launch.
5. **could**, K–1 p. 1 never defines "key"; add the 2–3 sentence "A key gives each picture a different row, all the same length."
6. **could**, guide p. 11 route note. I agree with math check item 1 and its wording, but rate it could: guide p. 4 says plainly that no two-counter key works.
7. **could**, guide p. 2 hour menu fills 0–60; context.md allows a five-minute run and 35–40 minutes of work.
8. **could**, 2–3 P3 is short after P1 and P2; it could close P2.
9. **could**, 4–5 P4 relists neighbourhoods as P1 did; its new content is completeness and n+1.
10. **could**, 2–3 P5 and 4–5 P2 restate the one-change rule.
11. **could**, Problem 1 starts 1.2–1.4 in lower than Problems 2–6 in every band.

## App fit

A, confirmed: a codebook-design group in Signal Lanterns, size S (M with a catalogue view). The child taps lanterns to set each picture's row within a length budget and presses check. The app plays the changer: it shows one received row two pictures both produce, with both flip paths (guide p. 3), or accepts. In a second mode the child is the changer: build a row two pictures share, or declare there is none (2–3 P2, 4–5 P1 and P3). "Can't" instances: two pictures in two lanterns, three in three or four, four in four; the child declares and the app checks. For four in four, a 16-cell catalogue shading each row's five received rows shows 20 > 16. A two-flip mode follows 2–3 P6.

Pitfalls:

- Leave out decoding a received row: it is a predict task.
- Live overlap shading invites wiggling; show it on check.
- For "find every key", the child declares done, with no counter.
- Keep the unchanged row in every received set.

Draw on K–1 P3, P4 and P6, 2–3 P2 and P4–P6, and 4–5 P1, P5 and P6.
