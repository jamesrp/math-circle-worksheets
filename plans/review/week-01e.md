## The mathematics

In the placement game a half-turn copy wins: with the centre at a grid point the second player answers each move with its image; at an edge midpoint the first player takes the one self-symmetric blue, then copies. Triangles have no half turn; triangle 4 is a second-player win by search only. A red covers two ups or two downs, so x − y = U − D and x + y = N/3 fix both kinds: all 220 fillings of triangle 6 have 3 two-down. A blue filling of a hexagon is a cube pile and a flip moves one cube, so no flip route returns in an odd number; red moves have a 3-cycle. Fewest-piece fills defeat greed: 7 yellows fit the 3× hexagon, but the best uses 6. A mathematician would enjoy it. The game runs through every band; 2–3 P4 and P7 carry the count, 4–5 P4–P8 the piles.

## App fit

Fit B, size M, as in themes.md. Rhombus gardens already covers 4–5 P4–P5: Twenty piles, flip budgets, the playground's 8 flips to fill the 2,2,2 box, and the odd return in Proofs 8–11. Its pack puzzles cover the imbalance behind K–1 P5's blue X. It lacks the game, red trapezoids, fewest-piece fills and P6's 1,3,3 shade count; `tri-grid.js` already places trapezoids and hexagons.

- **The duel.** The child lays a blue; the app plays the other side. The child first picks "Me first" or "You first", as in the app's duel proofs. Solve: a win against perfect play on fresh boards mixing grid-point centres, edge midpoints and triangles. Search on hexagon 3,3,3 did not finish in 600 s, so there the app's winning side copies; asymmetric boards stay small. Never ask only "who wins?".
- **Fewest pieces.** A four-piece palette and a budget ("fill with 12") on boards with a greedy trap; without a budget greens fill anything. No complete certificate exists for "fewer is impossible", so author boards whose bound shows in which yellows fit.
- **Red trapezoids.** List the 9 fillings of hexagon 2,2,2 and press That's all, with no slot count. A re-cut move allows a return in 3 moves, impossible on a blue board. Pose the kind count as a decision: "fill triangle 6 with 4 two-down" is impossible, refuted by counting ups and downs.
