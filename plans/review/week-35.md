## The mathematics

A border is an endless strip of one asymmetric notched F in four forms (A, two mirrors, a half-turn). A glide done twice is a slide, so when the flip alone fails the shortest glide is half the shortest slide. Two perpendicular flips force a half-turn at their crossing. Because the motif has no symmetry, where one footprint lands decides a move, so a full symmetry list can be proved. A mathematician would enjoy it. K–1 P1–P6 and 2–3 P1–P5 reach the moves and their contrasts; 2–3 P3 finds the half-length glide; 4–5 P3 the squared glide, P4 the forced half-turn, P6 completeness.

## Fix

1. **must**, all bands, guide p. 2: "Per pair supply 20-24 reusable footprint pieces" in four forms, and "The packet is not a complete manipulative kit." The only printed outline is the launch figure's 17.7 × 15.0 mm F, under the guide's 25 mm minimum, and over 100 notched pieces will not fit in the stated 30–45 minutes. A parent volunteer would design the kit (compare Week 23). Fix: a print-and-cut materials PDF of cards about 26 mm wide, each with the F and its arrow, 12 A and 12 mirror-A per pair. Turning A gives its half-turn; turning the mirror gives the other mirror. Keep cards at most 30 mm wide (Border E puts centres 3 cm apart). Restate preparation time to include cutting.
2. **should**, all bands, p. 1: "Use the loose footprints and long strips", while guide p. 2 asks for "strips at least 70 by 280 mm" and most problems print a 17.65 × 6.00 cm board that nothing assigns a use. With 26 mm pieces a board holds about three repeats of a 6 cm border. Fix (guide): build on the printed boards; use a long strip only for repeats over 6 cm.
3. **should**, guide pp. 2–4: no line says who traces, what, or that the copy needs the middle line to flip on, and nothing holds loose pieces still, so a child can nudge one into a match (prediction; the lamp failure in context.md). K–1 tracing up to nine notched outlines per border may also eat the hour. Fix: tack finished borders down with removable tape; at K–1 the adult traces; a rough outline with its arrow suffices, since the arrow's place and direction tell the four forms apart; always trace the middle line.
4. **should**, K–1, every footer: "W35-k-1-v2" also labels the archived six-problem packet, while guide p. 6 calls this one "K-1 v3"; on the old printout every key is one problem off (math check, item 4). Fix: W35-k-1-v3.
5. **should**, guide p. 3: recipes give undefined "motif anchors"; with corner anchors H, R and E overlap (math check, item 2), and "at least 25 mm" ignores E's 30 mm cap (item 3). Fix: anchors are footprint centres; size 25–30 mm; draw three repeats of each witness in the page's empty lower half.
6. **should**, 4–5 p. 2, P3: "matches after a flip and a 3 cm slide along the middle line" leaves the flip's line open; with a perpendicular flip, "Must a 6 cm slide also match?" becomes no (math check, item 1). Fix: "A border matches after a 3 cm glide."
7. **should**, 4–5 p. 4, P6: "Which claims can you justify for its endless repetition?" hides the completeness task; a child may restate the list. Fix: "Explain why no other move matches."
8. **could**, guide p. 2: the 0–60 minute plan ignores context.md's running time and 35–40 working minutes.
9. **could**, K–1 p. 1: trim the six rule sentences; the launch can carry "repeats forever" and "strip ends".
10. **could**, label the second board under one-border problems (2–3 P2, P4, P5; 4–5 P2, P4, P5) as extra workspace.
11. **could**, K–1 P4 repeats P3; ask for a longer glide.
12. **could**, choose "smallest" (2–3 P1, 4–5 P1) or "shortest" throughout.

I agree with every math-check item; its items 5 and 6 are in the bonus, which I did not review. It did not cover materials, boards or tracing.

## App fit

B, size M, confirmed. Tiling one cell endlessly removes the kit, the tracing and the imagined ends. The child places the F's four forms in a cell, then drags, flips or turns a translucent copy, which shows each footprint's match or miss. Solves: build with one move required and another forbidden (K–1 P3, P5, P6; 2–3 P2, P4, P5; 4–5 P5); find the shortest slide and glide (4–5 P2); edit a cell to break a move while it still repeats (2–3 P6); match two crossing flips, then tap the forced centre (4–5 P4). Certificates: the overlay for a match; for "no half-turn" or "no flip", one footprint whose image lands on a gap or needs a form the border lacks; for "shortest", the nearest footprint of the form the move needs. Pitfalls: keep the F fixed, not themes.md's "Edit a motif", since an edited motif can gain symmetry; no snapping or "which move works?" quizzes; the child declares "no half-turn anywhere" and the app checks. Draw on K–1 P1–P3, P5–P6; 2–3 P2–P6; 4–5 P2, P4–P5, with P3 and P6 in grown-up notes.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** Port, in Wave 8 (symmetry, words and reflections): one cell tiled endlessly, so the kit, the tracing and the imagined ends drop away. The F stays fixed rather than editable, since an edited motif can gain symmetry. The revise is for the worksheet's missing kit and doesn't hold up the port.
