  - Every printed key and row matches the problem text.
  - The practice page in all three bands shows start row 0101, with only the third cell marked, and final row 0111.
  - The counter cells are square: 2.05 cm in K–1 and 2.0 cm in grades 2–3, consistent with the guide's "at least 2 cm".

  - The number of answer slots is as the guide states: K–1 P4 has 6 key slots, middle P4 has 4.
- **Exhaustive mathematics** (`check_math.py` → `out_check_math.txt`, 0 failures):
  - Outcomes are generated by actually flipping positions. A key "works" when no received row can come from two of its rows.
  - The general criterion was checked over every pair of rows for n ≤ 6 and t ≤ 3: the sets of rows reachable with at most t flips are disjoint exactly when the distance is at least 2t+1.
  - Every finite answer in the guide is compared with my enumeration. Before each comparison, the quoted guide text is confirmed to appear verbatim in the guide PDF.

## K–1: checks out completely

- **P1:** with key 000/111, can the changer fool the receiver? No. The triangle's received rows {000,001,010,100} and the square's {011,101,110,111} are disjoint.
- **P2:** decode the printed final rows. With the three-counter key, 000 → triangle, 101 → square, 010 → triangle, 111 → square. With the two-counter key, 00 → triangle, 01 → either, 10 → either, 11 → square. The two-counter key is the one that fails.
- **P3:** none of the 16 ordered choices of two-counter rows works, so the answer is no.
- **P4:** exactly 8 ordered keys work, each a row with its opposite. The page has 6 slots and says "use more paper if needed".
- **P5:** for 0011, the square row may be 0100, 1000, 1100, 1101 or 1110 (5 rows, all mixed). For 0100, it may be 0011, 1001, 1010 or 1011 (4 rows). The fifth partner, 1111, is excluded by "use both kinds of counter".
- **P6:** impossible. All 56 triples of three-counter rows fail.

## Grades 2–3: checks out completely

- **P1:** 000/111 works. 001/101 fails, because the receiver can get 001 or 101 from either picture.
- **P2:** the pairs differ in 1, 2, 3 and 4 positions. Rows either picture can produce: 0000/0001 → {0000, 0001}; 0101/0110 → {0100, 0111}; 0001/1111 → none; 0011/1100 → none.
- **P3:** the shortest working length is 3, for example 000/111.
- **P4:** there are exactly 6 ordered balanced keys, each a pair of opposites. Two rows with two filled circles each always differ in an even number of positions.
- **P5:** a length-5 key for four pictures exists. There are 120 working unordered sets and 2,880 ordered keys, so "many other keys are valid" is true.
- **P6:** with up to two changes, the shortest length is 5, for example 00000/11111. Every pair of rows of length ≤ 4 has a common received row. The minimum equals the page's "at most five" limit, so the task can be done.

## Grades 4–5: checks out completely

- **P1:** 00/11 fails (shared rows 01, 10). 000/111 works. 0010/0100 fails (shared rows 0000, 0110).
- **P2:** same as 2–3 P5.
- **P3:** 000000/110000 differ in 2 positions and share 100000 and 010000. 010010/101010 differ in exactly positions 1–3 and share nothing. The threshold is 3 differing positions for every row length (checked for n ≤ 7).
- **P4:** the received rows are {000,010,011,110}, {0010,0100,0110,0111,1110} and {00011,00101,00110,00111,01111,10111}. A row of length 10 has 11 received rows; in general n+1 for n = 1…10.
- **P5:** the minimum length is 5. The count 4(n+1) > 2^n holds for every n ≤ 4.
- **P6:** impossible. All 560 triples of length-4 rows fail, even though 3·5 ≤ 16.
