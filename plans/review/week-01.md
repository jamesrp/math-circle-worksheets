## The mathematics

A grid-aligned blue covers one up and one down triangle, so imbalance forces gaps: the side-n triangle leaves exactly n, and equal counts are necessary, not sufficient. A purple is two blues, so blues never do worse; P3 boards B and D show they can do better. At 4–5 the six tilings of the 1-2-2 hexagon match the six words with two L's and two R's, a flip swaps one adjacent LR, and the flip map is connected with no odd cycles. A mathematician would enjoy it. 2–3 P1–P3 and 4–5 P1, P2 and P4–P10 carry it; K–1 only fills.

## App fit

Fit B, size M, as in themes.md. The child taps two neighbouring triangles to lay a rhombus, or a hexagon to flip it, with rhombi shaded as cubes. Solves: fill or pack a board with a stated piece set; find every tiling of a small hexagon and declare done, with no slot per answer; reach one tiling from another in the fewest flips. Certificates: for fewest gaps, a starred set of triangles with fewer neighbours than members. Blue packings are matchings of up and down triangles, so by Hall this is complete; it covers the imbalance and the bottleneck reserve. For fewest flips, the cubes in one pile and not the other; for no odd return, the two-coloured map (the app repository's docs/insight-without-ai.md, §3). Pitfalls: free piece choice makes fills trivial, as domino-only gardens were. Purples lack a complete certificate; author only boards refuted by area or an uncoverable corner (2–3 P3 Board B). Keep ribbon codes a tool, not a prediction task. Draw on 2–3 P1–P3, the bottleneck reserve, 4–5 P1–P4 and K–1 P3.
