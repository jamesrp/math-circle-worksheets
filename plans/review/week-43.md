## The mathematics

A direct chooser and left-to-right Fisher–Yates (slot 1 swaps with a ticket from {1, 2, 3}, then slot 2 with one from {2, 3}) each give six equally likely stories, one per order, from any start; n cards give n!. The tempting rule that draws from all slots at every step has 27 stories, which cannot split evenly over 6 orders (4, 5, 5, 5, 4, 4); forbidding self-swaps reaches only BCA and CAB. One fixed story never merges two starts. A mathematician would enjoy the bijection and a divisibility proof a third grader can do. K–1 P3–P5, 2–3 P2–P6 and 4–5 P2–P6 carry it.

## App fit

B, size M, as in themes.md. The committed cases.md (ca9d97e) asks for "A way to play a story on the cups step by step"; the family now being built is that story player. Lettered cups stand in numbered slots, and each step's ticket cup offers only its tickets, so the rule enforces itself. Solves:
- **Make a target** from a given start (K–1 P3, 2–3 P3, 4–5 P3).
- **Find every** row or story, then That's all: a ticket removed (K–1 P4), no self-swaps (4–5 P5: BCA, CAB), all-slots stories for ABC (four) or ACB (five) (2–3 P5).
- **Design** (4–5 P6): choose each step's tickets for four cups; Try it checks every story. Exactly six designs for slots 1, 2, 3 pass, Fisher–Yates among them, one for each order of the set sizes 4, 3, 2 (checked).

Certificate: the stories binned by row, one per bin for a fair rule; the 27 give 4, 5, 5, 5, 4, 4; a failed design shows a doubled or empty bin.

Pitfalls: no "which row will this story make?" quizzes; no visible counts; never tap through all 27 stories; a thousand-shuffle chart is not a proof; stay distinct from Cup swaps and Mixed-up cups, which have no tickets. Draw on K–1 P1, P3–P4; 2–3 P2–P3, P5–P6; 4–5 P3, P5–P6.
