## The mathematics

A strip joined matching (A) is an annulus with two boundary circles; joined reversed (B), a Möbius band with one. A middle cut makes halves U and L, and the seam's action on them decides the pieces: U→U, L→L gives two annuli, U↔L one annulus with two edges. An arrow carried once around B returns reversed. With b circles, four dots group exactly as the partitions of 4 into at most b parts. A mathematician would enjoy the seam-cycle reason for the famous cut, and the arrow; the partitions are pigeonhole bookkeeping. P1–P2 and P4–P5 carry it, 4–5 P6 the orientation.

## App fit

Change C to B, size M. The flat recipe is the mathematics; Week 41's portal board with a reversing seam could carry it. Tap an edge to light its whole circle through the seams, a seam to toggle M and R, a lane line to cut it. Solves:
- place four dots to match a ring picture or declare "can't" (K–1 P3, P6), certified by fewer lit circles than rings;
- choose seams in a three- or four-strip circle for one edge or two (bonus P2), certified by a carried arrow that returns flipped exactly when the R count is odd;
- choose seams and lane cuts for a target, such as a one-edge piece beside a two-edge piece (bonus P1); punch at most three holes for four edges (bonus P3).

Pitfalls: no "how many pieces?" quizzes; leave the cut's surprise to paper. Seam parity is one bit, so random tapping wins half the time: fix some seams or ask for fewest changes. No edge counter. Draw on K–1 P2, P3, P6, 2–3 P5, 4–5 P5–P6 and bonus P1–P3.
