## The mathematics

Two finite sets of whole numbers have at least m + n − 1 and at most mn different totals; with two or more cards in each, the minimum happens exactly when both are equally spaced with the same gap, and a one-card input, which only shifts the other, is the exception. Children meet collisions (P1–P2), design extremes and conjecture the count (P3–P4), find the common gap (P5), see that equal spacing alone fails (P6) and the exception (P7), then use right/up routes in the addition grid (P8) to prove the bounds (P9) and the inverse theorem by swapping a route around one square (P10). A mathematician would enjoy this simplest inverse theorem, with a proof a child can point at.

## App fit

A, size S, confirmed. The child taps cards into A and B trays; each pair drops a token on a 0–18 strip, and repeats merge.

- **Fewest and most** for given sizes. The certificate for fewest: trace a right/up staircase in the addition grid; the app checks it climbs, so m + n − 1 totals are forced (P8–P9).
- **Find every B** for a fixed A (P5), ending in a "That's all" claim with no answer slots.
- **Contrasts:** both inputs equally spaced but 9 totals ({0, 2, 4} + {0, 3, 6}); fewest with an uneven B, which needs one A card (P7). Hard tier: 4 + 4 with 16 totals (8 of 44,100 designs).
