## prompt @27
Make a four-counter picture on each left grid and write its counts. Can you make one so that your partner cannot make a different picture with those counts?

## key
\emph{Print guide, p. 7.} \textbf{P6 (revised November 2026 as a partner game):} the maker tries for counts that fit only one picture; the partner tries to make a different picture with the same counts on the right grid. The guide's printed P6 asked for one such picture on the left grids and one ambiguous picture on the right; its two answers still apply.

\textbf{Pictures the partner cannot match:} A full, plus B1. The 2-by-3 counts are 3, 1 and 2, 1, 1; the 3-by-3 counts are 3, 1, 0 and 2, 1, 1. Column 1 then forces B1, and B has no other counter; on the 3-by-3 board, C is empty. Of the 15 four-counter pictures on the 2-by-3 grid, 9 fit only their own counts; on the 3-by-3 grid, 45 of 126 do. A partner's success proves "more than one"; a partner's failure alone proves nothing, so check a claimed win with this forcing argument.
