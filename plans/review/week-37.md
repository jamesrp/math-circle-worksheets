## The mathematics

Four different letters on a rigid regular tetrahedron make a chiral object: seen from A, the other three run one way round on a model and the other way on its mirror twin, and no turn reverses that. Make any two letters equal and the twins match, because the model gains a mirror symmetry. The tetrahedron has 12 rotations (4 places for A, then 3 turns), so the 24 labellings with A–D form two classes. A mathematician would enjoy it. K–1 P2–P4, 2–3 P2 and P4–P6, and 4–5 P2–P6 carry it.

## Fix

1. **must**, guide p. 1 ("A finite list of unsuccessful turns alone is not an impossibility proof"), p. 3 ("A failed search is not proof of impossibility") and the p. 4 key for 2–3 P2. "A has to go on A; then there are only three turns, and we tried all three" is a complete proof, which the guide tells the adult it is not (math check item 1). On pp. 1 and 3 write "A failed search proves impossibility only when it covers every turn"; add to the K–1 and 2–3 P2 keys: "Accept a complete check: A must go on A, and all three turns about A fail."
2. **must**, guide p. 2: "ten congruent rigid regular tetrahedral frames … markers A, B, C, D with duplicate As and Cs". Nothing names a product, net or build, so a parent volunteer must design the kit; spare As and Cs cannot make the B–D merge in 2–3 P4 and 4–5 P4 (math check item 2). Fix: a materials page with a cardstock tetrahedron net of about 90 mm edges (Week 56 draws one at 30 mm), removable letter dots A, A, B, B, C, C, D per model, counts for eleven children, and folding time. Say a taped cardstock tetrahedron is a rigid model; write "model" where pages say "frame".
3. **should**, 2–3 P3: "Make another pair with A, B, C and D. Can you match the two models by turning?" The goal is unstated and one try ends it. Write: "Keep the pair from Problem 2. Put A, B, C and D on another model in any way you like. Which of the two does it match? Can you make one that matches neither?"
4. **should**, 4–5 P1: "What has to match besides the letter at the top?" The rules already require every corner, a hidden turn leaves no fixed top, and the keyed answer hints at P3. Cut it.
5. **should**, all bands, launch and materials (prediction): nothing shows how to hold two solids "in the same pose". A mat with two triangle footprints, corners marked front-left, front-right and back, makes matching a corner-by-corner check, shows that A on top leaves three ways to sit, and gives 4–5 P5 its "same four positions".
6. **should**, guide p. 4, 2–3 P4 key: six merges, each reset on both models, is twelve relabellings per pair. Add: split the merges between the pairs and pool them on the board.
7. **could**, guide p. 3: the "Flexible hour" runs 0–60 minutes; context.md gives 35–40 at the tables.
8. **could**, 4–5 P6: "four different letters on the frame" → "A, B, C and D on the model"; drop "genuinely".
9. **could**, 4–5 P2: "Seek a reason that settles this even if no more turns are tried" → "If not, explain why no turn can."
10. **could**, 2–3 P4: end with a question, as 4–5 does ("Which choices allow a match by turning?").
11. **could**, guide 4–5 P4 key: add that a mirror through A, B and the middle of the C–C edge swaps the two Cs, so the model is its own mirror image.
12. **could**, guide p. 1: cut "Status. Final v2 has 5 / 6 / 6 problems. Algebraic keys are verified."

## App fit

B, confirmed; size M on a flat board. Show the view from A, a triangle with a centre corner; tapping a corner turns the model a third around it. Taps reach all 12 rotations and never a reflection. Solves: turn a model to match a target (K–1 P1); merge two letters so the twins match (K–1 P3, 2–3 P4); place letters so a pair matches or cannot (2–3 P5–P6); sort labelled models into turn-to-match groups and declare done (4–5 P6). "Can't match" is a claim the app checks, certified by the two views from A with arrows running opposite ways (4–5 P3). Pitfalls: no "will these match?" quizzes, free 3D dragging, visible class count, or swap moves (a swap is what turning cannot do). Draw on K–1 P1–P4, 2–3 P4–P6, 4–5 P3–P6.
