## The mathematics

A board is three slots of R or B; a setting instruction puts one colour in one slot of both boards. Two boards need as many instructions as the slots where they differ, and a fixed story makes all eight starts agree exactly when it sets every slot (2^u finishes remain with u slots unset). Shared toggles permute the boards, so they never merge two; a mixed story forgets exactly when every slot is set at least once. With random cards, once every slot is set the final board is uniform whatever the start, yet no number of draws guarantees coverage. A mathematician would enjoy the toggle invariant and the uniform law (a strong uniform time), carried by 4–5 P2–P7 and 2–3 P6–P7; K–1 and 2–3 P1–P5 rest on counting differences and coverage.

## App fit

A, confirmed; size S, or M with the bonus menus. Show all eight boards; tapping a card applies it to every board, and identical boards stack. Solve: one board in at most k taps, checked by search. With all six setting cards the answer is "set each slot once", so puzzles need restricted menus: copy plus one reset (bonus P1, shortest 3); set-slot-1 plus rotate (bonus P3: STSTS, unique; 2n − 1 with n slots, for Hard); menus where some slot can only be toggled. Certificates: toggles or rotations alone keep eight boards (a bijection), and a slot no card sets leaves two boards differing there; for "best", bonus P3 needs three S and two T. Pitfalls: two-board repairs (domino-garden easy); counting finishes (4–5 P2) and random draws (K–1 P6, 4–5 P6–P7), which are prediction. Draw on 2–3 P2, P6–P7, 4–5 P4–P5, bonus P1 and P3.
