## The mathematics

Two sides of letters rank each other on strips. A pairing is stable when no two letters in different pairs both prefer each other to their partners. Deferred acceptance ends within n² requests in a stable pairing, and the asking side can change it (4–5 P2–P5). Stable pairings can be several (K–1 P2, 2–3 P3), need not give everyone a first choice (2–3 P4), and leave at most three last choices among three pairs (4–5 P6). K–1 P3–P6 settle two-pair markets: both pairings are stable exactly when the sides' first choices are opposite. This is Gale–Shapley with honest hypotheses; a mathematician would enjoy it.

## Fix

1. **must**, all bands, guide p. 2: "Separate full-size table materials are required", "Copy the exact profile for the selected problem", and the pages "are not cut-out cards, work mats, or substitutes for the separate materials." Nothing supplies the tokens, 120 mm strips, links or sixteen markers per table, and copying each case onto strips is unrealistic for a parent volunteer. The pages already hold every strip and board at a workable size. Fix: make them the working surface. Children draw pairings in pencil and join a blocker with a dashed line; in 4–5 P2 an asker ticks each asked choice on its printed strip, and holds are drawn and erased. Delete the copying, dry-erase strips and the 20 mm rule for older pages. Add a print-and-cut sheet: launch tokens, and 18 mm A, B, X, Y tiles that sit in the 20 mm K–1 strip boxes, so P3's swap and P4–P6's fills are physical. Restate preparation time.
2. **should**, guide p. 2, launch: "Use A: X > Y; B: X > Y; X: A > B; Y: A > B" is K–1 P1's bottom case and 2–3 P1's top case, and the launch works through both of its pairings, naming 2–3's A–X answer. Use A: Y > X; B: X > Y; X: A > B; Y: A > B, printed nowhere: with AY and BX, X wants A but A prefers Y; with AX and BY, A and Y block (math check, item 3).
3. **should**, guide p. 6: "Here is a full audit… the checker records all blockers", though rows list one; BZ, CX and CY also block AX / BY / CZ, and BZ and CY block AZ / BY / CX. Write "Each row shows one blocker; a child may find another." Like the math check, I rate it minor: "One witness is enough" frames the table.
4. **should**, guide p. 13: "Exact expected answer: 16". Any bound from 13 up with a reason is right, and 13 is reached. Retitle it "A sufficient answer" and add the math check's 4 × 3 + 1 argument (91 for ten).
5. **should**, 2–3 p. 5, P4: four boards for exactly four stable pairings print the count; P2 and P3 print six. Print six. K–1 P6 has two cases for two answers; the guide should ask "Is there another way?" before confirming.
6. **should**, guide p. 2: only the adult enforces the two-sided rule, that a blocking pair must "both prefer each other to their current partner", Week 2's failure in another form (prediction). Give each child of a pair one side to speak for; a pair blocks only when both say yes.
7. **should**, overview and guide p. 1: "only after the child can check that both sides prefer a switch"; "Participation requires understanding why only the first is a blocking pair." P1 is where that check develops, and the route note starts there. Write "Start in P1; ask each piece in turn, as in the P1 held hint."
8. **could**, K–1 and 2–3 P1: page 1 gives each panel its own strips, but P1 splits one case's strips across columns; say at the launch that all four serve both pairings.
9. **could**, 4–5 P2: label the two record boxes by asking side.
10. **could**, guide p. 14: before P5, run the rules on P1's bottom lists; the result must be AY / BZ / CX.
11. **could**, guide pp. 1, 11, 17: cut residue ("a library label, not a booked meeting", the "instance-specific count" hedge, "Fidelity", "Checks packaged").

## App fit

B, size M, confirmed. Two rows of creatures with strips; tap a circle and a square to pair them. Solves:
- **Find the blocker:** tap two letters that both want to switch; on a wrong claim the app shows who says no (K–1 P1, 2–3 P1).
- **Find every stable pairing:** each rejection needs a tapped blocker; the child declares "That's all", with no slot count (2–3 P2–P4).
- **Repair or fill one strip**, or claim impossible; the certificate is a blocker the change cannot touch (K–1 P3–P4).
- **Design lists:** exactly two stable (2–3 P5), sides that disagree (4–5 P2), a keep-first run ending unstable (P3), three last choices (P6), verified by enumeration.

Pitfalls: live blocker flags turn the search into wiggling; three pairs have six pairings, so use four-pair block markets for find-all; watching deferred acceptance run is prediction unless the child plays the receivers; no people or romance.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** Port, in Wave 5 (roads and graphs): two rows of letters with their strips, tap to pair. Solves are finding the blocker, finding every stable pairing with a tapped blocker for each rejection and a declared "That's all", repairing one strip or claiming impossible, and designing lists checked by enumeration. Blocking pairs are flagged only when the child submits, never live, and watching deferred acceptance run is not a solve. The revise is for the worksheet's missing kit and doesn't hold up the port.
