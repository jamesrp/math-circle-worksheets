## The mathematics

Children block finitely many dots on an endless map and count the pieces that go on forever. The most they can ever make is the number of ends. The line and the ladder have two, the square grid has one, and the 3-regular tree has infinitely many: blocking every dot within r roads leaves 3·2^r. A mathematician would enjoy it. The ladder matching the line is the quasi-isometry invariance of ends in small, and there are two real impossibility proofs: the two tails (P4) and the box round the blockers (P6). P1, P4, P6 and P7–P8 carry it.

## Fix

1. **should**, guide p. 2, Problem 3. The key gives only "cover both dots of one rung", and its hint reads "make an actual detour before deciding that a gap in one rail is a separation". 16 of the 25 separating pairs are diagonal: one rail at column k, the other at k+1 (check_math.out). Add the math check's diagonal sentence. The key is incomplete, not false, and the hint's detour test confirms a diagonal, so this is a should. The claim that children will find the diagonal first is a prediction.
2. **should**, p. 2, Problem 3: "Swap jobs, then allow two blocked dots. Keep one attempt that separates forever pieces and one that leaves them together." It is a script. Its recorded goal (split the ladder) is not the game's goal (join two dots), and "them" can mean the dots or the pieces. Keep the game and ask: "How few blocked dots can split the ladder into two forever pieces? Show a split on one ladder and a two-dot placement that does not split it on the other."
3. **should**, p. 1: "Before lifting counters, mark their dots with X." This covers every attempt, so P3's and P4's many attempts will leave old X's that read as closed dots (prediction). Change it to "When you have a placement to keep, mark its dots with X before lifting the counters."
4. **should**, p. 3, Problem 5, right grid. The wall covers rows −2 to 2 of a −3 to 3 window, so the route runs along the printed top row. "Routes may leave the printed window" is never needed, and the guide's page-edge hint has nothing to act on. Extend the X column to all seven window dots, so the route must leave the page before P6. The shortest route becomes 12 steps (4 up, 4 right, 4 down); update the guide.
5. **should**, guide p. 1, "First route": "10 minutes on the line, then 15–20 on the ladder… The grid and branching tree can be separate return visits." That visit meets the answer 2 twice and never the contrast in the title. Route third graders from P3 to P7 (ten counters, three counts). Leave P4–P6 to the older table, and close by asking which map gave the most forever pieces.
6. **should**, guide p. 1 launch. It shows blocking only. Add a one-minute board demonstration on a non-task ray: cover its third dot, so two dots are trapped and the rest is one forever piece. This doesn't give away P1.
7. **should**, guide p. 1 prepares "three sets for two third-grade pairs and an older pair" but never says what the K–1 table does. Name it: another week's K–1 packet, or an adult-read P1 and P7 with counters.
8. **could**, guide p. 2, Problem 5: add "(choose a dot not on the window's edge)" (math check 2).
9. **could**, guide p. 2: the hint "a finite wall has ends" uses the week's technical word; say "stops somewhere" (math check 3).
10. **could**, p. 1: "counters" and "blockers" name one object; use one word.

## App fit

The theme has no row in `docs/plan/themes.md`. Propose fit B, size M, with this row: | 68 | How many ways out (ends) | Block dots on endless line, ladder, grid and tree maps; count pieces that go on forever | Line and ladder have 2 ends, the grid 1, the 3-regular tree infinitely many (3·2^r after a radius-r ball) | Partly: the graph board draws maps and takes taps; nothing shows an endless map | B | Block dots on a panning board to hit targets; "can't" verdicts with certificates | M |

Panning makes "forever" real, and a tapped dot takes its roads with it. The checkable solves:
- Line: trap exactly two pockets with four blocks.
- Ladder: split it with the fewest blocks (2). After one block, the other rail goes round it.
- Tree: make seven forever pieces with three blocks. The bound 2m + 1, since each block adds at most two pieces, certifies "best" (checked exhaustively up to four blocks).
- "Can't": three pieces on the ladder (the two tails outside the blocked columns), and two on the grid (the app draws the box round every block and the ring road outside it).

Pitfalls:
- Don't colour forever pieces live, or trapped-against-forever stops being the child's judgement. Check on submit.
- Decide "forever" by the continuation rule, never by touching the window edge.
- Never ask the child to count arrows or to predict "how many pieces now?".

Draw on P1–P8 and fix 12.

The line, ladder, grid and tree give enough contrasting boards; forever is decided by the continuation rule, never by the window's edge, and pieces are judged on submit, not coloured live.
