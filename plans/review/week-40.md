## The mathematics

Colour each arc of a knot diagram R, B or G so every crossing is all one colour or all three. Reidemeister moves match colourings one to one, so the count is a knot invariant. The trefoil has 9 colourings and a plain loop 3, so a closed overhand knot can never become a plain loop. The figure-eight also has 3, so equal counts prove nothing. A mathematician would enjoy it: the first knot invariant, with a certificate children can hold. P2–P4 carry the count and the stuck cord; K–1 P5–P6 and the upper P5 (and 2–3 P6) carry unique completion and one move; 4–5 P6–P7 the certificate and its limit.

## App fit

B, confirmed; size M. A tap lights a whole arc and cycles its colour, and crossings show validity live, enforcing what paper cannot. Solves: complete a crossing; colour a knot with more than one colour (trefoil, granny, 6₁); find every colouring and declare "done" for the app to check; claim "one colour only" where that is true (figure-eight, 5₁, 5₂); match fixed end colours on a tangle; the bonus's crossing machine. A multi-colour colouring certifies a knot. Pitfalls: count predictions, visible answer slots, unverified crossing codes (one flipped crossing changes the knot), Neighbor Lanterns' "neighbours differ" check, and any hint that equal counts mean the same knot. Draw on P2–P3, K–1 P5–P6, 2–3 P5, 4–5 P7 and bonus P1–P2.
