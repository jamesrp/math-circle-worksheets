## The mathematics

Creases from one centre fold flat only if there is an even number of them, since each crease turns the paper over. Each alternating set of sectors must also make a half-turn. That is Kawasaki's condition, and it is also sufficient when the labels are free. Four right angles fold in exactly 8 of the 16 mountain/valley labellings, the ones with three of one letter: Maekawa's rule in miniature. Count and angles together still do not settle a labelling, since VVVM fails on 30/60/150/120. A mathematician would enjoy this: short proofs, an exact classification, and a parity certificate even K–1 can hold (P4). K–1 P1–P5, 2–3 P2–P5 and 4–5 P1–P5 carry it.

## Fix

1. **must**, all bands, guide p. 2.
   - The guide asks for "thirty thin disks about 18 cm across, ten plain replacements, six demonstration disks", marked with "a ruler and adult protractor".
   - It also asks for wedge sets of 7, 16 and 6 pieces, traced from the pages. Nothing prints disks, wedges, K–1 tabs or A–H cards.
   - No disk is listed for 2–3 P4's "Make a flat fold of each completed pattern". The disks on the pages are 5 cm diagrams, too small to fold.
   - Fix: a print-and-cut sheet on copy paper.
     - One 18 cm disk per page, with centre, numbered rays and a front mark: the right-angle disk, B, C, D, the P4 starts and a blank disk.
     - The wedge sets with half-turn templates.
     - The tabs and the A–H cards.
     - Counts for eleven children.
2. **should**, guide p. 1, launch: after "Demonstrate one legal refold", show how to record a fold on a large drawn disk. Unfold it with the front up and ray 1 at the top, then write M or V at each ray. The guide predicts the mix-up itself (p. 7: "Check the marked front…").
3. **should**, K–1 P3: six disks come already marked M, but there are four answers, and the guide never says two disks are spare (math check, finding 3). Print four marked disks and two blank ones labelled as extra workspace.
4. **should**, 4–5 P6: "How many different orders…?" has exactly 12 answer rows, and the answer is 12 (math check, finding 2). Print 16.
5. **should**, 2–3 P2–P4.
   - P2 measures alternating pairs before P3 folds the same disks, so the criterion arrives before the failure it explains.
   - P3's "explain any exact mismatch" and P4's "so the two alternating groups of wedges both make a half-turn" print the method.
   - Fix: swap P2 and P3, and end the measuring problem with "For any disk that could not close, explain why." Write P4 as "Add one ray to each pattern so it can close flat along every ray."
6. **should**, 4–5 p. 1: cut "A failed attempt alone does not establish impossibility." It is commentary, and the guide already says it.
7. **could**, 4–5 P3: ask for "an angle rule that decides" instead of stating it.
8. **could**, 4–5 P4: cut "Explain why each labeling on your list works", since a fold is its own witness.
9. **could**, 2–3 p. 1: cut "Loose wedges are for measuring."

## App fit

Change C to B, size L.

On screen, the child taps rays to cycle blank, M and V, then presses Fold. The app searches the layer orders, which is instant up to eight rays; foldcheck.py is an independent oracle. It then animates the collapse or shows the collision. Dragging rays or wedges covers the angle side. Draw on K–1 P3–P5, 2–3 P3, P4 and P6, and 4–5 P1 and P4–P6.

Certificates for "can't fold":
- the alternating fans set against a straight edge;
- clashing front and back colours;
- the tab count.

Pitfalls:
- On right angles, random tapping wins half the time. Use fixed labels, tab stocks and unequal angles (pattern D allows 4 labellings of 16).
- No answer slots.
- No "will it fold?" quizzes.
- Keep a visible front colour, so turning the disk over never swaps M and V silently.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** Port, in Wave 9: the theme moves from C to B. The child taps rays to cycle M and V and presses Fold; the app searches the layer orders (instant up to eight rays, with `foldcheck.py` as an independent oracle) and animates the collapse or shows the collision. Solves are completing labels around fixed ones, sharing a stock of M/V tabs across disks, and dragging in the ray that lets a disk close, with the alternating fans, clashing colours and the tab count as "can't fold" certificates. Right-angle puzzles use fixed labels or unequal angles so random tapping can't win, and there are no "will it fold?" quizzes. The revise is for the worksheet's missing kit and doesn't hold up the port. Tracked in the app's [decisions.md](https://github.com/jamesrp/small-math-adventure/blob/main/docs/plan/decisions.md).
