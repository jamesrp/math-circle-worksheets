## The mathematics

A layer kept around the old shape is the difference: 2n + 1 for squares, n + 1 for stairs, 2n + 2 for n × (n + 1) rectangles, and two stairs make that rectangle (P1–P6). Running totals undo differences up to the start (P7–P10). In a two-tray cascade the first input counts N times, so largest-first is best, proved by an adjacent swap (P11–P13). Four pictures do not force a fifth (P14–P15); a cube shell is three slabs (P16–P17); a third tray counts corner piles, n(n + 1)(n + 2)/6 (P18–P20). A mathematician would enjoy it: discrete calculus with objects, with real proofs in the exchange and the pile matching.

## Fix

1. **should**, 4–5, p. 11, P19: both pictures draw the corner cube after cube (1, 0, 0), so its grey face covers that cube's front and the stair layer looks impossible (math.md item 1). Fix: draw (1, 0, 0) after the corner cube or column.
2. **should**, p. 10, P18; guide p. 7: the worked turn (2, 3, 4) → (3, 6, 10) is the task's turn 2 → 3, yet the guide calls it "the non-task example" (math.md item 2). Fix: (3, 3, 3) → (4, 3, 3) → (4, 7, 3) → (4, 7, 10), and the guide sentence to match.
3. **should**, p. 1: the example, 1 × 2 grown to 2 × 3 by four shaded tiles, is P5's first growth and shows the row-plus-column layer P5 asks for. The plan wanted a non-task strip. Fix: 2 × 2 grown to 2 × 3 by one shaded column; use it for the guide's launch too, which now grows P1's own 2 × 2.
4. **should**, P2: "find every possible size" is answered at a glance (1–4). Fix: "Take the side-5 square apart one layer at a time, always leaving a square in the bottom-left corner. How many tiles come off each time?" The layers add back to 25.

6. **should**, K–1, guide p. 2: "The younger table may remain with shapes or a familiar previously prepared activity." Fix: a K–1 return route with adult reading: P7 and P10 with counters, P16 to side 3, P19 steps 2–3 in cubes. That K–1 manages it is a prediction.

## App fit

Not in themes.md yet. I propose Fit B, size M: a new family or a sibling of Gap rings (49). The child drags input cards into a lane and runs it; counters drop into tray 1 and a copy of the new first total flies into tray 2 (tray 3 on Hard). Zero cards take their turn. Solves: an exact final total, or largest and smallest, judged by the app. Can't past the maximum is certified by the child's sorted order, played. Hard uses three trays (weights 15, 10, 6, 3, 1; with {2, 2, 1, 0, 0} only 25 of the values 14–56 occur) or gapped two-tray decks ({4, 1, 0, 0, 0} misses 10, 15, 20, since 4a + b would need a = b) (card_appfit.py). P9's hidden starts suit Every start with That's all.

Pitfalls: no predict-the-total; with {2, 2, 1, 0, 0} and two trays every value 9–21 occurs in 30 orders, so drag-until-green works without gaps, three trays or a run budget; never show how many answers remain. Draw on P9, P11–P13 and P18. Shapes, shells and piles stay on paper: growth is watch-only, the solids are 3D, and P4's pairing is too easy for Tile gardens.
