# Week 49, Four towers and differences: cold adult read

Reader: a fresh agent briefed as a parent volunteer, handed the three print band PDFs (K–1 7 pages, 2–3 8, 4–5 8) ten minutes before the session, with no guide. November 7, 2026. Report as written by the reader, lightly trimmed.

**1. What I think the activity is (confidence: medium-high)**
Four numbers sit around a square of arrows, A→B→C→D→A. In one move, each corner becomes the difference between itself and the next corner along its arrow. Children repeat the move until all four are 0, then look for starts that last longest. They also compare a 3-corner triangle, and the 4–5 sheet asks why the process always ends. K–1 acts it out with cube towers; the older sheets mostly work with numbers.

**2. What I'd prepare**
Nothing on the pages lists materials. Inferred: stackable cubes (roughly 40 per pair); the old/new boxes printed on each page seem to be the mats (a guess); counters and a cup for K–1; pencils.
Could not work out: whether 2–3 and 4–5 use cubes or just numbers; how many copies to print; timing and which problems to skip; the answers (nothing says the longest start).

**3. Problem 1 launch**

It took a re-read and the numbers in the diagram to decode "extra part". The four small start diagrams have no A–D labels, and there's no space to record moves.

**4. The point (confidence: medium)**
Four-station rings of whole numbers always reach zero; triangles can loop (1,0,0 cycles); the biggest number never grows; parity forces all-even after four moves, which feeds the proof; doubling the start doubles every later step. Pieced together from the 4–5 questions; no page states it.
