# Week 24, Three random decks: cold adult read

Reader: a fresh agent briefed as a parent volunteer, handed the three print band PDFs (K–1 9 pages, 2–3 8, 4–5 8) ten minutes before the session, with no guide. November 7, 2026. Report as written by the reader, lightly trimmed.

---

**1. What I think the activity is**
Three small decks of number cards: A = 2, 4, 9; B = 1, 6, 8; C = 3, 5, 7. Two decks play each other; each side draws one card at random and the higher card wins. Children play, then count all 9 possible card pairs, and find that A beats B, B beats C and C beats A, each by 5 pairs to 4. Later problems ask them to build such decks themselves.
Confidence: high, but only from K–1 page 4, the triangle diagram. Page 1 alone would have given me low confidence.

**2. Preparation**
- Worked out: each table needs three bags labelled A, B, C with the cards above. K–1 also needs counters. Cards for later problems: 1–6 for two-card decks (K–1 P8/P9, 2–3 P8); 1–12 for 2–3 P7; 1–9 for 4–5 P8; duplicates for the "six-card" and "four-card" decks (2–3 P6, 4–5 P5).
- Could not work out: how many sets per table; whether bags must be opaque; whether the printed cards are meant to be cut out or I bring index cards; whether K–1 cards need the ten-frame dots. No answers anywhere; I would not trust myself on 4–5 P7 and P8 in ten minutes.

**3. Problem 1 launch**
- K–1: kids put counters in three pairs of rows (A/B, B/C, C/A), six rounds per pairing, then decide whether one deck beats both others. Me: show how to draw one card from each bag, compare, put a counter in the winner's row, return the cards. Missing: on page 1 I did not know what is in each deck (page 2 cleared that up); "Each player takes one deck" with three decks: two players or three?; "Play six rounds of each game": I had to infer from the row labels that "each game" means the three pairings.
- 2–3: partner picks a deck, child picks a different one, ten rounds, then swap who picks first. The First pick / Second pick table has 6 rows: I don't know what goes in a cell. Ten rounds at 5/9 may not show any advantage; I would not know what to say if the first picker wins.
- 4–5: same, plus a third player draws and keeps score. Same table puzzle.

**4. The point**
Nontransitivity: "beats" is not like "bigger than". No deck is best, so the second picker can always choose a deck that beats the first pick. Card-pair counts differ from a few rounds' results; deck totals don't decide the winner; one- and two-card decks can't make a cycle. Confidence: medium-high.

Most helpful: a half-page adult sheet: "Make three bags: A = 2, 4, 9; B = 1, 6, 8; C = 3, 5, 7. Each beats the next 5 of 9 ways, in a cycle"; what to record in the Problem 1 table; the answers.
