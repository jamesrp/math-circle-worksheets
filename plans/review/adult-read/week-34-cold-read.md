# Week 34, Hidden turns: cold adult read

Reader: a fresh agent briefed as a parent volunteer, handed the three print band PDFs (K–1 6 pages, 2–3 5, 4–5 6) ten minutes before the session, with no guide. November 7, 2026. Report as written by the reader, lightly trimmed.

---

**1. What I think the activity is**
Children put counters on the spots of a ring (3, 4, 5, 6 or 8 spots), copy the pattern onto tracing paper as coloured dots, then try to turn or flip the copy so every dot lands on a counter of the same colour, without just putting it back where it started. The goal is a pattern no turn or flip can match, using as few kinds of counter as possible. Confidence: medium. I only got this on the second read of page 1. The first time, "make every dot sit on its own color" sounded like it could never fail, because it's a copy of the same pattern.

**2. What I'd prepare**
Counters in at least 3 colours (no counts given anywhere; I'd guess about 10 of each per pair); tracing paper, several sheets per child; markers matching the counters; printed packets.
Could not work out: whether the tracing copy needs the ring outline or a centre mark; counter size (spots look about an inch); K–1 Problem 3 shows a filled dot, a small open circle and a square: which counters are those?; does "two kinds" mean colours or shapes?

**3. Problem 1 in each band**
- K–1: I'd build the 4-ring start from page 1, trace it, and act out the three pictures ("turn 1 spot: no match", "turn 2 spots: match", "flip over the dashed line: match"). Children then try two-colour patterns on the 3-ring and a partner tries to match each. I can't tell whether Problem 1 is meant to fail: every 3-ring pattern I tried could be matched. Nothing tells me whether to say "it's impossible" or let them keep trying.
- 2–3: same demo, then "Find how few kinds of counter can make a pattern on this ring that no turn or flip can match." I believe it's 3, but I'd be guessing.
- 4–5: same, plus "Can you rule out every pattern with fewer kinds?" I can't judge what counts as a good enough argument.

**4. The point or surprise**
Small rings can't be made lopsided with two colours; 3-, 4- and 5-rings seem to need three kinds; from about 6 spots two colours are enough. Symmetry, and finding patterns that have none. Confidence: medium, inferred from the order of problems. Nothing states it.

3. K–1 p1: "One partner builds. The other keeps the counters still…" Who makes the tracing? Is it a game with a winner? SLOWING
4. All bands: no materials list (colours, counts, tracing-paper size, lining up). SLOWING
5. All bands, intro diagram: only one flip line shown. Are other flip lines allowed? SLOWING
6. K–1 p3: diagram with a black dot, a small circle and a square: which counters? BLOCKING for that problem
7. 2–3 and 4–5 p1: "Only counter kinds and spots count." Can't tell what this means. MINOR
8. All bands: no answers. K–1 Problem 1 (3-ring, two kinds) and Problem 4 (5-ring, two kinds) I think have no solution; a five-year-old will keep trying and I won't know whether to stop them. Same for 2–3 P5 and 4–5 P5. BLOCKING for the later problems
9. 2–3 pp1–2: Problems 1 and 2 ask word-for-word the same question. MINOR
10. K–1 Problem 1: "Can you make one your partner cannot match?" hides that the real task is the tracing-paper test, fiddly for five-year-olds. SLOWING
11. Title "Hidden turns" told me nothing. MINOR

**6. Could I run it from the pages after 10 minutes of prep?**
Only with help. Most helpful: a half-page adult sheet with one plain sentence of the goal ("find a pattern whose only match is putting the copy straight back") and the answers for each ring (3-, 4- and 5-rings need 3 kinds; 6 and 8 need 2; one example each; the 8-ring minimum). A short materials list a close second.
