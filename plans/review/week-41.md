## The mathematics

A 3×3 board with opposite edges glued by translation is a torus. A trip back to H lifts to the tiled plane and finishes in some copy (m,n). Two such trips deform into each other by backtrack cancellations and square slides exactly when their copies agree: loops are classified by Z². K–1 meets real facts too: every cell is exactly two steps from H, an odd trip home must lap the board, and two pawns given the same steps never trade places. A mathematician would enjoy it. K–1 P2–P4, 2–3 P3–P5 and 4–5 P3–P6 carry it.

## App fit

Fit B, size M; confirm the row. The child taps arrows to move a pawn on the portal board while an unrolled view draws the trail straight into neighbouring copies. Solves:

- every cell in exactly two steps, declaring "that's all"; home in exactly 3, 5 or 7 steps; home in the original copy after visiting two others (K–1 P2–P3, 2–3 P5);
- trade two pawns: never on 3 wide, possible at offset 2 on 4 wide; the unrolled view's fixed gap certifies "can't" (K–1 P4);
- tap a step-and-reverse pair or slide a corner to shrink a loop, or turn RRRUUU into UUURRR (4–5 P4–P5). "Can't": the finishing copy, which no move changes. "Fewest": the nine squares between the trips, one per slide.

Pitfalls: P1's "where does it end?", 4–5 P3's arithmetic as a quiz, found-counts, auto-shrinking loops.

For 64, 70 and 75 the shared board needs from this side: a surface stored as squares with right and up neighbour maps, translations only, so this torus, 75's cylinder (one pair glued, rims kept) and 64's three or four squares are data. Exact positions snapping to cell centres (41), lattice crosses (70) or a fine lattice (75). A lift that develops each path from a fixed start copy and returns its copies and endpoint (m,n); a fixed periodic tiling is wrong for 64. Straight segments of any rational slope traced through seams in both views (70, 64). Loop editing as a separate layer, which 75 reuses for fixed-end arcs. A view that recentres and grows, so no edge is a wall.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** Port, first in Wave 4: Week 41 brings the shared portal board (squares glued by right and up neighbour maps, exact lattice positions, a step-by-step lift into a recentring unrolled view, and straight shots of rational slope), and Weeks 64, 70 and 75 are built on it. The solves are the moving ones above: every cell in exactly two steps with "that's all", home in an exact number of steps, home in the original copy, the two-pawn trade, and shrinking a loop with the finishing copy as the "can't" certificate. The verdict rests on rating the unsupplied 90 mm copy array a should: unlike Week 23's kit, the printed repeated maps hold every task.
