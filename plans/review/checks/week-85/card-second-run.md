## The mathematics

Two 160 mm bars with midpoint joints and two 80 mm bars make a rhombus. On the open branch P = O + 2(T − O), a dilation by 2 about the anchor. It holds in every pose: O to T is u + v, and O to P is 2u + 2v. So lengths double, shape and orientation are kept, and area is multiplied by 4. Swapping tips halves. Moving the anchor by d moves the copy by −d, and an input shift of d/2 undoes it. A mathematician would enjoy a machine that computes a map, with a proof a ten-year-old can do with strips. Shared P1–P4 carry copying, steering and the invariant; upper P1–P4 the proof, area, inverse and centre.

The plan rules out the obvious build: "Paper strips with loose brads are demonstration prototypes" (plans/seven-families-85-91/week-85-drawing-machines.md). An adult would have to invent a pencil that is also the T hinge, three times. The Week 23, 28 and 51 cards rated an undefined kit a must. Fix: one parts list on materials p. 2 and guide p. 2. For example:
   - 5 mm foam board or corrugated plastic strips, cut on the p. 1 template;
   - a brass fastener with washers at X, Q and Y, and a pushpin through O into the board;
   - a pencil through snug holes as the pin itself: blue at T, through both short bars, and red at P; T and P labelled on the bars;
   - tracing paper or matte drafting film for overlays, since pencil barely marks clear acetate.

## App fit

No row in themes.md yet. I propose Fit B, size M. Dragging the linkage is a playground; steering T while watching P is drag-until-green. So commit first, on a dot grid: tap the corners of T's path, then Run, so the copy hits dots or follows a box (shared P2, P4; reversed, upper P3); tap where to pin the anchor so the copy lands on a target; move the input after the anchor moves (upper P4). "Can't" fits a target that is rotated, mirrored or not exactly double, certified by tapping a side that is not parallel to, or not twice, its partner. Hard can chain ×2 and ×½ about different anchors: a translation by half the anchor difference. The proof and area counting stay on paper. Draw on shared P1, P2 and P4 and upper P3–P4.
