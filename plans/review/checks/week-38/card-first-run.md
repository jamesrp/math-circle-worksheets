## The mathematics

A strip whose ends join in matching order is an annulus with two boundary circles; reversed, a Möbius band with one. A middle cut splits A into two annuli but leaves B one piece, an annulus with two edges, because the seam sends U to U on A and swaps U and L on B; the cycles of the swap count the pieces. An arrow carried once around B returns reversed. A mathematician would enjoy it: boundary, connectedness and orientability read from a gluing recipe, with a famous surprise. P1–P2 carry the edges, P4–P5 the cut and its reason, P3 and P6 pieces against edges, 4–5 P6 orientation.

## Fix

1. **must**, bonus p. 2 (companion), visual above P2, and bonus guide p. 2. Strip 2's end has its dot on top in both rows, and the R row's "align the same marks" joins dot to dot. Like marks send top to top, which is M however the strip turns (math.md 1). A child who follows it builds only two-edge circles, never P2's 2, 1, 2, 1; the guide repeats "same-mark alignment with M versus R". Fix: draw strip 2's end half-turned (square over dot) and join straight across, dot to square; retitle the column "join"; in the guide write "M joins dot to dot; R, after a half-turn, joins dot to square."
2. **should**, guide p. 2: "four strips about 45 × 400 mm" per child. 400 mm exceeds Letter, nothing prints the strips or marks, and A and B are made only in the pretest, so who joins each child's pair is unstated. Fix: a print-and-cut Letter sheet of 45 × 250 mm strips with middle line, U, L and A's or B's corner marks; two per K–3 child, four per 4–5 child, plus spares; "Adults join A and B for each child before the session."
3. **should**, guide pp. 2 and 5 (math.md 2). On B, U meets L on the other face, and the 4–5 P6 arrow returns on the other face, which an adult told to check for "flipped by hand" may reject. Print U, L and the middle line on both faces; add to the P6 key: "On B the arrow returns on the other face; that is one-sidedness, not a flip."
4. **should**, K–1 and 2–3 p. 6, P7: "Arrange four edge dots so every cut piece has a dot and no edge trip reaches two dots." This is P6's 1+1+1+1 row: four dots on cut A's four edges reach both pieces, and cut B has two edges. Replace it: "Ask the adult to cut the piece from B along its middle again. How many pieces? Trace their edges." (Two linked annuli; P5's U/L reasoning predicts it. Unrehearsed on a 22 mm piece.)
5. **should**, guide p. 7: "A first visit can stay with P1-2 … On return, K-1 and grades 2-3 can use the middle cuts". P. 2 times the cuts at 22–37 minutes; a K–1 table on p. 7's route gets P1–P3 and no cut. Keep the cut in the first visit.
6. **should**, all bands p. 3, P3: "or cross it out" beside "A: ___ B: ___". A picture can work on A and fail on B. Write "Mark each blank yes or no."
7. **should**, all bands p. 2: "The rings record which dots share an edge." repeats the line above and narrates the diagram. Cut it.
8. **should**, 4–5 p. 6, P7: a 13 mm box for an explanation. Move P6's diagram up into the 40 mm gap below its text.
9. **could**, p. 1: the worked example is A's recipe, so its cylinder shows A's two rims before P1.
10. **could**, p. 1: "input / process / output" → "flat strip / join / band".
11. **could**, p. 1: "Keep the bands on the table" can stop a child lifting B to trace it.
12. **could**, bonus guide p. 1: "KK11 / 3333 / 445" → "K–1 / 2–3 / 4–5" (math.md 3).

## App fit

Change C to B, size M: the substance lives on the flat recipe; the paper cut stays the worksheet's surprise. On screen: strips with seam marks, an M/R toggle, lane cuts and holes, with a 3D view as reward, not solve. Solves: colour edge segments so each colour is one closed edge; place four dots to match a ring picture or declare "impossible", certified by fewer coloured edges than rings; a four-strip circle with one edge or two (bonus P2), certified by R-seam parity; lanes and a seam giving a one-edge piece beside a two-edge piece (bonus P1); at most three holes for four edges (bonus P3). Pitfalls: predict-the-cut levels, auto-traced edges, a running edge count; a flat recipe says nothing about linking. Draw on P1–P3, P5–P6, 4–5 P6 (a sign that flips at each R seam) and bonus P1–P3.
