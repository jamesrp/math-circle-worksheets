## The mathematics

If A lies in [L, U] and B in [V, W], each free, then A + B fills [L+V, U+W] and A − B fills [L−W, U−V]. One witness defeats a promise, and a tight bound needs two. The surprise is the difference: the smallest gap pairs the shortest long strip with the longest short one (K–1 P5, older P6). Dependency goes deeper. A repeated A cancels (K–1 P4, older P5, 4–5 P9), and a fixed total links the strips (4–5 P8). Subtraction also fails to undo addition (older P3). A mathematician would enjoy P3, P8 and P9; the sum fact alone is light.

## Fix

1. **must**, all bands, guide p. 1: "two sliding paper strips with opaque sleeves or a cover card, and a working ruler". The guide gives no lengths and no construction, and nothing prints the strips. The pages need them (K–1 P1 "Make the shortest and longest joins with these strips", older P1 "build allowed strips"), and page 2's rulers stop at 8 while its joins reach 12. A second strip can't butt against an end hidden in a sleeve (a prediction; the guide calls strip handling unpiloted). The K–1 parent volunteer would have to design the kit (compare Weeks 23 and 35). Fix: add a print-and-cut sheet per pair, at 10 mm per unit and 15 mm tall. It holds two strips each of 1 to 10 units, one each of 11 to 15, and the white add-ons; or name Cuisenaire rods. Every extreme and witness is whole. Use a centimetre ruler as the board. Change the launch to "pick strips the card allows and join them from 0", and add the cutting time.
2. **should**, every band, p. 1: the launch joins start 2 mm right of the ruler's 0 and end at 3.2 and 5.2, past the band edges (math check item 3). Draw them from x = 104 in make.py.
3. **should**, K–1 p. 4, P4: "keep A unchanged within each comparison; B may vary separately". Read literally, it freezes A in the bottom picture and gives 2 to 4, not the key's 1 to 5 (math check item 2). Replace that sentence with "In the top picture both A's are the same length. Each try may use new lengths."
4. **should**, older p. 5, P7: "Someone subtracts the two middle values…show why this is unsafe". It names no strips, states its conclusion, and P6's witnesses answer it. Add to P6: "Someone subtracts the middle values, 14 − 11, and says the gap is exactly 3." Delete P7, then renumber the problems, keys and route.
5. **should**, K–1 and older p. 4: "One possible setting; the white pieces are exactly 4 and 1 units long." narrates the picture. Put "The white pieces are exactly 4 and 1 units." in the problem and delete the caption.
6. **should**, K–1 P7 and 2–3 P8: three 9-unit boards for exactly three answers, where knowing the list is complete is the task. Print five.
7. **should**, guide p. 1: the "human strips" (22–25) and "exchange guarantees" (43–55) slots are described nowhere. The plan runs 50 working minutes; context.md gives 35–40. Describe or cut both, and fit the times.
8. **should**, guide p. 4: every first route is "P1-2…add P3", so the organizer's 4–5 table reaches P8–P9 only on a return visit. Route 4–5 through P2, P3, P5, P8 and P9.
9. **could**, K–1 P3: add "Fitting within 9 includes ending at 9." The math check calls this a problem. I don't: the natural reading includes 9, and the guide states it.
10. **could**, older P4: "A different pair" is P1's pair; say so.
11. **could**, guide overview: [7,9] − [3,4] = [3,6], yet P3's widest B is [4,5], which ties P3 to P9.
