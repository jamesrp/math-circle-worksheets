## The mathematics

A ball leaves the bottom-left corner of a w by h grid table at 45° and stops at the first corner. Flip copies of the table across its walls and the path is one straight line. It first meets a corner of the copies after lcm(w, h) steps. Divide out the common factor to get p by q. The ball stops on the right when q is odd and at the top when p is odd, after p + q − 2 bounces. So it never comes home, and a top-right stop always has an even count. A mathematician would enjoy it: gcd and lcm fall out of geometry, and parity gives real impossibility proofs. It is carried by K–1 P4 and P6–P7, 2–3 P2, P4 and P6–P8, and 4–5 P2–P8.

## Fix

1. **must**, guide p. 2, "How a child walks the path": "After a side wall, keep going up the grid but slant the other way." The P1 and P2 hints on p. 4 say the same. From the left dot the walk bounces at (3,3), (1,5), (0,4), (3,1), (2,0) and (0,2). At (0,4) the rule sends the walker back to (1,5), but the ball goes to (1,3). It is wrong again at (3,1) and says nothing about the near end at (2,0). The right-dot walk mirrors it. The guide's p. 4 picture and test step 4 disagree, so the parent directs wrong moves or is stuck. Fix: "At a side wall, keep going the same way along the grid and slant the other way. At either end, keep the slant and turn back." P1 hint: "Keep going the same way along the room and slant the other way." P2 hint: "Turn away from the wall you hit and keep the other direction."
2. **should**, guide p. 2, test step 1: "Measure the squares: 3/4 inch on the K–1 pages, 1/2 inch on the others." The first squares on page 1 belong to the opening picture, which is drawn smaller, so an adult may reprint a correct page. Fix: "Measure a square in a numbered problem."
3. **should**, guide p. 1: section 1 leaves out the p by q rule and the parity fact behind 2–3 P4 and 4–5 P6. Both appear only on p. 9. Add them to section 1, with their limits (slope 1, whole-number sides).

## App fit

Fit A, confirmed; the row's "rework candidate" stands. Mirror Couriers ships 12 instances. Four ask for a prediction, three a width from 1 to 12, and five an aim. `app_family_check.py` confirms every answer and every list of accepted settings, and billiard-03 is 4–5 P5's 6 by 10. But every solve is a menu choice and a reveal. A wrong prediction prints the right answer, a width search has 12 options, nothing is impossible, and the unfolding is shown after the launch.

Rework it in place, size S:

- **Draw the room.** Drag the room's corner on a grid to reach a given corner after n bounces (K–1 P5, 2–3 P4, 4–5 P6). Include "top right after 3" with a "No room" claim. The certificate: each side bounce flips left and right, so a right-hand stop has an even number of side bounces, and the same holds for the top.
- **Every room.** Tap every room up to 6 by 6 with one bounce, then "That's all", with no counter (2–3 P6).
- **Bring it home.** Make the start corner the target; the solve is "No room", with the halfway crossing as the certificate (K–1 P6, 2–3 P8, 4–5 P7).
- **Unfold.** On a sheet of copies, tap the first point where thick lines cross, then watch it fold (4–5 P3–P4).

Keep the aims as a late group. Pitfalls: the answer shown after a miss, typed counts for big rooms.
