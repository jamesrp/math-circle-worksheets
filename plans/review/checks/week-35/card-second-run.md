## The mathematics

A border is an endless strip of one notched F with an arrow, in up to four orientations; a move matches when a tracing copy lands every footprint on one of the same form. The motif has no symmetry, so one footprint's landing place decides a move. A glide done twice is a slide; when a border has a glide but the middle flip fails, the shortest glide is half the shortest slide; two perpendicular flips force a half-turn at their crossing. A mathematician would enjoy it: the frieze groups without the catalogue. 2–3 P2–P3 and 4–5 P2–P4 and P6 carry it; K–1 separates the four moves by building and testing.

## Fix

1. **must**, all bands, guide p. 2: "the exact notched F-like outline printed in the introduction, at least 25 mm long … The packet is not a complete manipulative kit. If the exact outline is hard to reproduce, trace and copy its printed example". Nothing prints pieces; the printed outline is 17.7 mm wide, and Border E needs 30 mm or less (math check, item 3). Every band builds from these pieces, so a parent volunteer must draw over 100 asymmetric shapes. Fix: a print-and-cut sheet, per pair 12 cards of A and 12 of its mirror, about 27 mm square with the motif about 24 mm wide (turning a card gives the other two forms), plus six tracing strips about 19 × 7 cm. Restate prep to include cutting.
2. **should**, all bands, p. 1: "Use the loose footprints and long strips." Each problem prints a 17.65 × 6 cm box, the guide asks for strips "at least 70 by 280 mm", and nothing says what the boxes are for; drawing notched F's for every border would be heavy transcription. A box holds three repeats of a 6 cm slide and two rows of fix 1's cards. Fix: "Build each border in its box."
3. **should**, 2–3 p. 2, P3: "On your border, how does the shortest matching glide compare with the shortest matching slide?" One comparison, though the guide calls "half" a theorem (p. 4). Fix: P2 asks for two such borders with different shortest slides (it has two boxes); P3 says "On each border".
4. **should**, 4–5 p. 2, P3: "A border matches after a flip and a 3 cm slide along the middle line." Read as a perpendicular flip, the math check's V8 matches yet fails the 6 cm slide the key requires (item 1). Fix: "A border matches after a 3 cm glide."
5. **should**, K–1 p. 3, P4: "Make a different border with the same kind of match." Heard once, "the same kind" doesn't say whether the flip alone must fail; the key (G at 8 cm) repeats P3. Fix: "Make another border like Problem 3's, with a longer slide."
6. **should**, 4–5 p. 4, P6: "Which claims can you justify for its endless repetition?" A child cannot tell what is asked, and about 2 cm is left for a full list. Fix: "Explain why no other move matches", with a half page.
7. **should**, guide p. 3: "Positions below are motif anchors". Only centre anchors avoid overlaps (math check, item 2), and the K–1 parent must decode "A at (6k, h) and H A at (6k + 3, −h)". Fix: "Positions are footprint centres", and draw each witness over three repeats.
8. **should**, K–1 footers: "W35-k-1-v2" is also the archived six-problem packet's id; the guide's keys are for "K-1 v3" (math check, item 4). Fix: W35-k-1-v3.

10. **could**, guide p. 2: "Start K-1 with one repeating row" in the common launch reads as one table.

## App fit

B, size M, confirmed. The screen gives a truly endless strip and an exact copy: the child edits one repeat cell, the app tiles it, and a tapped move (slide, flip, glide, mirror line, half-turn centre) shows its overlay with mismatches lit. Solves are builds to a spec, checked by computing the full symmetry group: glide without flip (2–3 P2), flip without half-turn (2–3 P4), half-turn without any flip (4–5 P5), break a move but keep repeating (K–1 P5, 2–3 P6), and find every move, the child declaring done (4–5 P6). Certificates: one lit mismatch refutes a move; arrows all pointing one way rule out every half-turn; no same-form footprint at the same height nearer than d rules out shorter slides. Avoid "will this match?" predictions and frieze names; snap to a grid.
