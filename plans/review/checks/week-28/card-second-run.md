## The mathematics

A paper disk is creased along rays from its centre, each ray a mountain or valley seen from a marked front. Every crease turns the paper over, so an odd number of rays cannot fold (4–5 P2), and every other sector must total a half-turn (Kawasaki; 2–3 P2–P4, 4–5 P1, P3). The four-right-angle disk folds in exactly eight ways, three of one letter and one of the other (K–1 P1–P5, 2–3 P5, 4–5 P4–P5); K–1 P4 makes that a parity puzzle across three disks. A mathematician would enjoy it; the guide's model D shows the 3:1 count alone does not certify a labeling.

## App fit

Change C to B, size M. A child taps rays to cycle M/V and folds; the app shows the flat layer stack (as on guide p. 4) or where two layers cross. Solves: complete labels around fixed ones (K–1 P3); share an M/V stock across three disks (K–1 P4), "can't" certified by the odd M count of every right-angle fold; drag in the ray that makes a disk fold (2–3 P4); ring six wedges so alternate ones make half-turns and declare all twelve found (4–5 P6); route through the eight folds (K–1 P6). Certificates: faces changing colour at each crease for odd rays (4–5 P2), half-turn bars for alternating totals, the crossing layer for model D's VVVM. Pitfalls: a free fold check per tap invites toggling all sixteen labelings, so use a declared "done" and no "3 of 8"; no "will it fold?" button; draw ridges and troughs; snap dragged rays. Draw on the problems named and on model D as a hard case.
