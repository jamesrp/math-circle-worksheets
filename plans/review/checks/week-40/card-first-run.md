## The mathematics

Each arc of a knot diagram gets R, B or G so that every crossing is all one colour or all different. The trefoil has 9 colourings, the plain loop 3. Two colours at a crossing force the third, so Reidemeister moves carry colourings across one for one and the count is a knot invariant: different counts prove two loops different, equal counts prove nothing (the figure-eight). A mathematician would enjoy it. P2–P3 give the counts, P5–P6 the forced completion and the type II move, 4–5 P6–P7 the certificate and its limit.

## App fit

Fit B, size M, confirmed. Tap an arc to cycle R, B, G; a crossing lights when all one colour or all different. Solves: complete given arcs (K–1 P5–P6; completion is forced, so a wrong fill clashes); find every trefoil colouring and declare done, with no visible count (P2); colour both sides of a move with matching ends (upper P5); "Can this loop untie?" on disguised trefoils, unknots and the figure-eight. A colouring using two or more colours certifies a knot. For "only one-colour colourings", colour two arcs at one crossing differently and follow forced arcs to a clash (checked from every figure-eight crossing). Pitfalls: equal counts never certify untying; no over/under flip while colouring; no "x of 9" counter; no predict-the-count levels like the bonus's joined loops. The bonus machine and closures suit Hard.
