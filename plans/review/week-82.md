## The mathematics

Two lines can match kid for kid (same size) without matching in order (same shape). A star before an endless line is absorbed (★ → 1, n → n + 1), so 1 + ω = ω; a star after every kid is a last kid, which the plain line lacks, so ω + 1 ≠ ω though a size match exists (P2). A red row then a whole blue row puts endlessly many kids before B1, while alternating pairs give every kid finitely many, so those match only if order may change (P3: ω + ω ≠ 2·ω = ω). P4 builds the six shapes ω·a + b, a, b ≤ 2. A mathematician would enjoy it: the first facts of ordinal arithmetic, each proved by an invariant. P1 is the finite control: of six complete matches, one keeps order.

## Fix

1. **should**, pp. 1–3, P1–P3: "Find every match between these two class lines." and "Can a match keep order?" The page defines only "complete match", and its third picture shows a match that leaves F out. Read so, P1 has 34 matches, not the guide's 6, and keeping order is trivially possible for A with C (leave the star out) and D with E (match the reds) (math.md item 1). An adult can redirect, so not a must. Fix: "Find every complete match" and "Can a complete match keep order?"
2. **should**, p. 4, P4: "Arrange one or two endless numbered rows …" P2's A with B and A with C answer both requests, and guide p. 3's two examples are exactly those. Fix: "Arrange two endless numbered rows and up to two ★ kids". That leaves ten arrangements, 18 same-shape pairs and 27 others, and a surprise: a star after a whole row is absorbed by the next (WSW with WW: ★ → B1, Bn → Bn+1). Put that pair and WW with WWS in the guide's key with math.md's six-shape table, and print a third "This kid comes after EVERY kid" panel on p. 6 (WSWS beside WWS needs three).
3. **should**, p. 3, P3: "There is no last red kid." In E every kid but R1 has a kid just before it, so this is the premise of one of the two obstructions (guide p. 1). "red numbers continue forever" already carries the convention. Cut it.
4. **should**, guide p. 2: the launch says only "Introduce the continuation-arrow convention", though P2 and P3 ask for "a rule for every kid" and guide p. 4 names reading the arrow as a rule as the thing to watch. Add a demonstration on a non-task pair: two plain endless rows, top 1 with bottom 2, top 2 with bottom 1, top 3 with bottom 4, and so on; then "Who is top kid 20's partner?" It shows a rule without the shift or either obstruction. Prediction.
5. **should**, K–1: no route; a should, as on the Week 76, 78 and 79 cards, because the band choice is deliberate. P1's cards and yarn need no reading (prediction).
6. **could**, footer "W82-Q-draft-v1" and guide header "Facilitator draft" on a promoted packet; Q is the old "queues" name.
7. **could**, guide p. 3: "ask about the first kid of block B" means B1 of D; the pages say "blue row", and B is P2's line.
8. **could**, guide p. 3: "an apparent unmatched card at the right edge" holds only when B shows the star and six numbers.

There is no real on-screen solve; the substance is a conversation about infinity, and it belongs on paper. P1's matches are permutations of three, which Cup swaps, Mixed-up cups and Ticket shuffles already cover. The endless part is new substance (no family has order types). Its nearest port is a group on Ways out's panning map: string a few kids, press "and so on", and the app checks one-to-one, onto and order for every kid. But the button takes the infinite step for the child, and the solves are two patterns, shift by one and take the rows in turn, which repeat after a puzzle or two, as Week 73's did. The Can't certificate is always the star after a row or the first kid after an arrow, so after one puzzle Can't is a yes/no claim with one obvious tap that cannot fail. Sorting P4's 16 arrangements into six piles under That's all reduces to one rule (count the rows, count the stars after the last row): the child predicts a shape and builds nothing.

Recommendation: not porting. The cards, yarn, panels and the talk about every kid stay on paper. If a later road wants infinity, start from Ways out's map, not from Pairs that stay or Circle strings, whose matchings are finite.
