## The mathematics

A shot in a room with lettered walls gives a bounce word. No rectangle shot goes A, B, A on adjacent walls: in the reflected copies both A seams lie on one line, which a straight shot crosses once. A 60° rhombus makes ABA at its acute corner. All lettered rectangles share every word, because stretching the unfolded picture keeps lines straight and crossings in order, both ways. So words tell the rhombus from a rectangle, never one rectangle from another. This is the elementary core of Calderon et al., "How to hear the shape of a billiard table", and a mathematician would enjoy it. Problems 2 and 4 carry it; Problem 1 builds the unfolding and Problem 3 makes a rule-out game.

## Fix

1. **should**, guide p. 3, P3 key, and the p. 1 overview. "An ABA drawing rules out both rectangles … It may or may not be possible in the rhombus" names one rule-out family; play produces others.
   - BAB, CDC and DCD rule out rectangles as ABA does.
   - Words with two horizontal walls in a row and also two vertical walls in a row (ACDB, BDCA, CABD, DBAC) rule out every rectangle: no rectangle shot can cross the full height without touching a side and also the full width without touching top or bottom. In a 300,000-shot sample I ran, 11% of 4-bounce rhombus words were of this kind (28% had an ABA-type triple).
   - ACB and its seven symmetric versions are impossible in the rhombus (math.md has the proof), and P1's dot gives four of them.

   Fix: math.md's replacement sentence, "An ABA (or BAB, CDC, DCD) drawing", one sentence on the second family, and an overview clause that words separate rooms both ways. I agree with math.md's fix; the guide is silent rather than claiming one direction.
2. **should**, p. 5 opening. "Draw new rhombus shots here. Stand the mirror on each wall hit: … Keep the checked drawing to reveal after the guess." says what the space is for (neg.md) and repeats two rules. Keep only "Extra workspace for Problems 2–3."

## App fit

Proposed B, size M. Suggested row: "| 71 | Can you hear the room? | Aim shots in a square, a wide rectangle and a 60° rhombus; read the bounce word | ABA impossible in rectangles, possible at an acute corner; all rectangles share every word | Partly: Mirror Couriers traces 45° rectangle paths, not free aims | B | Drag an aim through an unfolded window and watch it fold; target words; a word only one room can make | M |"

The child drags an aim; the app draws it through the copies, folds it into bounces, spells the word and rejects corner hits. Solves:
- A target word from a fixed start (P1).
- ABA in the rhombus, or "impossible" in the square, with the collinear A seams as the certificate (P2).
- A square word remade in the wide rectangle, with a stretch toggle as the reveal (P4).
- A word only one room can make (P3 as a design goal), checked exactly against the rectangle words.

Pitfalls: corner hits under dragging (exact rationals, Q(√3) for the rhombus); no predict-the-word or guess-the-room quizzes; no "x of 20" counter. As an aiming group in Mirror Couriers it would move that predict-heavy family toward clicking, and it removes the mirror, the paper's main unrehearsed risk.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** Not ported in the October 10, 2026 round: aiming through reflected rooms is a new interaction, and this card's own suggestion, an aiming group in Mirror Couriers, belongs with a revisit of that family rather than a new one.
