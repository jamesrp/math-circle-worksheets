## The mathematics

Children turn ordered pairs of draws from three red counters and one blue into a square, a circle or nothing. For independent draws from one fixed bag with both colours, RB and BR each have chance p(1 − p), so BR to one shape, RB to the other and RR, BB skipped is fair for every such bag. The 16 marked pairs fall 9, 3, 3, 1 into RR, RB, BR, BB; a colour-only rule cannot split a class, so that rule and its swap are the only fair ones, ten pairs must be skipped, and no fair rule always gives a shape (9 > 8). Two bags stay fair exactly when their red proportions match; draws without replacement stay fair by reversal. A mathematician would enjoy it. K–1 P3, 2–3 P2 and 4–5 P1–P4 carry the core; 2–3 P5 and 4–5 P5–P6 test the hypotheses.

## App fit

B, confirmed, size S (themes.md: "a short set rather than twelve puzzles"). A child taps two counters in order, the same one twice allowed, to keep marked pairs, and claims "That's all" with no visible count (K–1 P3, 2–3 P2). Then they cycle the four colour-pair tiles through square, circle and skip while the 16 pairs sort live into bins; equal non-empty bins solve it.

- **Fewest skips** on 3R/1B: 10, certified by RR's 9 against the other 7.
- **Contrasts**: RRBB, where first colour deciding skips nothing (possible exactly when red equals blue); RRRB then RBBB, where RB/BR fails (9 against 1) and RR against BB works; RRRB without replacement (skip 0).
- **Busiest bag** (K–1 P5, 2–3 P4: two red, two blue) and **three shapes** (bonus P1: 36 of 729 assignments fair for every bag).

Pitfalls: docs/cases.md's Week 42 row plans pairs from `sequences`, bins for the rule's results and `evenGroups`, and lists "tap a case, then a bin" as missing. A tap must move a whole colour class: with marked pairs as cases any 8–8 split passes, and colour words cannot be the cases because `evenGroups` counts keys. Cross-bag pairs (4–5 P5) need two lists; `sequences` has one alphabet, and the Week 43 story player's product of lists is not yet on main. With 81 rules, ask for fewest skips.
