## The mathematics

A row of towers is a permutation, and the towers seen from an end are its records. The tallest is seen from both ends and splits the row, so (1, 1) never happens and L + R ≤ n + 1. A city is a Latin square with these views as edge clues. A clue set can fit one city, several or none. A 3-by-3 city's twelve numbers always total 22. The fewest clues that fix a city are 2 for 3-by-3, with a child-sized proof, and 3 for 4-by-4, by computer. In 4-by-4, 204 cities share all sixteen numbers with another. A mathematician would enjoy it. Rows carry K–1, 2–3 P1 and 4–5 P1 and P7–P8; cities carry the rest.

But 204 of 576 cities share all sixteen numbers with another, including P4's A and D, which the children have just built, and 4 of the 24 cities with top row 1234, the start the guide's P2 hint suggests. A pair hiding one keeps finding a second city, even with all sixteen numbers written. The P5 entry ("Fewest: 3 … the referee settles it by building") doesn't say so. Its hint (1), "Problem 4 had two numbers and four cities. Add one number.", fails for A and D. The fact sits only under P4, P9 and section 5. The organizer, who runs this table, may recognise it from P4, but the guide should not depend on that. Fix the guide and leave the page, so P9 keeps its discovery: "About a third of 4-by-4 cities (204 of 576, including Problem 4's A and D) share all sixteen numbers with another city. If a second city still fits every number, nobody made a mistake: keep both for Problem 9 and build a new hidden city." Add "(for B or C)" to hint (1).

## App fit

A, confirmed: S for city puzzles on the Orchard grid, nearer M with rows and design. On the open question: add visibility clues as their own group and leave Symbol Orchard pure. A separate group keeps the September decision, one uniform rule in the Orchard, while the views carry this theme's mathematics (records, the L + R bound, impossible cards, ambiguous clue sets, twins). Without them the theme adds nothing the Orchard lacks.

- **Rows (K–1).** Drag three or four towers to match two end numbers, or say "no row". Find every row for a pair and declare "done", with no answer slots. Certificate for "can't": with the tallest in place j, L ≤ j and R ≤ n + 1 − j.
- **Cities.** Tap heights on the grid; tapping an edge number lights its sight line as a checking tool. Accept any city that fits. Include clue sets with no city, as in 2–3 puzzle 3.
- **Design.** Pick edge numbers within a budget (2 for 3-by-3, 3 for 4-by-4); the app shows a second city at once if one fits, which paper can't. Hide only the 372 cities that can be singled out.
- **Twins.** Build another city with the same sixteen numbers (A to D).

Pitfalls: plain Skyscrapers puzzles alone are a puzzle book; no "how many will you see?" predictions. Make 4-by-4 "fewest" a budget, since 3 is certified only by computer. Draw on K–1 P3 and P5–P7, 2–3 P3–P5, and 4–5 P1, P3–P6 and P9; leave the counting (4–5 P7–P8, 2–3 P7) on paper.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** Port, in Wave 6 (grids), at size M, as a visibility-clue group of its own in Symbol Orchard that reuses the Orchard's board while its existing puzzles keep their one rule. Solves are rows for a card or a checked "no row", one-city puzzles and a checked "no city", every city then one more clue with "That's all", making a puzzle while the app plays the partner and shows a second city, and twin cities. This reverses September's "leave them out", so it is put to James; until he says otherwise the wave plans for it. The revise is for one guide entry and doesn't hold up the port.
