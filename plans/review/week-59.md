## The mathematics

Width is the gap between two parallel lines that enclose a shape and both touch it. The disk and the Reuleaux triangle have one width in every direction; the oval, square and triangle do not (P1–P2). For the Reuleaux triangle, one support passes through a corner and the other touches the opposite arc, both perpendicular to one radius, so the gap is the radius; three 60° sectors and their reversals cover every turn (P3–P4). Constant width is not constant distance from a centre: from the centroid the distances run from 25.36 to 34.64 (P5). P6 is Barbier's case: three sixths of a radius-60 circle equal the radius-30 disk's boundary. A mathematician would enjoy this.

## App fit

Change C to B, size L. Turning a shape between closing rails with a gap gauge is a playground; predicting the gauge would repeat what children enjoyed least. A solve: build a shape that spins snug. Given corners (an equilateral triangle, a regular pentagon, then irregular Reuleaux pentagons), the child chooses each arc's centre; the app draws it and spins the result. A rail that never moves certifies success; the direction where the gap changes certifies failure. Pitfalls: no reading of millimetres, no prediction before the spin, and the centroid bob (P5) only as a playground. Draw on P1–P2 (spin test, contrasts), P3 (compass move) and P5 (playground).
