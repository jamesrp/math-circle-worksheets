## The mathematics

A start is a second-player win when every move leads to a first-player win. The rook race at (a, b) is piles a and b, so the second-player squares are the diagonal, and copying keeps the token on it. With any number of piles the second player wins exactly when every stack size 1, 2, 4, 8, … appears an even number of times: a move breaks a pair, and rebuilding the pile with the largest odd stack restores them (Bouton). The queen game's second-player squares are Wythoff's pairs (1, 2), (3, 5), (4, 7), … with differences 1, 2, 3. A mathematician would enjoy it: a real theorem whose proof a 4–5 child can give with counters. K–1 P1–P7, 2–3 P1–P6 and 4–5 P1–P7 carry it.

## App fit

Confirm A; shipped, size S. Pebble Duel's 12 puzzles ask for a win from a first-player start (2–3 P6, 4–5 P6). The three duel proofs ask who should start, then require three wins in a row from new starts against perfect play: the worksheet's central question (K–1 P5, P8; 2–3 P4, P7; 4–5 P3) with a better certificate than a circled box. Against the worksheet:

- How to play on each of the 12 puzzles has a folded "Explore … bundles" panel: "A balanced position has an even count in every column", with a live table of each pile (`nim.help`, dist/deduction.js). Duel 2's hint says "If every bundle size appears an even number of times, let me start." Both hand over 4–5 P5's discovery, which the streak should certify. Earn the table after Duel 1, drop the rule sentence, and point hints at the child's lost starts.
- Titles name the move: "Leave the pair", "Build a mirror", "Remove a whole component", "Break the eight bundle". Title by start.
- The main puzzles never ask who should start, and their losing opponent moves at random; the who-starts duels sit in Proofs, off the road. Put the two-pile duel early in the family.
- Missing: the rook board, the K–1 and 2–3 core. A board group on the duel engine: tap a square in the token's row or column, choose who starts, three wins from new squares, up to 8-by-8 (K–1 P1–P4, 2–3 P1–P3); a queen board with the same streak (4–5 P8–P9). A find-every-start group (2–3 P8–P9, 4–5 P4): mark the second-player starts and declare done with no count shown; the app plays the side a wrong mark gives away, and wins. Showing board and piles together would give away 2–3 P5.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** In the app as Pebble Duel and its three duel proofs. Wave 9's touch-ups of in-app families add the two groups this card proposes on the duel engine: a rook board (tap a square in the token's row or column, choose who starts, three wins from new squares, up to 8-by-8), with a queen board on the same streak, and a find-every-start group where the child marks the second-player starts and claims done with no count shown. The same pass folds the bundle table away until after Duel 1, drops the rule sentence from How to play and Duel 2's hint, retitles puzzles by their start rather than their winning move, and puts the two-pile duel early in the family. Tracked in the app's [decisions.md](https://github.com/jamesrp/small-math-adventure/blob/main/docs/plan/decisions.md).
