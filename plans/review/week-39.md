## The mathematics

A trip is an ordered list of road steps on a fixed map; the only move deletes two adjacent steps along one road in opposite directions. Every trip shortens to one final form whatever the order of deletions. On a tree every trip home vanishes; on a ring a shortened trip home is whole circuits; on two rings joined at H, ab and ba differ, and aba⁻¹b⁻¹ survives although each ring is circled once each way. This is the free group of a graph, done with a pawn, and a mathematician would enjoy it. K–1 P1–2 and 2–3 P1–3 carry tree against ring, 2–3 P4 and 4–5 P4 carry unique shortening, and the older P5–6 carry order.

## Fix

1. **should**, K–1 p.6 P7: "Can two of your remaining trips visit exactly the same roads in a different order?" ABCDABC and ADCBADC use the same four roads, but each crosses two roads twice, and different ones, so a child comparing tiles can rightly say no. The p.3 key answers yes and adds "They do not have the same directed-edge multiset; the question only asks for the same roads." Fix: "Can two of your remaining trips use the same four roads?" and add to the key: "Accept no with the reason that the tiles differ."
2. **should**, 4–5 p.3 P4: "Shorten each recorded trip in different orders." A→B→C→D→A→B→C→B→A has only one deletion order (math.md item 2). Fix: use A→B→C→B→C→D→A→B→A, which has three first choices and four orders and still ends at A→B→C→D→A. Update the p.5 key.
3. **should**, 4–5 p.4 P6: "Make another trip with those same counts and a different result" is answered by the first example a child tries. Fix: "Which orders of the four whole-ring trips disappear?" Of the 24 orders, 16 disappear; the 8 survivors are exactly the orders that alternate left and right (checked).
4. **should**, 2–3 p.4 P5: neither journey has a reversal, so one look settles it. Fix: ask for all six orders of left forward, right forward and left backward. Four shorten to H→C→D→H; the other two survive and differ (checked).

## App fit

Fit B, confirmed. A child taps roads to move a pawn, and each step joins a strip; tapping a turning point cancels the out-and-back pair around it and keeps the rest in order. Cancelling order never matters, so building trips is the puzzle. Solves the app can check by stack reduction: a trip of a given length that shortens to a given route; every shortened result of eight-step ring trips home, with the child declaring done and no answer slots shown; a two-ring trip that circles each ring once each way and loses no steps. The shortened form is the certificate: two trips match exactly when their shortened forms do. On a tree, the forced turn-back at the farthest stop shows that no trip home survives. Pitfalls: never shorten automatically or show a live shortened route, never cancel far-apart pairs, keep each step tied to its road, and skip "predict the result" levels. Draw on K–1 P1–2 and P6, 2–3 P2, P3 and P6, 4–5 P5–6, and the bonus's fewest-new-roads puzzle.
