## The mathematics

Each strip's length lies somewhere in a band. A join ends in [L+V, U+W], a gap in [L−W, U−V], and every endpoint occurs, so a partner can defeat any narrower promise (P1–P2, older P6). Widths add: the widest safe card for B is the target less A's width (P3), and uncovering the wider strip helps most (older P4). A repeated piece cancels: A+4 against the same A+1 is exactly 3, against a separate B+1 it is 1 to 5 (K–1 P4, older P5); a fixed total or removing the same A tightens the naive range (4–5 P8–P9). This is interval arithmetic and its dependency problem; a mathematician would enjoy the design and dependency tasks more than the sum rule, which is one fact.

## Fix

1. **must**, all bands, guide p. 1: "two sliding paper strips with opaque sleeves or a cover card, and a working ruler." No lengths, count or construction, and nothing prints strips; a sleeve hiding A's end would also hide where B starts. The pages require building (K–1 P1 "Make the shortest and longest joins with these strips"), so a parent volunteer would have to design the kit, as on the Week 23 card. Fix: a print-and-cut page per pair of 1 cm strips at 10 mm per unit, two of each whole length 1 to 15 plus the 4 and 1 add-ons (one Letter page; every key's extremes are whole). In the guide, drop the sleeves: check a strip's end against its printed band, then join strips on a 0–16 ruler. Cuisenaire rods also fit. Count cutting in the preparation.
2. **must**, K–1 p. 4, P4: "keep A unchanged within each comparison; B may vary separately." Heard once, this holds A still in the bottom picture; at the drawn A = 5 the gap runs 2 to 4, while the key's 1 and 5 need A = 4 and A = 6 (math check, problem 2). The parent volunteer, holding the key, would mark a child who obeyed wrong. Fix: "Both A's in the top picture are the same length. Each try may use new lengths."
3. **should**, K–1 P4 before P5–P6, older P5 before P6–P7: the bottom comparison needs the cross pairing (longest less shortest) that the next page meets alone. Fix: swap the pages and renumber.
4. **should**, older p. 5, P7: "Someone subtracts the two middle values and announces one exact gap." P6's witnesses, gaps 1 and 5, already answer it. Fix: "Make the widest card for the short strip so the gap always stays between 2 and 5. Explain why it cannot be wider." (10 to 11.)
5. **should**, K–1 P7, 2–3 P8: three boards for exactly three answers give away the count, and with A from 4 to 6 every A works. Fix: A from 3 to 7 (3 and 7 fail; answers unchanged) and four boards.
6. **should**, p. 1, all bands: the launch draws the shortest (2+1) and longest (3+2) joins beside "A + B: 3 to 5 units", P1's method, so older P1 is copying; both joins also start 2 mm right of the ruler's 0 (math check, problem 3). Fix: draw one join from 0 (A 2, B 2, ending at 4), and have the guide's launch ask for a shorter and a longer one.
7. **should**, K–1 p. 3, P3: "fits within 9", with no line saying 9 counts; B 4 to 5 ends at 9 (math check, problem 1). Fix: add "Fitting within 9 includes ending at 9.", as on p. 2.
8. **should**, guide p. 1, "Flexible hour": "5-22 joins … 55-60 reset" plans fifty minutes at the tables against context.md's 35 to 40, and "human strips" is never explained. Fix: re-time; drop or describe the human strips.
9. **could**, K–1 P6: one ruler for two ways; print two.
10. **could**, p. 4, all bands: cut "One possible setting;".
11. **could**, 2–3 P1 and P6: "the shortest and longest it can be" for "tightest guaranteed range".

I agree with all three math-check findings; I rate its problem 2 a must.

## App fit

B, size S, confirmed; "thin for a family" is fair, so a group of six to eight, sharing a strips-on-a-ruler board with Week 29's rods. The child drags each strip's end along its band and joins strips; the ruler shows where the join ends. Solves:
- break a claim ("always reaches 8 and fits within 10") with lengths that land outside, or declare "Can't break it" (P2);
- drag B's card ends to the widest safe card; the app shows any setting that escapes (P3, fix 4);
- one A drawn twice moves as one: make the biggest and smallest gap, then with a separate B (P5) or a total locked at 12 (4–5 P8).

Certificates: both extreme settings inside, for "can't break it"; the two settings that pin each card end, for "widest". Pitfalls: typing or predicting ranges (endpoint arithmetic, the less-liked predict mode), a running sum, answer slots in the fill-9 list. Draw on K–1 P2–P4, older P2–P3 and P5, 4–5 P8–P9.

**Decision, October 10, 2026.** Port, in Wave 7 (sets and numbers), as a short group of six to eight puzzles on a strips-on-a-ruler board, where each strip's end drags only inside its band so the screen enforces the rule: break the app's claim or declare "Can't break it", widen a card while the app looks for a setting that escapes, and one A drawn twice that moves as one. No typed or predicted ranges and no running sum. The revise is for the worksheet's missing strips and doesn't hold up the port.
