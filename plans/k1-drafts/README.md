What each set of pages is about, and what the reviews changed:

- **Week 57: halves and outline dots.** For a shape of squares and halves with no dot inside, the outline has two more dots than the shape has halves, and each dot closed inside takes the place of two outline dots (Pick's theorem counted in halves). Three pieces can never close in a dot, and four halves can, as the tilted square. After review, the joining rule says pieces join along whole sides into one shape with no gap inside, with a pictured yes and no, since pieces joined at a corner break the counts; a picture shows that a square counts as two halves; the problem that counts shapes closing in a dot says a turned shape is the same shape and now comes before the two-star problem; and the three-piece question asks both ways ("If they can, make one. If they can't, explain why.").
- **Week 66: fewest moves on a seven-lamp street.** The fewest moves is one flip for each lit lamp plus the shortest walk that visits them, some targets are harder than every target one move away (a dead end), and every way of building a target has the same evenness. After review, the partner points before the flip, the pages say "all lamps off" instead of "dark street", the record squares are larger, Problem 5 has a dot-and-arrow record with a small example, and Problem 8 is reworded.
- **Week 81: pairs and copying.** The second player can always win exactly when every bucket holds an even number, by copying the partner's move, and every game ends, with its shortest and longest lengths found by working together. After review, page 1's pictures show that refilling is optional (the draft's pictures read as "must"), the bucket-3 rule is in the shared rules, the answer rows and slots no longer hint at the number of answers, and the packet ends with "Could a game from this start go on forever?".
- **Week 87: circles of majorities and knockouts.** With the cards ABC, BCA and CAB, A beats B, B beats C and C beats A; who waits decides a knockout, and a letter wins every knockout exactly when it beats both others. After review, Problem 2 states the knockout rule with a worked example on the cards D, E and F, the build problems ask for C and then B, so no earlier printed card set answers them, and Problems 7 and 8 say "1st", "build" and "or use the cards to show why you can't".
- **Week 91: a switch takes turns, and a tree shares fairly.** One switch sends counters to alternate exits from any start, the four-exit tree shares any number of counters fairly from any start, and each of the 8 starts gives a different order. After review, the shared rules have children copy the starting arrows with spare cards before the first counter, since the cards flip and a start drawn afterwards is wrong; the coin question asks about 5 counters on one exit and 2 on the other, which most pairs will see; 8- and 12-counter sharing pictures were added; and Problem 5 has enough trees for all 8 starts.
- **Week 97: square leftovers.** Squares taken away in groups leave only some leftovers (in groups of 3, never 2), one leftover can hide several squares, and every odd square leaves 1 in groups of 8, built ring by ring. After review, the take-away rule names the leftover correctly, the worked strip shows the take-away repeating, the board moved to its own page at the back so a stapled packet works, the partner game comes second and returns with groups of 6 and 8, and groups of 2 were added. These three new problems were checked by the writer's script but not by a second reviewer.
- **Week 98: fill or stuck.** An n-by-n board needs n seeds, a slanting line always fills, and a start where every seed is needed need not be the fewest. After review, the rule says "two or more" neighbours (read as "exactly two", several boards stick), the rule pictures no longer show an answer, the printed starts to grow are Problem 2, and the seed-removal problem says to put each seed back before testing the next.

Student pages are unchanged. Workarounds and operating limits are recorded in full in each revision report:

- **57:** the miniature example is not a 20 mm template, pieces obscure dots and hand-cut cracks are not geometric evidence. Trace and reuse pieces rather than filling all boards simultaneously.
- **66:** Problem 5's example begins an answer and blank rows do not promise a number of solutions. Towers/record squares are records, not a quota or a mathematical bound; reuse cubes where four towers would exceed the kit.
- **81:** recording and turn order sometimes need adult reading/scribing; test the 11-counter setup in one printed bucket. The guide distinguishes optional refills and complete endings from play that stops early.
- **87:** temporary D/E/F launch labels are adult-made; optional printed cards can reveal information when reused. Keep preferences fixed throughout a vote/knockout and separate the two pairs' records.
- **91:** copy the start before cards flip, reset after slips using saved cards, and treat spare trees as workspace. Problem 7's corrected explanation is conditional on the starting arrows, not a false general ordering rule.
- **97:** pair sharing needs one shared board and concealed input/group cards, with separate records; two simultaneous 6×6 builds exceed 64 tiles. The small pictures are build specifications, not fitting mats. Follow the guide's launch split even while the old companion remains unchanged.
- **98:** grow with two or more side neighbors, distinguish running out of counters from being stuck, and restore each removed seed before the next test. Record guesses and ending states without requiring a duplicate running sketch.

## Rebuilding

Each `src/` folder rebuilds `k-1.pdf` with Python 3 and pdfLaTeX (XeLaTeX for Week 57), run inside `src/`; compile twice:

- Week 57: `xelatex k-1.tex`; this one needs XeLaTeX for its font. `python3 analyze.py` and `python3 pieces_enum.py` re-check the counts by enumerating every allowed shape.
- Week 66: `python3 pages.py`, then `pdflatex k-1.tex`. `python3 check.py` re-checks every count.
- Week 81: `python3 build.py`, then `pdflatex k-1.tex`. `python3 check.py` prints the solved answers.
- Week 87: `pdflatex k-1.tex`. `python3 verify.py` re-checks every vote, knockout and build.
- Week 91: `python3 build.py`, then `pdflatex k-1.tex`. `python3 check.py` simulates every answer.
- Week 97: `python3 gen.py`, then `pdflatex k-1.tex`. `python3 check.py` recomputes every leftover.
- Week 98: `python3 gen.py`, then `pdflatex k-1.tex`. `python3 check.py` enumerates every board.

On October 25 each draft rebuilt from a clean copy of its `src/`, with text identical to the committed PDF, and each checker ran without error.

To rebuild a section, run `sh guide-src/build.sh` from its draft folder (or `sh build.sh` inside `guide-src/`). It writes `../k1-guide.pdf`, rejects LaTeX errors/overfull boxes and ends with its checker reading the unchanged `k-1.pdf` beside `guide-src/`. Dependencies: Python 3, pdfLaTeX with the packages listed in `guide-src/README.md`, and Poppler; Week 81 also uses PyMuPDF. Copy the whole draft folder outside the repository to test portability. All seven packaged builds passed, with PDF bytes and extracted text identical to the pinned revision outputs; see [verification](../guide-packaging-verification.json).
