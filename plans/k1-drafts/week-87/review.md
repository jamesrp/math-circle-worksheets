# Review of draft W87-K-v1 (Week 87, Voting and agendas, K–1)

Reviewed: `draft/k-1.pdf` (5 pages, identical to `draft/src/k-1.pdf`), rendered at 90 and 200 dpi (`review-render/`), against `PROMPT.md`. I also compared it with the existing shared packet W87-S-v2, the materials W87-M-v1 p. 4 and the guide W87-F-v2 p. 1, to check overlap with the older bands and that it matches the launch. I checked the mathematics independently in `review-check.py`; I did not reuse the author's `verify.py`. The build log is clean: no overfull boxes or warnings, and fonts are embedded.

## Verdict

This is a sound draft. Header, footer, numbering and register all meet the standard. No titles, encouragement, hints, exclamation marks, dashes or Name/Date fields appear. Every vote, knockout and build claim is correct. The order follows the outline: pair votes, then prescribed knockouts on the circle, then two single-winner contrasts, then kernel 3 late. P3 and P4 make a real contrast, not a repeat. In P3, C is first on two cards and wins. In P4, each letter is first on exactly one card, yet A wins. That sets up P7.

Six things need fixing before the organizer sees it, in order of importance:

1. In P7–P8, "on top of" is ambiguous with physical slips.
2. P7–P8 name no action and give the children no way to respond.
3. P3's printed set already answers P6.
4. The knockout rule says letters vote, which contradicts the sentence before it.
5. The knockout has no worked visual on the page.
6. Smaller wording and layout items, listed at the end.

## Must fix

### 1. P7 and P8: "on top of" means something else when slips are being placed on cards (p. 5)

> Problem 7: Can C win, whoever waits, if C is on top of just one card?
> Problem 8: Can C win, whoever waits, if C is not on top of any card?

In these problems the children are putting letter slips onto blank cards. For a five-year-old, "C is on top of a card" most naturally means a C slip lying on the card, which is true of every slip they place. Heard once, the question can lose its meaning entirely. The cards already name the rows 1st/2nd/3rd, and the opening rule uses "higher up".

Fix: use the card's own word, for example "with C 1st on just one card" and "with no C in the 1st row" (or "with C never 1st").

### 2. P7 and P8 name no objects or action, and give no way to answer (p. 5)

Both are bare yes/no questions. They do not say "build", and they depend on P6's rule ("one A, one B and one C on each card") being carried over. K–1 children cannot write, but each problem leaves about 28 mm of blank space that looks like it wants writing. The outline says K–1 explanations are "shown with the cards ... not written". P8's whole point is impossibility, the case where the standard says an explanation should be asked for. The page never invites it.

Fix, with the letter change from item 3:

- P7: "Build three cards with B 1st on just one card. Can B win, no matter which letter waits?"
- P8: "Build three cards with no B in the 1st row. Can B win, no matter which letter waits? If it can't, show why with the cards."

Either remove the blank space or turn it into something an adult can use, such as three small empty card frames (V label and three rows) where the adult writes down a set the child found. The brief allows "the adult writing anything else".

### 3. P3's printed cards already answer P6 (pp. 3 and 5)

P6 asks: "Build three cards ... Make C win, whoever waits."

P3's printed set (V1 CBA, V2 BAC, V3 CBA) is already a set on which C wins every knockout: C beats B 2–1 and C beats A 2–1 (checked). A child who remembers P3 can copy it onto blank cards. The brief's physical constraints rule that out ("Children never copy a printed set onto blank cards"), and copying skips the thinking the build is meant to provoke.

The letter targets have to be chosen together:

- P4's winner is A, and P4 is itself the A-making swap V2 BCA→BAC.
- P5's result leaves a B-winning set on the table.

Fix: make P5's target C and P6–P8's target B. All of the following were checked:

- P5 with C still has 3 working swaps out of 9: V1 ABC→ACB, V1 ABC→CBA, V2 BCA→CBA. P4 still models the swap effect without giving P5's answer away.
- None of P3, P4 or the three P5-with-C results makes B win, so P6 must be built fresh.
- P7 with B: yes, but only 2 of the 20 card sets with B 1st on exactly one card work: {ABC, BAC, CBA} and {ABC, BCA, CBA}. P4 (A 1st once, A wins) still models it by analogy. A P5-with-C result does not hand it over.
- P8 with B: no. 0 of 20 sets work.

Keeping P7–P8 on C after this change would not work. P5's swap V1→ACB gives ACB, BCA, CAB, with C 1st on one card and C winning, which would answer P7 directly.

### 4. The knockout rule says letters vote, right after saying only cards vote (p. 1)

> Each card is one voter, and only the cards vote. ... In a knockout, two letters vote first.

A literal-minded child hears that the letters vote, one sentence after hearing that only the cards do.

Fix: "In a knockout, the cards vote between two letters first. Then they vote between the winner and the letter that waited."

## Should fix

### 5. The knockout has no worked visual, and its rule comes before Problem 1, where it is not used (pp. 1–2)

The pair vote gets a good worked visual: input cards DFE, EFD, FED, then tokens in the wells, then the circled E, with labels matching P1. The knockout gets only words, read aloud before P1 and about ten minutes before P2 needs them.

The knockout is the most complicated action in the packet: two votes on a mat whose labels are words K–1 cannot read. The shared packet gives the older bands a picture of it (W87-S-v2 p. 2: D and E first, F waiting, E wins, then F beats E). Page 2 of this draft has about 60 mm of free space below P2.

Fix: put a compact knockout picture with P2, drawn with the launch cards and matching the shared launch. Show First pair D E and Waiting F, then E wins, then E against F, then F as Winner, using the mat's own words "First pair", "Waiting" and "Winner". It reveals nothing about the circle, because F beats both others on those cards. If the visual goes in, the knockout sentence can also move down into P2, so it is heard when it is used.

### 6. Rebalance page 5 (pp. 2–5)

Page 5 carries four problems, including the two deepest, as lines of text. Pages 2, 3 and 4 each end with about 60–85 mm of empty space. Moving P7–P8, or P6–P8, to a sixth page would make room for the adult-recording frames in item 2. The packet would also stop looking as if the kernel-3 work is an afterthought. The brief's "four or more pages" is met either way.

## Wording and smaller items

- **"whoever waits" (P5–P8).** The phrase is compressed for one hearing, and "whoever" suggests people. Use "no matter which letter waits".
- **P3/P4 "With these voters in place of your cards".** This mixes "voters" and "cards". The cut V1–V3 cards on the table carry the same labels with different rankings. Plainer: "Use these cards instead of yours."
- **P5 "on one of cards V1, V2 and V3".** This is stiff. "Swap two letters on one of these cards so that C wins, no matter which letter waits." The sentence about building the card from a blank card and slips is needed; keep it.
- **P2's pictured mat boxes are smaller than the real tiles.** The pictures are at 70% scale: First pair 42.7 × 27.3 mm, Waiting 23.1 × 27.3 mm. A 28 mm tile does not fit, and the brief asks for printed tile boxes of at least 31 mm. The page says to use the mat, but young children put tiles on whatever looks like a tile place. Either drop the box outlines and keep the small tile pictures with their labels, or accept the risk knowingly.
- **P2 prescribes every agenda.** Each winner is simply the printed Waiting letter. This is acceptable: it is the K–1 first contact with the mat, it stays clear of shared P2, and P3/P4 then break the "waiting letter wins" pattern, which is a good contrast. No change requested. The guide should tell the adult to let children notice the pattern rather than point it out.
- **Launch picture.** The tally mat is drawn without its Tie well and token spots, and the ring around E cuts across the tile's corners (radius 8 mm against a half-diagonal of about 8.3 mm). Both are cosmetic. Leaving out the Tie well is defensible because card votes never use it.
- **P1 and the circle.** P1 records the three pair winners but never asks whether any letter beats both. That is correct, since the outline forbids reprinting shared P1's question. The guide should carry the "Is there a letter that beat both?" conversation for K–1.

## Unpiloted concerns (hypotheses, not required changes)

- **Bookkeeping in P3/P4.** Finding which letters can win means up to three knockouts: six votes, eighteen token placements, two mats and duplicate tiles, with nothing recording which waiting letters have been tried. K–1 pairs may lose track. A light adult record or a rule such as "leave the winner's tile on the Winner box" may be enough. Watch for it in a pilot before printing anything for it.
- **Staffing.** The one parent volunteer reads every card and checks every vote for two pairs at once. P1–P4 alone add up to about 21 votes per pair, roughly 63 token placements. The guide should name a first route, for example P1–P3, and treat P4 onward as readiness-dependent.
