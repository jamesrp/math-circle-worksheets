Week 15: Nearest sites: new distances and reversed questions

Mathematical kernels

1. Taxi distance is the number of horizontal/vertical unit grid steps, |dx|+|dy|; straight-line distance is a different rule. For A=(0,0), B=(2,2), every point in the quadrant x≥2,y≤0 is tied, as is the opposite quadrant. The tie set can have area. The closed A region need not be convex: (4,0) and (0,4) tie, while their midpoint (2,2) is B itself. A small worked route on a non-task grid must show counted horizontal+vertical steps before first use.

2. Farthest-site regions reverse the comparison: assign every point to the site(s) at greatest straight-line distance. Each pairwise comparison is still a half-plane, so a farthest cell is convex but can be empty. For square-corner sites (±2,±2), the origin added as E has no farthest region: average squared corner distance from (x,y) is x²+y²+8, strictly greater than its squared distance to E. Corner cells are opposite quadrants. Children can compare strings and reason by symmetry without formulas. Empty farthest cells differ sharply from nearest cells, which always include their own distinct site.

3. Largest-clearance location: in the bounded square [-2,2]² with its four corners as existing sites, maximize the distance to the nearest existing site. The unique optimum is the center, distance sqrt(8); divide the square into quadrants and compare with that quadrant corner. If a center site is then added, the four edge midpoints are precisely the new optimum locations, nearest distance 2. In each quadrant, either the center or the quadrant corner is within 2, with equality only at its adjacent edge midpoints (check by the separating diagonal and elementary squared lengths). This is an optimization for the worst nearby site, rather than simply drawing insertion regions.
