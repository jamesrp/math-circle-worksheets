Week 16: Three-color meshes: a fourth-label loophole, boundary parity and signed counts

Mathematical kernels

1. A fourth label G is permitted only at interior vertices; the usual R/B/Y corner and side restrictions remain. A specifically RBY triangle means its three corners use R,B,Y; an any-three triangle has three different labels, possibly G. Four labels allow zero RBY triangles. On a star with original R/B/Y corners and one interior G, all three cells use three labels but none is RBY. Give a more substantial free three-step mesh so children choose six boundary labels and the interior label, seek zero RBY, then minimize any-three cells. Among 256 legal four-label three-step fillings, 27 have no RBY; 18 of those have three any-three cells and nine have five. Witness bottom-to-top rows RRBB/RGB/YY/Y has zero RBY and exactly one RBG, RYG, BYG. For any edge-to-edge Sperner triangle with no RBY, at least three any-three cells are necessary. Elementary explanation: save the original; temporarily recolor every G to R and use ordinary Sperner to find an original GBY cell. Restore, then G→B gives original RGY; restore, G→Y gives original RBG. These are three different types. Deeper continuation: each type occurs oddly by the corresponding RB,RY,BY boundary-door parity. Do not print proofs/counts or supply the main witness. Contrast with original three-label impossibility; no formal topology prerequisites. P2 and P3 reset to only R/B/Y, with G forbidden.

2. For any triangulated disk, with arbitrary fixed R/B/Y boundary labels, the number of rainbow cells has the same parity as the number of boundary R-B edges. Interior doors count twice. A square split by a diagonal is enough to test a general boundary rule: R,B,R,B around the square has four boundary doors and no rainbow cell; R,B,Y,Y has one boundary door and one rainbow cell. Changing a boundary pattern can force even rather than odd totals; the standard triangular side rules are a special case. No hole or T-junction is allowed.

3. Orient every cell counterclockwise. A rainbow is positive when its labels read R-B-Y up to cyclic rotation, negative when they read R-Y-B. Under the usual Sperner triangular boundary rules with outer corners R,B,Y counterclockwise, positive count minus negative count is exactly 1. Count signed R→B boundary edges minus B→R edges: interior edge contributions cancel, two-color cells cancel internally, rainbow cells contribute +1/-1, and the permitted outer RB side telescopes to +1. Experimentally children can make pairs of opposite signs while one positive remains. Before first use show two labeled non-task triangles and the counterclockwise reading convention; do not print the signed theorem in the student task.
