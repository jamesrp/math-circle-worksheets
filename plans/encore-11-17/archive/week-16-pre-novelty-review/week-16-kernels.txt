Refine one small triangle by inserting one interior dot and joining it to all three old corners. If the old triangle has all three labels, every label for the inserted dot replaces it with exactly one rainbow triangle. If the old triangle has exactly two labels, using the missing label makes two rainbow triangles; using an old label makes none. A monochrome triangle always produces none. Thus this local refinement preserves the parity of the total and can change it by two. This is a concrete transformation, not another statement of arbitrary-mesh Sperner.

For any triangulated disk, with arbitrary fixed R/B/Y boundary labels, the number of rainbow cells has the same parity as the number of boundary R-B edges. Interior doors count twice. A square split by a diagonal is enough to test a general boundary rule: R,B,R,B around the square has four boundary doors and no rainbow cell; R,B,Y,Y has one boundary door and one rainbow cell. Changing a boundary pattern can force even rather than odd totals; the standard triangular side rules are a special case. No hole or T-junction is allowed.

Orient every cell counterclockwise. A rainbow is positive when its labels read R-B-Y up to cyclic rotation, negative when they read R-Y-B. Under the usual Sperner triangular boundary rules with outer corners R,B,Y counterclockwise, positive count minus negative count is exactly 1. Count signed R→B boundary edges minus B→R edges: interior edge contributions cancel, two-color cells cancel internally, rainbow cells contribute +1/-1, and the permitted outer RB side telescopes to +1. Experimentally children can make pairs of opposite signs while one positive remains. Before first use show two labeled non-task triangles and the counterclockwise reading convention; do not print the signed theorem in the student task.
