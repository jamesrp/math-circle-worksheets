# Teaching sources and design decisions

## Sources actually consulted

*Math Circle by the Bay*, preface printed pp. viii–x (PDF pp. 9–11), was read from the authorized retained reference archive. Its SHA-256 matches the public resource manifest: `84e0277cd94b1e501d153834b7123a72958f8c01b806e7bdfcca012a3191e427`.

The authors describe themes with deep mathematical context and naturally arising connections (viii), independent problem solving and manipulatives (ix), and wide variation in pace with substantial themes extending over years (x). They also say they rarely repeat a topic for the same children (ix). Our choice to label Week 92 as a different investigation of a familiar theme is our curriculum decision, not a claim that this source recommends repeated encore sessions.

The organizer's *Pattern Block Exercises – Google Docs*, PDF p. 1, begins with five minutes of free handling and then work in groups of two or three. Its construction questions permit multiple examples and comparisons. This is direct source evidence for a concrete launch and independent exploration, not evidence that any of the new activities have been tested.

## Applications to this batch

These are design inferences: show an unfamiliar operation with input, meaningful intermediate state and output; make the physical action recoverable; let the children choose examples or organize cases; provide prepared cards when transcription or fractions would dominate. Offer a manageable first route and preserve deeper mathematics for return visits. Prerequisites take priority over grade labels. Adult reading/recording support is appropriate, but a route that needs an adult to operate every mathematical decision should not be described as independent child work.

Physical handling and preparation must be rehearsed before classroom use. The released verification records will distinguish digital checks from this unperformed rehearsal.
