## Precise kernels to preserve

- **92:** On `[0,16]`, `T(x)=2 min(x,16-x)`. For an interval crossing 8, include the turning-point image, not just the endpoint images. For example `[7,9] → [14,16] → [0,4] → [0,8] → [0,16]`. Each dyadic branch interval of width `16/2^n` maps onto the whole track after `n` steps. It is false that every pair of nearby points separates.
- **93:** If `0<a≤b`, `A=ab`, `m=(a+b)/2`, and `h=A/m`, then `a≤h≤sqrt(A)≤m≤b`. The new gap is `(b-a)^2/[2(a+b)]`, strictly less than half the old positive gap. The area model and exact arithmetic differ from physical measurement/rounding.
- **94:** In `y=x+b`, repeated readings alone cannot separate `x` from `b`, whereas differences cancel `b` and one known standard identifies it. In `y=a+bx`, two distinct known standards identify both parameters. With independent bounded reading errors `±e`, the feasible slope interval from two standards has width `4e/|x2-x1|`. This is a worst-case bound under a stated affine model, not a universal claim about instruments.
- **95:** With `M` marked individuals and a uniform second sample of `n` distinct individuals from a closed population of size `N`, the overlap `R` has expectation `Mn/N`. The observed overlap only guarantees `N≥M+n-R`; it gives no deterministic finite upper bound. `Mn/R` for positive `R` is an estimate, not an identity or an unbiased estimator. At fixed `M+n`, balancing the two sample sizes maximizes expected overlap for fixed `N` when feasible. Mixing/catchability assumptions matter.
- **96:** For `n=product p_i^e_i`, every divisor corresponds uniquely to a choice of `0..e_i` copies of each prime, giving `product(e_i+1)` divisors. The empty bag means 1. Experiments with splitting trees do not prove unique factorization. Least numbers with 6, 8 and 12 divisors are 12, 24 and 60.
- **97:** For an odd prime `p`, equal square remainders imply `x≡y` or `x≡−y` because `p` divides `(x−y)(x+y)`. Thus `(p−1)/2` nonzero remainders are squares. Zero remains included in student output inventories. Composites break the prime conclusion: modulo 8 four inputs square to 1; modulo 9 three inputs square to 0; modulo 15 four inputs square to 1.
