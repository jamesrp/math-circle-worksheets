## Findings, in priority order within this range

No opening in this range showed a high-confidence blocker comparable to the missing day-by-day construction reported for Week 83. The following are narrower improvements, not reasons to remove the investigations or their deeper mathematics. They are reviewer judgments, not observations of children using the packets.

### 1. Week 35, Grades 2–3 and 4–5, page 1: the worked visual teaches the next problem's action

Both openings show an F-shaped footprint, then “flip over the line,” then “slide right.” Immediately below, Problem 1 asks children to build two repeating borders with different smallest matching **slides**. The glide, which combines flip and slide, is not requested or named until Problem 2 on page 2. A child using the picture as the procedure for the current task can reasonably include a flip while searching for a shortest slide. The visual also follows only one footprint, so it does not establish the current goal of matching an entire repeating border.

Minimal fix: move the existing flip-then-slide visual to page 2 immediately before Problem 2. The K–1 packet already follows this sensible sequence: slide exploration on pages 1–2, then the same visual immediately before the first glide task on page 3. The planned physical demonstration of a repeating row matching after one slide and failing after another is sufficient for the opening; no new printable is necessary. If a compact picture is desired on page 1, use a repeated row with a fixed original and moved tracing copy, not an isolated glide. Do not supply a solved glide-border construction.

This is a medium-priority sequencing concern. It affects two base PDFs, not the K–1 or bonus packet. I viewed pages 1–2 of both affected PDFs and pages 1–3 of K–1. The guide's common launch already says to introduce the single-motif flip-then-slide visual only before the first glide task; the upper page placement is what does not follow that sequence.

### 2. Week 27, Grades 4–5, page 1: stability has no concrete reference check in this band

The upper opening states that no two letters in different pairs should both prefer one another to their current partners, then asks for all stable pairings. The child must coordinate a proposed pairing and two preference lists, preserving one reference state. Unlike the K–1 and Grades 2–3 openings, it has no worked check showing the current partners, one proposed cross-pair, and the two separate yes/no comparisons. A plausible misunderstanding is to reject a pairing because one object wants to switch, or to update a pair after the first comparison before checking the second. Page 2's useful hold/release example introduces the later request algorithm; it does not illustrate the first problem's static two-sided test.

Minimal fix if improving packet independence: reuse a compact form of the existing younger-band P/U check at the upper opening, retaining both the yes/yes and yes/no cases and the unchanged current pairing. No new worksheet or broader simplification is needed. Alternatively, explicitly use the existing two-sided common launch and leave a small table example visible. That launch already demonstrates the exact distinction, so this is a moderate printed-reference gap, not a claim that the planned lesson cannot work. Upper-band reading ability alone does not guarantee retaining the simultaneous test.

I viewed pages 1–2 of all three bands. The younger openings are positive examples: they show both checks against the same current pairs and say that a failed candidate check concerns only that candidate. The bonus also includes a compact blocking-pair example and is explicitly intended after the blocking-pair readiness gate.

The planned finger-through-seam demonstration is still necessary, but that is normal material handling, not a missing mathematical prerequisite.

- Week 37, base openings: the labeled rigid-model before/after turn supports a partner matching game. Seeing and handling the actual model resolves the perspective drawing; no new flat-paper procedure is needed.

## Scope and uncertainty

This is an introduction audit, not a full mathematical verification or a whole-packet pedagogical review. Every current opening in this range was visually inspected. Relevant later pages were inspected where needed to assess the transition: Week 27 all bands page 2; Week 28 Grades 2–3 pages 2–3; Week 35 K–1 pages 2–3 and other base bands page 2; Week 38 all base bands page 2. Every bonus-companion page was subsequently visually inspected, including all separate band entry points and new-convention introductions. Remaining later base-packet pages were not all visually inspected and should not be described as cleared. Text was extracted for navigation but was not substituted for visual review of the listed pages.

The 13 bonus packets are return visits with stated base-action prerequisites, not independent beginner versions of every theme. Their openings were assessed on that basis. There is no missing-grade-band penalty. Physical model construction, material fit, child comprehension, and classroom piloting were not tested. In particular, the Week 28 and Week 38 guides themselves retain physical-pretest caveats. No finding here establishes that any new revision would improve actual classroom outcomes until it is tried.
