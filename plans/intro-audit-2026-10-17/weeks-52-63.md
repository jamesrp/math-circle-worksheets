## Prioritized findings

No high-severity missing-action or missing-core-rule introduction was found in this range. The findings below are narrower than the Week 77–83 benchmark failures and should not trigger broad redesign.

1. **Week 61, grades 2–5, student p2, Problem 2 — mathematical validity rules crowd the concrete entry. Moderate readability/launch risk; not an observed classroom failure.** Before making a triangle with right-angle corners, children encounter “smaller convex region,” “strictly inside an open half of the ball,” shorter great-circle arcs, non-opposite corners and inside angles. In particular, “an open half” can be mistaken for the northern half already marked on the model; the desired octant triangle has two corners on that reference equator. The adult guide explicitly says some hemisphere is intended and assigns the convexity/hemisphere check to adults. Minimal fix: keep the substantive task and local-angle visual, and place the technical admissibility convention with adults or mark it as an adult check. Give children the operational rule about three shortest surface sides and using the small inside region. The existing physical demonstration of surface contact and local right-angle testing is sufficient; an additional finished three-right-angle triangle would prematurely answer the task. Upper p5–7 geometry remains worthwhile and is not a candidate for removal merely because it is deep.

## Launch-dependent point, not a required redesign

**Week 60, grades 3–5, student p3:** Each outcome card has one score field while the task compares Plans A and B. This is a deliberate reusable record, not an unspecified workflow: the guide’s Problem 4 instructions say to score every card for A, save its labeled total, clear the score lines, then score every card for B. The physical pretest explicitly rehearses saving the total and erasing/rescoring. An ordinary demonstration is sufficient. Separate A/B score fields could make case-by-case comparisons more convenient, but they are optional and not an actionable introduction repair. This assessment supersedes the initial concern about ambiguous score bookkeeping.

**Week 58, grades 2–5, student p1–2:** The neutral-counter visual correctly teaches divider → chooser → remainder. It does not show the central comparison using A’s preferences for both unchanged trays and then B’s preferences for both. A child reading only p1 may compare each owner’s personal total against the other owner’s total, rather than comparing two trays through one observer’s values. However, the guide’s shared launch explicitly slides A’s preference card between the same two trays, then repeats with B’s card. That is an adequate short demonstration, not adults operating the entire investigation. Classify the packet as clear with this planned launch. If pages are expected to circulate independently, the smallest useful addition is a two-observer check of one non-task allocation; do not add another printable or a long fairness definition. This is a portability suggestion, not a missing-prerequisite finding.

## Representative strengths

- **Week 53, grades 2–5 p1 and 4–5 p5:** Whole links, prices, counter payment and the retained connected record are shown together. A separate before → buy → return example later makes the unfamiliar exchange operation complete without solving the target maps.
- **Week 54, grades 2–5 p1, p3–4; grades 4–5 p7:** The same physical units survive strip ordering, joins, splits and row/column exchange. The examples show a meaningful intermediate action and a labeled result. These are strong examples of adding a representation only where it has a purpose.
- **Week 57, grades 3–5 p1–2:** Area is grounded by joining copies; inside and boundary counts are introduced afterward. The visual explicitly counts boundary dots between corners. The later holes task changes the convention at the point it becomes relevant.
- **Week 59, grades 2–5 p1; grades 3–5 p3 and p5:** Parallel supports and a perpendicular measurement define width through an action. Compass construction and point-to-edge distance each get their own small bridge. The task asks children to vary a concrete arrangement, not infer an action from a title.
- **Week 62, grades 2–5 p1; grades 4–5 p7:** The conflict graph becomes movable cards in numbered slots and then a record in the circles. The isolated activity is still placed. First-fit order is later shown through a partial and completed schedule, preserving the actual legal rule.
- **Week 63, grades 2–5 p1–2; grades 4–5 p7–8:** Fixed home labels remain distinct from moving cards. A complete outcome becomes a grouping object; later a placement becomes directed arrows, then two different removal operations. The arrows are not silently read in the opposite direction. Material cards support sorting rather than transcription.

## Limits and remaining uncertainty

All 12 weeks and all 16 current student/material files have opening coverage; no file in the range remains uninspected. Not every continuation page was visually reviewed: the exact viewed pages are above, and all student text was read. Material pages not viewed are explicitly identified in their rows. The four guides consulted for launch/readiness distinctions were Weeks 52, 58, 60 and 61; this is not an adult-guide audit.

No printing, cutting, frame assembly, compass handling, globe manipulation or classroom rehearsal was performed. In particular, the hinge kits, polyhedron nets, parallel-support measurements and sphere angle comparisons depend on prepared physical equipment. The current index itself labels the material unpiloted and says physical pretests remain unperformed. Concerns about children’s retention or vocabulary are audit hypotheses, not classroom observations. The report does not certify all mathematics, all page layouts or source-package reconstruction, which were outside this introduction-only task.
