# Weeks 64–75: independent introduction review

All twelve current shared student PDFs were rendered. Page numbers below are PDF page numbers. No grade bands are missing: these packets explicitly target Grades 3–5 or 4–5. Guides were consulted for launch and prerequisite claims, not substituted for student inspection.

## Prioritized concern

- **Week 69, Grades 4–5, page 2, introduction of gap (moderate).** The rule asks for the shortest distance to either of the *other two* colored sides, but its worked picture has one uncolored point and only one dark target side. It demonstrates ordinary point-to-side distance, not the new measurement. A child can successfully imitate that example while measuring to a selected farther side or forgetting that a shared dot has gap zero. The guide repeats the same single-side demonstration. Minimal fix: replace, rather than append to, the example with a small already-colored road triangle; mark one dot, show its distances to both eligible sides, and identify the smaller as its gap. A quick demonstration on this picture suffices; do not solve either investigation board or remove the arbitrary-size question.

## Demonstration watch item, not a required packet rewrite

- **Week 67, Grades 3–5, page 1.** The text carefully says the same meeting dot must pass all three pair tests. Its example and guide launch check only two homes. This is a reasonable one-pair convention example, but the transition to three simultaneous tests is a likely rescue point. During launch, keep one candidate fixed and ask children to check all three pairs on a small non-task map; if the organizer wants a printed reinforcement, replace the two-home vignette with that one complete check. This is a lower-confidence teaching hypothesis, not an established failure.

## Clear examples worth retaining

- **Week 64, page 1:** two matched X points and parallel heavy travel arrows distinguish edge correspondence from travel direction. Page 3 explicitly bridges to translated copies. The ruler/tracing-paper launch can demonstrate a slanted-edge transfer; no solved full loop is needed.
- **Week 65, pages 1–2:** move then turn is shown before grid exploration, and the curved-road version has its own start/arrival/left-turn sequence. Page 5 separately bridges a physical room move to its room-letter record.
- **Week 66, page 1:** R then F shows walker position, lamp state and move word together; targets include both position and lamps, with a large usable board and only one recoverable move record.
- **Week 68, pages 1–4:** blocker removes incident roads before the infinite-line question; ladder, plane and branching continuations each explicitly say what lies beyond the paper. No need to print the answer to the first component-counting question.
- **Week 70, page 1:** repeated shield location and matching single-room path show what a point shield means; page 2 clearly introduces room copies for longer shots.
- **Week 71, pages 1–2:** the same AD shot is shown folded and unfolded before bounce-word work. Rhombus comparison is a continuation with mirror checking, not an assumed earlier prerequisite.
- **Week 72, page 1:** EENE separates position from stored memory at every meaningful change. The physical memory strip is explicitly referenced. Page 2 does require signed arithmetic; the guide explicitly names it and keeps Problem 1 available. This is a readiness gate, not a missing K–1 version or a blanket reason to remove the mathematics.
- **Week 73, pages 1–2:** a ratio is grounded in old/new segments, and the midpoint mapping is explicitly shown before testing more points. The guide asks for a legal O placement and pair test during launch. The ruler precision limit remains untested physically.
- **Week 74, page 1:** URD has all four states and a counted move cost. The guide calls for a physical replay on the board, including odd coordinates. Although the dense unit dots invite adjacent-dot moves, the printed level step sizes and a normal demonstration establish the legal move; no new printable is warranted from this review.
- **Week 75, pages 1–3:** same-height seam transfer, repeated copies, and the graded shift of a twist are separately illustrated. Cutting, securing, and sliding yarn are genuine physical demands, explicitly unrehearsed in the guide. A paper-only audit cannot certify them.

## Limits

This was an introduction/representation audit, not a full solution or physical rehearsal. Selected later pages were viewed to test dependencies. Text of every page was read. Readiness and handling concerns above are hypotheses; all substantive mathematics should remain available for children ready for it.
