## What counts as depth here

Week 1 moves from physical tilings to invariants, a provably optimal packing, an exhaustive state graph, shortest paths, and deeper extensions. Weeks 2–10 follow the same standard:

1. **A concrete experiment.** Children move pieces, switch counters, build towers, make guesses, or trace paths before being asked to generalize.
2. **A question with a mathematical answer.** Which states are possible? Have we found every case? Is this the fewest moves or clues? Does a strategy work against every response?
3. **An explanation beyond trying examples.** Pair an optimal construction with a lower bound; explain why a list is exhaustive; distinguish observed repetition from a proof for all sizes. Spoken explanations and arrangements of objects count as proofs.
4. **A faithful connection.** Identify the exact undergraduate object or theorem instantiated by the activity, then cite a research continuation with its assumptions and limitations. An advanced label is not the student task. A classroom-sized computation is not evidence for a general research claim.
5. **A real next question.** Each extra investigation develops one new investigation at roughly grades 6–7 readiness. It is a reserve for a ready child, not a fourth simultaneous teaching group or a required end to the hour.

The new Week 1 extension sources became available during this work and were consulted. Their independent matching, distance, route-counting, and random-walk investigations reinforce the principle of offering one coherent further question, with a separately checked solution.

## What changed from the earlier fall proposal

Shape subtraction becomes pair toggling, making reversibility, reachability, and optimality precise. Secret alphabets become permutation machines; the next week's rings isolate the special structure of repeated shifts, so the two coding weeks ask different questions. Skyscrapers now distinguishes finding a city, proving uniqueness, removing redundant clues, and proving a global minimum. Hidden-code feedback is specified exactly so an optimal-query claim has a well-defined meaning.

The game weeks separate one-pile subtraction, combinations of games, and unrestricted Nim. Billiards puts unfolding to work as an explanation, rather than leaving the strongest mathematics only in an adult aside. The generic puzzle workshop is replaced by Euler-trail and delivery-route design: children still invent puzzles, but now have a mathematical criterion for making and checking them.

## Running this with seven children and two adults

Keep the [shared hour](fall-k-5-year-a.md#a-shared-hour): ten minutes exploring materials, a brief common launch, sustained table work, a movement break, a second investigation block, and a short exchange of discoveries. Give pages one at a time. A packet is a menu for a session and possible continuation, not an assignment to finish.

The default is **0–10 / 10–15 / 15–35 / 35–40 / 40–55 / 55–60**, with movement/reset at 35–40. Each weekly plan and facilitator guide names a satisfying stop and an optional proof or explanation for each group. Use the log's distinctions between attempts, conjectures, checked cases, proofs, and supplied theorems, rather than treating a printed destination as completed work.

The supporting parent remains primarily with KK1. The organizer starts the three third graders, launches the fifth grader, and returns predictably to each. A supplied next experiment keeps a child working between visits; it does not replace conversation. Schedule an adult opponent in game weeks. Use two opposing sides, with a rotating checker or a team of two, instead of silently changing a two-player game into a three-player one.

K–1 instructions may all be spoken. The middle and upper pages aim to deepen reasoning rather than require earlier mastery of school arithmetic. The extra pages state additional needs such as fractions, binary place value, or algebra when those occur. A younger child can use a harder question with adult support, and an older child can investigate a smaller model deeply.

The source basis is recorded in [lesson-format source notes](lesson-format-source-notes.md). In particular, *Math Circle by the Bay*, preface pp. viii–x, explicitly advocates themes that develop from elementary to research mathematics, extra challenges, manipulatives, and flexible depth. Rozhkovskaya's lesson accounts support trying before explaining, checking legal play, minimizing copying, and making extensions available. These books do not establish that our exact timetable or activities have been field-tested.

## Returning children and the six-year library

The [use log](fall-k-5-year-a-use-log.md) assigns revision and extra-page IDs. Record the actual board, code, clue set, starting position, and theorem encountered, not just the topic or a PDF that was printed. Old proposed work stays archived and remains untaught unless the organizer records otherwise.

Links across this fall are deliberate: Week 2's parity returns in Week 10's odd-degree repair; Weeks 3–4 contrast general permutations with a cyclic action; Weeks 5–6 distinguish uniqueness from efficient identification; Weeks 7–8 contrast subtraction restrictions with Nim; Week 9 revisits periodicity in geometry. A returning child should encounter a new instance or a deeper question, not the same worksheet under a new grade label.

This is one enriched fall sequence, not a complete 120-session curriculum. Preserve unused extra questions for later years and use observed engagement to decide which themes deserve another session.
