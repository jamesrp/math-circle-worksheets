Week 38 bonus: Seams and cuts

Mathematical kernels

1. Dividing an annulus or Mobius band into n longitudinal lanes, then cutting all lane boundaries, yields components given by cycles of the seam action on lanes. Matching seam: n separate annuli. Reversed seam: pairs j<->n+1-j give annuli; an odd middle lane gives one Mobius component. Three and four lanes distinguish connected pieces, boundary counts and orientation; this is beyond the base middle cut and arrow trip.

2. Strips joined in a cycle with individual matching/reversing seams form one annulus if the total number of reversing seams is even and one Mobius band if odd. The composition determines boundary trips. Joining two or three separately marked strips gives a fresh construction and an accessible parity argument; knotting/linking is outside the claim.

3. Removing a small closed disk strictly inside a connected band adds one boundary component without disconnecting it; removing a small notch attached to only one existing boundary does not add a component. A hole that crosses a joined seam must be represented by matching half-holes on the flat recipe and still counts as one hole after gluing. Children can compare punctures and notches using edge trips, then design a band with a required boundary count. Provide exact preparations whose holes/notches stay separated and do not span the strip width; connectivity and knotting are separate from boundary count. This introduces new boundary creation rather than repeating the base middle cuts or arrow trips.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
