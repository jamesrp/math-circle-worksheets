Week 39 bonus: Road detours

Mathematical kernels

1. Adding one edge to a connected tree creates one independent cycle and allows closed journeys with nonempty reduced words. Removing different roads changes which detours can persist. Children can design a road addition/deletion with a specified surviving journey and explain using actual roads; the base already proves tree reduction, so the new task needs a design/optimization purpose.

2. On a three-arm star, length-2n closed walks at the center have 3^n choices when each excursion immediately returns; on longer branched trees, walks can have nested excursions and different counts. Exact return counts follow a small position-by-step recurrence. Choose contrasting small tree maps and a finite counting question; avoid forcing a long transcription table. All return walks reduce empty, but the number of unreduced records distinguishes them.

3. At a figure-eight junction, the reduced words a and b do not commute. Powers of the same loop do commute; words using both loops may also commute if they are powers of the same reduced word. Provide a small finite commutation challenge where children physically concatenate/reduce road records and find shared-word examples, rather than another single commutator from base. A full centralizer theorem is adult context only, not required.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
