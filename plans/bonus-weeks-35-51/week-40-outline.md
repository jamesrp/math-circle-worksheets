Week 40 bonus: Loop colors

Mathematical kernels

1. An open two-strand crossing transforms incoming colors (a,b) to (b,2b-a) mod 3, under one explicit strand/position convention. Equal colors remain equal; unequal color pairs cycle with period three. Repeating a crossing gives a concrete color machine with boundary predictions; do not confuse arc labels with positions or over/under choices.

2. Closing a two-strand braid of n same-handed crossings permits exactly color pairs fixed by the crossing machine power. For n divisible by three there are nine total colorings; otherwise three. The closure has one component for n odd and two for n even. Children can make/color closures and compare what color count detects or misses. State the diagram/closure convention and verify n=1,2,3,4,6; no unsupported knot equivalence claims.

3. Joining two knot diagrams at a crossing-free piece of one arc (connected sum) requires the joined arc color to match. Since renaming colors gives equal counts per distinguished color, total counts multiply and divide by three. Two trefoils give 27 colorings; trefoil plus plain loop gives nine. Give actual legible joined diagrams or an explicit cut/join visual. This is composition of invariants, beyond base trefoil/unknot comparison and type-II local check.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
