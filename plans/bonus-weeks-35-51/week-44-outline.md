Week 44 bonus: The bag that copies

Mathematical kernels

1. Opposite reinforcement (return draw and add one counter of the OTHER color) starting R,B gives two-draw red totals with probabilities 1/6,4/6,1/6, compared with copying (1/3 each) and constant bag (1/4,1/2,1/4). Compare balance-promoting versus balance-amplifying histories with independently distinguished counters. This is a changed feedback mechanism, beyond base draw-count enumeration.

2. Given a copying bag after a recorded history, next-draw probability equals current red count/total; the original count ratio is not a fixed prediction. A history with r red and b blue draws starting R,B gives next-red probability (r+1)/(r+b+2). Different orders with identical totals give identical predictive bags. An accessible next-draw forecasting game can ask for construction of stronger/weaker forecasts, not just another order-probability comparison.

3. With one counter each of three colors and the copying rule, the count vector after n draws is uniform among the C(n+2,2) weak compositions. For two draws six count vectors are equally likely, although nine ordered color words are not. Children can make/group all two- or three-draw histories in a triangular count board; this is multidimensional reinforcement, not larger binary draw counts. Verify marked histories and state that original colors persist.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
