Week 43 bonus: Shuffling picture cards

Mathematical kernels

1. Uniformly shuffling physically distinct labeled copies of repeated pictures yields uniform distinct picture words because every word has the same product of label-permutation multiplicities. Two As and two Bs have six picture words, each from four of the 24 labeled orders. Removing labels prematurely can lead to a biased chooser. Concrete multiset shuffling is new relative to three distinct cards.

2. A random cyclic cut of a fixed four-card order makes each card equally likely to be first but gives only four of 24 orders. Marginal fairness does not imply uniform whole orders. Repeated cuts remain rotations, while adding reversal gives only eight dihedral orders. Children can classify attainable orders and build a counterexample to full shuffling.

3. Uniformly inserting a new card into one of the four gaps of a uniformly shuffled three-card row makes all 24 orders equally likely, since deleting the new card recovers exactly one previous order/gap history. Random insertion provides a genuinely different shuffle algorithm and recursive explanation; insertion gaps need a small input/intermediate/output visual.
