Week 45 bonus: The visible side

Mathematical kernels

1. Choose one of RR,RB,BB uniformly and keep that SAME card. For each clue independently choose one of its two faces uniformly, replacing the face-choice ticket between clues. Two red clues make RR versus RB odds 4:1, so chance of RR is 4/5; mixed clues force RB. Different cards on the two rounds is a different experiment. Repeated evidence about one hidden object is new relative to base single-face clues and ticket subsets.

2. In a three-door prize game, an informed host who always opens an unchosen empty door gives switching success 2/3 before the reveal; a blind randomly opening host, conditioned on revealing empty, gives 1/2. Tie-breaking must be fully specified and exact histories distinguished. A concrete marked-ticket comparison develops the role of the clue-producing rule without copying the base RR/RB protocol.

3. A color reporter with explicitly prepared equally likely report tickets may sometimes name the wrong face color. Conditioning on a report uses actual reporting histories, not the report as a known truth. Construct a finite two-color sensor game whose report reliability and prior face multiplicities both matter, and choose a design/reverse-design question with exact outcome counts. Do not infer uniform continuous probabilities or rely on merely different numbers in the old deck.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
