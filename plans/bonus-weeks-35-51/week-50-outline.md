Week 50 bonus: Staircases and lengths

Mathematical kernels

1. On a rectangular grid, allowing diagonal steps changes the shortest-route metric. If right/up cost one each and a diagonal costs d, use as many diagonals as possible for d<2, none for d>2, and any mixture for d=2. For unit-cost king steps the minimum is max(width,height). Concrete physical step tiles allow threshold exploration without roots.

2. All monotone grid paths have equal geometric grid-edge length, but there can be many shortest paths. Counts satisfy a predecessor addition recurrence; obstacles remove choices and can preserve, lengthen, or prevent shortest routes. Choose a finite path-count/design task with a substantial small board and exact independent enumeration, distinct from measuring equal lengths or corridor turn minimization.

3. Closeness to a diagonal places no upper bound on length when backtracking is allowed. A route can repeat a short back-and-forth segment within an arbitrarily narrow corridor many times before finishing. Ask for long routes inside a fixed corridor under a clear repeat/self-overlap convention; explain that the base monotonicity invariant no longer applies. Keep finite constructions separate from infinite paths.

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
