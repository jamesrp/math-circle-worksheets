Week 42 bonus: Fair results from a bag

Mathematical kernels

1. Three independent draws with a fixed unknown red chance give three equally likely words with exactly one red, and three equally likely words with exactly two reds. Their position classes can yield three fair outputs; all-same words are discarded. A reversal argument for pairs does not alone settle three-output fairness. Provide marked outcomes and choose a concrete three-way selector.

2. In four draws, previously discarded equal pairs RR and BB can themselves be paired: RRBB and BBRR are equally likely p^2(1-p)^2. Recycling these makes extra fair output without changing the source; RR/RR and BB/BB still discard. Compare output yield on the SAME complete finite history set, and specify whether outputs from first-stage successes are retained. This is an efficiency investigation distinct from changing bag counts.

3. Two individual fair outputs need not give four equally likely ordered pairs: perfect copying gives only square-square and circle-circle. Independent disjoint extraction rounds do produce all four joint outputs equally often in the exact product model. Create physical selectors with matching single-output marginals and contrasting joint possibilities. Keep the fairness question and independence separate, and avoid claiming finite experimental tallies prove exact equality.
