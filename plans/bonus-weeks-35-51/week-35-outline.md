Week 35 bonus: Footprint borders

Mathematical kernels

1. Two superposed endlessly repeating borders have exactly the motions common to both layers (the layers must remain distinguished). If periods are a and b whole slots, the least common matching slide is lcm(a,b) when both layers have those primitive periods. Color can destroy a formerly matching motion without moving the footprints. This supports combined-pattern design, beyond the base construction of individual glide borders.

2. A prescribed glide or slide partitions a finite repeat into orbits of slots/mirror states. A pattern invariant under that move has constant compatible data on each orbit. The fewest recolorings is the sum, over orbits, of orbit size minus the largest color multiplicity. Physical edit puzzles can use two colors and small repeats, with explanations by pairing rather than formulas.

3. Closing an even-length alternating mirror border into a ring preserves its local alternating rule; an odd length cannot alternate two distinct forms all the way around. Finite rings and infinite strips have different global matching motions. Necklace rotations act cyclically; reflection reverses order. An asymmetric marked motif prevents accidental extra matches. The writer can choose a genuinely different finite-closure investigation, without claiming the seven-frieze classification.

Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
