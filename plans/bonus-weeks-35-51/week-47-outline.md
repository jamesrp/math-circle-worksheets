Week 47 bonus: Gentle-step landscapes

Mathematical kernels

1. On a cycle or branched graph, neighbor heights differ by at most one. Feasible fixed clues satisfy the same pairwise bound using SHORTEST graph distance, and extrema are cone envelopes over that distance. Closing a row into a ring introduces a last-to-first constraint; a shortcut can render formerly possible clues incompatible. Choose a concrete cycle/branched board so the new geometry matters.

2. Pointwise min/max of two feasible integer landscapes with the same fixed clues are feasible. Any two such landscapes can be connected by legal single-site unit moves, with shortest distance equal to sum of absolute height differences: lower first to pointwise min, then raise. A local highest differing site can be lowered. Children can investigate a genuine navigation/optimization puzzle; do not simply repeat the base K–1 five-move example.

3. Between the least and greatest feasible integer landscapes, every whole TOTAL VOLUME occurs: a unit-raising path changes the total by one. A fixed block budget makes a fresh feasibility/design task rather than another pointwise maximum question. Provide multiple target budgets and a precise no-total-budget rule for the other investigations; verify counts and constructions independently.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
