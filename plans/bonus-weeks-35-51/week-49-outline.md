Week 49 bonus: Four-tower differences

Mathematical kernels

1. Adding a common constant to all four starting heights leaves the FIRST gap ring unchanged. Complementing every height relative to a fixed ceiling does too. More distinct starts can share a gap ring than these two operations explain: gap (1,1,1,1) has normalized predecessors (0,1,0,1) and (0,1,2,1). Choose a collision/classification question with fixed bounds and a proof by signed edge choices, beyond base existence of a predecessor.

2. For binary heights, the gap rule is XOR on adjacent slots. On 2^k stations the 2^k-th iterate is zero for every binary start, because repeated squaring gives D^(2^k)=I+shift^(2^k)=0 in parity. A six-station ring can cycle; e.g. duplicate a three-station binary cycle. Concrete six/eight station comparisons test which structural station counts force erasure; do not demand general powers-of-two notation from children.

3. Keeping a DIRECTION on a gap instead of taking its absolute value changes the dynamics: the signed difference of next-old minus current-old allows negatives and is unbounded. A four-slot alternating (0,1,0,1) becomes (1,-1,1,-1), then (-2,2,-2,2). Directed/up-down tokens can represent signed gaps; suitable only for children comfortable with signed quantities, roughly 4–5. This reveals why absolute value and nonnegative boundedness matter, rather than adding a larger numeric start.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
