Week 41 bonus: Portals

Mathematical kernels

1. Repeating one fixed move word on a 3x3 torus translates a pawn by its net displacement modulo three. Its orbit is one cell if both coordinates are multiples of three, otherwise three cells. More general fixed boards yield gcd/lcm periods. Use repeat-pattern navigation with contrasting words; the destination-period goal is new relative to base single journeys and homotopy classification.

2. A tour visiting each of the nine torus cells exactly once and returning home is a Hamiltonian cycle of length nine. The lift must end at a nonzero winding copy for a nine-step cycle (odd length prevents zero signed displacement). Construct different tours and identify possible winding copies by exhaustive verification. Use physical pawn records, not all tour enumeration for children.

3. The shortest lattice journey to copy (m,n) of the starting cell has length 3(|m|+|n|). Obstacles on the periodic map can make that lower bound unattainable; a blocked cell can affect a winding route even when the projected endpoint is unchanged. Choose a small marked-copy/obstacle route challenge with independently enumerated shortest paths and sufficient repeated-map workspace. This is optimization with obstacles, beyond base loop classification.

Suggested emphasis by level

K-1: Concrete material-based entries only where they genuinely work; spoken adult reading and optional adult recording. Do not force a K-1 version of all kernels.
Grades 2-3: Independent manipulation, drawing or a small finite outcome collection with counting; proofs can use pairings and examples.
Grades 4-5: New design, optimality, changed-rule tests or systematic completeness explanations through elementary models. No formal undergraduate prerequisites.
