Week 37 bonus: Mirror twins

Mathematical kernels

1. A flat asymmetric tile can fail to match its mirror by in-plane slides/rotations but can match after being lifted and turned over in three-space. Restrict moves explicitly. Contrasting ambient dimensions provides an elementary investigation distinct from the base tetrahedral vertex coloring.

2. Color tetrahedron EDGES, not vertices, with two colors. A red Hamiltonian path of three edges has only the identity and reversal as graph symmetries; reversal is an even vertex permutation, so this two-color edge model is chiral under proper spatial rotations. Three-red stars and triangles are achiral. Verify all candidate edge patterns by all 12 rotations and odd permutations. Children can build and match models rather than learn permutation parity.

3. Proper cube rotations preserve the handedness of three perpendicular distinguishable directions. A rigid corner made of three mutually perpendicular arms of distinct lengths/colors can be chiral even though each arm is simple. Repeating or removing a distinguishing feature can destroy the obstruction. Choose a concrete, checkable corner or connected cube model whose mirror comparison is genuinely different from base vertex-tetrahedron relabeling; give exact geometric coordinates and permitted motions.
