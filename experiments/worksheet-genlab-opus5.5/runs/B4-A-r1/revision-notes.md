## Issue 2: K–1, Problems 3 and 6, more blank shapes than ways. SKIPPED

The counts are correct (2, 3 and 6), but both proposed fixes would make the page worse:

- Printing exactly 2 hexagons and 3 long hexagons gives away the answer. "Find all the ways" then becomes "fill every shape", and deciding when you have found them all is the mathematics of the problem.
- Adding "Some shapes may stay empty." is a hint about the count. It would also be a third sentence in a K–1 problem, and the standard asks for one or two.

Spare blanks follow the organizer's own walking problem, where the number of grids does not match the answer. The other bands do the same (grades 4–5 Problem 1 gives 10 boards for 6 coverings, and Problem 7 gives 25 for 20).

Whether turned copies count as different ways is something the adult can raise at the table. On the printed shapes, which are fixed in position, they are different placements. The deliverable has no facilitator guide where a note could go.

I confirmed that the shortest route from E to F is 8 flips. A child who wrote a larger number in Problem 3 would be asked to explain something false.
