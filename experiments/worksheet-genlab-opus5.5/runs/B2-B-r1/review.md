## K–1 (base/k-1.pdf)

### 1. MUST: page 5, Problem 10, the move rule is missing
Text: "Now there is one pile again, and whoever takes the last counter loses. Put an X on every trap from 1 to 20."
The last one-pile problem (Problem 7, page 3) changed the moves to "1, 2, or 3". Problems 8 and 9 restated "1 or 2", but only for two piles. So the adult reading aloud cannot tell whether Problem 10 uses 1 or 2 (traps 1, 4, 7, …, 19) or 1, 2, or 3 (traps 1, 5, 9, 13, 17). Children at one table may play different games and argue over different answers.
Fix (line 249): "Now there is one pile again, each player takes 1 or 2 counters, and whoever takes the last counter loses. Put an X on every trap from 1 to 20."

### 2. SHOULD: page 4, Problem 8, "the last counter" with two piles
Text: "Now there are two piles, and on your turn you take 1 or 2 counters from one pile." The only win rule is the one-pile sentence at the top of page 1, "Whoever takes the last counter wins."
The first case drawn is 1 and 1. A child who empties one pile has "taken the last counter" of that pile and will claim the win. That reading makes every case a win for whoever goes 1st, which hides the point of the problem: equal piles mean you would rather go 2nd.
Fix (lines 241–242): "Now there are two piles. On your turn you take 1 or 2 counters from one pile, and whoever takes the last counter on the table wins. Circle 1st or 2nd to show whether you would rather go first or second."

### 3. SHOULD: page 4, Problem 8, "1st" and "2nd" look like pile labels
Diagram: in each of the six frames, "1st" is centred directly under the left pile and "2nd" directly under the right pile. Children who cannot read see one word belonging to each pile and may read them as "first pile / second pile", for example circling the pile they would take from first. (This is not a problem in Problem 2, where there is only one pile.)
Fix (`two_pile_panel`, lines 106–110): put the two words together as a pair, centred under the whole frame, so neither one lines up with a single pile. The same function draws grades 2–3 Problem 7 (see below).

### 4. SHOULD: page 2, Problem 5, "always starts"
Text: "Ben always starts by taking 2 counters. Circle each pile where Ben can still be sure to win after that."
When this is heard once, "Ben always … taking 2 counters" is easily taken to mean that Ben takes 2 on every turn. That gives different answers and leaves Ben stuck when 1 counter is left.
Fix (line 224): "Ben goes first and takes 2 counters. Circle each pile where Ben can still be sure to win after that."

### 5. SHOULD: page 2, Problem 3, words that restate the picture
Text: "It is your turn with a bigger pile." The phrase "with a bigger pile" only describes the drawing.
Fix (line 218): "It is your turn. Circle the counters …" (rest unchanged).

## Grades 2–3 (base/grades-2-3.pdf)

### 6. SHOULD: page 5, Problem 8, "exactly when" is easy to miss
Text: "Find a rule … so that you would rather go second exactly when the starting pile is 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and so on."
The rule "1 or 4" makes 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, … all second-player piles, but it also makes 2, 7, 12, … second-player piles. A third grader who skims over "exactly" will offer "1 or 4" as the first or the "different" rule and think the problem is done. The correct answers are 1, 2, 3, 4 plus any extra moves that are not multiples of 5, for example 1, 2, 3, 4, 6.
Fix (lines 303–305): "… so that you would rather go second when the starting pile is 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and so on, and you would rather go first for every other pile. Then find a different rule that does the same thing."

### 7. SHOULD: page 4, Problem 7, two-pile win rule and choice labels

Fix (lines 295–296): "… takes 1 or 2 counters from one of the piles, and whoever takes the last counter on the table wins."
Diagram: "First" sits under the left pile and "Second" under the right pile in each frame. The same `two_pile_panel` fix as K–1 item 3 covers it.

## Grades 4–5 (base/grades-4-5.pdf)

### 9. SHOULD: page 4, Problem 6, no rule for a pile that cannot be moved
Text: "Find every game whose allowed moves come from the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 and whose losing piles are exactly the multiples of 4. Explain why your list is complete."
To explain why the list is complete, a child has to rule out move sets that leave out 1, such as {2, 3, 5}. In those games a pile of 1 has no legal move, and "whoever takes the last counter wins" does not say who wins. The child cannot finish the explanation without asking. The answer is {1, 2, 3}, {1, 2, 3, 5}, {1, 2, 3, 6}, {1, 2, 3, 5, 6}.
Fix (line 345): add a sentence before "Explain why …": "If a player cannot move, that player loses." Do not say that 1 must be allowed, because that gives away part of the explanation.
