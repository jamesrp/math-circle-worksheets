## Choose by prerequisites and visit

Grade labels are approximate. Shared K–5 menus do not mean every page suits every child. Weeks 88–89 offer younger concrete touch/build entry points; the other five do not claim independent K–1 packets. Ordinary adult reading or recording is welcome, while children retain the mathematical choices. Each guide accounts for the current three anchored tables and gives first routes, hints and later investigations.

| Week | Concrete entry and deeper destination |
|---|---|
| 85 | Trace with a preassembled linkage, copy several contrasting paths, and steer a copy to targets. Requires controlled tracing and recognizing the two tip roles. Return to the bar explanation, fourfold area, reversed control and moving the anchor. Formal vectors are adult background. |
| 86 | Take turns moving one road or staying, with a partner and visible turn tile; no arithmetic is needed for the basic game. Start with paths and trees, then compare rings. Return to minimal road additions, designing contrasting boards, fold certificates and two-cop strategies. |
| 87 | Read rankings with adult help and cast a token vote using unchanged cards. Compare knockout orders before designing profiles. The separate line visit needs distance comparison, fixed favorites and tie handling; repeated favorites, even voters and impossible line rankings extend it. |
| 88 | Place disks, keep them flat and distinguish touching from overlap. Younger children can build rings and improve a tray packing; later work uses centers, distances and geometric explanations. Unequal and mixed rings, rigid equality cases and restricted three-row trays are return investigations. Trigonometry is adult background. |
| 89 | Retain an old tile shape while adding or removing a layer. Begin with two of the square/staircase/rectangle tasks. Single totals need counting and addition; hidden inputs use subtraction or matching counters. Two-stage ordering, zero turns, competing full rules, cube shells and three-stage corner piles are distinct later routes. |
| 90 | Compare small labels and move the smaller immediate neighbor into a hole. Grades 2–3 readiness can operate one-hole boards; upper work constructs starts, pools catalogs, compares whole-slide orders, finds preimages and reverses recorded slides. Masks and full resets are prerequisites for the multiple-hole route. |
| 91 | Follow the displayed next arrow, flip it and retain its new state. Student pp.1–3 introduce Grades 2–3 tree programming; pp.4–5 deepen it for Grades 4–5; p.6 offers a Grades 2–5 coin comparison. The Grades 4–5 road packet introduces absorbing feedback before general counting arguments. |

## What changed from the reserved plans

The seven numbers, themes and substantive mathematical directions are preserved. Fresh writers chose the actual problems rather than reproducing tentative page lists. The historical plans remain available through the [production record](../plans/seven-families-85-91/production/STATUS.md).

- **85:** separate four-problem concrete and upper packets, a joined working mat, labeled inserts and a half-square-compatible area triangle. The actual anchor shift is 20 mm, with 10 mm input compensation. Coaxial mounts and a full working kit still require physical testing.
- **86:** more concrete triangle/ring contrasts; ordinary boards sit on task pages and fold copies remain separate. The six-ring two-cop task starts both cops at one entry so it does not collapse to a one-turn placement trick. The general two-cop and fold mathematics remains.
- **87:** card, line and upper routes are separately numbered. The line convention completes all five votes from one unchanged state; separate proposal/tally labels and enlarged wells preserve the live input and all voters.
- **88:** the practical 160×112 mm tray has clearance; later 160×110 and 160×108 mm trays address an exact restricted 4–3–4 row question. No unrestricted eleven-disk optimal-packing theorem is claimed.
- **89:** twenty substantial problems retain the planned layers, inverse questions, cascades, rule ambiguity, shells and corner piles. Movable input cards and retained current totals keep bookkeeping light; the guide offers several visits.
- **90:** nine problems preserve one-hole play, complete A/D/B catalogs, target-shape construction, nonunique preimages, reversal and local proofs. The general rectification theorem is attributed background; finite catalogs are not presented as its universal proof.
- **91:** tree programming and absorbing roads are separate visits with complete follow-then-flip demonstrations and recoverable states. For an absorbing path with vertices 0 through m (m ≥ 2), a fixed interior source 1 ≤ s ≤ m−1, follow-then-flip switches and each walk completed before the next launch, **every m consecutive launches have exactly s right exits from any initial rotor state, with no burn-in**. This does not imply every full rotor configuration is already periodic; the guide proves the count identity and distinguishes state transients.
