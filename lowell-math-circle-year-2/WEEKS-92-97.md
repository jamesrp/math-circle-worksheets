# Six connected investigations: Weeks 92–97

These are prepared activities for organizer review, not an adopted meeting schedule. All remain **unpiloted; physical rehearsal is untested**. The six investigations extend analysis/dynamics, inference, and multiplicative number theory. Week 92 deliberately revisits an existing atlas theme with a different question; none changes reserved Weeks 85–91.

Print US Letter, single-sided, at Actual Size. Keep guides separate from student pages and give only the pages appropriate to the current visit. The ten student packets total 46 pages; the six theorem-first guides total 32 pages. Every one of the 78 final pages received visual review. No new combined book is required: each packet is independently printable, and older combined sets are unchanged.

## Choose by prerequisites

Grade labels are approximate. Ordinary adult reading or recording is supported; the child should retain the mathematical choices. Each guide gives a brief concrete whole-group launch, realistic materials, a first route, and later investigations. Finishing a packet in one hour is not the goal.

| Week | Concrete entry, prerequisites and mathematical destination |
|---|---|
| 92 | Tokens and strips on a 0–16 track; counting, distance and doubling. Move a point, then a whole band of possible positions. Later work uses halves and the idea that a band contains every point between its ends. Every positive-width band eventually fills the track, even though some pairs of points merge. |
| 93 | Build equal-area tile rectangles, then cut and rearrange paper. Rectangular area, halves, division and supported fraction comparison are needed for reshaping. Supplied movable side cards reduce arithmetic load. Repeated reshaping preserves area and narrows guaranteed square-side bounds, without reaching exact equality from an unequal rectangle in finitely many moves. |
| 94 | The short K–1 entry compares lengths on a scale whose zero is missing; count spaces and handle prepared strips. Grades 2–3 use addition/subtraction and known reference lengths to recover a fixed shift. Grades 4–5 use ratios and alternative explanations to identify stretch and shift, test a model, and choose standards under bounded reading error. |
| 95 | Mark, return, mix and recapture identical objects. Grades 2–3 match identities, count within 20 and construct populations compatible with a record. Grades 4–5 need multiplication, division and equivalent fractions for an estimate. Repeated records expose sample variability; later tasks allocate sampling effort and test whether lost marks explain an observation. |
| 96 | Equal groups, prime-factor cards and supported multiplication. A divisor is a sub-bag of prime factors; the empty bag represents 1. Later work counts all sub-bags and finds least numbers with specified divisor counts. Completeness and unique factorization supply the explanation. |
| 97 | Make square arrays, remove complete equal bundles and compare leftovers. Supported multiplication and organizing small collections are prerequisites. Later work distinguishes experiments from an all-input argument and explains why odd-prime square outputs have paired inputs while composites can behave differently. |

Only Week 94 claims a K–1 packet. A shared launch in another week is not a substitute for an appropriate younger-table activity. Check the guide's staffing and preparation before choosing a session. Week 94 uses 12 mm printed units; Week 93 has measured cutouts and a drawing grid of a different scale. Digital dimension checks do not establish physical fit. Week 95 particularly needs a real test of mark retention, erasure and tactile indistinguishability.

## Connections that change the question

**Guaranteed ranges.** Week 51's measurement ranges lead into both dynamics investigations. Week 92 propagates uncertainty until it fills a domain; Week 93 constructs nested certificates whose widths shrink toward zero. Week 92's atlas GA-10 connection is explicit: periodic words and cycles are earlier work; uncertainty bands and prediction horizons are this visit's focus. Week 93 also connects Week 48's inner/outer certificates to an area-preserving transformation.

**What an observation identifies.** Week 94 asks which known references separate an unknown instrument from an unknown object. Week 95 distinguishes certain compatibility from model-based inference with variable samples. Week 97 offers a contrasting exact observation: several inputs can have the same remainder even without measurement error or chance. These are different causes of ambiguity, with different ways to investigate them.

**Products and prime structure.** Week 96's factor bags connect to earlier gcd/lcm work and support the prime-divides-a-product argument in Week 97. The optional square/nonsquare multiplication investigation is a further return visit. Week 95's balanced catch allocation reuses fixed-sum rectangle reasoning from Week 51's bonus to maximize expected overlap; this does not claim to optimize every measure of estimation error.
