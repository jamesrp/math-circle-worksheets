Week 48: Trapping an area between two covers

Mathematical kernels

1. An unknown plane area can be bounded by a union of nonoverlapping equal grid squares entirely inside it and a union of grid squares covering it. For nested grid refinements, the inside union can grow and the outside union can shrink; their area gap is an explicit uncertainty certificate. Refinement alone does not prove arbitrary sets have equal limiting inner/outer areas. Use ordinary polygonal regions or smooth closed silhouettes here, and prove finite bounds without invoking calculus.

2. Exact calibration example: the right triangle under the diagonal of a 4-by-4 square has six whole unit squares inside and can be covered by those six plus four diagonal-cut squares. Thus area lies between 6 and 10 big-square units. Halving the grid size gives 28 inside small squares and 36 in the chosen cover, equivalent to bounds 7 and 9 big-square units because four small squares tile one big square. The triangle area is 8 by pairing two triangles into the big square. Boundary-only corner/edge contacts do not demand additional cover squares when that boundary is already covered; define the printed inside/partial/outside convention visibly. A small visual example must distinguish a whole interior square, a partially filled square and an exterior square, then show four small squares reassembling one original area unit. Keep the target bound or best refinement open.

Sources: A. Sapozhnikov, Leipzig Mathematics 3, §1.2, inner/outer Jordan measures and nested dyadic boxes: https://www.math.uni-leipzig.de/~sapozhnikov/10-PHY-BIPMA3-2/Math3_LectureNotes.pdf. Finite triangle bounds are independently derived. This differs from the produced Cantor-set length activity, polyomino perimeter, and tomography: the central output is an honest area enclosure with a refinement certificate.

Suggested emphasis by level:

K–1: cover a silhouette and find guaranteed-inside tiles; distinguish what is certain from what is partly filled.
Grades 2–3: build two area bounds and improve the uncertain boundary without recounting everything.
Grades 4–5: explain why nested refinement cannot worsen the bounds, and compare refinements using a fixed area unit.

Materials

Each child has two or three reusable simple-shape mats inside a 120×120 mm box, a 4×4 transparent grid with 30 mm cells, and a matching 8×8 overlay with 15 mm cells. Supply at least 16 opaque 30 mm paper-square tiles per child (160 total) and colored pencils; finer cells can be marked instead of manipulated. All matching overlays and calibration squares print at 100% scale. A four-small-squares-to-one-big-square legend must remain visible. K–1 can begin with a 2×2 grid on the same-size box. No requirement to count hundreds of pixels or estimate fractional pieces by eye; the task is lower and upper certification, not a single guessed decimal.
