\subsection*{The mathematical core}
A picture is a binary matrix on fixed labeled rows and columns: each cell contains zero or one counter. Its margins are whole-row and whole-column sums. Both totals equal the number of counters, but margins need not determine the picture.

A \textbf{switch} replaces the two occupied opposite corners of a rectangle by its two empty opposite corners; all other cells stay fixed. The selected rows and columns need not be adjacent. Every affected row and column loses and gains one counter, so every margin is preserved.

\textbf{Uniqueness:} A binary picture is the only one with its margins if and only if it has no legal switch. Equivalently, the sets of occupied columns in its rows are pairwise nested. The guide gives the nested-row proof; finite enumeration alone is not its justification.

\textbf{Exact two-row distance:} For two pictures with the same margins, columns of sum 0 or 2 are forced. Among the s columns of sum 1, let S and T be the top-row occupied column sets of two pictures. Their equal size k is fixed by the row sum. The fewest switches is $|S\setminus T|$. One switch corrects at most one wrong top choice, and exchanging a wrong choice for a missing one attains the bound. All such pictures are connected; their greatest shortest distance is $\min(k,s-k)$.

\subsection*{What the activity develops}
\textbf{K--1:} Match line counts, find alternatives and organize small catalogs after a demonstrated picture-to-count conversion.

\textbf{Grades 2--3:} Discover ambiguity, follow a switch, and justify why a proposed route cannot be shorter.

\textbf{Grades 4--5:} Separate forced columns from free choices and prove the two-row distance rule for any number of columns.

\subsection*{From trials to explanation}
Two different matching pictures prove ambiguity. Failure to find a second picture does not prove uniqueness. A complete catalog, forcing argument or no-switch proof can do so. The distance formula above is for two rows; the guide cites broader switch connectivity separately.
