## Rebuild and diagram convention

Run `bash build.sh` with Python 3, a standard pdfLaTeX/TikZ installation and Poppler. Source `.tex` files are generated by `build_packets.py` and may also be compiled directly. All model drawings are original, mathematically derived orthographic projections of a regular tetrahedron. Their solid outside triangle is the front face; the three dashed edges run to the far vertex behind that face. The shaded vertex circles are markers rather than spherical model parts. Letters remain readable in grayscale and are category labels only. Each merge trial resets to the original four-distinct-label mirror pair. Rotation counts identify moves with the same resulting vertex destinations, regardless of motion history.

For each perspective frame, labels are listed in the order [apex, lower left, lower right, far vertex]. The successful-turn example is ABCD → ADBC. It is a 120-degree rotation about the axis from A through the center of the opposite face. The mirror pair is ABCD versus ACBD. The repeated-label example changes D to C, giving ABCC versus ACBC; these match by a proper rotation. It deliberately does not collapse into two identical fixed-position pictures.

For the upper view, A is nearest the eye and the child looks straight toward the center of opposite face BCD. The original model projects to B at lower left, C at lower right, D at top, with A in front at the center. Viewed consistently this way, the cyclic order is preserved under all allowed alignments fixing A; a mirror reverses it. Looking from the opposite side would reverse the certificate and invalidate a comparison. The page gives a matched perspective-to-view example before children use two blank view diagrams to record their pair.

## Digital checks and limits

`mathematical-checks.json` records 12 proper tetrahedral rotations obtained from exact integer coordinates and determinants. It checks the successful-copy example, distinguishes the four-label mirror pair, confirms the repeated-label match, and checks all six choices of two labels to merge. These finite checks verify the intended mathematical configurations, not model rigidity, marker attachment, visual occlusion in the actual kit, or classroom usability.
