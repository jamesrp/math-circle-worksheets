Week 26: Same area, different boundaries encore

Mathematical kernels

1. Shared fences are counted twice by separate perimeters. Partition a fixed tile region into two colors, each occupying whole unit squares. If P_R and P_B are the two color-region perimeters, P is the original outside-and-hole perimeter, and L is the number of sides where the colors meet, then P_R+P_B=P+2L. The outer sides appear once; each internal separating side appears in both color counts. A balanced partition of a 4 × 4 square into eight squares of each color has L≥4, attained by cutting straight through the middle. An elementary bound: if all four rows contain both colors, each row has a color transition, giving four. If a wholly red and wholly blue row both occur, every column has a transition, giving four. Otherwise, suppose a wholly red row occurs and no wholly blue row occurs. The eight blue squares lie in at least three mixed rows and at least three columns; each mixed row gives a transition and each blue-occupied column crosses red in the wholly red row, giving at least six. Interchange the colors for the other case. These cases exhaust all partitions, even disconnected ones, so the bound applies when both rooms must remain side-connected. The new object is an internal interface, not another minimum outside perimeter.

2. Boundary turns detect holes. Restrict to a side-connected polyomino whose boundary has no pinched grid vertex: forbid a vertex with exactly two diagonally opposite occupied squares and the other two empty. Let C be the number of convex boundary corners (one occupied square at the vertex) and R the number of concave corners (three occupied squares); straight vertices do not count. If there are h holes, C−R=4(1−h). Trace each boundary with tiles on the left. The outer loop makes four more left than right quarter-turns; every hole loop makes four more right than left turns. Adding those balances proves the formula. A rectangle has (C,R)=(4,0); the three-tile L occupying (row,column)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0) has (5,1); a 3 × 3 ring missing its middle has (4,4). Counting every corner, including those inside holes, matters. A failed attempt to make prescribed corner counts is an experiment, not a proof. The no-pinch assumption is essential; the base guide explicitly permits corner-sealed holes, so this investigation must state its narrower class rather than silently change base rules.

3. A three-dimensional boundary remembers a two-dimensional one. Put h layers of cubes on every square of a footprint consisting of m unit squares with perimeter P. Count all exposed cube faces, including the bottom. The surface count is S=2m+hP and the cube count is mh: one top and one bottom per footprint square, and h faces over each exposed footprint edge. Thus equal cube volume does not determine surface area. Eight cubes in a 2 × 2 × 2 block have S=24; a single layer with 2 × 4 footprint has S=28; a row of eight has S=34. For unequal positive integer column heights h_i on a fixed footprint, S=2m+Σ_boundary edges h_i+Σ_neighbor pairs |h_i−h_j|. A neighboring pair contributes only the height difference because lower faces are hidden. This optional terrain continuation invites designing surfaces, not merely counting all six faces of every cube. The formulas assume vertical stacks with no gaps, overhangs, or diagonally connected separate components; they do not cover arbitrary cube sculptures without adjustment.

Suggested emphasis by level

- K–1: Supported entry through building two colored rooms and counting their shared fence, or comparing small cube buildings; adult recording is routine. Corner-turn/hole reasoning is not a compulsory younger entry.
- Grades 2–3: Relate separate perimeter counts to one shared fence; walk outer and hole boundaries; compare cube surfaces by physical face covers.
- Grades 4–5: Certify the balanced-partition optimum, derive the corner/holes rule under its assumptions, and derive or use the footprint/height surface formula; each is a separate return visit.

Materials

Per table: 32 identical square tiles about 20 mm wide (sixteen in each of two distinguishable styles), a matching 4 × 4 working mat, forty short edge markers, forty snap cubes with square faces, removable face stickers or small paper face covers, a pencil, and large square-grid recording paper. Use labels/patterns as well as color if needed. Build cube structures on the table, rotating or lifting them to inspect bottoms; bottom faces count although hidden by the table. The footprint grid can be a small recording diagram because building occurs beside the page. For turn tracing provide a finger-sized marker with a visible facing direction, not a dense arrow code; demonstrate a non-task path corner before first use. If pages are working mats their tile scale must be measured at 100%. Keep a reference building and changed building simultaneously whenever possible.
