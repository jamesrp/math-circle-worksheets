Week 26: The longest and shortest boundary for the same area

Unscheduled library slot, unpiloted. This is a discrete isoperimetric investigation: choose the region and optimize its boundary while fixing its number of squares. Existing tiling packets instead cover a prescribed region, so reusing square manipulatives does not repeat that mathematical question. The writer chooses the student problems.

Mathematical kernels

1. Shared edges determine perimeter and its maximum. A polyomino consists of n unit grid squares whose edge-adjacency graph is connected. Count every exposed unit edge, including edges surrounding holes. If e pairs of squares share a full edge, the total boundary length is P=4n−2e: each square contributes four edges, and each shared edge cancels two contributions. Connectivity requires at least n−1 shared edges, giving P≤2n+2. A straight row of n squares attains that bound. More generally equality holds exactly when the adjacency graph is a tree; a hole is not the only way to create extra shared edges. Adding a square with exactly k shared neighbors changes perimeter by 4−2k, so adding area need not increase boundary. These formulas are shorthand for counting edge tokens and cancellations, not prerequisites in symbolic algebra.

2. Row and column spans certify the minimum. If an edge-connected shape occupies r rows and c columns, each occupied row has at least two vertical boundary edges and each occupied column at least two horizontal boundary edges, so P≥2(r+c); also n≤rc. Thus trying all positive integer dimension pairs with rc≥n supplies a lower bound independent of the shape's detailed outline. It is sharp for every n. Build nearly square rectangles successively: from k-by-k, add 1 through k adjacent squares along one new side; from k-by-(k+1), add 1 through k+1 adjacent squares along the other new side. The added partial strip always starts at a corner. These shapes fill the intervals k²<n≤k(k+1) with perimeter 4k+2 and k(k+1)<n≤(k+1)² with perimeter 4k+4; the square has perimeter 4k. To see the bound, among integer r,c with a given sum, moving one unit from the larger to the smaller when their difference exceeds one increases the product. Thus the most area fitting any proposed semiperimeter occurs at a square or a rectangle whose sides differ by one, giving exactly those thresholds. The compact adult formula is P_min=2 ceil(2 sqrt(n)), but no square-root calculation is needed to prove a particular optimum. Exact minimum/maximum pairs include n=4: (8,10), n=7: (12,16), n=10: (14,22), n=12: (14,26), n=13: (16,28). For seven squares, a 2-by-4 rectangle with one corner removed attains perimeter 12; insisting on a full rectangle would miss the optimum. Minimum perimeter need not have a unique shape.

Suggested emphasis by level

- K–1: build different edge-connected shapes from the same small set of squares and pursue genuinely shortest or longest boundaries, counting exposed edges with objects.
- Grades 2–3: use shared-edge cancellations to predict changes and justify a maximum instead of merely recording many perimeters.
- Grades 4–5: certify minimum perimeter by row/column bounds and constructions, then generalize through balanced rectangle dimensions without requiring square-root notation.

Materials

For each of three tables: twenty-four identical unit square tiles, a reusable square grid, forty short boundary markers or counters, one ruler, a pencil, and blank grid paper. Tiles should be about 20 mm per side; print a matching grid at 100% scale and measure it against the actual tiles before use. A 12-by-12-cell working area requires a separate 24 × 24 cm mat or two aligned sheets; narrow US Letter grids must not silently rule out long extremal shapes. A long straight row may be built directly on the table and recorded schematically. For each task put exactly the stated number of unit tiles in play, with no larger replacement blocks, stacking, cutting, gaps, overlaps, or corner-only connections. Count unit boundary edges, not tile corners or a taut string's convex-hull outline. If a hole is built, its inner edges count; do not substitute exterior perimeter without changing the mathematical question. Proposed quantities are preparation requirements, not a verified inventory of owned materials.
