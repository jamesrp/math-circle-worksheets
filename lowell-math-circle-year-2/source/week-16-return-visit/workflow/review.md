## Required clarification: count mesh cells, not composite triangles

**Pages 1–2 and especially page 4.** The companion does not carry forward the base packet's definition that a little triangle has no lines inside it. Problems 1–2 say “small triangle,” which usually suggests the intended cells but leaves a size-based ambiguity. Page 3 defines a rainbow triangle only by its letters, and page 4 says to read and mark “each rainbow triangle” without restricting it to a cell. The outer triangle on page 4 has R, B, Y permanently and has positive orientation. Counting it along with the cells changes the intended signed difference. Larger triangles made of several cells can create further spurious contributions.

Use a brief essential convention defining the counted triangles as the triangles with no subdivision lines inside. Keep it with the shared rules for Problems 1–2, and make the rainbow definition/read instruction used for Problem 4 equally unambiguous. For example, “Count only triangles with no lines inside them” is short and does not reveal either discovery. The reviser can choose the exact placement; do not add a separate explanatory heading or a solution example.

This is a rule-clarity issue, not a demand for a new mathematical scaffold. The adult guide should use the same counting unit, but an adult-only definition is not a substitute for the essential student convention.

## Recommended small improvement: retain a few signed results

**Page 4, bottom of the page.** The central question compares fillings, but the printed record contains only one pair of totals. Movable labels make changing a filling easy, while erasing/replacing the totals loses the previous result. The two broad blank lines could hold previous results, but then children must invent an additional record while also learning counterclockwise signs.

Prefer two or three paired blank rows for the + and − totals in approximately the existing bottom space. This externalizes a small history without prescribing differences, pairing signs, or revealing an invariant. Do not require simultaneous boundary-door tallies. This is a usability recommendation, not a mathematical blocker; spare paper or an adult-held light record could also support the experiment.
