# Sources and provenance

The student problems, drawings and facilitator guide are newly authored for this library. Mathematical facts are classical; no new research result is claimed. Source references identify mathematical context, not classroom validation of these worksheets. No downloaded book, paper, private record or borrowed workflow excerpt is included in this source package.

## Mathematical source

Gabriel Peyré and Marco Cuturi, *Computational Optimal Transport*, §2.6, Remarks 2.28 and 2.30, equations (2.33) and (2.37); §6.3 for graph transport.
https://arxiv.org/html/1803.00567v4

The ordered-position formula and cumulative-distribution viewpoint specialize to the integer-pile model. The guide derives the discrete gap bound and attaining construction directly. Its work is unnormalized counter-steps; probability-normalized Wasserstein-1 distance divides by the positive number of counters. No claim is made that sorted matching solves arbitrary higher-dimensional transport. Infinite capacity, equal total mass and unit gap costs are explicit assumptions.

## Exact connections

Week 2's return visit optimizes capacity-one binary swaps on a line or ring, a genuine restricted relative. This family treats whole pile distributions, arbitrary nonnegative counts, greedy failure and lower-bound certificates. Week 3's labeled sorting is different from interchangeable mass; Week 13 has route capacities, and Week 53 chooses a network rather than using fixed roads. Week 89's running totals are background for prefix imbalances, not the present optimization question. Week 47's Lipschitz landscapes may offer a later dual perspective but are not required here.
