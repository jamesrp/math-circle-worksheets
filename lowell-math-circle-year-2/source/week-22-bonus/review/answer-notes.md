This establishes the sharp threshold on a line: with any five positions in sorted order (including repeats), outer pair, next-inner pair, middle singleton always meet at the middle location; four distinct positions give the counterexample.
5. Regular hexagon: AD, BE, CF are opposite-pair segments and share center(4,4). Every label appears once and each group is nonempty. The plotted points have radius3.5, angles0,60,...,300 degrees; x/y scaling is equal. It is a common-point result, stronger than merely three pairwise intersections.
6. Generic strictly convex hexagon has no split. Every vertex is outside the hull of the others, so no singleton group can share a point with the other two; all three groups must be pairs. For three disjoint paired chords to meet in a convex hexagon, their endpoints must alternate around the cycle, forcing the opposite pairing AD, BE, CF. With the x-shifted coordinates printed, AD and BE meet at (37/9,28/9), while CF at that x has y=179/72, different from28/9. Thus even the only possible paired pattern fails. AD, BE, CF do meet pairwise at different points, which is insufficient. The writer's Fraction-based script independently checks all fifteen pairings and finds no common intersection. No general seven-point planar theorem is claimed or expected.
