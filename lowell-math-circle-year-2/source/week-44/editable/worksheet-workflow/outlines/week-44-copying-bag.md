Week 44: The bag that copies its draws

Mathematical kernels

1. Begin with one red and one blue counter. Draw uniformly, return the drawn counter, and add one new counter of the same color. This is Pólya reinforcement. With individually distinguished counters, two draws have six equally likely histories: two end with zero red draws, two with one red draw, and two with two red draws. A constant one-red/one-blue bag instead has four equally likely color histories, with red-count multiplicities 1,2,1. This small contrast is complete exact mathematics without simulation statistics or fractions.

2. For n draws of the copying rule, the number of red draws is uniform on 0,...,n. Every color word with r reds has probability r!(n−r)!/(n+1)!, independent of its order, and the word multiplicity cancels to 1/(n+1). This general proof is facilitator context; the child endpoint can be six identity histories or the 24 three-draw histories grouped rather than tabulated. Both original colors always remain, so do not claim color extinction. A necessary visual example is RB → draw R → return it and add another R → RRB; copied counters need separate small identity marks when exact histories are introduced.

Sources: Janko Gravner, UC Davis MAT235B lecture 3, Pólya urn: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~gravner/MAT235B/assignments/MAT235B_lec3.pdf. Finite identity histories independently verified for 2,3,4 draws. AP-24's fixed-size resampling/genetic drift is a different stochastic process.

Suggested emphasis by level:

K–1: enact and invent short copying stories, comparing final compositions with an adult.
Grades 2–3: distinguish six two-draw identity histories from four color words and compare copying with replacement alone.
Grades 4–5: investigate ordering versus counts and defend the finite distribution; general factorial proof is optional adult-supported continuation.

Materials

For each child: one opaque cup, six red and six blue equal-size/equal-feel counters at least 25 mm wide, a visible supply tray, and a short four-position draw-story strip. Ten cups and 120 counters total. Each run starts with one of each color, uses at most four draws, then resets; composition records the result so no frequency sheet is needed. Identity marks on counters must not alter feel. Provide diagram space for the changing bag, not long strings of arithmetic. Two-draw case is sufficient for lower entries; the writer must not impose a 24-case census on every child.
