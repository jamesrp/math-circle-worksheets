# Mathematical overview

For these polygonal shapes, the count of grid cells wholly gray is a lower area bound and the count containing positive gray area is an upper bound, with one cell as area unit. Boundary-only contact adds no cover cell. Translating a grid at fixed cell size is not nested refinement: for the fixed area-3 L polygon, the aligned bounds are [3,3], a half-unit horizontal shift gives [1,5], and a half-unit shift in both directions gives [0,8]. Moving from the aligned grid worsens both; reversing that move improves them. No monotonic improvement theorem holds for arbitrary translations.

Two guaranteed area ranges [L1,U1] and [L2,U2] for the same unchanged shape combine to [max(L1,L2),min(U1,U2)]. A reversed interval means inconsistent reports. This is intersection of deterministic possibilities, with no averaging or probability model. The cards are guarantees, not necessarily exact whole/partial cell counts from one specified grid.

Within the printed 2-by-2 board, the three-cell L mask permits connected hole-free polygons with arbitrarily small positive area or area arbitrarily close to 3, while every marked cell remains genuinely partial. A thick L corridor of width t, 0<t<1, has area 4t-t² and occupies exactly those three cells. Equal area and the same mask also permit arbitrarily large perimeter: replace a straight boundary segment with balanced triangular waves whose signed area changes cancel. Each construction is finite. Finite printing resolution limits physical examples; it does not make the ideal polygonal statement false.

Grades 2-5 can compare grids and combine guarantees; grades 4-5 can distinguish area from boundary length and explain equal-area modifications. Measurements and drawings supply experiments. Cell inclusion, intersections, and exact polygon calculations establish claims; a few jagged drawings alone do not prove unbounded perimeter.

# Solutions, hints, and continuations

1. The fixed polygon is the union of [0,2]x[0,1] and [1,2]x[1,2], with area 3. Aligned: 3 inside, 3 covered. Horizontal half shift: the middle bottom square is whole, two bottom edge squares and two top squares are partial, hence [1,5]. Both half shifts: 0 whole and 8 covered. The aligned grid gives an exact certificate; translations can make it worse. Optional hint: color cells that only touch separately from cells that contain gray. Extension: choose a shape with sloping edges and compare several translations; ask whether two translated certificates can help different ends of a range.

2. Cards 2-8 and 4-9 combine to 4-8. Cards 3-5 and 6-7 are inconsistent: the area would be simultaneously at most 5 and at least 6. Cards 1-6 and 4-4 combine to exactly 4. For possible pairs, a 2-by-2 unit square of area 4 works for both; a 2-by-3 rectangle of area 6 also works for the first. These are witnesses, not unique shapes. Endpoints are allowed. Optional hint: ask which areas one report rules out, while keeping both reports about the same object. Extension: introduce a third card or ask children to invent two cards, each of which contributes one endpoint of the combined range.

3. Use the polygon with vertices (0,0),(2,0),(2,2),(2-t,2),(2-t,t),(0,t). Its cell areas, bottom-left, bottom-right, top-right, are t,2t-t²,t, all strictly between 0 and 1. At t=1/4 total is 15/16<1. At t=3/4 total is 39/16>2. Both are connected and have no holes. Their perimeters happen to both be 8; area and perimeter need separate reasoning.

For an equal-area longer-boundary example, keep t=1/4 and replace the horizontal top edge over 0<=x<=1 in the bottom-left cell. For N waves use vertices with successive x spacings 1/(4N), heights t,t+1/8,t,t-1/8,t repeated. The positive and negative triangles cancel, so area remains 15/16; the height remains between 1/8 and 3/8, hence the same cell mask and connectivity persist. The changed edge has length sqrt(1+N²/4), so full perimeter is 7+sqrt(1+N²/4), exceeding any chosen bound for sufficiently large finite N. Children can argue that each up/down tooth contributes a fixed vertical amount, without calculating square roots. Optional hint: trade equal small triangles above and below one straight edge. Extension: set a minimum tooth size or number of vertices and investigate the new finite design limit. Holes and disconnected gray islands are forbidden in the printed task.
