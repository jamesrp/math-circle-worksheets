Mathematical kernels

1. A different object can certify an optimum. A deck has exactly one card for every subset of a fixed set of symbols, including the empty subset. A legal collection contains no two distinct cards such that every symbol of one appears on the other. Sharing some symbols is allowed; containment, not overlap, is forbidden. A nested chain is a group of cards ordered by containment. If the entire deck is partitioned into k chains, any legal collection contains at most one card from each chain, so has size at most k. A legal k-card collection then proves that the bound is attained. Every card must belong to exactly one chain in the certificate; just exhibiting some chains does not establish the bound. An inclusion-maximal collection need not have maximum size: the one-card collection containing all symbols cannot accept another card but is usually far from largest.

2. Complete elementary extremal theorems for small decks. For two symbols, the maximum is two; for three, three; for four, six. The lower bounds are the two singleton cards, the three singleton cards, and the six two-symbol cards, respectively. Here are independently checked covering certificates, using concatenation for a set and “empty” for its empty member. For two symbols: (empty < A < AB), (B). For three: (empty < A < AB < ABC), (C < AC), (B < BC). For four: (empty < A < AB < ABC < ABCD), (D < AD < ABD), (C < AC < ACD), (CD), (B < BC < BCD), (BD). They cover 4, 8, and 16 distinct cards with 2, 3, and 6 chains; every successive containment is valid. These proofs control all possible selections without listing them all. The general result is Sperner's theorem: among subsets of n symbols the maximum antichain size is choose(n, floor(n/2)). The small certificates prove only the stated small cases. Do not claim the general theorem from a pattern in three values, and do not make binomial coefficients, factorials, induction, or linear algebra prerequisites. Suggested emphasis by level

- K–1: make and compare collections from two- or three-picture decks; recognize containment, including the empty card, and count a small selection with oral support.
- Grades 2–3: maximize a complete small deck and connect a nested-group covering to an upper bound; no reading beyond picture symbols and counts up to sixteen.
- Grades 4–5: produce both a large legal collection and a certificate ruling out every larger one, distinguishing a locally unextendable choice from a true maximum.

Materials

At each of the three tables: two complete 16-card decks, each using four unmistakably different picture symbols (with small letter labels if useful), blank paper, pencils, and 20 selection counters. Cards should be about 5 × 5 cm with consistent symbol positions and enough blank margin to handle them. Identify the empty card with a tiny neutral border mark, not an extra symbol. Each deck needs a distinct back or corner mark so children never mix duplicate subsets into a single mathematical deck. Obtain a smaller n-symbol deck by retaining exactly the cards that avoid the unused symbols; do not erase symbols and create duplicate cards. Adults should cut the cards before the session. Provide an uncommitted supply of blank paper strips if children choose to organize chains; do not supply exactly the optimum number of strips or a fixed rank diagram. There is one card per subset in the active deck, and no reusing a card within a collection. The physical supplies permit paired work and free rearrangement rather than requiring children to copy all subsets repeatedly.
