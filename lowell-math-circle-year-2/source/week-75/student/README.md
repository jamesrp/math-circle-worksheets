# Twists on a cylinder student prototype

Independently reviewed prototype awaiting organizer review, unpiloted. Four US Letter student pages, Grades 4–5. Physical rehearsal remains untested: especially removable tape with yarn feeding at fixed rim marks, seam reading, and transferring drawings to a 6-inch-circumference, 2.4-inch-high cylinder. Per pair: print the single-sided packet at 100%, scissors, tape, two contrasting yarn strands about 1.2 metres each, pencil and ruler. The cutout has no seam overlap allowance; butt the edges and bridge with tape. Prerequisites: halves and quarters, adding and canceling signed counts, and following a drawing across identified edges. A mathematician should be available for the later invariance and minimum-crossing questions.

Build with `python3 build.py --out /chosen/output` using Python 3 and a normal TeX Live installation with pdflatex, TikZ, geometry, fancyhdr, and Latin Modern. Source entrypoint: `students.tex`; output: `students.pdf`. Run `python3 check_math.py` for exact checks.

The cylinder is held still. Re-laying a strand represents a new arc; the rigid paper sleeve is never described as literally deforming through a twist. The + map adds a height-proportional full turn, fixes each boundary point modulo a full circumference, and changes the strand winding by +1. The - map is its inverse. Physical intermediate manipulation is not asserted to be a boundary-fixed isotopy of the whole cylinder. For the sliding question, the arc stays on the cylinder and its two geometric endpoints remain fixed, with extra yarn fed if needed. Upward routes form a concrete sufficient family of simple joining arcs.

The crossing question uses identical endpoints, excludes both shared endpoints, and excludes shared subarcs and tangencies. For different windings, straight lifts attain max(|a-b|-1,0); equal winding needs a small bow of one route so the arcs are distinct. The difference-of-lifts intermediate-value argument establishes the lower bound for upward arcs. Minimum-intersection results do not change when both windings increase by the same integer. Twisting only one route changes the winding difference but can raise, lower, or preserve the minimum; (5,5) and (6,5) both have minimum zero. Route interiors remain strictly between the rims, with only their endpoints on the rims.

## Physical and facilitation handoff

Rehearse the exact tape/yarn/paper arrangement before calling the model physically ready: tight tape may prevent yarn feeding, loose tape may let the geometric endpoint slide, and a narrow 6-inch-circumference sleeve may buckle. The test is whether a child can alter a same-winding route while both marked endpoints remain fixed and the route stays on the surface. If the fastening fails, revise that detail or let a drawn route be the mathematical record; do not permit rim rotation or endpoint motion to evade the invariant. No physical rehearsal has been performed.

At a visible overlap, yarn represents the crossing of two projected surface routes. Three-dimensional lifting over another strand does not remove a surface intersection. Distinguish making a new route by lifting/re-laying, deforming a route continuously on the surface with fixed endpoints, and applying the whole-surface twist rule. The rigid sleeve is a routing model, not a literal deforming Dehn twist.

The first two pages can make a substantive first visit. The repeated-strip, whole-surface map and minimal-crossing arguments are readiness-dependent continuations; the four-page packet is not a requirement to complete everything in one session. Physical operation, representation changes and classroom pacing remain unpiloted. The later adult author should retain both the identical-endpoint hypothesis and the equal-winding distinct-route case.
