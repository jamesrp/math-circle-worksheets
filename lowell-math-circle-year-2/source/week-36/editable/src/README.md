# Week 36 revised student packet

K–1 and grades 2–3: four pages each. Grades 4–5: five pages. Draft and unpiloted. Build using `bash build.sh` with Python 3, a standard pdfLaTeX/TikZ installation and Poppler. The original diagrams use shape and fill independently; color is never needed.

## Readiness gate

The youngest packet is conditional. Before using it, an adult must establish that each child can judge shapes and fills independently. If that gate fails, stay with concrete one-attribute grouping using the physical tiles; do not present the nine-point maximum theorem as something the child has explored. This draft does not claim that gate has been tried with these children.

Use the nine separate 45 mm tiles in the outline. The small page-one diagrams are illustrations of pair completions, not cutouts or physical placement targets. The large parking mats are 180 mm square with 60 mm cells. The nine tiles may be moved on the table; the printed arrangements are storage/selection displays. Grid adjacency never defines an allowed three. No wraparound-grid representation or tic-tac-toe line rule is used in these packets.

The opening valid completion uses a bent arrangement with tile coordinates (circle, open), (triangle, solid), (square, striped). Its shapes and fills are each all different; tile positions have no mathematical role. The opening visual example separates both attributes under a valid completion, then shows a near miss with exactly two open fills. It leaves the maximum line-free size undisclosed. The revised upper packet leaves the maximum proof method open, and separately investigates intersections of triples and triples through a chosen tile. No ten-pair template prescribes the upper-bound argument. There is no requirement to catalogue 126 five-tile cases.

The independent builder enumerates 12 allowed triples, 54 four-tile line-free collections, no five-tile collection, and four partitions into three disjoint allowed triples. The inert banned-tile game was removed. The remaining play investigates whether legal collections can get stuck at different sizes. Every legal three-tile position has exactly three extensions, and every maximal legal collection has four tiles. The independent affine-coordinate checker verifies this along with all pair completions and triple intersections. These are finite checks, not a general cap-set result.
