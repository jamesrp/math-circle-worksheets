Week 14: Polygon triangulations and flips

Mathematical kernels

1. All the ways to split a polygon into triangles. Fix a convex polygon with n labeled corners; use only straight diagonals between its original corners, with no crossing interiors or added vertices, and fill the whole polygon with triangles. Every triangulation has n−2 triangles and n−3 diagonals: a diagonal splits the polygon into two smaller polygons, and induction adds their triangle and diagonal counts. For fixed labeled corners, the triangle touching a chosen boundary edge separates two independent smaller triangulations. Sorting by that triangle gives the Catalan recurrence C₀=1 and Cₘ₊₁=Σᵢ₌₀ᵐ CᵢCₘ₋ᵢ, with Cₙ₋₂ triangulations of an n-gon. A triangle, quadrilateral, pentagon, and hexagon have respectively 1, 2, 5, and 14 triangulations. The recurrence is a mathematical organizing principle, not a formula children must be taught first; finding a complete small collection and justifying that none are missing is already substantial. Rotating the page does not rename the fixed corners or identify different diagonal sets.

2. Local changes connect a whole configuration space, but need not preserve parity. Two triangles sharing a diagonal in a convex polygon form a convex quadrilateral. Replacing that diagonal with its other diagonal is one flip. Every triangulation reaches the fan at a chosen corner v: unless it is already a fan, a boundary edge of the union of triangles incident to v has another triangle across it; flipping that edge adds a diagonal incident to v without removing any such diagonal. Thus n−3−dᵥ flips suffice, where dᵥ counts the starting diagonals incident to v; they are also necessary, since one flip adds at most one. Reversing a route to the fan connects any two triangulations. The five pentagon triangulations form a five-cycle, giving an odd-length return route. This is a genuine new object related to Week 1's lozenge flip graph: the same configuration-space question has a different parity answer. Do not transfer the lozenge height/ribbon parity invariant to polygon flips. Associahedra and parenthesizations are adult mathematical context; neither is an extra prerequisite for this investigation.

Sources: Richard P. Stanley, *Catalan Numbers*, Tsuda lecture slides, PDF pages 16–18 and 26–28 (polygon counts and root-edge recurrence), https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/transparencies/tsuda.pdf. Vincent Pilaud, *Multitriangulations, pseudotriangulations and some problems of realization of polytopes*, Chapter 1, §1.1, Lemma 1.2 and Proposition 1.4 (triangle counts and fan proof of flip connectivity), https://www.ub.edu/comb/vincentpilaud/documents/reports/theseVincentPilaud.pdf. These sources supply mathematical background, not preselected student problems.

Suggested emphasis by level: K–1: make and distinguish complete triangle dissections of small convex polygons, with spoken rules and small counts; no reading or angle measurement prerequisite.
Grades 2–3: organize a complete small collection and discover what one diagonal replacement changes; fixed-corner identity and systematic comparison matter more than arithmetic.
Grades 4–5: investigate flip routes, odd return routes, and the fan's optimal-distance certificate; small counting and a picture-based always-works argument suffice, with recurrence optional.

Materials

For the ten-child group, prepare 10 pencils, 10 erasers, 10 short straightedges, 20 sheets of tracing paper, and at least 30 blank US Letter sheets in addition to the printed packet. These are preparation requirements, not a claim that special supplies have been inventoried. A main working polygon spans roughly 3–5 inches, with plainly distinguished original corners and enough separation for pencil diagonals and tracing. The polygons must be strictly convex; use fixed corner symbols/labels so that the definition of “different” is stable. Printed outlines are the fixed object: no moving corners, crossing diagonals, interior vertices, or extra edge points. Tracing may copy a configuration without rotating its labels. Paper and pencil carry the activity; pattern-block triangles are not the pieces here, since legal triangles can have different sizes and shapes. Pages print single-sided on US Letter at 100%; no physical tile scale is required.
