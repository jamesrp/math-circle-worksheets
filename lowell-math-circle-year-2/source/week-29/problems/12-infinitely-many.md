## prompt
Which pairs have infinitely many impossible lengths: 2 and 4; 3 and 6; 4 and 6; 3 and 5; 4 and 7? Find a rule that separates them and explain it.

## figure
draw(('ruled', 3, 8))

## key
\emph{Print guide, p. 3.} 2/4, 3/6 and 4/6 have infinitely many gaps (odd targets; nonmultiples of 3; odd targets and 2). The pairs 3/5 and 4/7 have only finitely many. The dividing line is a common divisor greater than 1; the coprime direction needs the argument on the next page.
