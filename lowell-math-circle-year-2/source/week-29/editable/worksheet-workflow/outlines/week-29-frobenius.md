Week 29: Two-length builders

Mathematical kernels

1. Eventual reachability and its short certificate. With unlimited copies of rods of positive integer lengths a and b, a target N is reachable precisely when N=ax+by for nonnegative integers x,y. Rod order is irrelevant; cutting and negative lengths are not allowed. If a consecutive targets are reachable, every larger target is reachable by repeatedly adding an a-rod. Thus a finite group of constructions can certify infinitely many future targets. For lengths 3 and 4 the impossible positive targets are exactly 1,2,5, since 6,7,8 are reachable and every larger target follows by adding 3. For 3 and 5 the gaps are exactly 1,2,4,7, followed by reachable 8,9,10.

2. The sharp last gap and the common-divisor obstruction. If gcd(a,b)>1, infinitely many positive integers are unreachable because every construction is divisible by that common divisor. If gcd(a,b)=1 and a,b>1, the largest unreachable integer is ab−a−b. For N>ab−a−b, the numbers 0,b,2b,…,(a−1)b have distinct residues modulo a; choose jb with N−jb divisible by a. The difference exceeds −a and hence is nonnegative. Conversely, a representation ab−a−b=ax+by would give a(x+1)+b(y+1)=ab; coprimality forces x+1≥b, an impossibility since y+1>0. In particular, 4/7 has final gap 17 and every target at least 18 is possible. The general formula is a facilitator destination, not a premise printed in the student questions.

This proposed rod adaptation is unpiloted.

Suggested emphasis by level: K–1 aligns and counts short rods, finds possible/impossible small lengths, and experiences repeating a construction further along a strip; grades 2–3 produce a short run of consecutive constructions that proves there will be no more gaps; grades 4–5 organize reachability and impossibility using remainders and common factors, with the sharp formula available after concrete evidence. Labels are readiness guides; multiplication and division with remainder are prerequisites for the general proof, not for entry.

Materials

Per working pair or trio: ten 3-unit rods, ten 4-unit rods, ten 5-unit rods, eight 7-unit rods, and a small optional set of 2-unit rods for a common-divisor comparison; paper or cardstock, scissors for adult preparation, pencils, removable target labels. Use a common physical unit of 1 cm, rods 1 cm wide, with printed unit divisions and length labels. The different lengths must be visibly distinguishable without depending on color alone. Use selected rod lengths at a time so the intended available set is unambiguous; unused sizes stay off the table. Target strips that physically align with rods must be printed at 100% and use the same 1 cm grid; a 0–24 strip fits landscape US Letter within half-inch margins. Shorter strips are preferable for the youngest group. The theorem assumes unlimited copies, but every printed finite construction must fit the supplied inventory. Whole-rod construction with no gaps/overlaps is the model; no coin totals table is required.
