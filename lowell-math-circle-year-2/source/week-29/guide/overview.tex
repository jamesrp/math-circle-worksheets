\subsection*{The mathematics in one view}
With unlimited copies of two positive integer rod lengths $a$ and $b$, a target $n$ is reachable exactly when $n = ax + by$ for nonnegative integers $x$, $y$. Rod order does not matter; cutting, overlaps, and negative rods are forbidden.

\textbf{Finite evidence can certify infinitely many targets.} If $a$ consecutive integers $N, \dots, N+a-1$ are reachable, then every integer at least $N$ is reachable: repeatedly add an $a$-rod to these $a$ starting builds.

\textbf{Exact boundary.} If $\gcd(a,b)=1$ and $a,b>1$, the largest impossible target is $ab-a-b$, so every target at least $(a-1)(b-1)$ works. If $\gcd(a,b)>1$, every target not divisible by that gcd is impossible, giving infinitely many gaps. A 1-rod makes every nonnegative target possible, so there is no largest positive gap in that case.

\subsection*{What each group is doing}
\textbf{K--1:} Investigate small builds, distinguish rearranging from a new combination, and physically continue a run. Counting and alignment to 18 are enough; adults can record. A repeated construction is a genuine explanation without the formula.

\textbf{Grades 2--3:} Make exhaustive small lists, then find a short certificate for no later gaps. Addition to 28 and willingness to organize cases help. The shared-factor example refutes an overbroad conjecture.

\textbf{Grades 4--5:} Explain completeness, compare last gaps, conjecture the formula, and justify eventual coverage for a chosen coprime pair. Multiplication and remainders support the last questions; the general formula proof is adult backup.
