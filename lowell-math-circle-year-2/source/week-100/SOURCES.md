# Sources and provenance

The student problems, drawings and facilitator guide are newly authored for this library. Mathematical facts are classical; no new research result is claimed. Source references identify mathematical context, not classroom validation of these worksheets. No downloaded book, paper, private record or borrowed workflow excerpt is included in this source package.

## Mathematical source

Oleksiy Dovgoshey, *Finite Ultrametric Balls*, §1, Propositions 1.2 and 1.5; Theorem 3.15 for finite ball families.
https://arxiv.org/html/1810.03128v2

The source gives the same-center and nesting properties of ultrametric balls. Our finite card worlds, clue-repair problem and worksheet progression are original specializations. The guide proves the relevant finite claims using nested containment and establishes the number-world inequality using divisibility. No completed p-adic field or research theorem is constructed by the activities.

## Exact connections

Weeks 67 and 69 use shortest-road distances and tree geodesics. Arbitrary tree vertices need not form an ultrametric: consecutive vertices have distances 1,1,2. Here cards are leaves and distance is merger height, not road length. Week 68's ends and Week 77's holes are different objects. The number continuation uses agreement modulo powers of 2 to define distance, rather than Week 96's factor sub-bags or Week 97's square-map collisions. Nonbinary groups are expressly allowed, unlike a forced binary routing tree.
