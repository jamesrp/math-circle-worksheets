Week 12: Pairings and paths: new restrictions and local changes

Mathematical kernels

1. Colored noncrossing pairings on six fixed ordered circle dots may join only unlike labels R/B. RBRBRB has 5 pairings, RRRBBB has 1, and RRBRBB has 2. In any balanced cyclic R/B word there is an adjacent unlike pair; remove it and recurse to construct a noncrossing unlike pairing. Equal counts are necessary and sufficient for existence. Unbalanced words cannot pair unlike colors. This new color restriction changes the count without changing the point labels or quotienting rotations.

2. Dyck paths with n up/down pairs and height at most two are in bijection with compositions of n: each excursion is U(UD)^kD, contributing k+1 pairs. Choosing break positions among n-1 gaps gives 2^(n-1) paths. For n=4, height bounds 1,2,3,4 give 1,8,13,14 paths. The bounded-height restriction is a new extremal/classification direction; plain complete enumeration was already in the base.

3. A local swap DU to UD raises a valley by two units and preserves nonnegative height. Its reverse is permitted only when the new path stays nonnegative. Every Dyck path reaches the mountain U^nD^n by raising valleys. Half the sum of intermediate vertex-height differences from the mountain is an integer that decreases by exactly one per raising move; it gives the exact shortest distance to the mountain. For n=4 the alternating path needs six moves. This turns familiar codes into a new move/optimization investigation; do not repeat base conversion exercises.
