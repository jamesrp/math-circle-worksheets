# Week 39 bonus adult solution notes

## Mathematical overview: facts, assumptions and limits
A journey is a finite ordered walk on a fixed graph, and shortening deletes only adjacent opposite traversals of the same road. Such reduction is unique. Every tree walk from one vertex to another reduces to their unique simple path; closed tree walks reduce to empty. New roads change the graph, so reduced closed walks may persist. A vertex of degree one cannot appear in a nonempty reduced closed journey, because visiting it creates an immediate reversal.

On the four-arm star in P1, each of the four leaves must acquire another incident road to occur in a reduced closed tour. Each new road touches only two leaves, so at least two additions are necessary. A perfect matching of the four leaves attains the bound. The resulting two triangles sharing H admit a six-step reduced tour through all four leaves, and six is optimal when there are only two added roads. Removing either addition leaves two degree-one outer stops and prevents any reduced closed tour visiting all four, although the other triangle still supports a surviving tour.

Return-walk counts concern unreduced histories. On a three-arm unit star, six-step closed walks are three successive outward/return excursions, hence 3³=27. On a star with each arm extended by one edge, six-step closed walks include nested excursions and number48. Every one still reduces to empty. Equal reduced journeys therefore do not imply equal counts of unreduced histories.

At a two-ring junction, whole loop words a and b freely concatenate. Capital A/B denote the corresponding reverse loop, so adjacent aA/Aa/bB/Bb cancel. Distinct loops a,b do not commute, but powers of a common reduced word do. The supplied finite card collection has exactly three commuting pairs. This finite result does not require the full free-group centralizer theorem; that theorem is adult context, not a K–5 prerequisite.

Grades 2–3 have physical route design and finite counting entries; Grades 4–5 can prove optimality/completeness and explore order trading. Prerequisites are replaying an ordered walk, recognizing immediate opposite road steps, counting to six, and maintaining an unchanged map. Macro words require reliable bridge from each symbol to a whole loop. No independent reading or abstract group notation is needed if an adult reads the task, but the child must own the route choices. No forced K–1 page is added to these investigations; the base pawn activities remain the concrete entry if this readiness gate is not met.

## Solutions, hints and extensions
**P1 / student p.1.** Two new roads are optimal. Exactly three unordered optimal additions exist: AB+CD, AC+BD, or AD+BC. A six-step witness for AB+CD is H-A-B-H-C-D-H. The other pairings have analogous tours. There are no immediate reversals. At least two roads are required because every outer stop needs a second incidence; with only two roads they must pair all four leaves. In that graph each excursion from H that survives is at least a triangle of3 steps. Visiting both triangles requires at least6 steps; the witness attains it. Closing either new road turns its two leaves back into degree-one stops. A nonempty reduced trip may survive around the other triangle but cannot visit all four outer stops. The phrase “keep every step after shortening” excludes adding disposable leaf excursions to a surviving triangle. Hint: try entering and leaving a leaf without using the same road backward. Extension: allow three added roads, or require a reduced tour that visits H only at start/finish; a five-step cycle through H and all four leaves uses three additions. Verify actual road identities rather than count apparent pencil crossings.

**P2 / student p.2.** Left unit star:27 six-step returns. Right extended star:48. A complete explanation groups walks by lengths between successive returns to H. In the unit star all returns consist of three two-step excursions:3 choices each. In the extended star: (2,2,2) gives27; (2,4) and (4,2) give 3×3 each, total18; one six-step excursion with no intermediate H return is H-A-D-A-D-A-H or its B/E and C/F versions, total3. Sum27+18+3=48. This grouping is adult scaffolding to offer only after children create examples. The raw words can be pooled by first arm or first return; a photograph/ordered route record is enough, without simultaneous tallies. Every trip reduces to empty because both maps are trees. Hint: ask whether there are walks that visit D/E/F before returning H. Extension: four-step counts are9 and12; use them to check a conjecture about growth, or add just one outer road and ask how many returns use it. Do not infer the six-step result solely from a small sample.

**P3 / student p.3.** Treat word cards as seven specific nonempty whole trips. Exactly these unordered pairs commute after reduction:

- a with aa: both orders reduce to aaa.
- ab with abab: both orders reduce to ababab.
- abA with abbA: both orders reduce to abbbA. Here abbA is the square of abA after the middle Aa cancellation.

All other18 pairs fail. The given aAb demonstration reduces to b. A complete finite solution may show a table of21 tested pairs with reduced outputs; the verifier checks both macro reduction and expansion to the actual six individual ring roads. For a deeper explanation, recognize powers of a common journey: the third pair is a whole left excursion, one/two right loops, and the return along the left loop in reverse. Shared net loop counts do not suffice to determine commutation. Hint: save one reduced route/word, reuse the individual step cards for the other concatenation, then compare. Keep the word-selector cards intact; never delete a whole composite card to cancel one symbol within it. Replay a disputed reduction on the unchanged mat. Extension: create a new pair using both rings that commutes but is not literally identical, and another with the same net loop counts that does not. The full theorem that commuting nontrivial free-group elements are powers of a common word may be supplied as adult context, never required as the elementary entry.
