## Required small edits

1. **Specify an end removal in the biggest-square rule.** The opening currently says only to remove the largest square that fits the remaining rectangle. Removing a square from the middle can split the remainder, so that statement alone does not define the intended Euclidean process. Use a compact rule such as “Remove the biggest square from one end of the remaining rectangle, then repeat.” This applies to the stipulated biggest-square process; it must not constrain the free minimum tilings on the right boards. A familiar whole-group demonstration is enough; another answer-revealing tiling example is unnecessary.
2. **Make the loss-of-size question about genuinely different sizes.** “Can two different rectangles have the same recipe?” can be answered by drawing the same rectangle rotated, without meeting the mathematical destination. “Can different-sized rectangles have the same recipe?” or “Can a larger rectangle have the same recipe?” keeps the construction open while excluding this trivial interpretation.
