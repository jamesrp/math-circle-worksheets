Week 46: Two boards that forget their starts

Mathematical kernels

1. Two rows of three two-color counters may start differently. At each step choose a position and a new color, then set that position on both rows to the named color, even if it already matches. Under the same instruction history, a position's initial value is irrelevant after its first overwrite. Once every position has been overwritten, every possible starting board gives exactly the same result. Matching positions never separate. By contrast, shared toggles preserve all initial disagreements. These deterministic pathwise claims are complete accessible coupling mathematics.

2. With independent uniform position choices and independent fair fresh colors, coordinate refresh is a Markov chain with uniform stationary distribution. The time by which all coordinates have been selected bounds coupling time. The last color assigned at each position is independent and fair, conditional on the position history covering every position. Do not claim that three updates suffice, or that any two boards can agree only after all positions are visited. Numerical mixing bounds are facilitator context, not a requirement. Show overwrite explicitly: RBR and BRB, instruction middle→B, give RBR and BBB. A toggle is a different operation and needs its own example.

Sources: Gautam Iyer, CMU probability notes, Coupling, coordinate refresh on the hypercube: https://www.math.cmu.edu/~gautam/c/2025-326/notes/conv3-coupling.html. Exact three-position histories independently checked. This asks about forgetting under common randomness, unlike fall toggle reachability or finite-state minimum memory.

Suggested emphasis by level:

K–1: make two boards agree through shared overwrite instructions and track which starting colors still matter.
Grades 2–3: explain the once-overwritten guarantee with three movable visited markers.
Grades 4–5: contrast resetting and toggling, and use a shared instruction history to justify forgetting of arbitrary starts.

Materials

Per child: two three-slot boards with slots at least 35×35 mm, six two-sided red/blue counters at least 25 mm wide, three small visited-position markers, and six equally selectable instruction cards combining one of three positions with one of two colors. Ten kits require 20 boards, 60 two-sided counters, 30 visited markers and 60 instruction cards. Draw with replacement from an opaque cup. Position icons should match board labels. No long state graph or history table is required; boards themselves display the comparison. Avoid the word flip for the overwrite rule.
