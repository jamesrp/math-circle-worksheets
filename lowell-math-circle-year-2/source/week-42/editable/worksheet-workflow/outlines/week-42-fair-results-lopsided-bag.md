Week 42: Fair results from a lopsided bag

Mathematical kernels

1. An independent source with the same fixed red probability p on every draw, 0<p<1, can generate fair binary outputs: take disjoint pairs of draws, ignore RR and BB, and assign opposite outputs to RB and BR. The kept pair types have equal probability p(1−p). For a bag of equally selectable labeled counters R1,R2,R3,B, the 16 ordered pairs contain three RB and three BR; reversal pairs these outcomes exactly. Children can investigate a fair-output rule rather than being told its conclusion. Counts in a short run need not be equal, and a fixed number of draws does not guarantee an output. Return and mix the counter before every draw.

2. The equality relies on symmetry of the pair mechanism; changing the source between first and second draw can defeat it. A source that always produces one color never finishes. The complete four-counter pair model is an exact proof opportunity without fraction notation. A small explicit input → pair → output example must explain an unfamiliar pair machine without revealing the whole target solution; if a 4-by-4 board is introduced, show how one row/column choice names one ordered pair.

Sources: Levin, Peres and Wilmer, Markov Chains and Mixing Times, Appendix B.2, von Neumann unbiasing: https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/260-FMIE/Levin-Peres-Wilmer.pdf. The labeled finite model was independently checked. This is new relative to the produced 10+18 and is not nontransitive dice.

Suggested emphasis by level:

K–1: physically process contrasting two-color pair cards and discover a balanced pairing; adult reads and handles recording.
Grades 2–3: design a rule and pair the equally likely labeled outcomes by reversal, using small counts.
Grades 4–5: justify fairness from the mechanism and diagnose a changed-source failure; no required probability fractions.

Materials

For each child: one opaque draw cup, four identical-feeling counters at least 25 mm wide (three red, one blue), a two-position mat with 35 mm spaces, and reusable output markers in two shapes. Small identity marks R1,R2,R3,B can be on the counters for exact-outcome work, with picture alternatives for nonreaders. Supply spare counter sets for changing contents. Ten cups and forty starting counters total. Physical trials are short (approximately six to eight pairs before a reset or new question); do not require a frequency ledger. Print any supplied pair cards and row/column boards large enough for fingers and counters. The writer chooses tasks; do not force all bands through a 16-cell table.
