## prompt @12
A friend says, "My rule is fair, so six pairs must give equal numbers of squares and circles." Another says, "At least one shape must appear." Decide which claims follow from fairness.

## figure
lines(17,7)

## key
Neither claim follows. Six RR pairs produce no shape; BR, RR, RR, RR, RR, RR produces one square and no circles. Each story has positive probability under the original mechanism. Fairness says the possible outcomes have equal chances when a shape is produced; it does not force equal realized totals or a finite waiting bound.

Facilitator checkpoint: keep three claims separate - a legal example shows possibility, a finite trial suggests a conjecture, and equal-weight exhaustive counting or a bijection proves the exact probability claim.
