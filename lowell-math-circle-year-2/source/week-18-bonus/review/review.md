## Verdict

Revise before release. The packet has three genuine investigations: known-hole recovery (Problems 1–2), single parity detection and invisible damage (3–4), and spatial localization/invisible patterns (5–7). These are distinct from the base hidden-flip codebook work; seven numbered problems are not seven investigations. The mathematical direction and the honest Grades 2–5 label are appropriate. The concrete counter games and roomy records provide worthwhile work, and the later proof questions can support a return visit.

## Required corrections

1. **Problem 6 must require a nonempty change.** “Find the fewest different counters you can flip while still passing every check” admits flipping zero counters. The intended invisible-damage investigation therefore has a literal answer of zero. Say that at least one counter must be flipped, while keeping the minimum and explanation open. Do not print the four-corner discovery.

2. **Show the new array-check completion convention before Problem 5.** The strip check has the required input → paired faces → output example on page 2, but the first two-dimensional check mat is introduced only by prose and an unfinished task instance. Add a small non-task array example with data, one meaningful row/column completion step, and the completed array; distinguish the check row and column including their shared corner. This demonstrates how to set the checks without revealing how single-change localization or invisible rectangles work. The new representation should not depend on repeated adult rescue.

## Further improvements

- **Problem 2's record depicts four six-position rows while asking for the shortest key.** The shortest key is already supplied in Problem 1, so the real new work is the impossibility of shortening it. Preserve that explanation; avoid letting the six-cell record imply a required six-entry code. A record where children can visibly choose the used length, or a more neutral drawing space, would better match the question.
- **Keep compact diagrams clearly as records when loose mats carry the counters.** The main Problem 5 mat has 25 mm cells. Several other diagrams are smaller: Problem 1's reference cells are 17.5 mm wide and its row is only 12.25 mm high; Problem 2 uses 13 mm-wide record cells; the page-2 five-counter strip is only 17.5 mm high. These do not accommodate the full stated 15–20 mm counter range within cells. Compact key/reference diagrams and “mark” records are useful, but the adult preparation must provide the separate full-size strips/mats promised by the outline. If the page-2 strip is intended for direct placement, make both cell dimensions at least 25 mm. Do not claim physical fit from this digital review.
- Problem 7 would be clearer if it explicitly begins with a mat that passes all checks, and specifies that the two choices are compared from that same starting mat. The prior protocol makes this inferable, but this is essential evidence for the ambiguity question.
- A sans-serif face would match the current Week 1 style. This is a consistency improvement, not a reason to remove the mathematical work.

## Page evidence and substance

- **Page 1:** One-hole transmission is concrete and the four message rows are legible. Twelve message/hole trials give enough comparison work. The shortest-key lower bound is a genuine deeper question, although its working answer can be copied from the preceding key.
- **Page 2:** The parity convention example has matching labels and a meaningful pairing intermediate. Problems 3–4 distinguish detection from recovery and ask for the damage classification, without giving its rule away. The flip-count table and rule space are usable.
- **Page 3:** The large initial array is labeled and correctly separates data from checks by both shading and heavy lines. Repeated sender/receiver attempts are substantial. The new completion convention still needs its own visual bridge.
- **Page 4:** The two minimum-pattern records and two ambiguity records are legible and have space for explanation. The minimum task needs the nonempty condition. The two-flip question is a meaningful limit of localization rather than a trivial changed-number version.
