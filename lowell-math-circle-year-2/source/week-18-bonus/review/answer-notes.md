# Week 18 final answers and assumptions

Shared Grades 2–5 packet: reading may be supplied by an adult; entry requires fixed-position matching, a distinction between an open-faced counter and a covered counter, and pairing/counting to five. Later arguments concern guarantees and invisible patterns. The three investigations are visible erasures (Problems 1–2), single even-parity detection (3–4), and spatial parity localization/limits (5–7). None repeats a shortest one-hidden-flip codebook from the base packet.

1. All four key words 000, 011, 101, 110 differ pairwise in two positions. Deleting any single coordinate while keeping its position visible leaves four different two-bit observations. Thus every transmitted message is recovered. There are twelve message/hole combinations. An erased counter must never close up the row or look like an open-face counter.
2. Three positions suffice using the displayed key. Two do not: four messages require all four binary pairs, and covering either position leaves only two possible visible faces, so at least two messages give the same observation. The explanation can use four cards and just two surviving possibilities. General adult fact: recovery from at most e known erasures holds exactly when all distinct key words have distance at least e+1.
3. The check counter is the parity of the four data counters. One flip, including a flip of the check counter, changes even to odd, so it is always detected. Detection identifies damage, not necessarily the original message. The printed worked convention uses data 101 (two filled faces paired) and a check of 0, yielding 1010.
4. With five counters and each selected position flipped once, exactly 2 or 4 flips can pass; 1, 3, 5 never pass. Toggling each chosen position changes the filled-count parity; changing the starting row cannot change this classification. Zero also passes but is outside the requested nonempty choices. The receiver cannot infer the original message from an arbitrary damaged row.
5. Displayed completion, in row order: 1100 / 0101 / 1001 / 0000. The data rows have parity 0,1,1 and data columns parity 0,0,0. The bottom-right check is 0. Completing data-row checks and data-column checks is always consistent at the corner because the same data total determines both parities. One changed position causes exactly its full row and full column to be odd; their intersection locates the change, even if it lies in the shaded checks. Reflip it to restore the mat.
6. Four distinct flips are the nonempty minimum. The final prompt explicitly requires at least one chosen counter and exactly one flip of each chosen counter; flipping the same counter twice is excluded. Any four rectangle corners work; for example (row1,col1),(row1,col2),(row2,col1),(row2,col2), and the analogous rectangle rows1,3/cols2,4. An invisible nonempty pattern has an even number of changed positions in every row and column. One changed cell forces a second in its row and a third in its column; the other endpoint row/column then force at least a fourth. Alternatively one, two, or three changes always leave an affected row or column odd. There are C(4,2)^2=36 four-flip invisible patterns.
7. Yes. Positions (1,1),(2,2) and positions (1,2),(2,1) both make rows1,2 and columns1,2 odd. The checks do not locate two changes uniquely. If both changes share a row, no row is odd and exactly their two columns are odd; the affected row is not identified. If they share a column, the symmetric limitation applies.

All claims concern distinct flips in fixed binary positions. More than one flip at one position cancels and is excluded by the phrasing. Diagrams treat filled/open faces as binary values and the shaded cells as ordinary counters with an additional check role. Mathematical verification is separate from physical readiness; handling and fit are untested.
