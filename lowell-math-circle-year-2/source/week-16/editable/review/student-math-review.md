## Grades 2–3 and grades 4–5: page 3, Problem 3

**Located issue: the enumeration instruction permits an answer-changing reading.**

Both pages begin with the exact sentence:

> Fill small triangles with R, B, and Y in every different way, counting turns and flips as the same.

The intended task is to enumerate all vertex labelings using the available letters, with repeated letters allowed, and classify their numbers of R–B doors. However, “with R, B, and Y” can also mean that each triangle must contain all three letters. Elsewhere in these packets, that same phrase is consistently used to identify an all-three-letter triangle. Under that reading, the task collapses to one filling up to turns and flips, and the necessary zero-door and two-door cases disappear.

**Independent evidence.** Exhaustively enumerating the 27 assignments of R, B, or Y to three vertices, and identifying assignments related by turns or flips, gives 10 classes:

- RRR, BBB, YYY, RRY, RYY, BBY, BYY: zero doors.
- RRB, RBB: two doors.
- RBY: one door.

Requiring every triangle to contain all three letters instead leaves just the last class. Thus the two readings give different complete answers, 10 versus 1, and only the first supplies the cases needed for the later path/parity explanation.

**Smallest fix in both packets.** Replace only the first sentence with:

> Write R, B, or Y on each dot in every different way, counting turns and flips as the same.

Keep the rest of each problem and its diagram unchanged. The other five problems and their diagrams in each older band check out.
