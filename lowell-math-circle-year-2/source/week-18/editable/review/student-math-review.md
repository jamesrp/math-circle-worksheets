## Grades 2–3, page 4, Problem 4: complete answer exceeds the recording diagram

**Exact text:** “Make four-counter keys for the two pictures with exactly two filled circles in each row. Find every key that always works. Swapping the pictures counts as a different key.”

**Diagram:** Four triangle/square pairs of blank four-cell strips, at source y-coordinates 4, 8.6, 13.2, and 17.8. The diagram therefore provides four key slots.

**What is asked and intended outcome:** Find all reliable keys, keeping the triangle and square assignments distinct. There are six, not four.

**Independent evidence:** Enumerating all six length-four binary rows with exactly two filled positions, and directly generating every unchanged or one-flip outcome for each row, yields exactly these six ordered (triangle, square) keys, where 0 is empty and 1 is filled:

- (0011, 1100)
- (0101, 1010)
- (0110, 1001)
- (1001, 0110)
- (1010, 0101)
- (1100, 0011)

Two weight-two rows have an even number of differing positions. Their one-change outcome sets are disjoint only when all four positions differ, so the square row must be the complement of the triangle row. Each of the six choices for the triangle gives one key. The four slots cannot contain the complete answer and may suggest that finding four finishes the task. The mathematical task itself is sound; the issue is the diagram's capacity for its explicitly requested exhaustive answer.

**Smallest fix:** Add “Use more paper if needed.” This preserves the exhaustive task without revealing the answer count or shrinking the counter cells.
