# Mathematical overview

Copies alone preserve the all-red and all-blue states, so those two states can never merge. Each copy leaves every coordinate dependent on one original coordinate. A color reset creates a constant coordinate. Exactly one reset followed by two appropriate copies synchronizes all eight three-slot starts, and three instructions is optimal: with fewer, at least one of the three coordinates has never been a destination and still retains its initial color. This universal claim is stronger than repairing a chosen pair.

For independent uniform draws of position tickets with replacement, coverage occurs in 6 of the 27 length-three stories and 36 of the 81 length-four stories. Each story of a given length has equal probability. No finite deadline guarantees coverage: a story repeating ticket 1 leaves two positions unseen. The probability of never covering is zero, since the probability a particular position is missing after m draws is (2/3)^m, and the chance at least one is missing is at most 3(2/3)^m. This is an eventual probabilistic statement, not an observed classroom result. Without replacement, three draws cover all positions deterministically.

For S=(set left to red) and T=(cyclic left rotation), STSTS makes every start RRR in five instructions. Three S instructions are necessary because only S can remove one surviving original dependency; two T instructions are necessary to bring all three original slots to the left. Thus five is minimal. The positions may move: counting physical slots visited by a reset is insufficient for this device.

Children discover concrete obstructions and design universal words (2-5), then distinguish exact finite history counts from a random deadline (4-5). Trials suggest a claim. The monochromatic counterexample, dependency argument, and exhaustive history counts establish the printed conclusions. The familiar base rules are not automatically valid after changing to copies or rotation.

Unpiloted; exact counter fit, ticket handling, and rotating a row have not been physically rehearsed. Page 1 is an accessible entry for children who follow left-to-right positions and can copy a color. Reading can be supplied by an adult. The universal/shortest questions suit grades 2-5, by readiness. Both boards use the same instruction but copy their own current color. Ask for a legal next instruction. Let the partner check each move. # Precise solutions, hints, and extensions

1. RRR and BBB cannot match using any number of copies, since every copy leaves each board monochromatic. RESET 1 to R; COPY 1 to 2; COPY 1 to 3 sends every start to RRR. A story with at most two instructions cannot target all three destination slots, so a starting difference at an untouched slot survives. This proof concerns a fixed story, independent of the starts. Optional hint: place a letter x, y, or z under each original counter, move that letter with a copy, and erase it only when reset. Extensions: constrain which directed copies are allowed; synchronization from one reset is possible exactly when the reset site can reach every site in that directed graph, if repeated copies are allowed. Test small graphs before generalizing.

2. Three draws: all three labels must appear once, so 3 x 2 x 1 = 6. Four draws: one label appears twice and the other two once. Choose the repeated label (3), its two positions (6), and the order of the remaining labels (2): 36. Thus probabilities are 2/9 and 4/9. The 81 stories may be pooled across pairs; children need not transcribe every story. Without replacement the six possible length-three stories all cover. With replacement, 111...1 is a counterexample for every chosen finite deadline. Optional hint: first sort covering four-draw stories by the repeated ticket. Extension: if each visited position also receives an independent fair new color, then conditional on a covering position story the final three colors are independent fair colors (use last visits), even without replacement. This does not hold for an arbitrary stopping rule inspecting colors.

3. From (x,y,z), STSTS gives (R,y,z), (y,z,R), (R,z,R), (z,R,R), (R,R,R). Neither rotation nor a reset at an already constant dependency removes a new original color. Each S removes at most one, requiring at least three S's. With fewer than two T's at most two original counters can have visited the reset position. The displayed five-letter word attains both bounds. Optional hint: move three differently marked counters and watch which original ones remain. Extension: n slots require 2n-1 operations in the same left-rotation/left-reset model, using S(TS)^(n-1); argue that n resets and at least n-1 rotations are needed. A requirement to restore the original orientation changes the optimum and is not imposed here.
