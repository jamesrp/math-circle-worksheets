# Week 92: scope, source evidence, and readiness

Status: new unpiloted investigation within the existing tent-map theme. Proposed number allocated by the batch coordinator after main inventory; reserved Weeks 85-91 are untouched.

The new question is what remains predictable when a starting point is known only to lie in a small interval. Children move points and whole ranges through a distance-and-doubling rule; the destination is an exact uncertainty-set statement, not a claim that irregular-looking arithmetic is random.

## Primary mathematical source

Gerald Teschl, *Ordinary Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems*, author's online preliminary version, §11.3, printed pp.297-298 (PDF pp.308-309), equations (11.13)-(11.14):
https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~gerald/ftp/book-ode/ode.pdf

The source was downloaded to ignored `external-resources/teschl-ode.pdf` and its relevant text inspected October 17, 2026. Equation (11.14) supplies the exact dyadic affine pieces of the iterates, and the paragraph below states that each cell maps onto the unit interval. The scale-16 interval examples and classroom range apparatus are original adaptations. Sections 11.4-11.5 concern other parameter regimes/symbolic dynamics and are not necessary for this packet's proof. No borrowed book is included in a source package or Git.

The all-interval eventual-coverage conclusion uses a genuine positive-width interval. A singleton is excluded. A symmetric pair may merge; an interval may stay the same width, but its width cannot shrink. The writer caught and corrected a preliminary outline claim to the contrary: across midpoint8 the image width is twice the larger of the two half-widths, at least their sum. A short list of tested points cannot establish the image of a continuum interval, especially when it crosses the midpoint.

## Pedagogy consulted and inference

*Math Circle by the Bay*, Preface printed viii-x (PDF9-11), authorized local original verified against the public manifest SHA-256, describes topics with deep context and connections, independent problem solving, manipulatives, and class-dependent timing extending across years. Its authors report rarely repeating a topic for the same children. The decision to make this a clearly named encore with a different question, and to separate finite experiments from full-range explanation, is our adaptation to this library's returning-child requirement; it is not a claim about the book's curriculum.

Grades 2-3 can operate a point map, compare pairs, and move bands with adult reading. The continuity of the printed track (all positions, not only ticks) must be established in the launch. Grades 4-5 extend to exact range certificates and dyadic subdivisions. K-1 is an optional oral materials demonstration, not a separate worksheet. One adult anchored to each operating table is realistic; an unfamiliar two-stage apparatus must be rehearsed before teaching. Physical rehearsal and classroom piloting are untested.
