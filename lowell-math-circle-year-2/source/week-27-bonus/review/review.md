Those sentences are the computed checks, but no ordered preference strips are shown from which children can make them. ## Other recommendations and limits

- The score is expressly defined by rank numbers and is never described as measured happiness. Keep that artificial scoring objective separate from mutual blocking in the guide; a minimum total is not evidence of stability.
- Problem 2's first profile genuinely allows exhaustion/unpaired objects, and the second permits multiple stable partner assignments with the same unpaired objects. The rule correctly requires mutual acceptability and ranks every allowed partner above being unpaired. Keep unpaired tokens visible throughout the check.
- For page 3, distinguish the two side-by-side preference profiles clearly during launch. It may help to label the corresponding record rows “first lists” and “second lists” as diagram labels, but a child can also make that record choice independently. All tokens are one type, appropriately removing the old two-side cue.
- The roommate impossibility needs the complete three-pairing argument with a blocker for each. A contrast profile with mutual first-choice pairs is useful and prevents the overgeneralization that one-group pairing always fails.
- Problem 4 preserves worthwhile deeper reasoning. Failure to invent a counterexample does not prove the matched-set invariant; the guide should supply an object/alternating-path explanation when ready. This is optional return work, not a requirement that the younger group reproduce a theorem proof.
- Keep one reference matching unchanged while checking a blocker. Do not move partners as the first person's preference is checked; the second check must refer to the same state. Partner/referee support should enforce that action without choosing all blocking decisions.
- The printed profiles and pair/unpaired tables externalize most of the state. Prepare the actual rewritable strips, tokens and links so score calculation does not crowd out the child's matching choices. A supported roommate trial is not an automatic independent K–1 entry.
