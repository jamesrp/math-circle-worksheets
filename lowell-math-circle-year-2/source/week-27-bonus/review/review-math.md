- Page 4, Problem 4 asks whether child-designed strict omitted lists can admit two stable pairings with different unmatched objects. They cannot under the stated mutual-acceptability and unpaired conventions. Independent supporting enumeration checked all 625 strict incomplete 2×2 profiles, including asymmetric omissions, and 2,048 3×3 allowed-edge/ranking profiles; every profile had stable matchings and invariant unmatched sets. The general proof is needed for all designed profiles: in the symmetric difference of two matchings, a component starting at an object unmatched in M but matched in N is a finite alternating path. M-stability forces the first N-partner to have a strictly preferred M-partner; N-stability forces that partner to have a strictly preferred N-partner, and so on. A component of matchings cannot repeat after starting at an unmatched endpoint. Either terminal side would create the just-forced blocking pair, a contradiction. Swap the matchings and the two sides to conclude equality of matched sets. Strictness and acceptable partners above being unpaired are essential; experimentation alone does not prove the theorem.
